## Replace two disjoint complete paths before maximization

For pure prime powers with prime p0 and first omitted depth m, let
r=1/p0, let a_i be the head-cross coefficient on carrier i, and let
c_i be its raw density cap. The complete cross/self-pair blocks are

    pair:   r^m max_i [a_i/(1-r)+c_i*r/(1-r)^2],
    square: r^m max_i [(2a_i+c_i)/(1-r)
                                  +2c_i*r/(1-r)^2]. (AM6)

Use p0=3,m=3 with a_c=sum_s p(c,s)B(c,s), and p0=5,m=2
with a_s=sum_c E(c,s)B(c,s). Their cap vectors are c3 and c5.
Different carriers contain disjoint prime cylinders. Within one carrier,
a compatible pair is contained in its deeper cylinder. At depth d, after
n_i previous choices of carrier i, the pair reward is consequently at
most p0^-d(a_i+c_i*n_i), and the square reward is at most
p0^-d(2a_i+c_i+2c_i*n_i).
The all-count discounted identities checked in265 prove(AM6) for
arbitrary residues along each path, including choices which change
carrier; nesting is not assumed. The checker re-evaluates those
identities for every new head coefficient.

Each path block replaces only its own cross, self-pair and, for the
square, diagonal terms in(AM5). Pure3 and pure5 are disjoint original
families. All mixed cross and pair terms retain their full payments.
The replacement is an identity of assigned bound expressions, not
subtraction of an upper bound on an unknown actual moment.

For a fixed head, all path quantities and T(B) are independent of
