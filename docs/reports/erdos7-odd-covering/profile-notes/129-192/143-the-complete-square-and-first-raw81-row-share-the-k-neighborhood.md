## 1. The complete square is an LCM-weighted cylinder sum

For each pair of independently labelled test congruences, their
intersection is empty or a cylinder at the least common multiple
modulus. For one prime, the number of ordered exponent pairs whose
maximum is k equals

    (k+1)^2-k^2=2k+1=W(k).

Because there is at most one original label for each modulus, summing
over the full exponent inventory gives an upper bound even when some
original labels are absent. Let C_(a,b) be a cap for every actual
surviving cylinder of modulus3^a*5^b, and B_(a,b) its raw-source cap.
The exact ordered-pair decomposition used in84 gives

    integral_mu A^2 <=Z+(2/3)*R,
    Z=sum_(a,b>=0)W(a)*W(b)*C_(a,b),
    R=sum_(a,b>=0)W(a)*W(b)*B_(a,b).             (SR4)

Indeed the zero-seven intersections use their surviving caps. At
seven depth e>=1, discard mixed-seven deletion and use the complete
raw cap6*B_(a,b)/(5*7^e). Its full weighted sum is

    sum_(e>=1)6*(2e+1)/(5*7^e)=2/3.

All terms are nonnegative; the passage from finite original families
to the full majorizing series follows by monotone summation. Residues
at different depths are never required to be nested.
