# Coloring literature: actual matching reconfiguration and complete repair choices

Strict Hall surplus gives a direct, cited connectivity criterion for
full root-to-cofactor matchings at one fixed actual source, after
contracting the forced columns of 364. It changes matching witnesses,
not the original AP family. Global AP repair requires a different
interface: complete choices of actual classes, with every joint
coverage liability retained. Conflict-free perfect-matching results
are relevant to that interface, but their degree and conflict bounds
have not been established for the AP choices.

This is an ordinary mathematical literature and interface report.
The cited statements are source-checked, not independently certified
proofs of the papers. No new Lean verification, literature novelty,
or resolution of unrestricted Erdős #7 is claimed. The period-12,
period-144 and affine period-921600 controls below are actual covers
with even moduli; they are not odd-cover or extremal-cover counterexamples.

separately preserve its actual labels and joint source.
The search cutoff is 20 September 2026. These five entries report
the inspected preprint versions, without asserting journal

## 2. Forced contraction and strict Hall at one actual source

Keep the same original classes `A_d=a_d mod d`, their complete period,
and all prime-power heights. Fix a prime `q` and an actual source
`y=(a_q,t,x) in Priv_q`. Let `H_y` be the bipartite graph whose left
side is the set of nonprime first-q roots and whose right side is the
set of actual nontrivial numerical cofactors. An edge `(r,m)` exists
only when an original `q^e m` covers the point `(r,t,x)`. This fixes
the same tail and cofactor for every edge. Under comparable-class
disjointness, at most one original witnesses an active `(r,m)`:
two such originals would be comparable and intersect. Retain that
original label and its height; the graph creates no new AP label.

We discuss matchings saturating every left root. Their existence at
this particular source is an explicit condition. The common-tail
existence statement of 354 does not assert it for every tail.

For `F_q` from 364, each `m in F_q` has a distinct degree-one root
`r_m=a_(qm) mod q`, with its actual height-one child `qm`. Every
full-root matching therefore contains

    M_F = {(r_m,m) : m in F_q}.

Delete these roots and these colors to obtain `H_y^-`, with left side
`R^-`. Deleting and adding back `M_F` gives a bijection between full
matchings of `H_y` and `R^-`-saturating matchings of `H_y^-`, preserving
the single-root reconfiguration adjacency. If `R^-` is empty there
is one matching. Otherwise impose the additional condition

    |N_(H_y^-)(I)| >= |I|+1   for every nonempty I subset R^-.

Wdowinski's Corollary 6.12 directly gives connectedness: one may
change the cofactor assigned to one root at a time, using a currently
unused cofactor and preserving a full matching after each change.
The forced edges stay fixed. The cited paper itself notes that
Corollaries 6.12 and 6.13 are consequences of an existing matching
reconfigurability criterion; this application is not a new theorem.

The adjacency in section 6.3 matters. Its hypergraph formulation
removes the root endpoint from each incidence, retaining a distinct
root-colored singleton-edge instance `{m}` for each `(r,m)`. Different
roots with the same cofactor remain different instances. Directly
coloring the original bipartite matching complex by roots would give
the wrong adjacency: the old and new edges share that root, so their
union is not an ordinary matching.

Forced contraction alone does not imply the strict condition. An
isolated forced edge together with a `K_(2,2)` leaves `K_(2,2)` after
contraction. Its two full matchings cannot change one root at a time
without reusing a color. This is an abstract graph control, with no
AP realization asserted.

All of this takes place inside one fixed `H_y`. It changes no
residue, modulus, private mass or cover. Comparing matchings at
different sources, proving the strict Hall surplus for the actual
source, and turning a matching into an improving whole-cover edit
are separate obligations.

## 3. Actual tail availability can be disconnected

Use the actual period-144 fixture in
[the private-vector checker](../../frontier/cover-geometry/prime_private_swap_vector.py):

    (0 mod 2), (0 mod 3), (1 mod 4), (5 mod 6),
    (7 mod 24), (7 mod 36), (19 mod 48),
    (67 mod 72), (91 mod 144).

It is a whole irredundant cover with distinct moduli and disjoint
comparable classes, but is not divisor-closed. At `q=2`, retain the
full height `4`, so `t in Z/8Z` and `x in Z/9Z`. The prime-private
cofactor region is `R_2={1,2,4,5,7,8}`. Fix `x=1`.

For color `m=3`, the original `6`-class is incompatible with this
cofactor because its residue modulo 3 is 2. The compatible original
color-3 prefixes are exactly

    label 24: t = 3 mod 4;
    label 48: t = 1 mod 8.

Their descendant subtrees are disconnected in the natural binary
tail tree. Their least common ancestor is `t=1 mod 2`, where no
compatible original color-3 prefix is available. Taking the tree
convex hull therefore adds false availability at intermediate nodes.
Interpreting a newly filled node as a covering prefix is also invalid:
it would claim availability on parts of that cylinder not covered
by the original color-3 classes.

The actual point `y=10` is in `Priv_2`, with `x=1`. Reset only its
first 2-digit to the other root, retaining its higher 2-digits and
cofactor. The resulting point is `91 mod 144`, with `t=5`. Its
complete original hit set is exactly `{144}`, so color 3 is absent.
This is a concrete loss of fidelity, not a missing formal tree node.

Huang's connected-support requirement consequently fails for this
natural availability representation. Adding hull nodes does not
prove the compulsory singleton-loop condition either. This control
does not exclude a different faithful tree representation, and its
even/nonextremal scope does not establish failure in every hypothetical
odd extremal cover.

## 4. The natural per-color stars fail the spectral threshold

At a fixed source take `a=q-1` roots and `M` cofactor vertices.
If a full matching is possible, `M>=a`; put `b=M`. For each cofactor
`m`, let `G_m` consist of its incident root/cofactor edges, with all
other vertices retained as isolates. This is `K_(1,d_m)` plus
isolates, where `d_m<=a`. Its signless Laplacian spectral radius is
`d_m+1<=a+1=q` when nonempty, and zero when empty.

For a full matching the spectral paper requires `t=a`, hence

    b+t-1 >= 2a-1 = 2q-3 > q       for q>=5.

Thus none of these color graphs meets the required threshold.
When `M>t`, use Theorem 2.2 or select `t` graphs before invoking
Theorem 2.1; the latter theorem requires exactly `t` graphs. Even
deleting all unselected cofactor vertices reduces `b` only to `t`,
which still gives the failed threshold `2q-3`. This excludes the
direct spectral application, not matching existence or every
alternative encoding.

## 5. Coverage-preserving edits are not hereditary

For the whole period-12 family

    {0 mod 2, 0 mod 3, 1 mod 4, 5 mod 6, 7 mod 12},

let `E_3` change only label 3 to `2 mod 3`, and `E_6` change only
label 6 to `3 mod 6`. All 12 residue classes give:

| Applied edits | Uncovered residues modulo 12 |
| --- | --- |
| None | Empty |
| Only `E_3` | `{3}` |
| Only `E_6` | `{11}` |
| Both `E_3,E_6` | Empty |

The feasible edit family on these two vertices is exactly
`{empty,{E_3,E_6}}`. It is not downward-closed. Independent sets of
a conflict graph, or of any conflict hypergraph, are downward-closed,
so this edit family is not directly such an independence complex.
One successful edit may repair a failure created by another.

The same actual restricted menus have a faithful complete-choice
encoding. Use parts `{3_old,3_new}` and `{6_old,6_new}`. The two
bad pairs are `{3_new,6_old}` (hole 3) and `{3_old,6_new}` (hole 11).
Their conflict graph is two disjoint copies of `K_(1,1)`, with
part sizes `2=2Delta`. The only independent complete transversals
are the all-old and all-new choices, and these are disconnected
under single-slot updates. Thus a complete-choice encoding can fix
the hereditary semantics while still lacking reconfiguration
surplus. This is a control for these menus with the other three
classes fixed, not for all possible residue menus of this cover.

For an arbitrary move set `S`, write `D(S)` for deleted old labels
and `B(S)` for newly supplied classes. At a point `x`, the exact
failure condition is

    every original label covering x belongs to D(S),
    and no member of B(S) covers x.

An extension of `S` may make the second line false, removing the
failure. This includes joint-private liabilities: a point may lose
several old covering labels together while lying in none of their
individual private regions. Bundled edits or auxiliary choices need
their own exact encoding; the control does not rule them out.

## 6. Conflict-free results that reach complete selections

Three further primary sources give more relevant global interfaces.
Their completeness conclusions still require all the listed
structural hypotheses.

**Joos, Mubayi and Smith.** *Conflict-free hypergraph matchings and
coverings*, Combinatorics, Probability and Computing **35**(2),
230–254, published online 4 December 2025,
[DOI 10.1017/S0963548325100291](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548325100291),
[arXiv:2407.18144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18144), Theorem 3.1 and
sections 1.1 and 3. This provides a two-stage perfect-covering
candidate interface. Its theorem parameters `p,q,r` are edge sizes,
not AP primes. For fixed `ell>=2`, `p>=1`, `q>=0`, `r>=1`,
`p+q>=2`, sufficiently small `epsilon>0` and sufficiently large `d`,
the setup has disjoint `P,Q,R`, with

    d^epsilon <= |P| <= |P union Q| <= exp(d^(epsilon^3)).

An `H_1` edge has `p` vertices in `P` and `q` in `Q`; an `H_2`
edge has one vertex in `P` and `r` in `R`. The degree assumptions are

    (1-d^(-epsilon))d <= delta_P(H_1) <= Delta(H_1) <= d;
    Delta_2(H_1) <= d^(1-epsilon);
    Delta_R(H_2) <= d^(epsilon^4) delta_P(H_2);
    d_(H_2)(x,v) <= d^(-epsilon) delta_P(H_2)  (x in P, v in R).

The exponent is `epsilon^4`, not `epsilon/4`. The conflict system
`C` on `H_1` must satisfy all of section 3.2: sizes 2 through `ell`,
`Delta_1(C^(j))<=ell d^(j-1)`, higher codegrees
`Delta_s(C^(j))<=d^(j-s-epsilon)` for `2<=s<j`, and both stated
binary-conflict bounds by `d^(1-epsilon)` (incidence at a base
vertex and common neighbors of disjoint base edges).

For the second conflict system `D`, put `delta=delta_P(H_2)`, and
let `D_x^(j_1,j_2)` consist of conflicts with `j_1` first-stage
edges and `j_2` second-stage edges whose second-stage part meets `x`.
The simple boundedness assumptions of section 3.3 are

    2 <= |D intersect H_2| <= |D| <= ell;
    |D_x^(j_1,j_2)| <= d^(j_1+epsilon^4) delta^(j_2);
    Delta_(s,0)(D_x^(j_1,j_2)) <= d^(j_1-s-epsilon) delta^(j_2)
        for 1<=s<=j_1;
    |D_(x,y)^(j_1,j_2)| <= d^(j_1-epsilon) delta^(j_2)
        for all x,y in P, as quantified in section 3.3.

Here `Delta_(s,0)` fixes `s` first-stage edges. These conditions
hold for the indicated types `0<=j_1<=ell`, `2<=j_2<=ell`; an
impossible type contributes zero. The result is a `P`-perfect
`C union D`-free matching, with at most `d^(-epsilon^4)|P|` vertices
of `P` covered through `H_2`. Conflicts containing only one `H_2`
edge require the more general conditions of section 4.3; they do
not satisfy the displayed simple version. A faithful AP-to-`P,Q,R`
map, all degree/codegree bounds, and both full conflict bounds
remain unproved.

**Delcourt and Postle.** *Finding an almost perfect matching in a
hypergraph avoiding forbidden submatchings*,
[arXiv:2204.08981v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.08981v4), revision
24 August 2026, identified there as referee-edited and to appear in
the Journal of the London Mathematical Society. Theorem 1.16 gives
an `A`-perfect, conflict-avoiding matching. For fixed integers
`r,g>=2` and `beta in (0,1)`, it supplies `alpha>0` and a sufficiently
large scale threshold. Its `r`-bounded bipartite multihypergraph
`G=(A,B)` has exactly one `A`-vertex per edge and must satisfy

    deg_G(a) >= (1+D^(-alpha))D  (a in A);
    deg_G(b) <= D               (b in B);
    Delta_2(G) <= D^(1-beta).

Conflicts are actual submatchings of `G`, of sizes 2 through `g`,
with `Delta_i(H)<=alpha D^(i-1) log D` and
`Delta_(k,l)(H)<=D^(k-l-beta)` for `2<=l<k<=g`. The maximum mixed
2-codegree and common 2-degree must each be at most `D^(1-beta)`.
Mixed 2-codegree counts binary conflicts `{e,f}` with `f` incident
to a specified base vertex outside `e`; common 2-degree counts
common binary-conflict neighbors of two distinct base edges. These
two conditions vanish if there are no binary conflicts; the higher
codegree conditions remain. None of these AP estimates has been
established.

**Glock, Joos, Kim, Kühn and Lichev.** *Conflict-free hypergraph
matchings*, Journal of the London Mathematical Society **109**(5),
e12899 (2024), [DOI 10.1112/jlms.12899](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.12899).
The precise statement inspected is the authors'
[arXiv:2205.05564v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.05564v1), Theorem 1.3.
For fixed uniformity `k>=2` and maximum conflict size `ell>=2`,
sufficiently small `epsilon` and large `d`, it requires a
`k`-uniform base hypergraph with `n<=exp(d^(epsilon^3))`, all degrees
in `(1 +/- d^(-epsilon))d`, and pair codegrees at most
`d^(1-epsilon)`. The conflict-size, degree, higher-codegree and two
binary restrictions are the same displayed `C`-bounds above.
Its conflict-free matching leaves at most `d^(-epsilon^3)n` base
vertices uncovered. This alone cannot discharge an exact AP
covering liability. The stronger tracking results in its section 3
also require hypotheses not established here.

### Full rainbow matchings and the required color-class surplus

Delcourt–Postle's Theorem 2.11 in the same inspected v4 gives a
full-rainbow interface without a separate conflict system. For
every fixed integer `r>=2` and real `beta>0`, there exist `alpha>0`
and an integer threshold `D_beta` such that, for every sufficiently
large integer `D>=D_beta`, an `r`-bounded multihypergraph `G` with
a not necessarily proper edge-coloring has a full rainbow matching
provided that

    Delta(G) <= D;
    |E_c| >= (1+D^(-alpha))D       for every color c;
    codeg_G(u,v) <= D^(1-beta)    for every pair of distinct vertices;
    #{e in E_c : v in e} <= D^(1-beta)  for every color c and vertex v.

The conclusion supplies even `D` pairwise edge-disjoint full rainbow
matchings. Each full rainbow matching chooses exactly one edge of
every color. A proposed global repair interpretation could use colors
for obligations and edges for complete resource certificates, but
would still have to prove all four conditions for those same
certificates, along with their AP composition and improvement
properties. The theorem by itself does not certify these properties.

Ronen Wdowinski's *Bounded degree graphs and hypergraphs with no full
rainbow matchings*, European Journal of Combinatorics **133** (2026),
104316, [DOI 10.1016/j.ejc.2025.104316](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2025.104316),
provides an exact boundary on weakening the surplus. The inspected
[arXiv:2401.06029v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06029v2), revised
18 December 2025, Theorem 7(2), constructs for every `Delta>=2` a
bipartite graph of maximum degree `Delta`, with a proper edge-coloring
in which every color class has at least `Delta+1` edges, but no full
rainbow matching. The proof in section 4.2 produces simple graphs;
for odd `Delta`, every color class has exactly `Delta+1` edges.

Simplicity gives vertex-pair codegree at most one, and properness
gives at most one edge of any fixed color at a vertex. Thus both
codegree conditions of Theorem 2.11 already hold for these examples
at `D=Delta` and any fixed `0<beta<=1`. Their obstruction cannot be
attributed to absent codegree control. The additive surplus `+1`
does not replace the theorem's surplus `D^(1-alpha)`: taking the
theorem's `alpha` smaller if necessary so that `0<alpha<1`, the
odd-degree examples fail the required color-class size for every
`D>=Delta`. The two theorems therefore do not conflict.

The inspected Delcourt–Postle v4 still labels the `Delta+1`
bipartite assertion Conjecture 2.9; Wdowinski's inspected version
explicitly refutes that assertion. This is a discrepancy in the
reported conjecture status, not a counterexample to Theorem 2.11.

## 7. A faithful completed-choice interface and its remaining gap

Fix finite domains of permitted actual AP choices, including keep
and deletion/absence where allowed. Choose a common finite period
`Q'` divisible by every candidate and untouched original modulus;
all statements below concern the same points of `Z/Q'Z`. One can
equivalently use their complete joint membership cells. Let `E`
be the points not covered by the untouched original classes.

Use one slot per permitted output numerical modulus, with exactly
one option selected in every slot. This preserves distinct output
moduli, and keeps each actual residue choice separate. In an
alternative slot scheme where different slots may select the same
modulus, give them a shared modulus-resource vertex, so a matching
cannot select both. Different residues with the same slot and
modulus require labeled parallel edges or a unique option vertex;
merging them would destroy the coverage data. Absence consumes no
output-modulus resource. Other shared resources require the same
faithful incidence accounting.

For each `x in E`, define

    J_x = {i : some option in slot i covers x};
    B_i(x) = {options in slot i which avoid x}.

For every tuple `(b_i) in product_(i in J_x) B_i(x)`, form the
forbidden set `{(i,b_i):i in J_x}`. Retain only those forbidden
sets which are themselves matchings in the base resource model;
a set which is not a base matching cannot occur in a feasible
selection. If `J_x` is empty, the task is infeasible: this empty
conflict cannot be ignored. If some `B_i(x)` is empty, that slot
always covers `x`, so the point generates no conflict. Unary
conflicts forbid their option and must be removed from the domain,
with consistency rechecked, before applying theorems requiring
conflict size at least two.

For a complete resource-compatible selection, avoidance of every
such forbidden set is equivalent to covering all of `E`: if `x`
is missed, all its selected `J_x` options form one retained
forbidden set; if a forbidden set is selected, every potentially
covering slot avoids `x`. Completeness is essential. A partial
conflict-free matching need not give a cover.

This is a finite exact encoding, not a proof of the literature's
boundedness assumptions. In particular, the sizes `|J_x|` have no
established uniform bound, and the resulting high-order conflict
codegrees need not be small. Incidence witnesses of the same
original AP cannot be duplicated and counted as independent AP
choices. To contradict an extremal cover one must first require a
specified deletion or a strict improvement in the allowed output
family. With unrestricted keep options, the unchanged cover is
already a complete feasible choice, so mere existence adds nothing.

The obstruction to a pairwise or sparse encoding already occurs
at one liability point. Take three distinct-modulus slots, each
with `n` literal residue choices, exactly one through `x` and
`n-1` avoiding it. The forbidden sets are all bad triples, one
option per slot. Every bad pair extends to a cover of `x` using
the good option in the third slot. Thus no conflict graph on
these unaugmented choice vertices expresses the exact condition.
For the natural three-star base model,

    number of ternary conflicts = (n-1)^3;
    Delta_1 = (n-1)^2;
    Delta_(3,2) = n-1.

The slot degrees are `n`, option degrees are one, and base
codegrees are at most one. Any Delcourt–Postle scale must have
`D<n`, while its high-order conflict bound would require
`n-1<=D^(1-beta)`, which fails for fixed `beta>0` and sufficiently
large `n`. There are no binary conflicts in this example. This
is a literal local AP-choice gadget, not an asserted whole odd
cover or a rejection of every auxiliary encoding.

The remaining global task is therefore precise: construct one
completed-choice model for a strict whole-cover improvement and
prove, for that same model, bounded-size fatal-choice certificates
with the necessary degree and codegree bounds, or give another
faithful encoding with those properties. Fixed-source matching
connectivity, connected tree supports, and conflict-free perfect
selection address different parts of that task; none supplies the
missing joint AP repair estimates by itself.

## 8. A shared-capacity flow needs an actual joint selection theorem

Use 363's actual nonsingleton antichain masses
`w_E=H({x in Z:E(x)=E})` and `K(E)=intersection_(d in E) supp(d)`.
A separate transportation model may declare one slot `(E,d)` for
every `d in E`, each of capacity `w_E`, accessible to every prime
in `K(E)`. For nonnegative demands `s_q`, max-flow/min-cut gives
the exact fractional feasibility criterion

    sum_(q in S) s_q
      <= sum_(E:K(E) intersect S nonempty) |E| w_E
                      for every prime subset S.

This criterion belongs to that declared model: a source-to-prime
edge has capacity `s_q`, prime-to-accessible-slot edges have
unbounded capacity, and slot-to-sink edges have capacity `w_E`.
The shared slot capacities are additional assumptions. Neither
the exact antichain marginals in 363 nor its separate q-wise
selection injections supplies those assumptions, or permits moving
a selection to an arbitrary other atom with the same kernel.

In particular, if `L_(q,d)(x)` indicates an actual selected child
lift at `x`, 363 proves

    sum_d L_(q,d)(x) <= 1         for each fixed q.

It does not prove `sum_q L_(q,d)(x)<=1`. Its actual residual
selected mass is at most `epsilon_q`. The distinct inequality
`2 epsilon_q<=sum_(d:q|d) delta_d` counts active covering incidences;
it does not provide a selected demand `s_q=2 epsilon_q`.
An abstract weight-one antichain with such demands therefore
cannot certify an obstruction for the original Haar selection.

### Actual matching choices can force shared use

The affine refinement already checked in
[364](364-singleton-cofactor-ideal-and-forced-colors.md) gives a
whole even-cover control. Start from the period-960 fixture

    (0 mod 2), (0 mod 3), (3 mod 4), (0 mod 5),
    (1 mod 8), (1 mod 10), (13 mod 16), (17 mod 20),
    (13 mod 40), (69 mod 160), (149 mod 320),
    (469 mod 480), (629 mod 960).

Replace its last, entirely private class by the affine copy of
all thirteen classes: `a mod d` becomes
`629+960a mod 960d`. Keep the other twelve originals. This gives
25 distinct labels, full period `Q=921600`, an irredundant cover,
and disjoint comparable classes. It is not divisor-closed.

At the actual point `x=6389=629+960*6`, the original hit set is
`E(x)={1920,2880}` and `K(x)={2,3,5}`. Reset only the first digit
at q, retaining every higher digit and all other coordinates.
The resulting source `y_q=T_q x` has hit set exactly `{q}`.
Its complete root/cofactor graph is as follows. Each entry lists
the root and its available original numerical labels; its cofactor
is always the actual `d/q^(v_q(d))`.

Each graph has exactly two maximum matchings, of full ranks 1, 2
and 4 respectively. In every one, exactly one chosen edge lifts
to `x`, with original label 1920 or 2880. Thus every joint choice
of the three maximum matchings has three uses of only two slots
`(x,1920)` and `(x,2880)`. Of the eight joint choices, six have
maximum slot load two and two have maximum load three. The
minimum integer congestion is exactly two. Even a randomized
joint selection has maximum expected load at least `3/2`, attained
by giving every prime either label with probability one half.
Every point has original mass `1/Q`; no source law is changed.

This excludes automatic shared unit capacity even after optimizing
the maximum-matchings jointly for this whole even-cover control.
It does not refute such a theorem under the additional odd,
divisor-closed extremal hypotheses, nor does it establish a failure
of the aggregate cut above. Reducing selected ranks or using a
different repair interface also changes the obligation.

For a fixed `x in Z`, the exact pointwise question has a standard
capacitated Hall formulation. Let `I_x` consist of primes `q in K(x)`
whose `x`-root is matched in every maximum matching of
`H_(q,T_q x)`, equivalently those for which deleting that root
decreases matching rank by one. Let `N_q(x)` be the original labels
at `x` that occur on that root in some maximum matching. The smallest
integer slot congestion achievable at this point, preserving every
source's maximum rank, is

    ceil(max_(empty != S subset I_x)
                 |S| / |union_(q in S) N_q(x)|),

with value zero when `I_x` is empty. Optional roots can be omitted;
each chosen edge in `N_q(x)` extends to a maximum matching at its
own source. Applying capacitated Hall to the remaining mandatory
primes gives the formula. For fractional expected congestion the
same flow argument omits the ceiling. In the displayed control,
`I_x={2,3,5}` and every `N_q(x)={1920,2880}`. Pointwise choices at
different `x` need not agree when they arise from the same source
matching; simultaneous compatibility is still a separate obligation.

If one additionally retains the q-wise atom bound `w_E` while
postulating shared total atom capacity `|E|w_E`, the corresponding
flow criterion is instead

    sum_(q in S) s_q
      <= sum_E min(|S intersect K(E)|,|E|) w_E.

Here prime-to-atom edges have capacity `w_E` and atom-to-sink edges
have capacity `|E|w_E`. This is another conditional transport
model, not an established AP constraint. Either use requires an
actual simultaneous selection or allocation theorem, followed by
the complete return and strict-improvement obligations of section 7.
