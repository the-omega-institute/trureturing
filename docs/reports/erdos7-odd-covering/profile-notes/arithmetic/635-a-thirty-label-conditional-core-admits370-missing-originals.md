### Why thirteen orbit variables suffice for a common thinning

There are80 live, unmasked central cells. The fixed-null stabilizer acts by
permutations inside the following row and column blocks:

    rows: {0}, {1,2}, {4,5};
    columns: {0,1,2,3,4}, {10}, {11,12,13,14},
             {6,7,8,9}, {15,16,17,18,19}.

The deleted row3 and column5 stay fixed. Products in the central15 rectangle
are removed. The remaining block products are exactly13 cell orbits.
These permutations preserve STAR activations, the central15 mask, all32 raw
responses, numerical-modulus/exponent costs, and all16 central menu types.
They send a source with weak leaves(w,w') to the source with correspondingly
permuted weak leaves. The full domain consists of all5 times19=95 such source
corners and is closed under this action, with15 representatives.

For an arbitrary80-cell t, let F(t)=min_v G_v(t) over this complete weak domain.
Mass is linear in t, and each selector screen is a maximum of linear forms.
Because all costs are nonnegative, every G_v and their pointwise minimum F
are concave. The simultaneous action on t and source menus yields

    G_(hv)(ht)=G_v(t), hence F(ht)=F(t).

Average t over the finite stabilizer. The average stays in[0,1]^80 and is
constant on the13 cell orbits. Concavity gives

    F(average_h ht)>=average_h F(ht)=F(t).

Thus any strictly positive robust80-cell gate would produce a strictly
positive13-orbit gate, contradicting the exact dual. The zero gate is attainable
in both domains. This averaging statement keeps ONE common t over the entire
weak-corner domain; it makes no assertion about a family t_v depending on v.
