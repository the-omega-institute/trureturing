# Induced responses and the central mask locate the old-domain boundary

The actual induced responses of [Report648](648-induced-pair-responses-release-all-square-pairs.md) strengthen the four/five-parent comparison of [Report655](655-five-parent-owners-begin-at-seventy-one.md). A further central-mask correction is valid when the noncentral coordinates are compared first. This correction is sharp at the central transport layer. With the inherited domain debit `D=v-3`, even both improvements and every real row parameter `10 <= h <= v-3` leave the resulting comparison-fee expression unable to certify five parents from owner67: its finite compulsory branch fees already exceed the available head reserve by more than `0.000202495`.

This is a boundary of this sufficient comparison expression with its stated domain debit. A stronger ordinary-domain lower bound changes the expression and is not ruled out. The result is not a lower bound on actual violation probabilities, an impossibility result for other source constructions, or a covering example. Report655's positive policy remains four parents from37, five from71 and arbitrary finite parent unions from2^46, with its stated density and unchanged hypotheses. No new Lean verification is claimed here.

All statements retain648/655's actual common source, ten literal head primes, sixty remaining root-incidence conditions, central conventions, private interfaces and globally fixed full phases. Original numerical labels are distinct; each owner has one fixed union of earlier declared parents, independent of sampled history. Heights, sizes and depths remain arbitrary finite quantities. The comparison auxiliaries below do not replace these actual-source requirements.

## 3. A central-mask rebate with the required comparison order

Fix a parent branch containing3 and5. Let M be the actual central mask, zero on the distinguished root0-by-root0 rectangle. Under the dominating product of the actual normalized pure3 and pure5 survivor laws,

    rho3(root0)>=1/3, rho5(root0)>=1/5,
    Pr(M=0)>=1/15.                                           (B6)

Keep M while comparing all noncentral queried coordinates, including the permitted backwards comparison of later head and outside rows. In the branches(B5), write

    C_r=product_(p in T_r)(L_p+1)-1,
    B_r=product_(p in T_r\{3,5})(L_p+1)-1.                    (B7)

Condition on the resulting independent noncentral auxiliaries. The actual load is a noncentral baseline plus a nonnegative central rectangle load. Complete the former to B_r and call the latter A. This only increases the load. The unit cofactor pattern is absent because pure owner powers have already been handled by the ordinary domain.

For any nonnegative increasing convex phi with finite complete expectation, split

    phi(B_r+A)=phi(B_r)+psi(A),
    psi(x)=phi(B_r+x)-phi(B_r).

At this stage B_r is fixed. The function psi is nonnegative, increasing and convex for x>=0. The baseline is independent of the central state and pays at most(14/15)phi(B_r). Drop M only from the nonnegative psi term and then perform the central3/5 comparisons. Each original still has its own fixed phase during those comparisons. Numerical uniqueness and the full owner-height coefficient sum bound the completed central load by C_r-B_r. Missing patterns are added only as nonnegative terms in this upper comparison. Thus

    integral phi(actual load) dPi
      <= H_r [E phi(C_r)-(1/15)E phi(B_r)].                   (B8)

Infinite positive completion follows by monotone convergence. The displayed difference is well defined because the complete expectation is finite.

The order is essential. Subtracting a baseline before comparing the noncentral coordinates would leave a difference that need not be convex in those coordinates. Equation(B8) first completes their comparison, then uses a constant-baseline decomposition in the central variables.

## 6. Further boundary data include the actual domain and cylinder union

The residual budget in(B14) is not closed by the complete induced graph and the sharp central correction while D=v-3 is held fixed. That restriction leaves at least two distinct improvement routes.

First, the inherited actual-domain induction already has a stronger form. [Report599](599-the-ten-prime-head-admits-arbitrary-twelve-vertex-attachments.md), equation(HB3), and [Report600](600-two-head-coordinates-admit-unbounded-large-prime-entries.md), equation(TP3), give

    Haar(V_v)>=1-1/(v-1)-2e_v/(v-1),
    e_v=sum_(strict private descendants r)2^(-(r-1)/2).

The unchanged exterior-prime lower bound37 yields

    e_v<=sum_(n>=18)2^(-n)=2^(-17),
    (v-1)Haar(V_v)>=D*=v-2-2^(-16).                         (B15)

The Type I subtraction from the head is a separate charge and does not replace this ordinary-domain bound. Using D* instead of v-3 would require its own complete row, query-cap and tail calculation. Equation(B14) does not obstruct that calculation and makes no numerical cutoff claim for it.

Second, the actual union can save more than a scalar load bound records. Additional information about the actual originals and their shared owner prefixes can produce another simultaneous saving.

[Report583](583-actual-selected-overlap-improves-joint-query-and-loss-budgets.md) already gives the exact overlap credit on one actual history. If U is the sum of selected cylinder masses, f their actual union mass, D=U-f, tau=1-1/C and

    credit=C min(D,(U-tau)_+),

then the actual loss satisfies

    C(f-tau)_+=C(U-tau)_+-credit.                             (B16)

That report transports this same-source credit into both query and loss budgets. It also shows that high residual mean alone does not force overlap. [Report568](568-actual-root-unions-give-four-general-common-law-certificates.md) supplies actual root-union certificates and examples where root projection still loses the depth information needed for a stronger result. Its auxiliary two-copy examples must not be silently transferred to the present distinct-modulus scope.

For a selected original set S at a fixed actual prior state b, distinguish its active weighted load m_S(b) from the scaled domain cost q_S(b), defined as (v-1) times the Haar mass that its actual cylinder union removes from the ordinary owner domain. Both must use the same globally fixed phases and the same domain. With D0 a lower bound on the original domain's scaled Haar mass, a proposed owner-interface refinement would have to prove bounds of the form

    (v-1) Haar(remaining domain) >=D0-q_S(b),
    remaining load <=L0(b)-m_S(b),

together with a normalized, fully paid fallback on every history where the preferred row is unavailable. Its candidate fixed-cap hinge would then involve

    [L0(b)-(D0-h)+q_S(b)-m_S(b)]_+/h.                        (B17)

A unit debit q_S=N and load removal m_S<=N cannot improve the expression. Even q_S=m_S cancels. A benefit requires actual union saving m_S-q_S, or another proved joint relation. The missing theorem on this union route is a uniform same-source mechanism forcing enough such saving on the histories carrying the adverse charge, while preserving all actual row and query invariants. A favorable individual configuration does not provide it.

These routes identify additional boundary data worth retaining: the quantitative ordinary-domain guarantee as well as original-label identity, parent-cylinder activation and shared owner prefixes. The scalar completed count remains useful as an upper comparison, but does not determine these union relations.
