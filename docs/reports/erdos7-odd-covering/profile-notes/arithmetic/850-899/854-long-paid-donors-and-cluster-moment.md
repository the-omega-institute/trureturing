# Long paid donors and the contribution of one actual cluster

A depth-five inverse can receive its group's $27s$ output
and absorb depth-four target inverses. The donor's digit can
therefore lie outside the selected short set, and its old
word need not be sampled. For one actual cluster of $M$
targets, the resulting correction to the common-code moment
is $981M$, $936M$ or $2678M/3$, according to how many
specified donor digits lie in the fixed sampling domain.
Each coefficient requires the actual conditions below.

The proof preserves one complete source, all literal cofactor
phases, and one paid $27s$ owner per cofactor. It subtracts
part of this same cluster's bad-group contribution. It does
not supply a cluster from whole coverage, justify adding
separately optimized clusters, or exclude an odd distinct
cover. These are conditional ordinary mathematical deductions,
without a claim of Lean verification.

## 1. Fixed domain and actual cluster hypotheses

Keep the actual globally EB1-minimal family, $H_3=2$,
$q=113$, $Q=9q^GW$, $(W,3q)=1$, and the five actual
safe words $\mathcal A$ from
[Report 853, Section 1](853-paid-packet-absorption-and-residual-moment.md#1-one-actual-family-and-one-common-code).
The actual terminal digits are $\alpha,\beta$; the actual
$9q$ digit is $\gamma$. Fix once

$$
U\subseteq\mathbb F_{113}\setminus\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\},
\qquad |U|=110.
\tag{LD1}
$$

All pattern counts and all probabilities use this same $U$.
Choose $R$ uniformly from its six-element subsets and, for
each $c\in R$, choose $f(c)$ independently and uniformly
from $\mathcal A$. Geometry and deeper dictionaries are
completed once for all originals above each sampled $(R,f)$.

Fix $c\in U$, $z\in\mathcal A$, and an actual original

$$
n_j=3^aqs,\qquad 0\le a\le2,\quad s>1,\quad(s,3q)=1.
$$

Its actual first q-digit $d$ and ternary phase must satisfy

$$
d\notin\{\alpha,\beta,c\},\qquad
\rho_{n_j}\equiv d\pmod q,\qquad
\rho_{n_j}\equiv z\pmod{3^a}.
\tag{LD2}
$$

Thus the donor is at q-height exactly one and is not an
actual terminal digit. It is permitted that $d\notin U$;
this is an additional actual condition, not a supplied donor
inventory.

Let $\mathcal M$ consist of $M$ distinct potentially active
$C_1$ cofactors in the fixed domain $U$. Every group in
$\mathcal M$ has all three actual q-digits equal to $c$,
final word $z$, and the compatible actual middle first-3
root. Require, for each $m\in\mathcal M$,

$$
s\mid m,\qquad \rho_{9qm}\equiv\rho_{n_j}\pmod s.
\tag{LD3}
$$

These are literal cofactor conditions, not merely support
inclusion or pairwise intersection. They may arise from the
actual private-fibre supplier interface in Report 853,
Section 4, but whole coverage alone does not establish them.

Finally, require an actual **different** member of the donor
group $\{qs,3qs,9qs\}$ whose first q-digit is

$$
e\ne c.
\tag{LD4}
$$

The other member has a different ternary exponent from $a$.
Its digit may satisfy $e=d$; the same owner cannot be used
twice to meet LD4. Define

$$
\mathcal E=\{c\in R,\ d\notin R,\ e\notin R,\ f(c)=z\},
\qquad t=|\{d,e\}\cap U|\in\{0,1,2\}.
\tag{LD5}
$$

## 2. A long owner must have an admissible designation

Report 388 SC470 allocates the lowest available labels from
$27s,81s,243s$ within one actual cofactor group. A nonempty
long inverse can be designated for $27s$ when either

- at most two inverses in the group are nonempty; or
- all three are nonempty and another inverse is long.

In the first case any remaining inverse fits $81s$. In the
second case give $243s$ to the other long inverse and $81s$
to the third. Each enclosure keeps its own original s-phase.
A good group with exactly one long and two nonempty short
inverses cannot designate its unique long owner for $27s$:
one short inverse would be left with the invalid label $243s$.
Goodness of the group alone is therefore insufficient.

On LD5 the other actual member at $e$ has either no
continuing inverse or a long inverse. If it is empty, at
most two inverses remain. If it is nonempty, it provides the
other long inverse. This proves admissibility of the particular
donor $j$ for every outcome in LD5. If $e=d$, both actual
members use the same long code leaf when their respective
ternary tests are compatible; incompatible tests give empty
inverses. Their actual cofactor phases need not agree.

A group with at most two actual available members needs no
other-owner hypothesis at all. For this separate sufficient
case, omit $e\notin R$ and use $t=\mathbf1_{d\in U}$ in
the conclusions below. A code-dependent claim that at most
two inverses are nonempty must instead be verified on every
outcome credited in the event.

## 3. One completion for all prescribed words

Fix the entire sampled $(R,f)$ in LD5, and write
$n_w=\#f^{-1}(w)$. Since $f(c)=z$, $n_z\ge1$ and
every other word $v\ne z$ has $n_v\le5$.

The actual first-3 root of $3q$ has at least two safe words:
it differs from the pure 3 root, and the pure 9 guard removes
at most one of its three words. Choose a compatible safe
word $v\ne z$. Using canonical representatives modulo nine,
place the actual q terminal at $[v]_{27}$ and the actual
$3q$ terminal at $[v+9]_{81}$. Their modulo-27 parents
differ, so they are disjoint. The latter has the required
actual first-3 root. Together they consume four of v's nine
depth-four slots, leaving five for all $n_v\le5$ prescribed
short leaves.

Place c at the short leaf $[z]_{81}$. Reserve the different
depth-four child $[z+27]_{81}$ from short-leaf use, and put
d at the ordinary long leaf $[z+27]_{243}$. The two leaves
are disjoint but share the parent $[z]_{27}$. Apart from
the fixed c leaf and the reserved child, seven depth-four
slots remain above z, enough for its at most five other
prescribed short leaves. Every remaining safe word has nine
slots and at most five requests. All six prescribed words
are therefore respected simultaneously, including the case
$n_v=5$ where the terminal word has no spare short slot.

The two terminals and six short leaves occupy $9+3+18=30$
of the 135 admissible depth-five cells. Complete the other
105 cells as ordinary long leaves, including the reserved
d cell. Thus

$$
1+1+6+105=135-8-2-2\cdot6=113
\tag{LD6}
$$

leaves remain. Assign the actual terminal digits, six selected
digits, the actual d digit and the other digits bijectively
to the corresponding leaves. An excluded nonterminal gamma
digit stays long; if $\gamma=\beta$, its actual digit is
already terminal.

For $G\ge2$, the usual 81-way continuation of short leaves
and 27-way continuation of ordinary long leaves both fit
in 113 next q-digits and reach depth eight. Subsequent levels
use the existing 27-way dictionaries. Fix terminal suffixes
once. At $G=1$ no later dictionary is needed. This completes
one common source on the entire safe corridor, preserving
the full old modulo-nine and $W$ coordinates.

Choose this completion deterministically over each outcome
in LD5 and any lawful completion elsewhere. No different
geometry is chosen for different targets. The source is
fixed before an original covering owner is selected. Badness
depends only on the original short-compatibility tests and
$(R,f)$, so the SC479 sampling law and its event probabilities
are unchanged. There is no sampled word $f(d)$ to condition:
$d\notin R$, and choosing its long leaf above z adds no
word-probability factor. The assertion concerns this compatible
completion, not every arbitrary previously completed geometry.

### Finite initial-code verification

The [geometry program](../../../frontier/cover-geometry/paid-packet/long_paid_donor_geometry.py)
constructs the forest with natural-number congruence classes.
Its verifier independently expands little-endian ternary
words and checks the multiplicity of every depth-five cell.
The [all-phase result](../../../frontier/cover-geometry/paid-packet/long_paid_donor_geometry.json)
covers all 18 disjoint actual pure-3/pure-9 phase pairs,
both compatible $3q$ roots, all five shared words z, and
all $5^5$ ordered assignments of the other five short leaves:

$$
18\cdot2\cdot5\cdot5^5=562500
$$

configurations and $562500\cdot135=75937500$ depth-five
cells. Every configuration has the inventory LD6, respects
the actual terminal root and all six prescribed words, and
places the short target and long donor under one modulo-27
parent. The enumeration exits zero with no missing or
multiply covered cell and a bijection of all 113 digit labels.

The executable uses fixed names for digit roles, including
a representative excluded nonterminal gamma digit. The
actual donor, companion, phase and domain hypotheses remain
LD1--LD5; no original covering system is enumerated. The
actual phase pairs are fully enumerated without a normalization
assumption. From the repository root:

```sh
python3 docs/reports/erdos7-odd-covering/frontier/cover-geometry/paid-packet/long_paid_donor_geometry.py \
  --mode all-phases --output /tmp/long_paid_donor_geometry.json
```

The result records the script hash and deterministic
construction-stream hash; elapsed time can vary. This is
finite verification of the initial geometry. The later
dictionaries use the preceding cardinality argument. It is
not Lean verification of the whole paid-packet theorem.

## 4. Whole inverse containment and one group payment

In the geometry above, LD2 makes the donor's continuing
inverse nonempty and gives the full cylinder

$$
B_j=[z+27]_{243}\times[\rho_{n_j}]_s.
\tag{LD7}
$$

Its designated enclosure is

$$
E_j=[z]_{27}\times[\rho_{n_j}]_s.
\tag{LD8}
$$

Each target group in $\mathcal M$ is bad on LD5, with

$$
B_{m,2}=[z]_{81}\times[\rho_{9qm}]_m
\subseteq E_j
\qquad(m\in\mathcal M).
\tag{LD9}
$$

The inclusion uses the complete target inverse, the common
modulo-27 parent and the actual divisibility and phase
conditions LD3. No private-region mask or separately
optimized cofactor law is introduced. The long donor leaf
and short target leaf need not intersect; their common
ancestor LD8 is the paid enclosure covering both.

The designation in Section 2 leaves the donor group's label
multiset and SC471 cost unchanged. The one $27s$ output is
charged once, regardless of $M$. Each absorbed bad target
group omits one separate enclosure and pays

$$
27m+81m=108m<13qm.
\tag{LD10}
$$

Good-group payment remains joint; an individual owner may
receive a larger label without invalidating the strict group
comparison. No supplier is copied. The supplier group is
good and the targets are bad. Also $s=m$ would identify the
donor with one of the target's three same-c originals,
contrary to $d\ne c$, so the divisor containment is proper.

All numerical distinctness and complete-coverage conditions
are those of Report 853, Section 2: one copy of each shallow
label per cofactor, different 3-free cofactors give different
labels, the unit terminals retain $27,81$, and $243$ is
reserved for any continuing unit-cofactor $9q$ inverse.
Retained labels have ternary height at most two and deeper
labels have height at least six. The entire deletion hole
is covered through the same source, and retained originals
cover its complement. Thus LD9 is an absorption by one
lawful packet, not a count of intersecting supplier classes.

## 5. One formula for the three coefficients

Use $b(\omega),a(\omega)$ from Report 853 PP8: $b$ counts
bad groups and $a$ counts absorbed bad groups once each.
The paid-packet lemma gives $b-a\ge1$ for every lawful
code and packet. With the packet consisting of LD8 on LD5
and the empty packet elsewhere,

$$
a(\omega)\ge M\mathbf1_{\mathcal E}(\omega).
\tag{LD11}
$$

The $t$ distinct specified exclusions in $U$ differ from c.
After choosing c, the other five short digits may be chosen
in $\binom{109-t}{5}$ ways. Consequently

$$
\Pr(\mathcal E)
=\frac{\binom{109-t}{5}}{5\binom{110}{6}},\qquad
\kappa_t=89925\Pr(\mathcal E),\qquad
\Delta=\mathbb E[a]\ge\frac{\kappa_tM}{89925}.
\tag{LD12}
$$

The exact values are

| $t$ | Specified distinct exclusions inside fixed $U$ | $\Pr(\mathcal E)$ | $\kappa_t$ |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | Neither $d$ nor $e$ is in $U$ | $3/275$ | $981$ |
| 1 | Exactly one distinct digit of $\{d,e\}$ is in $U$ | $312/29975$ | $936$ |
| 2 | $d,e\in U$ and $d\ne e$ | $2678/269775$ | $2678/3$ |

For example, with distinct $c,d\in U$, the middle row
applies when $e=d$ or $e\notin U$; the last applies when
$c,d,e$ are distinct members of $U$. No probability is
multiplied across the M targets: one event absorbs them all.

The first row requires additional actual conditions:
$d\notin\{\alpha,\beta\}$, $d\notin U$, and $e\notin U$,
together with the actual word compatibility in LD2 and all
other hypotheses. A gamma donor is allowed only when gamma
is nonterminal and that actual donor is compatible with z.
Its unit-cofactor $9q$ inverse is then long or empty and
keeps its own $243$ slot. This does not supply a gamma donor,
change its original phase, or override original irredundancy.
A choice of $U$ cannot be changed separately for different
targets while retaining the same counts.

Combining LD11--LD12 with Report 853 PP9 gives

$$
89925+\kappa_tM
\le981C_1+45C_{2A}+27C_{2B}+C_3.
\tag{LD13}
$$

The subtraction concerns exactly the M actual groups already
counted in $C_1$. Their uncorrected bad event is the common
event $c\in R,f(c)=z$, with weight $981/89925$. Thus LD13
can equivalently be written

$$
89925\le981(C_1-M)+(981-\kappa_t)M
              +45C_{2A}+27C_{2B}+C_3.
\tag{LD14}
$$

For $t=0$, the cluster's entire bad-group contribution is
removed on every outcome where it is bad. For $t=1,2$, its
remaining coefficients in LD14 are respectively $45$ and
$265/3$. This is the same common-code expectation throughout;
separate donor choices or separately completed geometries
for different clusters cannot be added without a new global
packet certificate.

Retain the original support and individual-prime height
bounds used for the numerical envelope $39101$ in Report 388
SC481. Then Report 853 PP10 and LD12 imply

$$
\boxed{
N_{\ge3}+980C_1+44C_{2A}+26C_{2B}
\ge50824+\kappa_tM.}
\tag{LD15}
$$

This gives the corrections $981M$, $936M$ and $2678M/3$
under their respective hypotheses. The $39101$ envelope is
an explicit dependency; the local packet argument does not
establish it. If all C1 groups belonged to this one cluster
in the $t=1$ case, algebra would leave coefficient 44 rather
than 980 on $C_1$. No assertion that $M=C_1$ is supplied.

For comparison with the short-donor construction of Report
853, the same distinct in-U escape gives coefficient ratio
$(2678/3)/(26/3)=103$. The same automatically excluded
escape gives $936/9=104$ when $d\in U$. The case $e=d$
is a new admissible condition: it could not satisfy the
old event requiring both $d\in R$ and $e\notin R$.
The ratio $936/(26/3)=108$ therefore does not compare
identical escape hypotheses.

## 6. What remains to be extracted from whole coverage

The long-donor construction removes the need to select the
donor among the six short digits or to sample its word.
It retains the actual cofactor divisibility and phase
containment LD3, the admissibility condition for this chosen
long owner, and the need for a sufficiently large common
cluster. A good group whose designated owner is uniquely
long and whose two companions are nonempty short remains
the exact local allocation obstruction.

The more general whole-coset packets and acyclic rescue
certificates of Report 853 remain available, with permanent
donor choices and one numerical $27s$ label per cofactor.
Their existence, a compatible simultaneous collection of
clusters, and a useful lower bound for M have not been
deduced from the original whole cover. The height-two branch
and unrestricted Erdős #7 remain unresolved by LD1--LD15.
