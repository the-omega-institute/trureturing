# Paid packets, whole-inverse absorption, and the residual code moment

An actual enclosure of modulus $27s$, already paid by its
cofactor group, can cover whole inverses from many other
cofactors. Paying this enclosure once gives a stronger
common-code obstruction: at least one bad group survives
every simultaneously payable packet. For a specified cluster
of $M$ actual $C_1$ targets sharing one admissible supplier,
this strengthens the finite-height alternative to

$$
N_{\ge3}+980C_1+44C_{2A}+26C_{2B}
\ge50824+\frac{26M}{3}.
\tag{PP1}
$$

The cluster conditions include actual phase agreement,
cofactor divisibility and an actual escape digit. No theorem
here forces such a cluster or enough packet absorption to
contradict whole coverage. These are conditional ordinary
mathematical deductions, without a claim of Lean verification
or a resolution of the height-two branch or Erdős #7.

## 1. One actual family and one common code

Keep the hypothetical globally EB1-minimal distinct odd whole
cover from [Report 388, Section 74](../350-399/388-source-global-substitution-collision-moment.md#74-grouped-shallow-slots-force-actual-divisor-triples-and-a-common-code-moment), with $H_3=2$, $q=113$ and

$$
Q=9q^GW,\qquad (W,3q)=1.
$$

Here EB1 minimizes first the class count $K$ and then the
sum $\Sigma$ of numerical moduli among all distinct odd
nonunit whole covers. Use the same divisor closure, actual
phases, five safe words $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z$,
and actual terminal digits $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ from SC466.
Choose a fixed set

$$
U\subseteq\mathbb F_{113}\setminus\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\},
\qquad |U|=110.
$$

A six-code chooses $R\subseteq U$, $|R|=6$, and one safe
word $f(c)\in\mathcal A$ for each $c\in R$. Complete its
geometry and deeper dictionaries once for all originals.
The actual $q$ and $3q$ terminals have depths three and four;
selected continuing leaves have depth four, and ordinary
initial continuing leaves have depth five. The common output
carrier is

$$
\mathcal X=\mathbb Z/3^{3G+2}\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/W\mathbb Z.
$$

The complete source preserves the full old modulo-nine and
$W$ coordinates. Every output is assigned its source before
an original covering owner is selected. SC470--SC472 establish
that each nonempty shallow continuing inverse is the whole
cylinder

$$
B_{m,a}=[\ell_{m,a}]_{3^{d_{m,a}}}\times[r_{m,a}]_m,
\qquad d_{m,a}\in\{4,5\},
\tag{PP2}
$$

for an actual original $3^aqm$, $a\in\{0,1,2\}$,
$m>1$, $(m,3q)=1$. The residue $r_{m,a}$ is its literal
original phase modulo $m$. There is no private-region mask
on PP2. Groups with a missing original or empty inverse are
included in the construction.

A group is **bad** if all three originals $qm,3qm,9qm$
have nonempty depth-four inverses. Every other nonunit
cofactor group is **good**. Thus bad groups include all
surviving digit-pattern types, not only $C_1$.

This uses an ordinary common code. It requires no movable-digit
inventory from [Report 850](850-cofactor-dependent-protected-codes.md).
The all-height protection requirement there remains necessary
for codes depending on a cofactor row; the inventory gap and
all-height grouped-hull criterion in
[Report 852](852-protected-digit-inventory-gap-and-grouped-hulls.md)
do not supply the packets considered here.

## 2. Admissible donors and simultaneous payment

In a good group $s>1$, designate at most one nonempty owner
$j$ for its output label $27s$. The designation is admissible
under exactly the following allocation condition:

- With one or two nonempty inverses, any designation works.
- With three nonempty inverses, at least one inverse other
  than the designated one must have depth five.

Assign $243s$ to such a remaining long inverse when three
outputs are needed, and $81s$ to the other remaining inverse.
Each assigned exponent is at most its inverse depth. In
particular, every short owner in a good group is admissible;
the unique long owner of a three-inverse group is not.

The designated output is

$$
E_j=[\ell_j]_{27}\times[r_j]_s.
\tag{PP3}
$$

Its phase is forced by its own whole inverse. For a global
selection $J$ with at most one admissible designation per
good cofactor, define the paid packet

$$
P=\bigcup_{j\in J}E_j.
\tag{PP4}
$$

Call a bad group absorbed when at least one of its whole
inverses satisfies $B_{m,a}\subseteq P$. Several packet
members may jointly cover that inverse.

**Simultaneous absorption lemma.** If every bad group is
absorbed by one such packet, the common-source transformation
gives an EB1 improvement.

To prove this, keep every selected good group's designated
$27s$ output and its admissible remaining allocation. In
each bad group choose one absorbed inverse and omit its
separate enclosure. Give its two other depth-four inverses
$27m$ and $81m$. These containments are valid, and the
omitted inverse is covered everywhere by outputs already
present in the good groups. The complete-source argument
therefore covers the entire deletion hole. Retained originals
cover its complement. The argument does not restrict to
private points or choose different sources for different
owners.

Each good group retains SC471's payment. Each absorbed bad
group has the strict joint comparison

$$
27m+81m=108m<13qm=(1+3+9)qm.
\tag{PP5}
$$

The supplier is charged once, regardless of how many groups
its enclosure absorbs. The old cost in PP5 includes all three
actual originals, including the one with no separate output.
Empty inverses, deeper originals and unit-cofactor terminal
donors retain their SC471--SC472 accounting. If $b$ is the
number of bad groups, the one resulting family satisfies

$$
K'\le K-b,\qquad \Sigma'<\Sigma.
\tag{PP6}
$$

For $b=0$, the ordinary allocation already has $K'\le K$
and strict sum descent. The actual $q$ and $3q$ terminals
supply strict savings even if no nonunit donor is selected.

Numerical distinctness is preserved. A group uses each of
$27m,81m,243m$ at most once. Different $(3q)$-free cofactors
cannot give equal shallow labels: equality forces both the
ternary exponent and cofactor to agree. The unit-cofactor
labels $27,81$ remain reserved for the actual terminals,
and any continuing $9q$ inverse uses $243$. Retained labels
have ternary height at most two, shallow labels have heights
three to five, and deeper labels have height at least six
with SC470's decoder. All new moduli are odd nonunits.
Thus PP6 is a comparison of one actual distinct whole cover,
which EB1 prohibits.

## 3. A residual obstruction and its moment

For a lawful common code $\omega$ and an admissible packet
$P$, the lemma implies

$$
\exists m\in\mathscr B_\omega\quad
\forall a\in\{0,1,2\},\qquad B_{m,a}\setminus P\ne\varnothing,
\tag{PP7}
$$

where $\mathscr B_\omega$ is the set of bad groups. The
three residual points need not coincide. In particular PP7
does not turn the separated $C_1$ cofactor phases of SC483
into a common intersection.

Choose one globally admissible packet $P_\omega$ for each
code. Its choice may use the entire actual family. Put

$$
\begin{aligned}
b(\omega)&=|\mathscr B_\omega|,\\
a(\omega)&=\#\{m\in\mathscr B_\omega:
          \exists a,\ B_{m,a}\subseteq P_\omega\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Then PP7 is equivalent to

$$
b(\omega)-a(\omega)\ge1.
\tag{PP8}
$$

The statistic $a$ counts each absorbed **bad group once**,
even if two or three of its inverses lie in the packet.
It counts neither absorbed inverse multiplicity nor private
mass.

Sample $R$ uniformly among six-subsets of $U$, and sample its
six words independently and uniformly from $\mathcal A$.
The counts $C_1,C_{2A},C_{2B},C_3$ denote potentially active
actual groups of the four digit-pattern types in SC479. Badness
is exactly their SC468 event. With $\Delta=\mathbb E[a]$,
PP8 and the unchanged SC479 probabilities give

$$
89925(1+\Delta)
\le981C_1+45C_{2A}+27C_{2B}+C_3.
\tag{PP9}
$$

No independence between different targets is used. With the
original support and height envelope entering SC481,

$$
C_1+C_{2A}+C_{2B}+C_3\le39101+N_{\ge3},
$$

where $N_{\ge3}$ counts potentially active cofactors having
at least three distinct cofactor primes. Consequently

$$
N_{\ge3}+980C_1+44C_{2A}+26C_{2B}
\ge50824+89925\Delta.
\tag{PP10}
$$

The constant $39101$ and this numerical specialization retain
SC481's height-envelope assumptions. The packet lemma itself
does not supply that envelope.

## 4. From an actual shallow supplier to whole-inverse containment

For one short target inverse and one designated donor,
$m,s\mid W$ give the exact test

$$
B_i\subseteq E_j
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\ell_i\equiv\ell_j\pmod{27},\qquad
s\mid m,\qquad r_i\equiv r_j\pmod s.
\tag{PP11}
$$

The first condition compares their actual ternary parents.
The other two are precisely containment of the target's full
cofactor coset in the donor coset. Compatible phases without
$s\mid m$ provide an intersection, not this containment.

Let $9qm$ be an actual $C_1$ target with private point $x$,
common first q-digit $c$, and final safe word $z$. Suppose an
actual supplier on a different first q-digit $d$ in the full
private fibre is

$$
n_j=3^aqs,\qquad 0\le a\le2,\quad s>1,\quad(s,3q)=1.
$$

The full-fibre interface of
[Report 851, PF1--PF3](851-private-fibre-shallow-supplier-cost.md#one-actual-private-fibre)
preserves the complete non-q coordinate. Thus

$$
\rho_{n_j}\equiv x\pmod{3^as}.
$$

If also $s\mid m$, it follows that

$$
\rho_{n_j}\equiv\rho_{9qm}\pmod s,\qquad
\rho_{n_j}\equiv z\pmod{3^a}.
\tag{PP12}
$$

Assigning the supplier's digit $d$ to a short leaf with old
word $z$ makes its inverse nonempty. If its leaf shares a
27-parent with the target's leaf, PP11 says its $27s$
enclosure contains the entire target inverse. Actual phases
are preserved throughout.

For a $C_1$ target this divisor is proper: $s=m$ would make
the supplier one of $qm,3qm,9qm$, whose first digits are all
$c$, contrary to $d\ne c$. A supplier at larger q-height
retains q-suffix constraints and does not automatically give
a $27s$ donor. The unit-cofactor actual $q,3q$ donors are
already terminal outputs, not additional continuing donors.

## 5. A shared-supplier cluster and one geometric completion

Fix distinct $c,d\in U$, a safe word $z$, and one actual
supplier $3^aqs$, with $s>1$, satisfying

$$
\rho_{3^aqs}\equiv d\pmod q,\qquad
\rho_{3^aqs}\equiv z\pmod{3^a}.
$$

Suppose $M$ distinct potentially active $C_1$ cofactors have
common digit $c$, final word $z$, and

$$
s\mid m,\qquad \rho_{9qm}\equiv\rho_{3^aqs}\pmod s.
\tag{PP13}
$$

Require one other actual member of the supplier's group
$\{qs,3qs,9qs\}$ with first q-digit

$$
e\notin\{c,d\}.
\tag{PP14}
$$

The escape digit $e$ is an actual original phase. Consider

$$
\mathcal E=\{c,d\in R,\ e\notin R,\ f(c)=f(d)=z\}.
\tag{PP15}
$$

On this event all $M$ target groups are bad. The supplier's
$d$ inverse is nonempty and short; its member at $e$ is
empty or long. Its group is therefore good, and its short
$d$ owner is an admissible $27s$ designation.

### Prescribed leaves and both terminals fit together

Fix the entire sampled pair $(R,f)$ on PP15. Let
$n_w=\#f^{-1}(w)$. At least two of the six prescribed leaves
have old word $z$, so $n_v\le4$ for every $v\ne z$.
The actual first-3 root of $3q$ contains at least two safe
words: it differs from the pure 3 root, and the pure 9 guard
removes at most one of its three words. Choose such a word
$v\ne z$.

Place both terminals above $v$. The $q$ terminal occupies
one depth-three prefix, consuming three of its nine depth-four
slots. Place the $3q$ terminal in one depth-four slot under
a different depth-three prefix above $v$. It has its actual
required first-3 root. The terminals are disjoint and consume
four slots, leaving five for the at most four requested short
leaves above $v$.

There are no terminals above $z$. Put the $c,d$ leaves in
two distinct depth-four children of a single depth-three
parent above $z$. Its other $n_z-2\le4$ requested leaves
fit in the remaining seven slots. All other safe words have
nine free slots and at most four requests. The resulting
initial prefix code has

$$
135-8-2-2\cdot6=113
\tag{PP16}
$$

leaves. Assign the actual terminal digits $\alpha,\beta$,
the six prescribed digits, and then the remaining digits
bijectively to the remaining ordinary leaves. Since
$\gamma\notin U$, the actual $9q$ exceptional cell is not
made short.

If $G\ge2$, a short leaf has 81 ternary extensions to depth
eight and an ordinary depth-five leaf has 27. Both admit
injective assignments into 113 next q-digits. Continue with
the existing 27-way dictionaries at subsequent q-levels.
Fix terminal suffixes once. If $G=1$, no deeper dictionary
is required. This completes one source for all originals and
all $M$ targets.

Choose such a completion deterministically for every $(R,f)$
in PP15 and any lawful completion off that event. This changes
no sampled $(R,f)$ probability. Badness depends only on the
actual digit/word tests and $(R,f)$, not on leaf positions
within old words or the deeper dictionaries. The full $W$
coordinate is preserved. No different geometry is chosen for
different targets. An arbitrary previously completed geometry
need not already put $c,d$ together; the assertion is the
existence of this single compatible completion over each
sampled pair.

By PP11 and PP13, the one resulting $27s$ output absorbs the
$9qm$ inverse of every target in the cluster. Hence

$$
a(\omega)\ge M\qquad\text{on }\mathcal E.
\tag{PP17}
$$

Every target cofactor properly contains $s$, so none is the
supplier group. The supplier remains charged only once.

### The restricted correction

If $e\in U$, selecting $c,d$ while excluding $e$ leaves
four choices from 107 digits. The two prescribed word choices
have probability $1/25$. Therefore

$$
\Pr(\mathcal E)=\frac{\binom{107}{4}}{25\binom{110}{6}}
=\frac{26}{269775},\qquad
\Delta\ge\frac{26M}{269775}.
\tag{PP18}
$$

Since $89925\cdot26/269775=26/3$, PP10 proves PP1.
If $e\notin U$, its exclusion is automatic and

$$
\Pr(\mathcal E)=\frac{\binom{108}{4}}{25\binom{110}{6}}
=\frac3{29975},\qquad
N_{\ge3}+980C_1+44C_{2A}+26C_{2B}\ge50824+9M.
\tag{PP19}
$$

No probability is multiplied across the $M$ targets. On each
sampled pair in PP15, the compatible completion and PP7 force
some bad group outside this absorbed cluster, so $b\ge M+1$.
Neither the existence of the cluster nor a lower bound for
$M$ follows from this argument.

### Finite geometry verification

The [geometry program](../../../frontier/cover-geometry/paid-packet/paid_packet_geometry.py)
constructs the initial forest with natural-number congruence
classes. Its verifier independently expands little-endian
ternary words and checks every admissible depth-five cell
has multiplicity exactly one. The
[all-phase result](../../../frontier/cover-geometry/paid-packet/paid_packet_geometry.json)
covers all $18$ disjoint actual pure-3/pure-9 phase pairs,
both compatible $3q$ roots, all five shared words, and all
$5^4$ ordered assignments of the four other short leaves:

$$
18\cdot2\cdot5\cdot5^4=112500
$$

configurations and $112500\cdot135=15187500$ depth-five
cells. Every configuration has one depth-three terminal,
one depth-four terminal, six short depth-four continuing
leaves and 105 ordinary depth-five leaves, for 113 leaves
and 111 continuing leaves. The program exits zero on this
complete enumeration.

The q-digit names in the program designate two terminals,
six distinct short digits and a representative excluded
nonterminal digit. Exclusion of the actual $\gamma$ from
$U$ is an input of the code domain, not a conclusion about
an original cover. When $\gamma=\beta$, its actual digit
is already terminal. The enumeration assumes no normalization
of the actual pure-3/pure-9 phases.

From the repository root, the result can be regenerated with

```sh
python3 docs/reports/erdos7-odd-covering/frontier/cover-geometry/paid-packet/paid_packet_geometry.py \
  --mode all-phases --output /tmp/paid_packet_geometry.json
```

The stored result includes its script hash and the deterministic
construction-stream hash; elapsed time is not a deterministic
field. This is a finite check of initial code geometry. The
deeper dictionaries are justified by the preceding cardinality
argument. No odd distinct whole cover is enumerated, and the
program is not Lean verification of the packet theorem.

## 6. External-cofactor packets and the exact relative cover

A divisor donor is sufficient for absorption, but a packet
can also cover a target using several external cofactors.
For each ternary parent $\lambda\pmod{27}$ define

$$
V_\lambda=
\bigcup_{\substack{j\in J\\\ell_j\equiv\lambda\ (27)}}[r_j]_{s_j}
\subseteq\mathbb Z/W\mathbb Z.
$$

A short target inverse then satisfies exactly

$$
B_i\subseteq P
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
[r_i]_m\subseteq V_{\ell_i\bmod27}.
\tag{PP20}
$$

Parameterize its cofactor coset as $u=r_i+mt$ with
$t\in\mathbb Z/(W/m)\mathbb Z$. For a donor modulus $s$,
put $g=\gcd(m,s)$. The restriction is empty unless
$g\mid r_j-r_i$, with the difference taken in the integers.
Otherwise it is the actual relative class

$$
t\equiv\frac{r_j-r_i}{g}
\left(\frac m g\right)^{-1}\pmod{s/g}.
\tag{PP21}
$$

The inverse exists because $m/g$ and $s/g$ are coprime,
and $s/g\mid W/m$. Thus PP20 is full coverage by the
prescribed relative classes PP21, not merely pairwise
compatibility. Distinct original cofactors can produce the
same relative modulus.

**Relative collision consequence.** Without a compatible
relative modulus one, an absorbing packet has two relative
classes of the same modulus and different phases.

Indeed, all nonempty relative moduli are then odd nonunits.
There are fewer than $K$ packet members: at most one is
selected per nonunit cofactor group, and the actual $q,3q$
originals are not such donors. If every repeated relative
modulus carried just one reduced phase, merge identical
classes. PP20 would give a distinct odd nonunit cover of
the whole $t$ quotient, hence of the integers, with fewer
than $K$ classes. This contradicts the first EB1 coordinate.
A compatible modulus one is exactly a divisor donor with
the required phase. A collision is only a necessary condition
for an external packet; it does not supply PP20.

For a fixed code this is also an exact finite certificate.
Choose admissible binary donor variables $y_j$, at most one
per good cofactor, and binary omission variables $v_{m,a}$
with $\sum_{a=0}^2v_{m,a}=1$ for each bad group. Require

$$
\sum_{\substack{j:\ell_j\equiv\ell_{m,a}\ (27)\\
                  u\equiv r_j\ (s_j)}}y_j\ge v_{m,a}
\qquad\text{for every }u\in[r_{m,a}]_m.
\tag{PP22}
$$

Feasibility pays all omissions simultaneously. These are
whole-coset constraints; the formulation supplies neither
a small witness nor feasibility for an actual EB1 family.

## 7. Acyclic enlargement with permanent donors

After the current paid packet covers one inverse of a bad
group, omit that inverse and permanently retain the other
two enclosures at $27m,81m$. One of the two surviving owners
can be designated as a donor for later groups through its
single $27m$ output. Order such rescues so that each omitted
inverse is covered by outputs already paid at that stage.

Induction through this order proves all omissions covered
by the final family. Group payment and numerical distinctness
are exactly those of Section 2. If all initially bad groups
are rescued, the exchange contradicts EB1. Each group must
keep its chosen donor; it cannot later omit an owner whose
enclosure funds an earlier rescue, or donate multiple
independently phased copies of $27m$.

Proper-divisor edges $s\mid m$, $s<m$, supply an increasing
numerical order automatically. They do not supply an initial
paid donor, a compatible owner choice, or the needed phase
containment. Circular promises that unpaid groups fund one
another are not this acyclic certificate. Acyclicity is a
sufficient certificate condition, not a claim that every
possible simultaneous exchange must be acyclic.

## 8. Literature mechanisms and the remaining extraction problem

The following results provide related tools with specific
hypotheses; none supplies the actual packet required above.

Balister--Bollobás--Morris--Sahasrabudhe--Tiba,
[*The structure and number of Erdős covering systems*,
arXiv:1904.04806v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.04806v2),
Lemmas 4.7--4.9, bound reuse along a selected depth-first
exploration. A hyperplane occurring in two selected garbage
sets lies along one ancestor chain, and its remaining fixed
coordinate ranks strictly decrease. Their total weighted
charge for one hyperplane is bounded by

$$
\sum_{\ell\ge2}2^{-\ell/4}
=\frac1{2^{1/2}-2^{1/4}}<5.
$$

To use this for packet suppliers would require a common
exploration in which repeated actual uses force those distinct
ranks. Different $C_1$ targets sharing one first-q direction
do not by themselves provide such ranks. Their Theorem 2.3
extracts a generalized frame from a minimal hyperplane cover
with full fixed-coordinate union and
$|\mathcal C|\le C\sum_i(|S_i|-1)$; it does not prescribe
the q-digits, cofactor phases or donor choices in PP13--PP14.

In the same authors' [*Erdős covering systems*,
arXiv:2211.01417v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.01417v1),
the proof of Lemma 4.3 first retains actual intersections:

$$
\mathbb E_{k-1}[\alpha_k^2]
\le\frac1{|S_k|^2}\sum_{A_1,A_2}
\mathbb P_{k-1}(A_1^{[k-1]}\cap A_2^{[k-1]}).
$$

Only afterward are these terms majorized by support data.
The measure is their constructed distorted measure, extended
uniformly on each new fibre. This identifies a useful place
to retain PP21's actual phase intersections; it neither
changes our common law nor turns pair intersections into
whole-coset coverage.

Nagy--Pach--Tomon, [*Additive bases, coset covers, and
non-vanishing linear maps*, arXiv:2111.13658v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13658v1),
Theorem 1.2, gives

$$
\left[A:\bigcap_{i=1}^kH_i\right]
\le\exp(O(k\log\log k))
$$

for an irredundant coset cover $\{H_ix_i\}_{i=1}^k$ of
an abelian group $A$. Its applicable object here is an
irredundant subcover of PP21, after PP20 has been established.
It constrains the effective quotient but supplies neither
coverage nor a small subcover from divisor closure. The
citation gives no explicit numerical constant in the bound.

Bispels and coauthors, [*A further investigation on covering
systems with odd moduli*, arXiv:2507.16135v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16135v1),
Theorem 2.5 and the proof of Theorem 3.1, give a cover with
odd moduli distinct except for three copies of 9, using phases
$0,3,6\pmod9$. The same version leaves unresolved the
variant with phases $4,5,r\pmod9$, where
$r\in\{0,1,2,3,6,7,8\}$ and all other moduli are odd,
distinct nonunits unequal to 9. This distinguishes existence
with repeated moduli from existence with prescribed phases;
it does not authorize changing PP21's actual residues.

The remaining requirement is an abundance or extraction
theorem from the original whole cover to compatible payable
packets on one common code. The private-fibre cost in
[Report 851, PF8--PF11](851-private-fibre-shallow-supplier-cost.md#a-c1-target-at-q--113)
constrains missing shallow service. It does not force the
shallow suppliers that occur to have dividing cofactors, to
cover a whole cofactor coset collectively, or to admit one
consistent $27s$ owner per group. The unrestricted supplier
reuse problem, the height-two branch and Erdős #7 remain
unresolved by these results.
