# A locally realized paid residual with expensive supported laws and incompatible owners

This finite family has 78 distinct odd AP moduli, normalized prime classes, an explicit private point for every original, and pairwise disjoint comparable originals. One fixed lawful six-code makes the initial paid base leave a four-point cofactor residual. Every probability on that residual has complete divisor-query norm at least 90; its congruence hull is unchanged; and no permanent choice of one owner from each of the two available good groups covers the residual.

The family is a **noncover**, with the explicit uncovered integer 1076125640591. Its numerical palette is not divisor-closed: 113·13 = 1469 divides the target modulus but is absent. No EB1 realization or counterexample to a theorem using whole coverage is asserted.

The [exact data](../../../frontier/cover-geometry/paid-mask-owner-obstruction/result.json) are generated by the [integer/CRT checker](../../../frontier/cover-geometry/paid-mask-owner-obstruction/verify_paid_mask_owner_obstruction.py). The checker evaluates the explicit family and reduced Boolean grid; it never enumerates the full period.

### Single-prime exclusions

Let $t_0<\dots<t_{23}$ be the increasing list

$$
\{3^a d:0\le a\le2,\ d\mid m\}.
$$

For each external prime $p\in\{5,7,11,19,23\}$, list its forbidden roots in the order

$$
b_0=0,\qquad b_i=i+2\quad(1\le i\le p-3).
$$

For $0\le i\le p-3$, write $t_i=3^{a_i}d_i$, and include the class of modulus $3^{a_i}d_i p$ with phases

$$
z\equiv2\pmod{3^{a_i}},\quad z\equiv1\pmod{d_i},\quad z\equiv b_i\pmod p.

The modulus-one congruences impose no condition. These give $3+5+9+17+21=55$ distinct classes. The root-zero class has tag $t_0=1$, so it is the normalized prime class $0\bmod p$.

## The actual initial paid base and its exact residual

Let H be precisely the union of the q-free originals on the common source and all raw inverses of the two good groups. Their raw inverses are long initial leaves, so none meets the target short leaf 2 modulo 81. Hence only the q-free family affects H on that target leaf.

On the target cofactor coset $[1]_m$, every tag $d_i\mid m$ has its required phase; on the target leaf all ternary tags have their required phase. The 55 single-prime exclusions therefore leave exactly roots 1 and 2 at each external prime. The six pair constraints then enforce equality along the path $5-11-19$ and the edge $7-23$.

Consequently the residual cofactor set is exactly

$$
K=\{w_{xy}:x,y\in\{0,1\}\},
$$
$$
w_{xy}\equiv1\pmod m,\quad
w_{xy}\equiv1+x\pmod p\ (p\in A),\quad
w_{xy}\equiv1+y\pmod p\ (p\in B).

Its representatives modulo W are

| (x,y) | $w_{xy}$ |
|---|---:|
| (0,0) | 1 |

Each external prime takes both roots 1 and 2 on K. Thus no external prime divides all differences. All differences are divisible by m, giving exactly

$$
\Gamma(K)=\gcd\bigl(W,\{w-w_{00}:w\in K\}\bigr)=m.
$$

The target original $9qm$ is the only q-bearing original with first q-digit 1. Its complete private region modulo Q consists exactly of the four CRT lifts with old word 2, q-digit 1, and cofactor in K. Thus K is also its complete projected private region.

## Query norm and permanent choices

For any probability $\mu$ supported on K, write $\mu_X,\mu_Y$ for its two Boolean marginals and put

$$
\mathcal Q(\mu)=\sum_{s\mid W}\max_{r\bmod s}\mu([r]_s).
$$

The unit divisor is included. Since W is squarefree, a divisor's query on K observes no Boolean coordinate, X alone, Y alone, or both. The respective numbers of divisors are

$$
8,\quad8(2^3-1)=56,\quad8(2^2-1)=24,
\quad8(2^3-1)(2^2-1)=168.
$$

All divisors of m belong to the first category, including $s=1$. Therefore

$$
\mathcal Q(\mu)=8+56\max_x\mu_X(x)+24\max_y\mu_Y(y)
                    +168\max_{x,y}\mu(x,y)\ge90.
$$

Uniform mass on the four points attains equality.

The two effective outputs of group 5 cover respectively X=0 and X=1; those of group 7 cover respectively Y=0 and Y=1. Selecting one effective owner per group misses the opposite Boolean corner. An ineffective third owner only removes coverage. Therefore all nine complete owner assignments fail to cover K, though the union of all four effective candidate outputs covers K.

None of these individual candidate outputs contains K, in agreement with $5,7\nmid\Gamma(K)$. The four raw shallow nondivisor indicators sum to exactly two at every point of K. For the represented family there are no deeper q-bearing originals; the actual six-sibling nondivisor load on K is therefore exactly two, under either source measure. This does not satisfy the six-unit whole-coverage inequality: the fixture is a noncover.

## Explicit noncoverage and remaining boundary

The integer

$$
1076125640591\pmod Q
$$

has old word 2, q-digit 50, and phase 1 at every cofactor prime. Its cofactor point is $w_{00}$, so every q-free original misses it. No q-bearing original has q-digit 50. Thus no original covers this integer.

Even with actual initial paid-mask provenance, normalized prime phases, local source geometry, original privacy, comparable disjointness and the displayed collision condition, every supported law has $\mathcal Q(\mu)\ge90$, the hull remains $\Gamma(K)=m$, and all nine permanent owner plans fail. Thus those properties together with this query-cost lower bound do not force hull growth or coherent coverage. It does not disprove such an implication under whole coverage, divisor closure, or the remaining EB1 exchange constraints.

The positive interfaces remain the existing exact hull test (Report858 SF8 / Report385 PH4), actual admissible-owner incidence, and the fixed-plan covering tests of Reports855–856. A query statistic alone supplies none of those missing availability or simultaneous-choice conditions. Report855 already gives permanent-plan incompatibility, and Reports529/571 give distinct source/query obstructions. This example includes actual paid-mask provenance and an unchanged hull.

## The obstruction disappears under another lawful common code

The fixture also fails a necessary global property of an EB1 whole cover.
[Report388 SC468--SC471](../350-399/388-source-global-substitution-collision-moment.md)
requires a bad group under every lawful common six-code. Here the only
nonunit shallow q-bearing cofactors are $5,7,6409$. Choose the six short
digits to be $30,31,32,33,34,35$, keeping the same six short leaf positions
and the two terminal assignments. All actual nonterminal q-bearing digits
can then be assigned to long leaves matching their literal old ternary
phases. The exact alternative assignment is included in the data.

There are no bad groups for this code. Replace each of the twelve
q-bearing originals $3^a113s$ by its enclosure with numerical label
$3^{a+3}s$, retaining its literal cofactor phase. The alternative code
makes each whole inverse lie in that enclosure. The twelve new labels
are distinct, odd, and absent from the original palette; all 66 q-free
originals are retained. The count and price are

| q-bearing classes | Original | Replacement |
|---|---:|---:|
| Number | 12 | 12 |
| Sum of numerical moduli | 9,433,918 | 2,254,122 |

Every replaced modulus is multiplied by $27/113$, so the saving is
$7,179,796$. If the input family covered all integers, the common-source
pullback argument would make the replacement a whole cover, contradicting
EB1 minimality. The retained 3/9 guards cover the unsafe old words; on a
safe word the unique prefix leaf supplies the old first-q digit while
preserving the old word and complete cofactor coordinate. This explains
why every original owner at that source is still paid.

This implication uses whole coverage. It is not containment of the old
numerical AP union in the new one: the explicit integer
$20,444,261,395,637$ is covered by the old family and missed by the new
family. Thus the fixture's fixed-code obstruction cannot replace the
universal-over-codes obstruction in the actual EB1 problem.
