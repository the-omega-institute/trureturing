## 2. Why a finite frontier classifies every budget

Divide B4 by P_d=product_q A_q>0:

    F_d(w,v)=G_d(w,v)/P_d
       =g S_0(w,v)−sum_j C_j S_j(w,v)/product_(q in T_j)A_q.  (B5)

All C_j and S_j are nonnegative. Increasing any d_q decreases A_q, enlarges each affected reciprocal denominator factor and therefore cannot increase F_d(w,v). This holds for every individual source corner and hence for their minimum. Consequently the strictly positive budget set is downward closed and the strictly negative budget set is upward closed.

Only positivity has this unconditional monotonicity statement. The unnormalized gate need not be globally monotone throughout the negative region. Within the positive region, if d<=e and every gate at e is positive, then both P_d>=P_e and F_d>=F_e>0, so G_d>=G_e. This also proves the common positive lower bound used in B1.

The exact certificate verifies18 positive boundary vectors and29 negative boundary vectors. They are two antichains. It checks, for every one of the126225 budgets, that exactly one alternative holds:

    d<=one of the18 positive vectors;
    one of the29 negative vectors<=d.                   (B6)

Their closures therefore partition the whole domain. There are58 vectors in the first part and126167 in the second; no zero gate occurs in this domain. The29 negative certificates reject this product-source envelope only. They do not prove a cover, failure of actual survival, or impossibility of another thinning/source.

If d_q=q−1, the chosen outside source has zero mass in that coordinate, so this construction cannot yield a positive survivor. Such budgets are excluded by B2. Budgets allowing some d_q=0 are a different domain and are not included in the count126225.

    sum_(l,m)x_l y_m M15(l,m)
      =(sum_l x_l)(sum_m y_m)
        −(sum_(l<3)x_l)(sum_(m<5)y_m).                 (B9)
