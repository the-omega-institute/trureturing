## 2. Clip against the unused part of each raw cap

If one label has face cap c, error e and raw cap u>=c, then

    mu(C)<=min(c+e,u)=c+min(e,u-c).              (JE7)

Thus the four shallow errors to be summed are

    min(2delta+3R,29/120),
    min(2delta+3R,1/15),
    min(4delta+35R,11/75),
    min(3delta+30R,2/75).                        (JE8)

For the two pure-axis tails define

    A3(delta,R)=sum_(a>=3)
           min(3delta*3^-a+3R,(9/20)*3^-a),
    A5(delta,R)=sum_(b>=2)
           min((2delta/15)*5^-b+R,(17/30)*5^-b).

Let E(delta,rho) be the sum of(JE8) and(JE9), with R from(JE3).
Equations(JE4)--(JE7) prove this complete additional-error bound
directly. Mixed-family error(JE6) is zero. Summing these bounds
does not require the labels' residues to agree.

The new error is at most136's B1(100(delta+rho)) throughout the
domain. Indeed R<=delta+rho, every displayed individual source
and residual error is at most100(delta+rho), and each raw-minus-face
gap is at most the corresponding raw Haar cap. Apply these two
bounds inside each minimum and include the other nonnegative
terms of the old double series. This domination is pointwise;
it does not rely on the numerical rectangles below.

For completeness, each infinite series in(JE9) is an exact finite
calculation plus a full geometric continuation. For p>1 and
0<=v<c, put

    A=sum_(n>=b) min(v*p^-n+e,c*p^-n).

At e=0 it equals v*p^(1-b)/(p-1). At e>0, let N be the first
integer n>=b with e>=(c-v)*p^-n. Then

    A=(N-b)*e
        +v*[p^(1-b)-p^(1-N)]/(p-1)
        +c*p^(1-N)/(p-1).                       (JE10)

The last term contains every n>=N. Equality at the crossing causes
no ambiguity. Both relevant drifts are smaller than their cap gaps
throughout delta<=1/1000. A second exact computation starts with the
full geometric raw-gap sum and subtracts the finitely many savings
at depths before N.

E is nondecreasing in both delta and rho: each original summand in
(JE8)--(JE9) is. This establishes uniform rectangle bounds from
their upper corners. The monotonicity is a property of the full
series, not an inference from evaluated examples.
