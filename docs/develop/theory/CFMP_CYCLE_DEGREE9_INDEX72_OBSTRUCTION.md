# No normal torsionfree [4,9,3] orientation quotient of index 72

## Scope

This is an obstruction for the normal (regular-cover) Coxeter-sector construction of CFMP_CYCLE_DEGREE9.md. It does not rule out an arbitrary pseudo-simplicial face pairing of 24 tetrahedra. The six-sector block is the regular Coxeter block KQ, with

    W = <a,b,c,d | a²=b²=c²=d²=1,
        (ab)^4=(bc)^9=(cd)^3=1,
        [a,c]=[a,d]=[b,d]=1>,
    W+ = ker(det:W -> {+1,-1}),

and K=<c,d> ~= S3, |K|=6. A torsionfree subgroup Gamma <= W+ gives N=[W+:Gamma]/3 tetrahedral blocks; the regular finite-cover route asks for Gamma normal in W+.

## Theorem

There is no torsionfree Gamma normal in W+ with [W+:Gamma]=72. Consequently the regular Coxeter-sector construction has no normal N=24 quotient.

## Proof

Assume such Gamma exists and put G+=W+/Gamma, so |G+|=72. Let

    L+ = <r,s> <= W+,    r=bc,    s=cd,    t=db=(rs)^(-1).

The last identity is the cancellation bc*cd*db=1. Let H be the image of L+ in G+. Torsionfreeness of Gamma makes the finite parabolic inclusions faithful: r has order 9, s has order 3, and t has order 2. Hence |H| divides 72 and is one of 18, 36, 72.

Let P=<r>. It is a Sylow 3-subgroup of H, since |P|=9. If |H|=18, Sylow gives a unique P, so s and t=(rs)^(-1) would lie in P, impossible because P has odd order and t has order 2. Thus |H| is 36 or 72. For either of these orders, the Sylow number is 1 or 4; the case 1 gives the same contradiction, so there are exactly 4 Sylow 3-subgroups. Conjugation gives a transitive permutation representation alpha:H -> S4 on those four Sylow 3-subgroups.

The image alpha(H) has order divisible by 4 by transitivity. It also has order divisible by 3: if both 3-elements alpha(r), alpha(s) were trivial, then alpha(t)=alpha((rs)^(-1)) would be trivial, contradicting transitivity. Therefore 12 divides |alpha(H)|.

Because alpha(r) has order dividing 3 in S4, z:=r^3 lies in ker(alpha). If |H|=72, then |H/<z>|=24. The kernel of alpha has order 3 or 6; in the latter case its unique Sylow 3-subgroup is characteristic, so <z> is normal in H in either case. In H/<z>, the images rbar,sbar satisfy rbar^3=sbar^3=(rbar*sbar)^2=1. The spherical triangle presentation <R,S | R^3=S^3=(RS)^2=1> ~= A4 has order 12, so every quotient generated by rbar,sbar has order at most 12. This contradicts order 24. Hence |H|=36. Now ker(alpha) has order 3, so ker(alpha)=<z>, and H/<z> ~= A4.

The element z is central. Indeed, r centralizes z=r^3, while conjugation by s gives a homomorphism of the order-3 cyclic subgroup <z> to Aut(C3) ~= C2, necessarily trivial; r,s generate H.

The image sbar is nontrivial: if s lay in <z>, then tbar=(rbar*sbar)^(-1)=rbar^(-1) would have both order 3 and order dividing 2. Thus rbar,sbar are nontrivial 3-cycles in A4. The two conjugacy classes of 3-cycles in A4 are inverse classes, so there are xbar in A4 and epsilon in {+1,-1} with xbar*rbar*xbar^(-1)=sbar^epsilon.

Lift xbar to x in H. For some k in {0,1,2}, centrality of z gives x*r*x^(-1)=s^epsilon*z^k. Cubing yields z = x*r^3*x^(-1) = (s^epsilon*z^k)^3 = 1, contrary to r having order 9. This proves the theorem.

## Exact finite-group inputs

- Sylow's theorem gives n3 congruent to 1 modulo 3 and n3 divides |H|/9.
- A transitive subgroup of S4 whose order is divisible by 3 has order 12 or 24; the order-12 subgroup is A4.
- The spherical triangle group Delta(3,3,2), with presentation <R,S | R^3=S^3=(RS)^2=1>, is A4.
- The finite-parabolic orders and faithfulness used above are checked exactly in verify_cycle9_coxeter_local.py (orders 16, 12, 18; K order 6).

The Coxeter-sector hypotheses are documented in PR 12065 (https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/pull/12065), and the exact finite-quotient audit and double-coset count in PR 12077 (https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/pull/12077).

## Limitation

The argument is about a normal torsionfree subgroup of W+ (the regular finite-cover route). It does not prove that every arbitrary 24-tetrahedron pseudo-simplicial face pairing comes from such a subgroup, and therefore makes no unrestricted minimality claim for N=24.
## 1. Abstract finite-group obstruction

**引理 1.1（Orders nine, three and two）。** Let $H$ be any finite group, and let $r,s\in H$. Write $\operatorname{ord}(x)$ for the exact order of an element and $\langle r,s\rangle$ for the subgroup they generate. Then

$$
\forall H\ \text{finite group},\quad \forall r,s\in H,\qquad
\left(
\langle r,s\rangle=H\ \land\ |H|\mid72\ \land\
\operatorname{ord}(r)=9\ \land\ \operatorname{ord}(s)=3\ \land\
\operatorname{ord}(rs)=2
\right)\Longrightarrow\bot.
$$

Proof. Suppose all five hypotheses hold. Lagrange's theorem gives $9\mid|H|$ and $2\mid|H|$. Together with $|H|\mid72$, these imply $|H|\in\{18,36,72\}$. The subgroup $P=\langle r\rangle$ has order nine and index two, four or eight, so it is a Sylow $3$-subgroup. Sylow's divisibility and congruence theorems give

$$
n_3\mid[H:P],\qquad n_3\equiv1\pmod3,
\qquad n_3\in\{1,4\}.
$$

If $n_3=1$, every $3$-subgroup is contained in $P$, so $s\in P$. Then $rs\in P$, contradicting its exact order two because $|P|=9$.

Thus $n_3=4$. Let $X$ be the four Sylow $3$-subgroups, let $\alpha:H\to\operatorname{Sym}(X)$ be the conjugation action, and put $I=\alpha(H)$. Sylow conjugacy makes this action transitive, so orbit-stabilizer gives $4\mid|I|$. Since $|\operatorname{Sym}(X)|=24$, the order of $\alpha(r)$ divides both nine and twenty-four and hence divides three. The order of $\alpha(s)$ also divides three. These two images cannot both be the identity: generation by $r,s$ would make $I$ trivial, contradicting $4\mid|I|$. A nonidentity one therefore has exact order three, giving $3\mid|I|$. Consequently $|I|\in\{12,24\}$.

Set $K=\ker\alpha$ and $z=r^3$. The subgroup $K$ is normal as a homomorphism kernel. The relation $\alpha(r)^3=1$ puts $z$ in $K$, and $\operatorname{ord}(z)=3$. The identity $|K|\,|I|=|H|$ and the possible orders above give $|K|\in\{3,6\}$. Inside $K$, the subgroup $Z=\langle z\rangle$ has order three and index one or two. Its Sylow number divides that index and is congruent to one modulo three, so $Z$ is the unique Sylow $3$-subgroup of $K$. It is characteristic in $K$ and therefore normal in $H$. In particular, normality of $Z$ is a consequence of the hypotheses.

Conjugation on $Z$ gives a homomorphism $H\to\operatorname{Aut}(Z)$. Since $Z$ is cyclic of order three, its automorphism group has order two. The automorphism induced by $s$ has order dividing both two and three, because $s^3=1$, so it is the identity. Thus $sz=zs$. Also $r$ commutes with $z=r^3$; as $r,s$ generate $H$, the element $z$ is central.

The remaining word argument uses only $r^9=s^3=(rs)^2=1$ and commutation of $z=r^3$ with $s$. Put

$$
u=rs,\qquad v=rur^{-1},\qquad w=r^2ur^{-2}.
$$

All three are involutions, and direct multiplication in the given order yields

$$
wvu=r^3s^3=z.
$$

The element $z$ commutes with $u$ and $w$. Multiplying the displayed identity by $w$ on the left gives $vu=wz$, whence

$$
(vu)^2=(wz)^2=z^2.
$$

Conjugating by $u=u^{-1}$ gives $(uv)^2=z^2$, since $uzu^{-1}=z$. On the other hand, $uv=(vu)^{-1}$, so

$$
z^4=(vu)^2(uv)^2=(vu)^2\bigl((vu)^2\bigr)^{-1}=1.
$$

We also have $z^3=r^9=1$. Hence $z=z^4=1$, contradicting $\operatorname{ord}(r)=9$. This proves the lemma.

The ordinary finite-group argument in the preceding proof supplies the provenance of this obstruction. Its Sylow, orbit-stabilizer, characteristic-subgroup and cyclic-automorphism inputs are the classical group-theoretic statements also presented in the Mathlib contributors' [Sylow theory](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/db584cd6d46c92f209a44c0f1c829460d327499d/Mathlib/GroupTheory/Sylow.lean) and [cyclic groups](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/db584cd6d46c92f209a44c0f1c829460d327499d/Mathlib/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Cyclic.lean). The conjugate-involution argument proves the contradiction directly, without classifying the image as an alternating group.

For the Coxeter application, one must separately construct the finite image of $\langle bc,cd\rangle$, establish generation by the two specified images, show its cardinality divides seventy-two, and justify their exact orders nine, three and two for the product. The lemma assumes these group-theoretic hypotheses; it does not establish finite-parabolic faithfulness or the passage from a Coxeter quotient to them. Its conclusion therefore retains the preceding normal-cover restriction and supplies no obstruction to arbitrary pairings of twenty-four tetrahedra.
