## 1. Native sources, owned weights and credited suppliers

**Definition 1.1 (the unchanged observation task).** Let

$$
\mathcal T::=\alpha\mid\beta\mid\langle u,v\rangle,
\qquad h(\alpha)=h(\beta)=0,
\qquad h(\langle u,v\rangle)=1+\max\{h(u),h(v)\}.
$$

Pairing is free and ordered. Every finite word in $L,R$, including

alpha or beta at the corresponding leaf, branch at a pair, and absent

$$
\mathcal A=\{\alpha,\beta,\mathsf{branch},\mathsf{absent}\},
\qquad \rho\alpha=\beta,
\quad \rho\beta=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,
\quad \rho\langle u,v\rangle=\langle\rho u,\rho v\rangle.
$$

For public $D\ge1$, retain exactly

$$
\mathcal F_D=\{\langle\alpha,v\rangle:h(v)\le D\},\qquad
W_D=\{q:|q|\le D-1\},\qquad
\mathcal P_D=\{Rq:q\in W_D\}.
\tag{RCE.1}
$$

later acquisition. The iterates $\rho^kt$ specify prediction targets.
A deterministic effective preparation starts source-blind from $D$ and
common installed tables, receives no later target, acquires source
dependence only by charged literal Reads, and terminates on every $t$.
Its export $E(t)$ includes every accessible surviving source distinction:
selectors, control, buffers, operands, lengths, allocation, counters,

dependence and supplies lawful entry.

Only after export is a public source-independent $p\in\mathcal P_D$
supplied. A deterministic reader uses $E(t),D,p$ and common tables to
choose zero Reads or one fresh Read of that same $t$ at any finite
original-root word. The choice precedes its reply and every supplied
count. The resulting predictor $Z$ must satisfy

$$
\operatorname{Dec}(Z,D,p,k)=r_{\rho^kt}(p)
\quad\text{for every }t\in\mathcal F_D,\ p\in\mathcal P_D,
\text{ and subsequently supplied authentic }k\ge0,
\tag{RCE.2}
$$

without further Reads. Define $M_1(D)=\min|E[\mathcal F_D]|$ over these
jointly correct procedures. There is no subtree, equality, source-identity,
age, reset, copying, free-navigation or advice port. Handoff, isolation,

Preparation Reads and work, temporary storage, installed tables,

and letters, count representation and physical time are separate
resources. These are the original [FMR, §§1–2] and [NPC, §1] premises.

## 2. The support-complete joint functional

**Definition 2.1 (finite interfaces and full support tests).** A finite
interface $I$ consists of a nonempty finite state set $S$, finite target
maps $g_q:S\to G_q$, and finitely many experiments $r_a:S\to A_a$,
each with at most four replies, including a constant experiment.
A nonempty $P\subseteq S$ is range-feasible when $|g_q(P)|\le4$
for every target. A target is hard on $P$ when no single experiment
determines it there. Write

$$
H_I(P)=\{q:\nexists a\ \forall x,x'\in P,
             \ r_a(x)=r_a(x')\Rightarrow g_q(x)=g_q(x')\},
\qquad U_P(x)=(g_q(x))_{q\in H_I(P)}.
$$

For every nonnegative $w:S\to[0,\infty)$ define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\kappa_I(w,P)^2
 &=\sum_y\left(\sum_{x\in P:U_P(x)=y}w(x)\right)^2,\\
J_I(w)&=\max_{\substack{\varnothing\ne P\subseteq S\\
                                  P\text{ range-feasible}}}
                                  \kappa_I(w,P).
\end{aligned}
\tag{RCE.10}
$$

An empty hard-target vector has one cell. For the native depth-$d$
interface write $J_d$. This is the exhaustive joint functional of
[JTC, Definition 2.1 and Corollary 2.3], with every feasible support
retained in its maximum.

**Lemma 2.2 (zero weights and finite norm structure).** The maximum
in (RCE.10) is unchanged if only subsets of $\operatorname{supp}(w)$
are used, with value zero when $w=0$. The function $J_I$ is monotone,
positively homogeneous, subadditive and convex on nonnegative weights.
It satisfies $J_I(w)\le\sum_xw(x)$ and, for probabilities,

$$
|J_I(\mu)-J_I(\nu)|\le\operatorname{TV}(\mu,\nu).
\tag{RCE.11}
$$

Proof. For $P^+=P\cap\operatorname{supp}(w)$, removing states can
only make additional experiments determine targets. Thus
$H_I(P^+)\subseteq H_I(P)$, and its joint partition coarsens the
old partition restricted to $P^+$. Merging nonnegative cells cannot
decrease their squared mass sum. Consequently
$\kappa_I(w,P)\le\kappa_I(w,P^+)$ when $P^+\ne\varnothing$;
if it is empty the original norm is zero. Feasibility is inherited
by subsets, proving the first assertion.

For fixed $P$, its cell masses are a fixed nonnegative linear map
$A_Pw$, and $\kappa_I(w,P)=\|A_Pw\|_2$. The norm triangle
inequality and monotonicity on nonnegative vectors give the asserted
properties after maximizing over the finite support list. Each norm
is at most its total cell mass, proving $J_I(w)\le\sum_xw(x)$.
Finally $\mu\le\nu+(\mu-\nu)_+$ gives
$J_I(\mu)\le J_I(\nu)+\sum_x(\mu(x)-\nu(x))_+$.
For probabilities that sum is their total variation. Exchange the
two probabilities. $\square$

**Proposition 2.3 (finite optimized joint envelope).** For $s=|S_d|$,
put $j_d=\min_{\mu\in\Delta(S_d)}J_d(\mu)$. The minimum exists,
$1/s\le j_d\le1$, and it is exactly the finite second-order cone
optimization

$$
\min t\quad\text{subject to}\quad
\mu\ge0,\quad\sum_x\mu(x)=1,\quad
\|A_P\mu\|_2\le t
\quad\text{for every nonempty range-feasible }P.
\tag{RCE.12}
$$

For the denominator-$M$ simplex grid
$G_M=\{(k_x/M)_x:k_x\in\mathbb N,\ \sum_xk_x=M\}$,

$$
j_d\le\min_{\mu\in G_M}J_d(\mu)
       \le j_d+\frac{s-1}{M}.
\tag{RCE.13}
$$

In particular $j_d$ is uniformly computable from the finite native
tables, with no native length cutoff asserted.

Proof. Continuity from Lemma 2.2 and compactness attain the minimum.
Singleton supports give $J_d(\mu)\ge\max_x\mu(x)\ge1/s$;
the opposite bound follows from its total mass. The matrices $A_P$
have entries zero or one, so (RCE.12) is precisely the epigraph
description of the same maximum.

Round down $s-1$ coordinates of a minimizer to multiples of $1/M$
and assign the remainder to its last coordinate. The resulting
probability has total variation at most $(s-1)/M$ from the minimizer.
Apply (RCE.11). At each grid point $J_d^2$ is a finite maximum of
explicit rational quadratic expressions; square roots admit computable
rational enclosures. Finite grids and (RCE.13) give two-sided
enclosures for $j_d$. This computes a certificate-language threshold,
not $b_d$ unless an additional equality is proved. $\square$

## 3. Conditional boundary entropy and exact tensorization

**Lemma 3.1 (entropy charge with a boundary vector).** Let
$X=(X_1,\ldots,X_m)$ have any one finite joint law. For each $i$,
let $U_i$ be a finite vector of functions of $X_i$, let $Y_i$ be
a subvector, and put $Y=(Y_1,\ldots,Y_m)$. Suppose every component
of $U_i$ outside $Y_i$ is also a function of some single $X_j$
with $j\ne i$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_iH(X_i\mid Y)-H(X\mid Y)
    &\ge\frac12\sum_iH(U_i\mid Y),\\
H(X)-\frac12H(Y)
    &\le\sum_i\left(H(X_i)-\frac12H(U_i)\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{RCE.14}
$$

Repeated donors, dependent components, empty subvectors and directed
cycles are allowed.

Proof. Fix a donor for each component outside $Y_i$ and an order of
the components in each $U_i$. For a permutation $\pi$ of the block
indices, let $T_i(\pi)$ be the components whose selected donor
precedes $i$. Those components form a vector that is a function both
of $X_i$ and of the preceding blocks. Conditional data processing gives

$$
I(X_i;X_{\operatorname{pre}_\pi(i)}\mid Y)
\ge H(U_{i,T_i(\pi)}\mid Y)
\ge\sum_{a\in T_i(\pi)}
 H(U_{i,a}\mid Y,U_{i,b}:b<a).
$$

The second inequality is the chain rule with conditioning monotonicity.
Each selected donor precedes $i$ in exactly half of the permutations.
Components belonging to $Y_i$ have conditional entropy zero given $Y$.
Average over permutations and sum over $i$. The left sum is
$\sum_iH(X_i\mid Y)-H(X\mid Y)$ for each permutation;
the right sum becomes $\frac12\sum_iH(U_i\mid Y)$.
This proves the first inequality on the same original law.

Set $D_i=I(X_i;Y)$ and $E_i=I(U_i;Y)$. Since $U_i$ is a function
of $X_i$, $E_i\le D_i$. Also $Y_i$ is a function of $X_i$ and
a component of $Y$, whence

$$
\sum_iD_i\ge\sum_iH(Y_i)\ge H(Y),\qquad
\sum_iD_i-\frac12\sum_iE_i\ge\frac12H(Y).
$$

Expanding the first inequality of (RCE.14) now gives

$$
H(X)\le\sum_i\left(H(X_i)-\frac12H(U_i)\right)
     +H(Y)-\sum_iD_i+\frac12\sum_iE_i,
$$

which proves the second. No independent replacement of a block or
conditional law was introduced. $\square$

**Lemma 3.2 (the exact finite Gibbs identity).** For $P$ with positive
weights and any finite function $U$ on $P$, let
$b_y=\sum_{x\in P:U(x)=y}w(x)$. Then

$$
\log_2\sqrt{\sum_yb_y^2}
=\max_{\mathcal L(X)\text{ on }P}
\left\{H(X)+\mathbb E\log_2w(X)-\frac12H(U(X))\right\}.
\tag{RCE.15}
$$

A maximizing law has full support $P$.

Proof. Conditional Gibbs in the $y$ cell bounds
$H(X\mid U=y)+\mathbb E[\log_2w(X)\mid U=y]$ by
$\log_2b_y$, with equality at conditional probabilities $w(x)/b_y$.
For the cell law $p$, the remaining maximum is
$\frac12H(p)+\sum_yp_y\log_2b_y$. Relative entropy against
$p_y=b_y^2/\sum_zb_z^2$ proves that its value is
$\frac12\log_2\sum_yb_y^2$. These choices are positive on every
state of $P$. $\square$

**Definition 3.3 (the product interface).** For finite interfaces
$I_1,\ldots,I_m$, their product interface has states
$\prod_iS_i$, individual targets $g_{i,q}(x_i)$ and individual
experiments $r_{j,a}(x_j)$, together with a constant. An experiment
returns exactly one constituent reply. It never returns a vector
of experiments. Feasible supports in this interface may be arbitrarily
correlated.

**Theorem 3.4 (support-complete exact tensorization).** For every
choice of finite interfaces as in Definition 2.1 and nonnegative
constituent weights,

$$
\boxed{
J_{I_1\times\cdots\times I_m}(w_1\otimes\cdots\otimes w_m)
=\prod_iJ_{I_i}(w_i).}
\tag{RCE.16}
$$

Proof. If one weight is zero both sides vanish. Otherwise, by Lemma
2.2, consider any feasible product-interface support $P$ on which
the product weight is positive. Write $P_i=\pi_iP$ and
$U_i=U_{P_i}(X_i)$. Let $Y_i$ consist of the targets at constituent
$i$ that are hard on $P$ in the product interface. An own experiment
working on $P_i$ works on $P$, so $Y_i$ is a subvector of $U_i$.
For every component of $U_i$ outside $Y_i$, a single product
experiment determines it on $P$. Its donor cannot be $i$, since
that would determine this hard target on $P_i$. Nor can it be a
constant, since the projection has the same target range. It is
therefore a function of some $X_j$, $j\ne i$.

Choose a maximizing full-support law in (RCE.15) for the joint
hard vector $Y=(Y_i)_i$ and weight $\prod_iw_i$. Every $P_i$ is
range-feasible. Lemma 3.1 and the one-constituent Gibbs inequalities
give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_2\kappa_{\mathrm{prod}}(\otimes_iw_i,P)
 &=H(X)+\sum_i\mathbb E\log_2w_i(X_i)-\frac12H(Y)\\
 &\le\sum_i\left[H(X_i)+\mathbb E\log_2w_i(X_i)
                                      -\frac12H(U_i)\right]\\
 &\le\sum_i\log_2\kappa_{I_i}(w_i,P_i)
 \le\sum_i\log_2J_{I_i}(w_i).
\end{aligned}
$$

This proves the upper inequality for every correlated support.

For the other inequality choose maximizing positive supports $P_i$
and take their Cartesian product. A hard constituent target is
nonconstant there, and an experiment in another constituent cannot
determine it: fix that constituent and vary the target constituent.
The hard vector of the product is consequently exactly the tuple of
the constituent hard vectors. Its cell masses multiply, so finite
distributivity gives
$\kappa_{\mathrm{prod}}(\otimes_iw_i,\prod_iP_i)=
\prod_i\kappa_{I_i}(w_i,P_i)$.
This is a feasible attaining support, proving equality. This product
is an analytical witness for the reverse inequality; no competing
support was required to factor and no actual source was copied.
$\square$

## 4. Exact acceptance by the unchanged recursive calculus

**Definition 4.1 (the fixed actual scaffold).** For $h\ge0$ and
$k=2^h$, let $T_h(x_1,\ldots,x_k)$ be the perfect ordered
height-$h$ scaffold filled by the listed actual trees, with $T_0(x)=x$.
For $w:S_d\to[0,\infty)$ define $w^{\uparrow}$ on $S_{d+h}$ by

$$
w^{\uparrow}(T_h(x_1,\ldots,x_k))=\prod_{i=1}^kw(x_i),
\qquad w^{\uparrow}=0\text{ off this image}.
\tag{RCE.17}
$$

The parsing map is injective. Every frontier occurrence is actually
present, and this lift changes no source or operational interface.

**Corollary 4.2 (exact joint-functional scaffold transport).** For all
$d\ge1,h\ge0$ and nonnegative $w$,

$$
J_{d+h}(w^{\uparrow})=J_d(w)^{2^h}.
\tag{RCE.18}
$$

Proof. Remove zero-weight states using Lemma 2.2. On the scaffold
image, each target strictly above the frontier is constant branch;
every other target is one constituent target in $W_d$. Every
boundary experiment is constant or one constituent experiment.
Conversely prefixing by that constituent's public frontier address
realizes each of its targets and experiments in $W_{d+h}$.
These maps identify feasibility and hard targets in both directions
with the product interface of Definition 3.3. Weights occur once,
as in (RCE.17). Apply Theorem 3.4. This statement concerns $J$;
the distinct exact $\beta$ scaffold identity is credited to
[JTC, Theorem 4.2]. $\square$

**Convention 4.3 (the precise grammar being characterized).**
$V_{\mathrm{rec}}$ means exactly [JTC, Definition 9.1]. Each label
is $(d,\mu,C)$ with $d\ge1$, a rational probability on the whole
$S_d$, and rational $C>0$. Its only justifications are:

1. An exhaustive joint leaf, an arbitrary finite grouping of that
   leaf with one constituent reply per experiment, or any finite
   bounded-bundle leaf, checking every feasible positive support.
2. A one-parent scaffold node, with $d=d_0+h$,
   $\mu=\nu^{\uparrow}$ and $C\ge C_0^{2^h}$.
3. A one-parent stability node at the same depth, with
   $C\ge C_0+\operatorname{TV}(\mu,\nu)$.

The grouped leaf tests squared norms against $C^{2m}$. A bundle
leaf checks a selected nonempty bundle of hard targets on each
support with $\mu(P)>C$; supports of mass at most $C$ require no
bundle. Malformed or incomplete tables are rejected. No finite
superposition rule or other inference rule is implicit here.

**Theorem 4.4 (pointwise acceptance characterization).** For every
rational native input $(d,\mu,C)$,

$$
\boxed{
\exists\pi\ V_{\mathrm{rec}}(d,\mu,C,\pi)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad C\ge J_d(\mu).}
\tag{RCE.19}
$$

Thus every accepted label has an exhaustive ungrouped joint leaf
with the same input and same cap.

Proof. Induct over the certificate nodes. An ungrouped joint leaf
gives exactly $C\ge J_d(\mu)$, including all zero-weight cases
by Lemma 2.2. For an $m$-grouped leaf Theorem 3.4 makes its
maximum equal to $J_d(\mu)^m$, hence its test forces
$C\ge J_d(\mu)$. For a bundle leaf, the full hard partition
refines the selected bundle partition. Its norm cannot exceed
the bundle norm. On a light support its norm is at most
$\mu(P)\le C$. Therefore this leaf gives the same inequality.

At a scaffold node apply the inductive premise and (RCE.18):
$C\ge C_0^{2^h}\ge J_{d_0}(\nu)^{2^h}=J_d(\mu)$.
At a stability node (RCE.11) gives
$J_d(\mu)\le J_d(\nu)+\operatorname{TV}(\mu,\nu)\le C$.
This accounts for every rule, every support and every grouping size.

Conversely, if rational $C\ge J_d(\mu)$, list all feasible
positive supports and their exact hard-target cell masses. There
are finitely many, and each squared comparison is rational. These
tables pass the ungrouped joint leaf at that same $C$.
$\square$

**Corollary 4.5 (compatibility of the optimized envelopes).** Let
$c_{\mathrm{rec}}(d)$ be the infimum of caps of accepted rational
labels at depth $d$, optimizing over their rational probabilities.
Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_{\mathrm{rec}}(d)
 &=j_d
  =\min_{1\le k\le d}j_k^{\,2^{d-k}},\\
L_d:=-2^{-d}\log_2j_d
 &=\max_{1\le k\le d}[-2^{-k}\log_2j_k].
\end{aligned}
\tag{RCE.20}
$$

In particular $L_d$ is nondecreasing and uniformly computable.
These are infimum and supremum statements; rational optimizing
labels need not attain them.

Proof. Theorem 4.4 and rational density, with continuity, give
$c_{\mathrm{rec}}(d)=j_d$. Lift a minimizing depth-$k$
probability to depth $d$ and use (RCE.18) to obtain
$j_d\le j_k^{2^{d-k}}$ for every $k\le d$. The list includes
$k=d$, so its minimum is exactly $j_d$. Taking negative logarithms
proves the endpoint formula and monotonicity. Computability follows
from Proposition 2.3 and $j_d\ge1/|S_d|$.

One can also obtain the displayed minimum without first using the
pointwise scaffold identity: grouped and bundle leaves reduce to
ungrouped leaves, every nonleaf node has one parent, stability
never reduces its cap, and the scaffold heights on a parent path
sum to $d-k$. Such a path has final cap at least
$j_k^{2^{d-k}}$. Conversely a near-minimizing rational depth-$k$
joint leaf followed by one scaffold attains that infimum in the
limit. The exact tensorization theorem strengthens this ancestry
description by showing that its minimum always equals the
depth-$d$ term. There is no conflict between the two formulas.
$\square$

## 5. A range-feasible Euclidean floor for every old rule

**Definition 5.1 (the range-feasible floor).** For nonnegative $w$ on
$S_d$, define

$$
F_d(w)=\max_{\substack{\varnothing\ne P\subseteq S_d\\
                                  P\text{ range-feasible}}}
                       \left(\sum_{x\in P}w(x)^2\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{RCE.21}
$$

Zero-weight states may be removed. This quantity uses point masses,
not hard-target cells.

**Theorem 5.2 (whole-calculus floor).** Every acceptance by the exact
$V_{\mathrm{rec}}$ grammar satisfies $C\ge F_d(\mu)$. In particular
arbitrary grouping, scaffold recursion and stability cannot cross
this floor.

Proof. Every partition into target cells has

$$
\sum_y\left(\sum_{x\in\text{cell }y}\mu(x)\right)^2
\ge\sum_{x\in P}\mu(x)^2,
\tag{RCE.22}
$$

so $F_d\le J_d$ and Theorem 4.4 already implies the assertion.
Here is also a direct rule-by-rule proof, independent of the
boundary entropy lemma.

The ordinary joint leaf obeys (RCE.22). For any feasible $P$,
$P^m$ is feasible in the grouped interface, and its squared
point-mass sum is $(\sum_{x\in P}\mu(x)^2)^m$. The grouped
test therefore forces $C\ge\|\mu|_P\|_2$ at every $m$.
A light bundle support obeys
$C\ge\mu(P)\ge\|\mu|_P\|_2$; a heavy one obeys (RCE.22)
for its selected bundle.

For a height-$h$ scaffold, let $k=2^h$ and pull any feasible
positive support back to a subset of $S_{d_0}^k$. Each projection
$P_i$ is feasible, since its every fine target is a coarse target.
Nonnegativity gives

$$
\sum_{x\in P}\mu(x)^2
\le\prod_{i=1}^k\sum_{u\in P_i}\nu(u)^2
\le F_{d_0}(\nu)^{2k}.
$$

Thus $F_d(\nu^{\uparrow})\le F_{d_0}(\nu)^k$, the direction
needed for the scaffold rule. Finally $F_d$ is a maximum of
Euclidean restriction norms, so it is monotone and subadditive,
and $F_d(v)\le\sum_xv(x)$ for $v\ge0$. The same positive-part
argument as Lemma 2.2 proves
$F_d(\mu)\le F_d(\nu)+\operatorname{TV}(\mu,\nu)$.
Induction through stability completes the direct proof. $\square$

## 6. Perfect four-hash supports on actual native trees

**Definition 6.1 (the credited four-state core).** Put

$$
A=\alpha,\quad B=\beta,\quad C=\langle A,A\rangle,
\quad J=\langle B,A\rangle,
\qquad (u_0,u_1,u_2,u_3)=(C,J,\langle C,C\rangle,\langle J,J\rangle).
\tag{RCE.23}
$$

These are the four core states of the existing NCM/SRP motif, all
in $S_3$. They are reused, not a new motif. Their relevant tables are

$$
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &u_0&u_1&u_2&u_3\\\hline
r_L&\alpha&\beta&\mathsf{branch}&\mathsf{branch}\\
r_{RL}&\mathsf{absent}&\mathsf{absent}&\alpha&\beta\\
\Gamma_{LL}&(3,0)&(2,0)&(2,1)&(1,0)
\end{array}
\tag{RCE.24}
$$

**Lemma 6.2 (three replies and triple identification).** Every finite
literal has at most three replies on this core. Every subset of at
most three core states is identified by one literal, hence every
target factor on that subset is determined by that same literal.

Proof. The root reply is constant branch. The $L$ and $RL$ rows
are in (RCE.24). The $R$ replies are
$(\alpha,\alpha,\mathsf{branch},\mathsf{branch})$; the $LL$
replies equal the $RL$ row; the $LR$ and $RR$ replies are
$(\mathsf{absent},\mathsf{absent},\alpha,\alpha)$.
All length-three or longer words are absent, since the core has
height at most two. This exhausts every finite word.

For a triple omitting $u_2$ or $u_3$, $L$ is injective; for one
omitting $u_0$ or $u_1$, $RL$ is injective. A smaller subset is
contained in a triple and inherits that experiment. Identification
determines every $\Gamma_q$, including an initially absent target.
$\square$

**Definition 6.3 (a four-hash native support).** Let $m\ge4$ and let
$H:[m]\times[k]\to\{0,1,2,3\}$ have $k=2^h$ columns and the
following property: every four distinct rows have a column displaying
all four symbols. This is the standard perfect four-hash, or
four-separated, property of [GR, §1]. Define

$$
z_s=T_h(u_{H(s,1)},\ldots,u_{H(s,k)})\in S_{h+3},
\qquad P_H=\{z_s:s\in[m]\},\qquad d=h+3.
\tag{RCE.25}
$$

Perfect hashing implies distinct rows, by extending any two rows
to four. Thus the $z_s$ are distinct. No hash-rate theorem or
probabilistic construction is assumed.

**Theorem 6.4 (exact top-three pressure at every length).** For every
support in Definition 6.3, every $n\ge1$, and every nonempty
$B\subseteq P_H^n$,

$$
B\text{ is natively admissible}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|\pi_iB|\le3\quad\text{for every }i.
\tag{RCE.26}
$$

The full $P_H$ is range-feasible, and every finite literal has at
most three replies there. If $w_i:S_d\to[0,\infty)$ vanish
outside $P_H$, let $L_3(w_i)$ be the sum of their three largest
weights on $P_H$, counting zero weights. Then

$$
\max_{B\subseteq S_d^n\text{ admissible}}
       \sum_{x\in B}\prod_iw_i(x_i)
       =\prod_iL_3(w_i).
\tag{RCE.27}
$$

In particular, for any nonnegative whole-block $w$ supported there,
$A_n^{(d)}(w)=L_3(w)^n$ and $\beta_d(w)=L_3(w)$.

Proof. Any finite literal either ends above the fixed frontier and
is constantly branch, or enters one actual module and returns one
of its core replies. Lemma 6.2 gives at most three possibilities.
Every target above the frontier is constant; any other target is
one of the seven core targets. It has at most four values, so
$P_H$ is range-feasible.

Suppose some projection contains four distinct states. A separating
column of $H$ makes the target at that module's $LL$ take the four
distinct factors of (RCE.24). But every possible donor literal in
every coordinate has at most three replies on $B$. No decoder of
one such reply, and no constant, can produce those four target
values. This contradicts admissibility, regardless of donor topology,
correlations or off-target choices.

Conversely suppose each coarse projection has at most three states.
At any module its core projection has at most three states. Lemma
6.2 supplies one $L$ or $RL$ experiment identifying that module's
possible core states. Prefix it by the module's public actual
address. It determines the requested factor; targets above the
frontier are constants. These are own-coordinate native witnesses
for every target and remain valid on $B$. This proves (RCE.26).

For (RCE.27), restrict any admissible relation to its positive
product-weight rows. If none remain its mass is zero; otherwise its
projections are subsets of $P_H$ of size at most three. Containment
in their Cartesian product and nonnegativity bound its mass by
$\prod_iL_3(w_i)$. Conversely choose a maximizing triple in each
coordinate and take their product. It is admissible by the converse
just proved and attains that mass. If some weight is zero, both
sides of (RCE.27) are zero. The original weights are never split
among modules; they may correlate all $k$ module symbols. $\square$

## 7. An explicit binary hash and a full-support separation

**Proposition 7.1 (sixteen rows and thirty-five separating columns).**
Let the rows be $s\in\mathbb F_2^4$. For every two-dimensional
subspace $U$ of $(\mathbb F_2^4)^*$ choose the lexicographically
first ordered basis $(\ell_1,\ell_2)$, using the ordinary binary
ordering of nonzero linear forms. Give that column the symbol

$$
H(s,U)=2\ell_1(s)+\ell_2(s)\in\{0,1,2,3\}.
\tag{RCE.28}
$$

There are $35$ such columns; padding them by $29$ constant-zero
columns gives a perfect four-hash with $16$ rows and $64$ columns.
Consequently (RCE.25) gives an explicit sixteen-state support
$P\subset S_9$ satisfying Theorem 6.4.

Proof. The number of two-dimensional subspaces is
$(15\cdot14)/(3\cdot2)=35$, since each has six ordered bases.
For four distinct rows translate one to zero and call the other
three differences $a,b,c$. Their span has dimension two or three.
In dimension two they are exactly its three nonzero vectors, so
an isomorphism to $\mathbb F_2^2$ sends them to its three nonzero
vectors. In dimension three they are a basis, so prescribing their
images as the three distinct nonzero vectors defines a linear map
onto $\mathbb F_2^2$. Extend either map to $\mathbb F_2^4$.
Its two component functionals are independent and span one of the
listed subspaces $U$. Replacing those components by the selected
basis of $U$ applies an invertible output map. The four outputs,
and hence their symbols, remain distinct. Padding does not remove
this column. This independently proves the explicit binary
construction; terminology from perfect hashing is not a substitute
for this argument. $\square$

**Theorem 7.2 (strict pointwise failure of the complete old calculus).**
Let $\mu_0$ be uniform on the sixteen actual states $P$ of
Proposition 7.1 and zero elsewhere in $S_9$. Then

$$
\beta_9(\mu_0)=\frac3{16},\qquad
F_9(\mu_0)=\frac14,
\qquad
V_{\mathrm{rec}}(9,\mu_0,C,\pi)\Longrightarrow C\ge\frac14.
\tag{RCE.29}
$$

Let $u$ be uniform on the entire $S_9$, put $\delta=1/128$ and
$\mu_\delta=(1-\delta)\mu_0+\delta u$. This is a strictly positive
rational probability on the full native alphabet. It satisfies

$$
\beta_9(\mu_\delta)\le\frac{25}{128}
 <\frac7{32}<\frac{127}{512}
 \le F_9(\mu_\delta).
\tag{RCE.30}
$$

Thus the valid strict rational cap $C=7/32$ on this full-support
probability is rejected by every finite old $V_{\mathrm{rec}}$
certificate, including all grouping, support and stability rules.

Proof. Theorem 6.4 gives $\beta_9(\mu_0)=3/16$, attained at
length one by any triple. The full $P$ is feasible, so its
Euclidean norm is $\sqrt{16}/16=1/4$. No restriction can have a
larger norm, giving the exact floor. Apply Theorem 5.2.

Now $\operatorname{TV}(\mu_\delta,\mu_0)\le\delta$, so
(RCE.9) gives $\beta_9(\mu_\delta)\le3/16+1/128=25/128$.
Every state of the full alphabet has mass at least $\delta/|S_9|$.
On the feasible support $P$, monotonicity gives
$F_9(\mu_\delta)\ge(1-\delta)F_9(\mu_0)=127/512$.
The two strict comparisons in (RCE.30) are rational inequalities.
Theorem 5.2 again excludes every old proof of this cap. $\square$

**Corollary 7.3 (unbounded pointwise multiplicative loss).** For every
$m\ge4$ there is some native depth $d$ and a uniform probability
$\mu$ on an $m$-state actual support such that

$$
\beta_d(\mu)=\frac3m,\qquad
V_{\mathrm{rec}}(d,\mu,C,\pi)\Longrightarrow C\ge\frac1{\sqrt m}.
\tag{RCE.31}
$$

The ratio of the old minimum cap to the true pressure is at least
$\sqrt m/3$, and is therefore unbounded over this family.

Proof. Use one column for each four-element subset of $[m]$,
assign its four rows distinct symbols and all other rows symbol
zero, and pad to a power-of-two number of columns. This is a
finite effective four-hash. Apply Theorem 6.4 and the feasible
full-support floor $\sqrt m/m$. The strict loss occurs for
$m>9$. No optimization over the entire $S_d$ is involved.
$\square$

## 8. A sound range-separation extension

**Definition 8.1 (range-separation leaf).** Retain every old rule of
$V_{\mathrm{rec}}$ and allow the following additional leaf. Its
finite data are $P\subseteq S_d$, an integer
$1\le r\le\min\{4,|P|\}$, and native tables satisfying:

1. $\mu$ vanishes off $P$, and every boundary experiment has at
   most $r$ replies on $P$, including the constant experiment.
2. Every $(r+1)$-element $Q\subseteq P$ has a supplied target $q$
   with $|g_q(Q)|>r$.
3. For every $r$-element $Q\subseteq P$ and every target there is
   a supplied constant or own-coordinate experiment and decoder
   valid on all of $Q$.
4. $C$ is at least $L_r(\mu)$, the sum of the $r$ largest weights
   on $P$.

The verifier checks all indicated subsets and equations, the full
native tables, rational probabilities and the rational cap comparison.
As in the old grammar it rejects malformed or incomplete data.
Write $V_{\mathrm{rec+range}}$ for the extended finite-node grammar.

**Theorem 8.2 (totality, exact leaf pressure and strict extension).**
The new verifier is total and sound for every complete native
template at every length. More generally, if $P,r$ satisfy the
three structural conditions above, then, for any nonnegative
$w_i$ supported on $P$,

$$
\max_{B\subseteq S_d^n\text{ admissible}}
       \sum_{x\in B}\prod_iw_i(x_i)=\prod_iL_r(w_i).
\tag{RCE.32}
$$

The extended grammar accepts $(9,\mu_0,3/16)$ and
$(9,\mu_\delta,7/32)$ from Theorem 7.2. Neither label is
accepted by the old grammar at those caps.

Proof. Each structural check is on finitely many states, subsets,
targets, experiments and decoder entries, so it terminates. Longer
native words are functions of boundary experiments or constants,
and therefore retain the reply-range bound.

Restrict any admissible relation to its positive product-weight rows.
Every remaining projection lies in $P$. If one contained $r+1$
states, condition 2 would give a target taking more than $r$
values, whereas its chosen donor has at most $r$ replies by
condition 1. A constant also cannot serve it. Thus every projection
has size at most $r$, and the nonnegative product mass is at most
$\prod_iL_r(w_i)$.

For the reverse inequality choose maximizing $r$-subsets and use
their Cartesian product. Condition 3 supplies own-coordinate
witnesses for all targets. If fewer than $r$ positive weights
occur, extend them by zero-weight states to an $r$-subset; the
same witnesses remain valid. An identically zero weight makes both
sides zero. This proves (RCE.32), including the degenerate case
$|P|=r$, when condition 2 is vacuous and the whole support is
locally admissible.

Consequently a new leaf certifies $\beta_d(\mu)\le L_r(\mu)\le C$.
The old joint, scaffold and stability nodes keep their original
soundness by [JTC, Theorem 9.2], its scaffold pressure identity and
(RCE.9). Induction proves soundness of the extension.

For $P$ of Proposition 7.1, take $r=3$. Theorem 6.4 supplies all
three structural conditions and the exact cap $3/16$. One
existing stability node accepts the perturbation at $7/32$, since
$3/16+\operatorname{TV}(\mu_0,\mu_\delta)\le25/128<7/32$.
Theorem 7.2 excludes both labels from the old grammar. Thus this
is a strict sound extension with actual native witnesses, not just
a different representation of an old proof. $\square$

This new leaf uses the reply range on the common support of all
positive rows. Range-feasibility alone uses the ambient four-reply
limit and does not imply its stronger projection bound. It is this
additional relation that crosses the old Euclidean floor.

## 10. Exact remaining cofinality obligations

**Proposition 10.1 (the old optimizer and rate gaps).** With $b_d,j_d$
as defined above, $b_d\le j_d$. The old $V_{\mathrm{rec}}$ is
cofinal at every fixed depth in the precise sense of [NPC,
Definition 8.1] if and only if $j_d=b_d$ for every $d$. Its best
depth-$d$ original-rate endpoint is $L_d$, and exactly

$$
\lambda-L_d
=\left(\lambda-2^{-d}\sigma_d\right)
 +2^{-d}\log_2(j_d/b_d).
\tag{RCE.37}
$$

Its endpoints over increasing depths approach the original
$\lambda$ if and only if

$$
2^{-d}\log_2(j_d/b_d)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{RCE.38}
$$

Proof. The joint theorem in JTC gives $\beta_d(\mu)\le J_d(\mu)$,
so minimization gives $b_d\le j_d$. Theorem 4.4, rational
density and Corollary 4.5 give supremum accepted block endpoint
$-\log_2j_d$. NPC's endpoint target is
$\sigma_d=-\log_2b_d$, yielding the first equivalence.
Subtract and add $2^{-d}\sigma_d$ to obtain (RCE.37).
Its first term lies in $[0,2^{-d}\phi]$ by (RCE.8), and its
second is nonnegative. This proves the limiting equivalence.
$\square$

**Open problem 10.2 (optimizer coverage and effective original convergence).**
Neither $j_d=b_d$ for all depths nor (RCE.38) is proved or
disproved here. The strict gaps of Theorem 7.2 concern specified
weights, whereas cofinality optimizes over all probabilities.
They refute the stronger premise that every rational strict
pointwise cap has an old proof; they do not refute optimizer
cofinality. The cofinality of $V_{\mathrm{rec+range}}$ likewise
remains open. A sound new leaf and exact finite conic optimization
do not supply a uniform effective length error or a proof that
optimized accepted endpoints reach the unchanged $\sigma_d$.

Sharp $\lambda$, general exact $M_1(D)$, terminating two-sided
effective approximation of the original rate, and the broader
minimal-relations/internal-observer mutual-recovery programme
remain unresolved. The operational recovery proved and reused is

$$
(E(t),D,p,\text{at most one actual reply})
\longrightarrow\Gamma_p(t)
\longleftrightarrow\bigl(r_{\rho^kt}(p)\bigr)_{k\ge0}.
\tag{RCE.39}
$$

The inverse recovers the temporal factor, not a whole source,
unknown age, hidden history, authentication or physical space and
time. There is no conclusion of native noncomputability or of
universal obstruction to different certificate languages, lawful
finer cuts or global recoloring.

## 12. Two attained replies imply locality at every native depth

**Convention 12.1 (native scope and ownership).** All objects in
Sections 12–16 are the actual $S_d,W_d,\Gamma,\operatorname{Pred}$
of Convention 1.2. Admissibility means Definition 1.3 on the same
tuples, with every donor coordinate, self donor, repeated donor, cycle
and correlation allowed. A weight is owned once by its entire original
block. The certificate grammar is exactly Convention 4.3 together with
Definition 8.1, retaining all four values $r=1,2,3,4$ and all conditions
on the entire declared support, including its zero-weight states.

The additional structural statements are the path-record argument in
Theorem 12.3 and the depth-two hard-partition comparison in Theorem
14.3. Their pressure and certificate consequences use the previously
specified transport, tensorization and soundness results. In particular
the depth-two native tables and maximal local sets are inherited from
[NCM, Convention 3.1][NCM], and $17/25$ and its attaining law are
inherited from [NCM, Theorems 3.3 and 4.1][NCM] and [NPC, Theorem
6.2 and Convention 7.3][NPC].

**Definition 12.2 (records along one target).** Fix $q\in W_d$, put
$\ell=|q|$, and write $q_{\le t}$ for its prefix of length $t$.
The record of $x\in S_d$ along $q$ is $(t,\lambda)$ if the first
leaf on that path occurs at $q_{\le t}$, with
$0\le t\le\ell$ and $\lambda\in\{\alpha,\beta\}$. Otherwise
the record is $\mathsf B_q$, meaning that $q$ itself is an original
branch endpoint. Assign $\mathsf B_q$ formal depth $\ell+1$ solely
for ordering records. For a leaf record the prefix replies are branch
before depth $t$, $\lambda$ at depth $t$, and absent afterwards.
For $\mathsf B_q$ every prefix through depth $\ell$ replies branch.
These are records of the stationary original tree. Formula (RCE.4)
makes $\Gamma_q$ a function of the record; that function need not
be injective.

**Theorem 12.3 (universal two-reply locality).** Let $d\ge1$ and let
$\varnothing\ne P\subseteq S_d$. Suppose

$$
|\{r_x(a):x\in P\}|\le2\qquad(a\in W_d).
\tag{RCE.40}
$$

For every $q\in W_d$, $\Gamma_q$ is constant on $P$, or there
are $a\in W_d$ and a total decoder
$\delta:\mathcal A\to\Gamma_q(S_d)$ such that

$$
\Gamma_q(x)=\delta(r_x(a))\qquad(x\in P).
\tag{RCE.41}
$$

Thus $P$ is locally admissible, with own-coordinate witnesses on its
entire declared set.

Proof. Fix $q$ and its records. Any three distinct records force
three replies at one allowed prefix. First suppose their formal depths
are distinct, $t_1<t_2<t_3$. The middle record is a leaf record,
so $t_2\le\ell$. At $q_{\le t_2}$, the first record replies
absent, the middle record replies its leaf label, and the last record
replies branch. This includes a terminal branch as the last record.

If two depths coincide, the corresponding distinct records must be
the alpha and beta leaves at that depth $t\le\ell$: there is only
one terminal-branch record. A third record at a greater depth gives
the three replies alpha, beta and branch at $q_{\le t}$; a third
record at a smaller depth gives absent, alpha and beta there. Three
distinct records cannot all have the same depth. These cases exhaust
three-record choices. Every prefix used has length at most
$\ell\le d-1$, contradicting (RCE.40). Therefore at most two
records occur on all of $P$.

One record makes $\Gamma_q$ constant. For two distinct records at
equal depth, the prefix at that depth separates their alpha and beta
replies. For different depths, use the smaller depth. It is a leaf
depth at most $\ell$, and its leaf reply differs from the branch
reply of the later record, including when that later record is
$\mathsf B_q$. Within each record class this chosen reply is fixed.
It therefore identifies the record class. Map each of its two attained
replies to that record's factor from (RCE.4), and map unused replies
to any fixed factor attained on the nonempty $P$. This gives the
total decoder in (RCE.41). If the two records have equal factors,
one may instead use a constant witness.

The argument includes a target initially absent because its first
leaf is strictly before $q$, a leaf exactly at $q$, and a branch
exactly at $q$. At $q=\varepsilon$, including the only target when
$d=1$, all distinguishing prefixes used are still permitted. No
presence assumption is imposed beyond the original prefix replies.
Make this choice separately for every target on the same $P$;
weights have not entered the argument, so declared zero-weight states
are included. $\square$

**Corollary 12.4 (the same self donors at every length).** Under
(RCE.40), $P^n$ is admissible for every $n\ge1$. For every
probability $\mu$ on $S_d$ with $\mu(P)=1$,

$$
A_n^{(d)}(\mu)=1\quad(n\ge1),\qquad \beta_d(\mu)=1.
\tag{RCE.42}
$$

Proof. For target $(i,q)$ on $P^n$, take the constant or the exact
word and decoder of Theorem 12.3 in coordinate $i$ itself. These
equations hold simultaneously on every tuple; no change of witnesses
or correlations is needed. The product probability of $P^n$ is one,
and no subset of $S_d^n$ has probability greater than one. This
proves both equalities. Same-witness saturation also gives the
complete-template maximum one. $\square$

## 13. Exact small range leaves and their analytical elimination

**Lemma 13.1 (injectivity of the whole boundary table).** For every
$d\ge1$, the map

$$
x\longmapsto(r_x(a))_{a\in W_d}
\tag{RCE.43}
$$

is injective on the actual $S_d$.

Proof. At $d=1$, the root replies distinguish $\alpha,\beta,C_0$.
Assume the claim at $d$. On $S_{d+1}$ the root reply distinguishes
the two leaves from each other and from the branch case. In the branch
case $x=\langle u,v\rangle$, the replies at $La$ and $Ra$ for
all $a\in W_d$ give the two whole child boundary tables. These words
belong to $W_{d+1}$. Induction identifies $u,v\in S_d$, and
ordered parsing identifies $x$. This also proves the claimed
injectivity without changing the canonical endpoint state $C_0$.
It is consistent with the boundary reconstruction in Convention 1.2.
$\square$

**Proposition 13.2 (the original $r=1$ and $r=2$ leaves).** Under all
the conditions of Definition 8.1, an $r=1$ leaf has exactly one
declared state and cap at least one. An $r=2$ leaf has exactly two
declared states and cap at least one. Conversely any singleton,
respectively any pair of distinct states of the same actual $S_d$,
with a rational probability vanishing off that set and rational
$C\ge1$, has lawful full data for the corresponding leaf.

Proof. For $r=1$, every boundary reply is constant on the whole
declared $P$. Lemma 13.1 gives $|P|\le1$; Definition 8.1 forces
$|P|\ge r=1$. Its supported probability has $L_1(\mu)=1$, so
$C\ge1$. Conversely on a singleton supply the full native tables
and, for each target, its attained constant value. The one-reply
bound holds, the separation requirement for two-element subsets is
vacuous, the singleton target witnesses are valid, and the cap
comparison is precisely $C\ge1$.

For $r=2$, the reply bound holds on every declared state of $P$.
Theorem 12.3 shows that every target has at most two values on all
of $P$. If $P$ contained three states, no target could take more
than two values on that triple, contradicting Definition 8.1's
separation condition. Thus $|P|\le2$; the original requirement
$r\le|P|$ forces $|P|=2$. The two largest declared weights have
sum $L_2(\mu)=1$, even when one is zero, giving $C\ge1$.

Conversely let $P=\{x,y\}$ with $x\ne y$. Every literal has at
most two replies on this set. Lemma 13.1 supplies an actual
$a\in W_d$ with $r_x(a)\ne r_y(a)$. For each target supply the
decoder taking these two replies to $\Gamma_q(x),\Gamma_q(y)$,
respectively, with unused replies mapped to $\Gamma_q(x)$; a
constant may be used when the factors coincide. These data witness
every target on the sole two-element subset. The three-element
separation requirement is vacuous, the supported law has $L_2=1$,
and the full native tables with $C\ge1$ meet every original
condition. A zero weight does not remove a declared state from any
structural test in either direction. $\square$

**Theorem 13.3 (unchanged accepted labels after omitting small leaves).**
Analytically omit only the $r=1$ and $r=2$ range leaves from the
grammar of Convention 12.1. The set of accepted rational triples
$(d,\nu,C)$ is exactly unchanged. Consequently the infimum of
accepted caps over rational laws at each depth is unchanged.

Proof. Every nonleaf rule has one parent. Starting from either small
range leaf, the initial cap is at least one by Proposition 13.2.
A height-$h$ homogeneous scaffold satisfies
$C\ge C_0^{2^h}\ge1$, including $h=0$; a stability node
satisfies $C\ge C_0+\operatorname{TV}(\mu,\nu)\ge1$.
Induction along any finite ancestry gives final cap at least one,
whatever its heights and intervening rational law changes.

Here is an exhaustive old ungrouped joint leaf at that identical
final label $(d,\nu,C)$. Its data contain the full native reply and
target tables. For every nonempty range-feasible
$Q\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\nu)$, include every hard target
of Definition 2.1 and its exact joint cells. A target is listed as
hard exactly when each of the finitely many boundary experiments,
including a constant, has a reply cell containing two different
target values. For each target outside that list, supply a determining
experiment and the decoder given by its common target value on each
attained reply cell, with any attained factor on unused replies.
List all states in each resulting joint cell $D$,
with rational mass $m_D=\sum_{x\in D}\nu(x)$. These prescriptions
specify all the finite tables and all the required supports, with
no selected-support omission. For every one of them,

$$
\sum_Dm_D^2
\le\left(\sum_Dm_D\right)^2
=\nu(Q)^2\le1\le C^2.
\tag{RCE.44}
$$

Thus this very leaf satisfies every squared comparison at the
original final cap. The same inequality holds directly on supports
with declared zero-weight states, and Lemma 2.2 also justifies the
old leaf's positive-support convention. The final rational law need
not resemble the initial range law.

One may replace each small range leaf in the finite certificate
directly by the just described old leaf at its own identical label,
since its cap is already at least one. All later labels, parent
references and inequalities remain valid. Alternatively, follow the
final unary ancestry: if its initial leaf is small, replace the whole
ancestry by (RCE.44) at the final label; if not, that ancestry uses
only retained rules. Unused list entries can be omitted. Either
description gives a certificate using neither small range leaf for
every previously accepted final label. The reverse containment holds
because every retained rule was already in the original grammar.
Taking the same infima over the same rational laws proves the last
assertion. This equivalence leaves the $r=3$ and $r=4$ rules, their
declared zero states and all their conditions intact. $\square$

## 14. The complete depth-two hard partition

**Convention 14.1 (the inherited depth-two data).** Write
$Q_1=S_1=\{\alpha,\beta,C_0\}$ and

$$
\mathcal G=\{g_{xy}=\langle x,y\rangle:x,y\in Q_1\},
\qquad S_2=\{\alpha,\beta\}\sqcup\mathcal G.
\tag{RCE.45}
$$

Use precisely the table and maximal local sets of
[NCM, Convention 3.1, (NCM.14)–(NCM.15)][NCM]. In the notation
here, put $f(\alpha)=(2,1)$, $f(\beta)=(1,0)$,
$f(C_0)=(0,0)$. The root target is determined by the root reply;
the side factors at a branch are
$\Gamma_L(g_{xy})=f(x)$ and $\Gamma_R(g_{xy})=f(y)$.
At the root leaves the two side-factor rows are

$$
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &\Gamma_L&\Gamma_R\\\hline
\alpha&(3,0)&(4,1)\\
\beta &(2,0)&(3,1)
\end{array}
\tag{RCE.46}
$$

For each side its two root-leaf values are distinct from each other
and from the three $f$ values. Both root leaves give absent on
either side experiment, while a branch gives the root reply of its
selected child. These are inherited table entries, not a new native
interface. The inherited maximal local sets are

$$
H_\alpha=\{\alpha\}\cup\mathcal G,\qquad
H_\beta=\{\beta\}\cup\mathcal G,\qquad
T_{xy}=\{\alpha,\beta,g_{xy}\}.
\tag{RCE.47}
$$

For arbitrary $w:S_2\to[0,\infty)$ set

$$
W=\sum_{x\in S_2}w(x),\quad a=w(\alpha),\quad b=w(\beta),
\quad t=\max_{g\in\mathcal G}w(g),\quad
M(w)=\max\{W-a,W-b,a+b+t\}.
\tag{RCE.48}
$$

Thus $M(w)$ is exactly the largest local mass, by (RCE.47).
Every set of at most three states is local: it either omits a root
leaf or contains both and at most one branch. Each $w$ remains one
whole-block weight, with arbitrary dependence between its two children.

**Proposition 14.2 (feasible supports and exact hard cells).** All
nonempty supports omitting a root leaf are locally admissible and
range-feasible. If a support contains both root leaves, write

$$
P=\{\alpha,\beta\}\cup T,\qquad T\subseteq\mathcal G,
\qquad I=\{x:g_{xy}\in T\},\quad J=\{y:g_{xy}\in T\}.
\tag{RCE.49}
$$

It is range-feasible exactly when $|I|,|J|\le2$, with empty $T$
allowed. It is locally inadmissible exactly when $|T|\ge2$.
On every nonlocal feasible support, $L$ is hard exactly when
$|I|=2$, and $R$ is hard exactly when $|J|=2$. The root target
is easy. The complete hard vector is injective on $P$, so

$$
4\le|P|\le6,\qquad
\kappa(w,P)^2=\sum_{x\in P}w(x)^2.
\tag{RCE.50}
$$

On every local support the hard vector is empty and
$\kappa(w,P)=w(P)$. All classifications refer to the whole declared
support, including states of weight zero.

Proof. For a support missing one root leaf, query the same side as
each side target. The absent reply identifies its sole possible root
leaf, and the other replies identify the selected child in $Q_1$.
The root target uses the root reply. This verifies the inherited local
witnesses, and target ranges are at most four. For a support with
both leaves and at most one branch, the root reply identifies all
states, again giving local admissibility and feasibility.

For (RCE.49), the side target ranges have sizes $2+|I|$ and
$2+|J|$, because of the disjoint values in Convention 14.1. The
root range has size at most three. This proves precisely the stated
feasibility condition. If $|T|\ge2$, two branches differ in some
child coordinate. That side target is not determined by the root
reply, which merges the two branches; neither side reply determines
it, since both side experiments merge the two root leaves with
different target values. A constant also fails. These are every
boundary experiment at depth two. Every longer experiment is a
function of one of them or a constant by Convention 1.2, so cannot
repair that failure. This proves nonlocality and exhausts the local
classification (RCE.47).

On a nonlocal feasible support $T\ne\varnothing$. If $|I|=1$,
the root reply determines $\Gamma_L$: its branch class has one
$f$ value, and its two leaf classes are singletons. If $|I|=2$,
the root reply merges branches with different $f$ values, and
every other experiment merges the two different root-leaf values.
Thus $L$ is hard exactly in this case. The proof for $R$ is the
same with $J$ and its explicitly given factor row. At least one
side is hard because $T$ has two distinct ordered pairs.

Any hard side distinguishes the two root leaves from each other
and from every branch. Any two distinct branches differ in some
child coordinate; that coordinate varies on $T$, so its side is
hard and separates those branches, since $f$ is injective. Hence
the full hard vector is injective on all of $P$. Its cells are
singletons irrespective of the weights. Finally
$2\le|T|\le|I||J|\le4$, giving the size bounds in (RCE.50).
On a local support each target has an own experiment, so there
are no hard targets and there is a single cell. $\square$

**Theorem 14.3 (exact joint-functional comparison for every whole-block
weight).** For every nonnegative $w$ on the entire actual $S_2$,

$$
J_2(w)=M(w).
\tag{RCE.51}
$$

Proof. Consider every nonempty feasible $P$ in Definition 2.1,
without deleting any zero-weight states. If $P$ is local, Proposition
14.2 gives $\kappa(w,P)=w(P)\le M(w)$. Otherwise it has between
four and six states and singleton hard cells. Order its weights as
$u_1\ge\cdots\ge u_m\ge0$, where $4\le m\le6$, and pad
with zeros through $u_6$. Then

$$
\sum_{j=4}^6u_j^2\le3u_3^2
\le2(u_1u_2+u_1u_3+u_2u_3),
\qquad
\sum_{j=1}^6u_j^2\le(u_1+u_2+u_3)^2.
\tag{RCE.52}
$$

Indeed all three tail entries are at most $u_3$, and each product
in the parenthesis is at least $u_3^2$. The three actual states
with largest weights form a local set by Convention 14.1.
Consequently $u_1+u_2+u_3\le M(w)$, and (RCE.50)–(RCE.52)
give $\kappa(w,P)\le M(w)$. This includes every feasible support.

Conversely choose one of the finitely many nonempty local sets
in (RCE.47) attaining $M(w)$. It is feasible and has an empty
hard vector, with norm exactly $M(w)$. Thus the maximum defining
$J_2$ equals $M(w)$. The argument also applies to $w=0$, when
every compared quantity is zero. $\square$

## 15. Direct pressure and certificate consequences at depth two

**Corollary 15.1 (all laws, all lengths and mixed whole-block weights).**
For arbitrary nonnegative $w_1,\ldots,w_n$ on the entire $S_2$,

$$
\max_{B\subseteq S_2^n\ \mathrm{admissible}}
\sum_{x\in B}\prod_{i=1}^nw_i(x_i)
=\prod_{i=1}^nM(w_i)\qquad(n\ge1).
\tag{RCE.53}
$$

The maximum over complete templates has the same value. In particular,

$$
A_n^{(2)}(w)=M(w)^n\qquad(n\ge1),\qquad
\beta_2(w)=M(w).
\tag{RCE.54}
$$

The homogeneous assertion is a direct application of
[NCM, Theorem 2.2][NCM]; the mixed assertion is a direct consequence
of Theorem 3.4 and (RCE.51).

Proof. To make the NCM application explicit, first let $w>0$.
In NCM Theorem 2.2 take $m=4$, $r=3$ and
$C_0=c_0=M(w)>0$. The inherited maximal local sets bound every
local projection by $M(w)$. For every nonlocal feasible $P$,
Proposition 14.2 gives two root leaves and child projections of
size at most two. Its root experiment has at most three replies;
each side experiment has absent and at most two child replies;
longer words add no replies beyond a boundary function. Some side
varies between two children, and its target has exactly four values.
Every cell of that target is either a singleton root leaf or a
set of branches with one child fixed. Each such cell is local,
so has $w$-mass at most $M(w)$. These verify every local and
cell-mass premise of NCM Theorem 2.2. Its same-tuple section
argument bounds every admissible mass by $M(w)^n$, and products
of a maximizing local set attain it.

For nonzero $w$ with zero entries, restrict a competitor to its
positive-product-weight rows, retaining its equations. On the finite
interface restricted to $\operatorname{supp}(w)$ the weights are
positive. Its local sets and feasible sets are exactly the subsets
with the same properties under the original experiments; all the
just verified bounds persist. Its maximum local mass is $M(w)$:
intersect a maximizing original local set with the positive support,
which preserves its mass and witnesses. Thus the same NCM application
applies to the restricted rows. An empty restriction has mass zero,
and $w=0$ is immediate. This establishes (RCE.54) as the claimed
direct sufficient-transport application.

For the mixed statement take the product interface of Definition
3.3 on $n$ copies of the full depth-two interface. Its targets are
exactly $\Gamma_q(x_i)$, and an experiment returns exactly one
$r_{x_j}(a)$ or a constant. Arbitrary finite donor words reduce
within that same donor block by Convention 1.2. Therefore each
admissibility equation for any $B$ is precisely a single-experiment
determination of that target in this product interface. Every target
is easy on $B$ and has at most four values there, so $B$ is a
feasible support with empty hard vector. Its cell norm is exactly
its original product-weight mass. Theorem 3.4 and Theorem 14.3 give

$$
\sum_{x\in B}\prod_iw_i(x_i)
=\kappa_{\mathrm{prod}}(\otimes_iw_i,B)
\le J_{\mathrm{prod}}(\otimes_iw_i)
=\prod_iJ_2(w_i)=\prod_iM(w_i).
\tag{RCE.55}
$$

This is an identity of the stated finite interfaces; the product
interface is not being substituted for a different native alphabet.
Neither $B$ nor any original whole-block weight was required to
factor between its children. Every arbitrary donor, reused donor,
cycle and correlation of $B$ is retained.

Choose a nonempty maximizing local set for each $w_i$. Their
Cartesian product has own-coordinate witnesses and attains the
right side of (RCE.53), including when a weight is identically
zero. Same-witness saturation of Definition 1.3 places each such
admissible set in its complete simultaneous solution set. That
solution set is itself admissible when nonempty and is bounded by
(RCE.55); nonnegativity gives equality of the two maxima. Taking
identical weights and all-length roots also gives (RCE.54).
$\square$

**Corollary 15.2 (exact threshold for the whole original grammar).**
For every rational probability $\nu$ on $S_2$ and rational $C>0$,

$$
\exists\pi\ V_{\mathrm{rec+range}}(2,\nu,C,\pi)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad C\ge M(\nu).
\tag{RCE.56}
$$

Proof. If $C\ge M(\nu)=J_2(\nu)$, use the exhaustive ungrouped
old leaf of Theorem 4.4 at this exact label. More explicitly, its
tables are the native tables of Convention 14.1, and its supports
are every nonempty feasible subset of $\operatorname{supp}(\nu)$.
Proposition 14.2 identifies all their hard cells: a local support
has the one cell of mass $\nu(P)$, while a nonlocal feasible
support has singleton cells with masses $\nu(x)$. Theorem 14.3
proves every squared sum at most $M(\nu)^2\le C^2$. All listed
entries and squared comparisons are rational. The classification
also covers supports with zero-weight states, consistently with
Lemma 2.2. Hence the specified old leaf supplies the same-cap
attainment without an additional inference rule.

Conversely Theorem 8.2 is sound for the entire grammar of
Convention 12.1, including all $r=1,2,3,4$ leaves and arbitrary
finite homogeneous scaffold and stability ancestry from arbitrary
rational laws. Acceptance therefore gives
$C\ge\beta_2(\nu)=M(\nu)$ by Corollary 15.1. This proves both
directions for every rational input. $\square$

**Corollary 15.3 (certificate equality at the inherited optimum).**
Let $c_+(d)$ be the infimum of rational caps of accepted rational
laws for the grammar of Convention 12.1, and let
$b_d=\min_{\mu\in\Delta(S_d)}\beta_d(\mu)$ as in Convention
1.4. Then

$$
c_+(2)=b_2=\frac{17}{25}.
\tag{RCE.57}
$$

The pressure-minimizing real law is unique and is the inherited law

$$
\nu_*(\alpha)=\nu_*(\beta)=\frac8{25},\qquad
\nu_*(g)=\frac1{25}\quad(g\in\mathcal G).
\tag{RCE.58}
$$

Here $b_2$, its numerical value and the attaining law are the NCM/NPC
results credited in Convention 12.1. Equality for $c_+(2)$ is a
certificate consequence of (RCE.51), and uniqueness is the routine
equality analysis below.

Proof. For completeness the local inequalities identify all equality
cases over the full real simplex. Write $z=M(\mu)$,
$p=a+b$, and let the nine branch weights have total $1-p$ and
largest value $t$. Then

$$
a,b\ge1-z,\qquad p\ge2(1-z),\qquad
t\ge\frac{1-p}{9},\qquad
z\ge p+t\ge\frac{8p+1}{9}\ge\frac{17-16z}{9}.
\tag{RCE.59}
$$

Thus $z\ge17/25$, agreeing with the inherited optimum. The law
(RCE.58) has all three expressions in (RCE.48) equal to $17/25$.
It is a rational law on the whole $S_2$, and Corollary 15.2 gives
its old joint leaf at the rational cap $17/25$. Hence
$c_+(2)\le17/25$. Full-grammar soundness gives
$c_+(2)\ge b_2=17/25$, proving (RCE.57).

At equality $z=17/25$, (RCE.59) gives $a,b\ge8/25$, whereas
$z\ge(8p+1)/9$ gives $p\le16/25$. Thus
$a=b=8/25$. The branch total is $9/25$ and
$t\le z-p=1/25$. Nine nonnegative numbers at most $1/25$ with
sum $9/25$ are all $1/25$. This proves uniqueness among all real
laws, with zero coordinates allowed at the start of the argument.
$\square$

## 16. Attribution and the unchanged optimized frontier

**Convention 16.1 (ownership and applicability).** The path-record
theorem and the hard-partition/$J_2$ comparison are ordinary
repository-derived deductions from the native definitions and stated
suppliers. The classification of the $r=2$ range leaves and the
accepted-label invariance are consequences of the path theorem and
the original grammar. Boundary-table injectivity is a direct native
induction consistent with inherited canonicalization; the $r=1$
classification follows from it. The finite ordered-weight inequality
in (RCE.52) is an elementary norm comparison.

The depth-two table, maximal local sets, local weighted maximum and
two-side feasibility constraints are inherited or direct normalizations
of [NCM, Convention 3.1 and Lemma 3.2][NCM]. The homogeneous all-law
pressure statement instantiates [NCM, Theorem 2.2][NCM]; the mixed
statement uses Theorem 3.4 on the exact product interface. The
complete-template and acceptance conclusions use Definition 1.3 and
Theorems 4.4 and 8.2. The optimum and its attaining law belong to
[NCM, Theorems 3.3 and 4.1][NCM] and [NPC, Theorem 6.2 and
Convention 7.3][NPC]; uniqueness is equality analysis of those local
inequalities. These corollaries do not each constitute an additional
transport mechanism.

[*Native joint potential limits*, Theorems 3.1 and 3.3 and Section
4][JPL12] concern its particular core-scaffold supports and their
whole-block weights; its diagonal/separable obstruction in Sections
5–7 is already bypassed by the existing joint leaf. [*Native
asymmetrically tagged products and certificate escape*, Theorems
3.2 and 4.1 and Sections 5 and 7][NTP12] owns the tagged-product
pressure and specified-law full-grammar gaps. Those statements retain
their original support and language hypotheses. Their restricted
ceilings and specified-law gaps do not bound every optimized law
on a full native alphabet.
