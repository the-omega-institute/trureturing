## 5. One horizontal request and its exact marked failure law

**Proposition 5.1 (complete specialization of the canonical instrument).**
Fix $d,L>0$, $v=d/L$, and $0<x<c-d$. For the request
$k(t)=x+vt$, constant relative phase, the reduced live operators on
$(+,-,2)$, with an arbitrary untouched reference, are

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_x(t)&=\frac{x}{x+vt},\qquad
f_x(t)=\frac{vx}{(x+vt)^2},\qquad s_x=\frac{x}{x+d},\\
K_{x,t}&=\operatorname{diag}\left(1,
 \sqrt{\frac{x(c-x-vt)}{(x+vt)(c-x)}},\sqrt{\frac{x}{x+vt}}\right),\\
J_{x,2}(t)&=\sqrt{f_x(t)}|f\rangle\langle2|,\\
J_{x,1}(t)&=\sqrt{\frac{c f_x(t)}{2(c-x)}}|f\rangle\langle-|,
\qquad
J_{x,3}(t)=\sqrt{\frac{c f_x(t)}{2(c-x)}}|f\rangle\langle-|,\\
J_{x,0}(t)&=J_{x,4}(t)=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{FH.13}
$$

The two equal Kraus fields retain different original labels. On (FH.3)
the terminal live output is $s_x\rho_{x+d,\beta,m}$, and the five
scalar failure densities are

$$
(0,c,Z,c,0)f_x(t),\qquad 0<t\leq L,
\tag{FH.14}
$$

each with its exact time, original mode and absorbing $f$. Consequently
the unconditional probability of failure during this request by $u/v$ is

$$
G_x(u)=\frac{u}{x+u},\qquad 0<u\leq d.
\tag{FH.15}
$$

Proof. The positive ramp has $\lambda=v/(x+vt)$, so integrating gives
$S_x$. Insert this and $p(k)$ into (FH.5). The constant plus attenuation
has zero loss. The middle squared attenuation has negative derivative
$f_x$; the minus squared attenuation has negative derivative
$c f_x/(c-x)$. Its two source modes contribute half each after compression
to $V$. This gives all of (FH.13), as in [IB][IB] Proposition 17.2.
Their integrated effects, together with $K_{x,L}^\dagger K_{x,L}$,
sum to $I_V$ entry by entry. Thus these are complete CP operations on
arbitrary reference inputs, not just assigned scalar probabilities.

Multiplying the three source amplitudes by $K_{x,L}$ gives
$\sqrt{s_x}\psi_{x+d,\beta,m}$. The middle input population is $Z$;
the minus population is $2(c-x)$, so each labelled minus failure has
trace $cf_x$. Since $Z+2c=1$, total failure density is
$f_x=-S_x'$, with mass $1-s_x$. Its integral through $u/v$ equals
$1-x/(x+u)$, proving (FH.15). All five mode fields and absorbing
successors have been specified even though the scalar lower test forgets
their labels. $\square$

The CDF in (FH.15) is a prescribed law of the operation. A single actual
failure is one outcome, not a delivered CDF, whole density or height sensor.

## 6. Exact Cauchy independence and the full-nuisance dimension

**Lemma 6.1 (independent horizontal failure CDFs).** For every $M\geq1$
and distinct positive $x_1,\ldots,x_M$, the real functions
$G_{x_j}(u)=u/(x_j+u)$ are linearly independent on every nonempty open
interval of positive $u$. For distinct positive $u_1,\ldots,u_M$ the
matrix

$$
C_{ij}=\frac{u_i}{x_j+u_i}
\tag{FH.16}
$$

is invertible. If both lists are increasing, its determinant is

$$
\det C=
\frac{(\prod_i u_i)
 \prod_{i<j}(u_j-u_i)(x_j-x_i)}{\prod_{i,j}(x_j+u_i)}>0.
\tag{FH.17}
$$

Proof. A relation $\sum_j a_jG_{x_j}(u)=0$ can be divided by $u>0$
and multiplied by $P(u)=\prod_j(x_j+u)$. The resulting polynomial

$$
Q(u)=\sum_j a_j\prod_{k\ne j}(x_k+u)
$$

has degree at most $M-1$. Vanishing on an interval makes it identically
zero. Evaluating at $-x_j$ gives
$a_j\prod_{k\ne j}(x_k-x_j)=0$, hence every $a_j=0$.
For a vector in the kernel of $C$, the same $Q$ has the $M$ distinct
roots $u_i$, so the same argument proves invertibility. Formula (FH.17)
is the classical Cauchy determinant identity, with the row factors $u_i$
extracted. It can also be checked by bringing the determinant over the
common denominator: its numerator alternates separately in the two lists,
has their Vandermonde degrees, and its multiplicative constant is one by
successive residues at $u_i=-x_j$. Only the elementary root argument is
needed for invertibility below. $\square$

**Theorem 6.2 (exact horizontal dimension for arbitrary joint inputs).**
Fix one horizontal request, an integer $M\geq1$, and $M$ distinct heights
in $(0,c-d)$.
Under §4, exact correctness on one selected actual nuisance history at
each height requires

$$
R\geq M.
\tag{FH.18}
$$

Thus no fixed finite handoff realizes this one complete request at every
height of a nondegenerate interval. If correctness is required for every
phase and signed winding at those heights, the stronger necessary bound is

$$
R\geq M+5.
\tag{FH.19}
$$

Eight actual nuisance histories per height suffice for (FH.19):
$\beta\in\{0,\pi/2,\pi,3\pi/2\}$ and $m\in\{0,1\}$.
Indeed their ideal complete reduced outputs have real linear span of
dimension exactly $M+5$, which is also the span dimension for the full
nuisance family at these heights. Neither bound assumes any independence
of the incoming archives from phase or winding.

Proof. The output projection retaining just the requested operation's
live and new-failure sectors induces one real linear map
$\Psi:\mathcal B_{\rm sa}\to\mathcal O_{\rm sa}$ on the live input.
Absorbing acquisition inputs cannot produce these new executed sectors.
Exactness says $\Psi(L_h)=\Omega_h$ there; it also forces zero
unmatched acquisition/abort mass. In particular, any real relation among
$M$ live inputs gives the same relation among their ideal failure CDFs.
Lemma 6.1 forces all coefficients to vanish. The $M$ inputs are therefore
independent in a real space of dimension $R$, proving (FH.18).

For the stronger assertion write $z=e^{-i\beta}\in\{1,-i,-1,i\}$,
$\eta=(-1)^m\in\{1,-1\}$ and, at height $x_j$, set

$$
A_j=\sqrt{2(x_j+d)},\quad B_j=\sqrt{2(c-x_j-d)},\quad C_0=\sqrt Z.
$$

All three are nonzero. The live vector is
$C_0|2\rangle+z(A_j|+\rangle+\eta B_j|-\rangle)$, with live
weight $s_j>0$. Its failure blocks do not depend on $z,\eta$.
Let $T_{j,z,\eta}$ denote the complete stopped ideal output after forgetting
other archive/reference fields for this necessary test, and let $\mu_j$
be its average over these eight nuisances. Its live block is
$s_j(A_j^2P_++B_j^2P_-+ZP_2)$ and its failure CDF is $G_{x_j}$.
The $\mu_j$ are independent by Lemma 6.1.

Define the following five Hermitian matrices, supported only in the
terminal live block:

$$
\begin{aligned}
X_{+-}&=|+\rangle\langle-|+|-\rangle\langle+|,\\
X_{+2}&=|+\rangle\langle2|+|2\rangle\langle+|,
&Y_{+2}&=i(|+\rangle\langle2|-|2\rangle\langle+|),\\
X_{-2}&=|-\rangle\langle2|+|2\rangle\langle-|,
&Y_{-2}&=i(|-\rangle\langle2|-|2\rangle\langle-|).
\end{aligned}
\tag{FH.20}
$$

They are independent by inspecting their disjoint offdiagonal entries and
real/imaginary parts. At any one fixed height they belong to the output
span. To see this explicitly, put
$A_z=(T_{j,z,+}+T_{j,z,-})/2$ and
$B_z=(T_{j,z,+}-T_{j,z,-})/2$. Expanding the projector gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tfrac14\sum_z B_z&=s_jA_jB_jX_{+-},\\
A_1-A_{-1}&=2s_jA_jC_0X_{+2},\qquad
A_i-A_{-i}=2s_jA_jC_0Y_{+2},\\
B_1-B_{-1}&=2s_jB_jC_0X_{-2},\qquad
B_i-B_{-i}=2s_jB_jC_0Y_{-2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{FH.21}
$$

Each expression has zero failure block. No additional states are prepared
by forming these real linear combinations; this is span analysis of
actual-source outputs. A combination $\sum_j a_j\mu_j$ with zero
failure CDF has every $a_j=0$. Thus its span has zero intersection with
the five-dimensional space (FH.20), proving a lower span dimension $M+5$.

## 7. A finite event-test certificate of complete-output error

**Theorem 7.1 (explicit Cauchy error floor).** Fix $M>R$, distinct tested
heights $x_j\in(0,c-d)$, one actual nuisance history at each, and distinct
$u_i\in(0,d]$. Let $C$ be (FH.16) and define

$$
K(C)=\|C^{-1}\|_{\ell_\infty^M\to\ell_1^M}
 =\max_{s\in\{-1,1\}^M}
   \sum_j\left|\sum_i(C^{-1})_{ji}s_i\right|.
\tag{FH.22}
$$

For every §4 handoff and common processor for this one request,

$$
\max_j e_{h_j}(q)\geq\frac1{K(C)}>0.
\tag{FH.23}
$$

Thus every fixed finite algebra has a positive uniform-error floor on
every nondegenerate height interval. This is a finite-test lower bound,
not the sharp complete-instrument minimax value or a sampling theorem.

Proof. Since $M>R$, there is a nonzero real relation
$\sum_j a_jL_{h_j}=0$. Normalize it so $\sum_j|a_j|=1$.
Let $p_{ij}$ be the actual unconditional probability of a newly executed
failure of this request by time $u_i/v$, summed over original modes.
Absorbing acquisition stops cannot execute it. Its effect on the live
algebra is one common linear functional, so $\sum_j a_jp_{ij}=0$.
With $\varepsilon=\max_j e_{h_j}(q)$, the event inequality in
Definition 4.3 gives $|p_{ij}-C_{ij}|\leq\varepsilon$. Therefore

$$
\|Ca\|_\infty\leq\varepsilon,\qquad
1=\|a\|_1\leq K(C)\|Ca\|_\infty\leq K(C)\varepsilon.
$$

Lemma 6.1 makes $K(C)$ finite and positive. For the vertex formula,
$\|C^{-1}s\|_1$ is convex in each coordinate of $s\in[-1,1]^M$.
Successively replacing each coordinate by an endpoint never decreases
some maximizer's value, so the maximum is attained at a cube vertex.
This proves (FH.22) and (FH.23). The argument uses normalized complete
outputs, while keeping the live input blocks subnormalized. No accepted
subensemble or failed acquisition is divided away. $\square$

**Example 7.2 (exact finite matrices).** At the rational source of

## 8. Unrestricted finite phase requests and a uniform approximation floor

**Proposition 8.1 (the complete phase tail).** A phase request of finite
action $\alpha\in\mathbb R$ and duration $L>0$ has no new failure in
any of the five modes. Its live unitary on $V$ is

$$
U_x(\alpha)=\operatorname{diag}_{(+,-,2)}
 (e^{-i\alpha/2+ix\alpha/r},e^{ix\alpha/r},1).
\tag{FH.26}
$$

Its normalized ideal output is
$\rho_{x,\beta,m}^{\alpha}=U_x(\alpha)\rho_{x,\beta,m}
U_x(\alpha)^\dagger$ in the live sector, with zero new failure,
abort and rejection mass.

Proof. Height is constant, so $\lambda=0$ and every $g_i=0$ in
(FH.5). The integrated phase is $\alpha w(x)$. Equal weights within
each paired direction and the original negative phase convention give
(FH.26). This is also the response (RA.17) of [RA][RA]; the statement
does not supply its separate blank-probe port. $\square$

**Theorem 8.2 (uniform floor for an arbitrary finite joint handoff).**
Fix $0<Z<1$ and any nondegenerate interval $I\subset(0,c)$.
For any one fixed finite algebra and arbitrary handoffs of §4, acquired
before the later phase request is supplied, every family of common CPTP
processors satisfies

$$
\sup_{x\in I,\ \beta\in\mathbb R,\ m\in\mathbb Z,\
               \alpha\in\mathbb R} e_{x,\beta,m}(\alpha)
 \geq\sqrt{Zr}.
\tag{FH.27}
$$

It already suffices to test $\beta=m=0$. All requests in the supremum
have individually finite action and may have the same duration $L=1$.
There is no common action or phase-speed bound. At $Z=1/2$ this is
$1/2$, also a lower bound for the full original future menu under this
finite handoff access condition. No full-menu minimax value is identified.

Proof. Choose distinct $x_n\in I$ converging to an interior $x_*$.
The normalized states $\sigma_{x_n,0,0}$ in (FH.11) lie in the compact
state space of the fixed finite algebra $\widehat{\mathcal B}$.
This is the finite-dimensional Heine–Borel theorem, independent of any
regularity of the encoding. Some subsequence converges. Pair consecutive
members of that subsequence, with heights $x_j,y_j$. Then both heights
tend to $x_*$ and

$$
t_j=\tfrac12\|\sigma_{x_j,0,0}-\sigma_{y_j,0,0}\|_1\longrightarrow0.
$$

For each pair choose the one common public request
$\alpha_j=\pi r/(y_j-x_j)$. It is finite and legal on both histories.
This choice is a witness to worst-case failure: it supplies neither height
label to the processor. Let
$v_x=\sqrt{2x}|+\rangle+\sqrt{2(c-x)}|-\rangle$. A common output
unitary $\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\alpha_j/2},1,1)$ removes the known
plus-only phase. The two target vectors then have inner product

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z+e^{i(y_j-x_j)\alpha_j/r}\langle v_{x_j},v_{y_j}\rangle
 &=Z-\langle v_{x_j},v_{y_j}\rangle,\\
\langle v_x,v_y\rangle
 &=2\sqrt{xy}+2\sqrt{(c-x)(c-y)}\longrightarrow2c=r.
\end{aligned}
\tag{FH.28}
$$

The trace distance of two pure states with unit vectors $u,v$ is
$\sqrt{1-|\langle u,v\rangle|^2}$: diagonalizing their difference
on their two-dimensional span gives eigenvalues of those opposite
magnitudes. Thus the target distance tends to
$\sqrt{1-(Z-r)^2}=2\sqrt{Zr}$.
For the same $\Phi_{\alpha_j}$, contraction and the triangle inequality
give

$$
\operatorname{dist}(\rho_{x_j,0,0}^{\alpha_j},
                         \rho_{y_j,0,0}^{\alpha_j})
 \leq e_{x_j,0,0}(\alpha_j)+t_j+e_{y_j,0,0}(\alpha_j).
\tag{FH.29}
$$

Take the limit and bound each error by the displayed supremum. This proves
(FH.27). Acquisition losses were included in the normalized $\sigma$;
they were not conditioned away. Stopped archives can be forgotten only
because their successor cannot assist a live continuation. Keeping them
in the full error can only strengthen this lower test. $\square$

The conclusion is a supremum: for every $\varepsilon<\sqrt{Zr}$
some actual histories and one finite request force error above
$\varepsilon$. It does not assert that one fixed pair attains the limit.
With a common phase-speed bound $v_\chi$, action $|\alpha|$ needs
duration at least $|\alpha|/v_\chi$. The same unrestricted-action
argument then uses arbitrarily large finite horizons. It gives no such
constant for a fixed bounded-action, bounded-horizon menu.

## 9. The attained relaxed phase-only value and its exact limitation

**Theorem 9.1 (sharpness in the common-CPTP phase comparison class).**
Fix the source and nondegenerate height interval of Theorem 8.2. For its
phase-only output task, if retention of the actual live qutrit is allowed,
then

$$
\inf_{\substack{\text{fixed finite handoffs}\\
                 \text{common CPTP phase processors}}}
 \ \sup_{x,\beta,m,\alpha} e_{x,\beta,m}(\alpha)=\sqrt{Zr}.
\tag{FH.30}
$$

Here the error is for the complete stopped phase-only source-family output;
arbitrary unread conditional spectator archives/references may be
retained. The infimum is attained by an explicit comparison channel. The
claim does not assert that this channel is executable from the original
canonical menu, or that it implements the full future menu.

Proof. The lower bound is Theorem 8.2. For the upper take no additional
acquisition and retain $\rho_{x,\beta,m}$ unchanged in $M_3$. Put

$$
P_B=P_++P_-,\qquad
D_\alpha=\operatorname{diag}(e^{-i\alpha/2},1,1),\qquad
\mathcal E_\alpha(X)=D_\alpha(P_BXP_B+P_2XP_2)D_\alpha^\dagger.
\tag{FH.31}
$$

Its two Kraus operators are $D_\alpha P_B,D_\alpha P_2$, whose
effects sum to $I_V$, so it is CPTP and has no failure output.
Since $U_x(\alpha)=D_\alpha e^{ix\alpha P_B/r}$, (FH.31) is

target vector as $\sqrt Z|2\rangle+\sqrt r|b\rangle$ with a
normalized bottom vector $|b\rangle$, including its actual phases.
The difference between the target and its pinching is

$$
\sqrt{Zr}(|2\rangle\langle b|+|b\rangle\langle2|).
$$

On the orthonormal pair $|2\rangle,|b\rangle$ this has eigenvalues
$\pm\sqrt{Zr}$, and it vanishes on the remaining direction. Its half
trace norm is exactly $\sqrt{Zr}$ for every $x,\beta,m,\alpha$.
One may use the identity channel at $\alpha=0$ to give exact waits
without changing the supremum. Matching zero-duration identities can
likewise remain exact.

At a fixed actual history the ideal specimen is pure. Any joint extension
of that pure marginal factors as $\rho_h\otimes\tau_h$: positivity

specimen ray. Tensor (FH.31) with identity on $\tau_h$. The same error
holds, and arbitrary actual classical mixtures of histories satisfy the

## 10. A finite-action packing lower bound

**Theorem 10.1 (action range versus retained matrix-space dimension).**
Set $Z=r=1/2$, $c=1/4$, $I_0=[1/8,3/16]$, and

$$
\tau_0=\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{4-\sqrt6-\sqrt2}{8}\right)^2}.
\tag{FH.32}
$$

Fix $0\leq A<\infty$. Suppose one §4 handoff and its later processors give complete-output
error at most $\varepsilon<\tau_0/2$ for every $x\in I_0$,
$\beta=m=0$, and every phase request $|\alpha|\leq A$.
For every integer $M\geq2$ with $A\geq8\pi(M-1)$, necessarily

$$
M\leq\left(1+\frac{2}{\tau_0-2\varepsilon}\right)^R.
\tag{FH.33}
$$

If there are at most $2^b$ classical blocks, each quantum block having
dimension at most $d_Q$, this implies

$$
2^b d_Q^2\geq
\frac{\log M}{\log(1+2/(\tau_0-2\varepsilon))}.
\tag{FH.34}
$$

This is a necessary packing estimate, not a sharp finite-action memory law.

Proof. Choose $M$ equally spaced heights in $I_0$. Their minimum gap is
$1/(16(M-1))$. For each distinct pair $x,y$ the common antiphase action
$\alpha_{xy}=\pi r/(y-x)$ obeys
$|\alpha_{xy}|\leq8\pi(M-1)\leq A$. Set
$\theta(x)=\arcsin\sqrt{x/c}\in[\pi/4,\pi/3]$. Direct substitution
into $v_x$ from §8 gives

$$
\langle v_x,v_y\rangle=r\cos(\theta(x)-\theta(y)).
$$

The target inner product at this antiphase action is
$\tfrac12(1-\cos(\theta(x)-\theta(y)))$, lying between zero and
$(1-\cos(\pi/12))/2=(4-\sqrt6-\sqrt2)/8$. Every target pair has
trace distance at least $\tau_0$. Applying (FH.29) to the pair-specific
common request shows that the handoff packets $\sigma_x$ are pairwise
separated by $\delta=\tau_0-2\varepsilon>0$ in half trace norm.

The real affine space of trace-one Hermitian elements of
$\widehat{\mathcal B}=\mathcal B\oplus\mathbb C$ has dimension
$(R+1)-1=R$. With the norm $\tfrac12\|\cdot\|_1$ on its
translation space, all normalized states lie in the radius-one ball about
any fixed state. The open radius-$\delta/2$ balls centered at these
$M$ packets are disjoint, and lie inside the concentric ball of radius
$1+\delta/2$. Translation-invariant $R$-dimensional volume scales by
the $R$th power of radius, even though these balls include nonpositive
Hermitian elements. Comparing volumes gives
$M(\delta/2)^R\leq(1+\delta/2)^R$, proving (FH.33).
Finally $R=\sum_\alpha n_\alpha^2\leq2^b d_Q^2$, giving (FH.34).
$\square$

The phase action is $|\alpha|$, the variation of one original phase leg.
At duration one it is also the phase speed. It is not a physical energy
or the spectral action of the extra probe port (RA.18), whose full
implementation and access contract differ. Increasing duration to respect
a speed bound is paid separately. Neither (FH.33) nor the exact symbolic
actions certify noisy timing, gain, reference or gate precision.
