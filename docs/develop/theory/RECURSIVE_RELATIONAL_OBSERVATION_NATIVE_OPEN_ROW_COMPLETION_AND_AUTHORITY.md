## 1. The original row and the scope of completion

**Definition 1.1 (fixed apparatus).** Use [ARC] Definitions 1.1–1.4,
4.1–4.3 and [UAS] Definitions 1.1–1.4. There is one finite original
preparation $\xi$, one actual initial tree $u$, one initial $H_0$, the
same private source on port 16 and the same evolving address relation
$\phi_K$. The inequalities $\lambda(t)\le h\le K$ concern completed
source boundaries. Intermediate source bytes need not encode a tree.
Reserve extends this source's backing; Raise changes its current guard.
No future capacity bound is part of the initial apparatus.

The original actor table/state/seed/waiting packet occupy 24–27;
acquired material occupies 28–29; the original verifier occupies 32–39;
the original consumer occupies 40–47. Routes 48–51, producer ingress 13,
source descriptors 17 and all original tokens keep their meanings.
ARC's ten additional owned ports are 64–73, not borrowed verifier
workspace. In particular, the twelve original workspace loans are twelve
actual TOK rows followed by their prescribed CTL, even when the ultimate
principal is unchanged.

An original row means one actual RD, WR, ML, MR, EX, BF, IN, OUT, TOK
or CTL, including its declared finite boot variant, finite control
effects and one meter receipt. It does not mean a whole source request,
bank assignment, channel transfer or history request. [ARC] Definition
6.2 and [UAS] Definition 2.3 specify its literal ordered effects and
identity-based alias combination. Internal whole-row patches in [UAS]
are a stronger admitted basis, not a construction of exposed fine steps.

**Definition 1.2 (original partial actions and tasks).** Write
$N(W,a)$ for the actual partial original transition from physical
record $W$. A primitive action $a$ is Supply$(b)$ at its prescribed IN,
or Advance at its prescribed enabled non-IN row. A failed physical
guard gives no executed row, no input consumption and no receipt. Both
bits at a physically enabled raw IN execute their real successors.
Canonical extendibility restricts the original task's Supply domain;
it does not install an input filter. These distinctions are [AIF]
Definitions 2.2–2.6 and [ARC] Definitions 5.3 and 6.1.

Let $Q$ retain exactly [ARC] Definition 11.1's original continuation and
audit experiments, complete original joint successors and original
terminal output. Its empty response adds no history or full-state
terminal readout. The additional history-service task $Q_G$ and a task
observing fine stages are different maps. None is substituted for $Q$.

For a finite original history $h$, its length $|h|$ is its number of
original receipts. Write $I(h)$ and $O(h)$ for its chronological input
and released-output words. An input letter retains its actual site
kind

$$
\tau=(\text{active version},\text{PC},\text{inlined call kind},
       \text{declared ingress}),
$$

and its actual bit. A letter's occurrence is additionally bound to its
position in $h$. The position is a proof index, not an installed
timestamp. Two equal strings at different calls, two INs using latch 5,
and a computed command with the same bytes are not the same input
occurrence. Computed Append/copy/RD work has no IN letter.

**Definition 1.3 (the local split protocol being tested).** At a stable
Ready image of an original record, admission checks the actual original
guard, binds the actual row and bounded old operands, and on IN consumes
its actual site bit. Native data and the original meter are unchanged
by this admission. A retained Busy phase then selects a finite list of
data writes, constructors and head handoffs. Only the final Finish
completes the original row and returns to Ready, or to Halt on the
original final halt. In the unfused output protocol a distinct OUT
reaction precedes Finish. No second original row starts during Busy.

This definition isolates rules used in the obstructions below. It
assumes neither a generated all-row compiler nor an installed matcher.
A predecessor reconstruction $W_-(x)$ at Busy is a mathematical
interpretation of actual partial material and before-images. It is not
the actual current source, a restore instruction, or an actor right.

## 2. Actual input and output cuts defeat completed-row erasure

**Proposition 2.1 (an original payload IN supplies the obstruction).**
Fix the one initialization of [ARC] Definition 9.1:
$H_0=h=K=1$, $u=\beta$, prior $\{\alpha,\beta\}$, and the truthful
original INITIAL-Stop client. After its canonical activation, choose 01
at an outer $R_r$, entering the separately numbered original caller
$F^A$. Supply TagRead header 010 and length $U(1)=10$. Let $h$ end
after the first positive payload-length RD and before its payload
IN(5). Then both Supply$(0)$ and Supply$(1)$ are in the original task
domain. For the split protocol of Definition 1.3, no causal
original-prefix interpretation of the post-admission cut can preserve
both the actual consumed input word and the number of completed
original rows.

Proof. [CAP] Definition 4.6 allocates and zeroes the payload before the
length-controlled five-row reader. [ARC] Definition 4.1 changes only
the final return CTL of that original caller. Its payload sequence is

$$
\operatorname{RD}(\text{length}),\quad
\operatorname{IN}(5),\quad\operatorname{WR}(13,5),\quad
\operatorname{MR}(13),\quad\operatorname{MR}(\text{length}).
$$

Thus the current wire prefix is exactly 01010. The two complete frames
010100 and 010101 are canonical one-letter addresses. Their later
TagRead answers on beta may both be absent; this does not identify
their primitive input successors. The reached producer/source admission,
actual length and payload positions and current permissions are those
of this ordinary canonical caller. No additional entry is assumed.

Let $x_0$ be its claimed stable image, $n=|h|$, and let $x_1$ follow
just admission with the actual bit 0. The actual input word has changed
from $I(h)$ to $I(h)(\tau,0)$, but completed-row count and original
meter material remain $n$. Suppose a map $p$ starts with $p(x_0)=h$,
maps later cuts to extensions of $h$, and preserves completed rows.
Then $h\preceq p(x_1)$ and $|p(x_1)|=n=|h|$, so $p(x_1)=h$.
Input preservation would give $I(p(x_1))=I(h)(\tau,0)$, contradicting
this equality. Mapping to the post-IN original cut instead increases
the original receipt count to $n+1$; postponing the input letter to
Finish changes its actual occurrence. The contradiction uses no
receipt per fine event. ∎

**Proposition 2.2 (the opposite Supply also excludes silent draining).**
At $x_1$ of Proposition 2.1, interpreting Busy as its original
predecessor neither transports the original Supply menu directly nor
repairs that menu by silently completing the outstanding row.

Proof. The original predecessor permits both Supply values. Busy
permits no second input. Its actual internal continuation is not
original Advance either: Advance is disabled at the prescribed IN.
Now test original Supply$(1)$. Its demanded complete successor has
latch 5 equal to 1 and $n+1$ original receipts. Completing Busy instead
finishes the already accepted IN(0), giving latch 5 equal to 0 and
$n+1$ receipts. Every later completed original row has at least
$n+2$ receipts, so cannot yield that demanded complete successor.
Receiving 1 at a later IN would have another site occurrence. Erasing
the accepted 0 or overwriting it with a second input also violates
the required input correspondence. ∎

**Proposition 2.3 (a canonical disabled-Advance test for a separate
scanner).** Consider representations with the same additional fixed
Ready controller at every original stable record. Suppose the first
fine reaction for an attempted Advance inspects only that controller,
the fixed rules and the action name, and no original native fields.
There is no strict action-by-action refinement that both matches every
enabled original Advance by a positive finite fragment and has no fine
successor when that original Advance is disabled.

Proof. Use two consecutive original records on Proposition 2.1's
canonical path: $W_0$ immediately before the positive length RD and
$W_1$ immediately after it. Advance is enabled at $W_0$ and disabled
at $W_1$, where the prescribed action is IN. The extra Ready component
and supplied action name Advance are identical. Positive matching at
$W_0$ requires a first fine reaction. Its controller-only test is also
true at $W_1$, contradicting the strict disabledness requirement there.
This is already a valid original-source pair; no malformed-code
reachability claim is needed. ∎

The strict requirement in Proposition 2.3 is, for each fixed attempted
action, the `none` branch of pinned mathlib's `StateTransition.Respects`.
An atomic guard-aware admission escapes its controller-only hypothesis.
A paid preliminary check with explicit refusal cuts can instead use a
different weak or completed-call interface. It cannot simultaneously be
called an original executed Advance at $W_1$. A readiness certificate
is another possible resource, requiring its own truthful initialization
and updates; Ready alone is not that certificate.

**Proposition 2.4 (released OUT is also ahead of completion).** In the
unfused protocol, a mandatory real original OUT gives a Busy cut whose
actual released-output word is not that of its completed original
prefix. This occurs both in ARC's publication consumer and on the
original INITIAL stopping route.

Proof. [ARC] Definition 7.1 specifies the actual receiver print body
RD(length,5), RD(73,4), OUT(4), MR(73), MR(length), with its terminal
length test. For example its CoverRef serialization is nonempty and is
actually printed, not merely represented by Step74. Fix one such OUT
after a completed prefix $h$, and let its permitted bit be $b$.
Immediately after the fine OUT but before Finish, completed-row
erasure is still $h$ while actual output is $O(h)b$. The words differ
in length. A successor interpretation would instead complete row
$|h|+1$. The same proof applies to an actual OUT on [ARC] Theorem
9.2's original target-copy/OUT/ACK/return route, before its real final
halt CTL. It adds no new historical-service consumer. ∎

## 3. Self-invalidating dispatch: code, permission and final stop

**Definition 3.1 (raw bootstrap conditions).** For either fixed fresh
version $v=L,G$ in [ARC] Definition 4.2, let $e_v=|E(v)|$ be its
actual finite installation-slot count. Separate the prescribed literal
word $E(v)$, actual supplied installation bits $z$, active fixed
bootstrap and inactive partially written main store. [RCA] Definition
14.1 gives NEXT, INPUT, WRITE, ADVANCE and DONE; [CSA] Definition 3.3
distinguishes a latched boot bit from a prescribed literal constructor
value. [ARC] Definitions 5.3 and 6.2 and [UAS] Definition 2.3 extend
the carrier to raw supply and actual post-DONE decoding.

Define $\mathsf{Load}_v(z)$, for $z\in\{0,1\}^{e_v}$, to mean the
following concrete conditions all hold from that actual bootstrap
entrance: each NEXT selects its actual fixed next slot; each prescribed
INPUT is enabled and consumes the corresponding actual bit; each WRITE
passes its actual owner/constructor/fresh-slot and operand guard and
installs that bit at that slot; each ADVANCE and the final NEXT/DONE
are enabled. The bootstrap mechanism's own active control and cursor
must remain separate from those supplied raw bits until DONE. Any
literal-value guard of the selected boot variant is included, rather
than replaced by an unconditional latch-copy rule.

Write $W_v^+(z)$ for the resulting actual original post-DONE record
when these conditions hold. Define $\mathsf{Blocked}_v(z)$ by

$$
\text{not genuinely halted at }W_v^+(z),\qquad
N(W_v^+(z),a)\text{ undefined for every prescribed raw main action }a.
$$

Sufficient reasons include no unique well-typed current row, an invalid
actual entry field, or a failed actual entry guard. An unprovided input
bit at an otherwise enabled IN is not this structural blockedness.

**Proposition 3.2 (what the loader proves, and the bad-code witness
condition).** For every $z$ satisfying $\mathsf{Load}_v(z)$, its full
bootstrap uses exactly $4e_v+2$ original rows and that many original
receipts, preserving the raw installed bits and using the fixed
bootstrap until DONE. A bad-code DONE witness requires the additional
condition $\mathsf{Blocked}_v(z)$. Malformed unary syntax by itself is
not a proof of either condition.

Proof. At each non-end slot the actual sequence is NEXT, INPUT, WRITE,
ADVANCE. There are $e_v$ such slots, followed by the final NEXT and
DONE. The defining guards ensure all are actual rows. Each row has
one original receipt. Only WRITE installs the selected fresh bit;
only DONE changes the complete active version. Induction on these
slots proves the preservation and the count, even for a noncanonical
$z$ satisfying the guards.

PR57.03 requires seven terminated unary fields per row. All ones in
a decoder's complete row-field region cannot contain such a complete
row. But applying this observation to a bootstrap additionally
requires identifying that region in actual $E(v)$, showing it contains
all decoder-consumed row fields with no supplied or inherited delimiter
elsewhere, checking its actual initial PC/effect fields, and proving
every changed bit survives its real WRITE guards through DONE. Changing
all of $E(v)$ can also change initial finite-control or token fields;
it is not the same argument as changing only a row list. A first
mismatched literal WRITE, if disabled, leaves an executed wrong IN
followed by a blocked WRITE, rather than a completed bad DONE.
Conversely, successful loading alone allows an actually enabled
noncanonical row. It does not imply blockedness. ∎

For the declared raw latched-write schema, absence of an additional
value test and disjoint bootstrap fields can establish
$\mathsf{Load}_v(z)$ for every length-$e_v$ word by the same induction.
That is an explicit schema condition, not a consequence of the phrase
“wrong supply.” The fixed sources supply canonical $E(L),E(G)$
completion and the raw/active distinction. They do not display a fully
expanded literal $E(L)$ or $E(G)$ and its decoded all-ones post-DONE
entry here. Accordingly no particular all-ones word is asserted here
to satisfy $\mathsf{Load}_v\land\mathsf{Blocked}_v$. A claimed concrete
bad-DONE example must discharge exactly that finite predicate. This
restriction leaves Propositions 2.1–2.4 and 3.4–3.5 unaffected.

**Theorem 3.3 (the guard-separated code self-lock).** Suppose
$\mathsf{Load}_v(z)\land\mathsf{Blocked}_v(z)$. Suppose a splitting of
that actual admitted DONE writes the new active version/entry before
its final Finish, and at some following Busy cut the current raw main
selector is disabled. If every remaining fine reaction first requires
that current selector to be enabled, DONE has no completing positive
finite fragment from that cut.

Proof. DONE was admitted using the fixed bootstrap at its completed
cursor, so the new main selector was not its entry condition. At the
specified Busy cut that selector is false. It is now a necessary
condition of every next fine reaction, in particular Finish. There
is no next reaction, although the admitted row has not finished.
Thus the required stable successor cannot be reached. The correct
original result, when $\mathsf{Blocked}_v(z)$ holds, completes DONE
and its receipt and then has no enabled main row. It is neither an
unexecuted DONE nor global halt. A core continuation selected before
raw decoding, or a final atomic publication of all gate-invalidating
fields, escapes the stated hypothesis. Saving the old code value
without a currently executable continuation does not. ∎

**Proposition 3.4 (the actual split-TOK stall).** Use [ARC] Definition
7.3's Recover handoff after its actual CoverRef print/check/ACK:
TOK(handler slot, client slot), followed by CTL ClientCover. A
replacement that changes at most one of these two native bits per
data reaction and requires the original current TOK guard before
every such reaction cannot complete this enabled row.

Proof. The actual distinct availability slots start at $(s,d)=(1,0)$;
the actual guard is $s=1\land d=0$, and the required result is
$(0,1)$, by [ARC] Definition 6.2. There is a first reaction changing
one of them. Changing the source first gives $(0,0)$; changing the
destination first gives $(1,1)$. Either falsifies the guard, while
the other required change remains outstanding. No further guarded
data reaction can make it. Intervening scans, receipt births or
unchanged assignments do not make that same guard true. This is a
current permission failure on an ordinary mandatory callback, not
a failure caused by absent historical information. ∎

**Proposition 3.5 (self-stopping the actual last row).** Suppose the
original final halt CTL on [ARC] Theorem 9.2's original return route
is split so that its raw halted condition becomes true before Finish.
If every fine continuation requires that raw condition to be false,
the final row cannot finish. Allowing its already admitted bounded
continuation until Finish, and allowing no reaction after Finish to
Halt, avoids this particular obstruction without posthalt service.

Proof. At the first specified Busy cut, the outer nonhalt guard is
false while the unique completion remains outstanding. This excludes
the next step just as in Theorem 3.3. With the alternative phase rule,
the already bound next stage, rather than that changed raw predicate,
selects the remaining finite suffix. Its final transition completes
the original receipt and selects Halt, which has no rules. Thus no
scanner, input, historical entry or cleanup is enabled afterwards. ∎

**Corollary 3.6 (placement of continuing authority).** For the protocols
above, rechecking the original admission predicate is not in general
a continuing permission. A sufficient local dispatch order is: first
interpret retained core phase; at Halt take no step; at Busy execute
only the admitted row's next authorized stage; only at Ready consult
current original code and current admission guards.

Proof. In Busy this order does not depend on the predicates made false
in Theorem 3.3 and Propositions 3.4–3.5. Induction on a fixed finite
stage list therefore removes those three causes of self-lock. It does
not establish the list's data/head/constructor guards or install that
dispatch order; those are separate hypotheses in §5. ∎

## 4. Exact events and the one outstanding receipt

**Definition 4.1 (open original-row relation).** A serial fine prefix
$w$ has completed original word $h_c(w)$ and at most one outstanding
row. A stable interpretation is $\operatorname{At}(W(h_c))$. A Busy
interpretation is

$$
\operatorname{Working}(W_-,r,\sigma,k,p,\mathcal R_{\rm stage}),
$$

where $r$ is the admitted actual raw row with its source/version/PC/
site and operand binding, $\sigma$ is its bounded retained before-image,
$k$ is its actual retained stage, $p$ is its already occurred wire
event or the empty word, and $\mathcal R_{\rm stage}$ is this row's
actual partial receipt material. The actual partial graph is always
retained. $W_-=W(h_c)$ is its comparison predecessor, not a second
stored source. $p$ is determined by actual stage and captured bit;
it need not be a new permanent event record.

The row footprint contains the original ordered guard/operand and
effect stencil, all named current/predecessor markers and moved-to
neighbors, actual finite code/control/token fields used, both meter
lanes and the old append tail, their specified bounded routes, and
only this row's designated new occurrences. Repeated probes remain
repeated. Aliased writes are combined after literal ordered effect
evaluation. A core-owned continuing permission is bound to precisely
this row, footprint, captured operands and stage sequence. It is not
an exported read token, an arbitrary next-row permission or an actor
lease inferred from old permission bytes.

**Definition 4.2 (wire and receipt rules).** The open-row relation has
the following requirements.

1. Only Ready and the actual original guard admit a new row. IN
   admission consumes exactly one bit at its bound actual site. Other
   admissions consume none. Disabled admission has no wire event or
   original receipt. Every physically enabled raw input value retains
   its actual successor. Canonical-prefix admissibility restricts only
   the original task comparisons, not this raw admission relation.
2. Internal stages consume no input. The row has at most its one
   prescribed OUT, at its bound authorized site and bit. No next
   original row starts before this row finishes.
3. Every overwrite has its required old value captured beforehand.
   All unmentioned material persists. All current and partial
   constructors, marks, links, controls and rights remain actual
   physical material, including partially shaped receipts.
4. There is one identified original receipt append per admitted row,
   with the prescribed ordinary/F kind, one new meter R and distinct
   read/append lanes. No constructor prefix or receipt is repeated.
   Even if its final geometry is present while Busy, it is staged.
5. Finish is enabled only after the complete original successor,
   both head lanes and the one receipt have their prescribed final
   values. It completes that row once, retires its continuing
   permission, and selects the actual successor's Ready, or Halt
   exactly for the original final halt. Halt has no outgoing action.

**Theorem 4.3 (all-cut wire and receipt balance for this interface).**
For every finite initialized fine prefix satisfying Definitions
4.1–4.2, let $\operatorname{Wire}(w)$ be the chronological actual
typed IN/OUT word. Then

$$
\operatorname{Wire}(w)
 =\operatorname{Wire}(h_c(w))\,p(w),\qquad |p(w)|\le1.
\tag{OR.1}
$$

The completed original receipts correspond bijectively, in their actual
append-chain order and ordinary/F kinds, to the rows of $h_c(w)$.
Thus their number is $|h_c(w)|$. Any outstanding row contributes one
identified staged append or its partial construction, and contributes
zero completed receipts. No event-word equation uses a postponed actual
input or a duplicated output.

Proof. Induct on the actual fine reactions. Initially both original
word and wire word are empty and there is no outstanding receipt.
A non-IN admission creates no wire letter; an IN admission appends
exactly its site-bound letter and records it as $p$. No second
admission is allowed, so it cannot be overwritten by another input.
An original row has at most one wire action; hence it cannot also
release an OUT while retaining an accepted IN.

An internal write, scan, birth or handoff changes neither wire word
nor completed original word. Its actual receipt-prefix growth stays
attached to the uniquely admitted append, not a new completed row.
If an unfused OUT occurs, it appends exactly the row's one letter
and makes that letter $p$. All subsequent internal reactions retain
it. At a nonfused Finish, the actual admitted row moves to $h_c$:
its wire projection is exactly that previously accepted/released
letter, or empty. Removing $p$ therefore leaves (OR.1) unchanged.
At a fused OUT/Finish, the actual output and $h_c$ gain that same
letter together. These are all wire cases.

For receipts, admission binds the actual old append position and
its unique successor template. Internal stages never repeat a
constructor or publish the next row. Finish occurs only after the
specified append and lane handoff are complete, so it extends the
completed receipt correspondence by exactly this one row/receipt
pair. Its successor phase disables a second Finish for that same
row. The induction preserves append-chain order and the computed
ordinary/F kind, including a core meter move on an aliased cell.
It also proves that the receipt-shaped but Busy suffix cannot be
counted a second time. ∎

Taking input/output subwords, or restricting input letters to any
actual site kind $\tau$, gives the corresponding exact word identities.
For early IN and fused OUT, these specialize to

$$
I(w)=I(h_c(w))\,A(w),\quad |A(w)|\le1,
\qquad O(w)=O(h_c(w)),
\tag{OR.2}
$$

where $A$ is nonempty exactly when the outstanding row has already
accepted its original IN. These equations concern the open-row wire
and completion interface at every fine cut. They do not identify
that interface with original $Q$.

**Proposition 4.4 (the two pending-input intervals are different).**
On the original port-13 payload path of Proposition 2.1, fine IN
acceptance before original IN completion and original IN completion
before its matching original WR are distinct intervals. The correct
input extraction counts the accepted bit exactly once through both
and through a partially executed WR.

Proof. Write $I_0$ for the input word before the bit $b$.

| Fine cut | Completed original input | Outstanding accepted input | Original input frontier used for comparison |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before IN admission | $I_0$ | empty | No occupied native input latch |
| After IN admission, before its Finish | $I_0$ | $(\tau,b)$ | Pre-IN native record; actual fine snapshot holds $b$ |
| After IN Finish, before WR admission | $I_0(\tau,b)$ | empty | Native latch 5 occupied; destination still pre-WR |
| During the following WR | $I_0(\tau,b)$ | empty | Mathematical pre-WR frontier, with its one occupied latch |
| After WR Finish | $I_0(\tau,b)$ | empty | Written payload extent includes $b$; stale latch contributes nothing |

In the second line the completed-row projection's native IN-to-WR
frontier contributes no bit: original IN has not completed. An early
physical write to the fine latch still belongs to that outstanding
row and is not another consumed bit. In the third line the outstanding
fine row is absent but the original latch is occupied. During WR, restoring
its old destination/frontier for comparison retains that one original
occupied latch. Adding both a newly written physical payload bit and
that restored latch would double count. After WR, [UAS] Theorem 3.4's
written-extent rule replaces the latch contribution by the written bit;
MR changes only the frontier. Preallocated payload zeros never count
as consumed bits before the corresponding WR. This proves every
table entry and the unique transfer between them. The same reasoning
applies to ARC's separate 70 IN/EX/WR/MR path, with its actual EX blank
and different ingress latch/site, not by equating those two sites. ∎

## 5. The positive local completion interface

**Definition 5.1 (explicit local premises).** Fix one enabled actual
original row $r$ at $W_-$. The following are additional operational
premises for its proposed fine fragment, rather than consequences of
two tree leaves or of a finite truth table.

There is actual retained finite Ready/Busy/Halt phase material and
a bounded row/operand before-image, acquired by authorized bounded
reads before the corresponding overwrites. Its next-stage selection,
every phase update and each guard check have an explicitly declared
execution mechanism. A continuing core permission as in Definition
4.1 is acquired at admission and survives exactly until Finish.
Current staged native PC/token/seal/halt encodings neither dispatch
another original row nor grant a callback.

The row has a fixed finite remaining stage list. For every reached
stage its designated operand is reachable through its actual current
links, all constructor parents and vacancies exist, and the declared
limited write/birth/handoff operation is enabled by the continuing
permission. The list computes the actual original core and finite
effects in written order, combines physical aliases correctly, preserves
both head lanes, and prepares exactly the original successor and one
receipt. Its bound output operand, if any, is the actual public bit
authorized by the original OUT at admission. A later raw finite effect
on that latch cannot silently replace it.

These premises can be supplied as priced strong operational atoms.
They are not a statement that the current native basis has installed
them. In particular bounded guard matching can still have the whole
original read footprint, even when each data mutation is smaller.
Under §7's stipulated atom convention, a setter, one-vertex birth,
head retarget and first activation have respectively 6, 11, 6 and 5
designated endpoint assignments including their phase update. Each
creates respectively 0, 1, 0 and 0 vertices. Admission and marker-only
Finish each have three phase assignments; FinishOUT additionally
releases one bound bit. This measures the decreased mutation scope.
It does not decrease the admitted guard's read scope or turn a finite
case table into an installed evaluator. Any implementation which
performs separate checking work must expose and price those cuts,
including refusal, as in Proposition 2.3.

**Theorem 5.2 (local continuation and completion under those premises).**
For any row satisfying Definition 5.1, core-phase-first dispatch and
one final Finish give a positive finite completion with its actual
source/guard/site binding, complete original successor and exactly one
completed original receipt. The changes to native code, TOK entry
bits or halt encoding in §3 do not obstruct its remaining stages.
The original consumer can receive the original completed result at
that boundary; no original experiment is thereby supplied at an
interior Busy cut.

Proof. Admission has the original guard and binds the actual row;
on IN it consumes its one authentic bit. Suppose $k$ stages of the
finite list have executed. The retained phase selects stage $k+1$
before consulting the changed native admission fields. Its actual
operand/constructor/link and current continuing-permission premises
enable it. It performs exactly its designated limited effect and
the charged phase update, preserving the interpretation of all other
material. This inductive step is independent of whether a staged
native token pair still equals $(1,0)$, a new raw selector is disabled,
or a raw halted encoding is already present. Finite list length proves
arrival at the ready-to-finish cut.

For the actual Recover pair, the two token data stages can therefore
be SetBit$(s,0)$ then SetBit$(d,1)$ under that bound permission.
They follow $(1,0)\to(0,0)\to(0,1)$ without reusing the first pair
as a current guard. Each setter includes its own phase update;
Busy grants no client callback at the intermediate pair. The
remaining fixed control/head/receipt stages and Finish are still
required. Likewise, selecting the raw halt field while Busy leaves
the final permission available to Finish, which alone selects the
absorbing core Halt. These are applications of the declared limited
atoms, not native TOK or CTL executions inside the fragment.

At that cut the original data, both links, control and single receipt
are the specified successor. Finish publishes exactly this successor
and retires its permission once. Definitions 4.1–4.2 and Theorem 4.3
give exact wire and receipt correspondence. If its actual original
successor is blocked nonhalt raw code, Ready has no new admission;
if it is genuine final halt, Halt has no rule at all. Thus neither
case is repaired into a different task or serviced after halt.
Original consumers of completed rows see their original binding and
result. The proof constructs no invocation/return adapter or read
capability for fine-stage material. ∎

**Proposition 5.3 (OUT may be the unique final publication).** Suppose
an unfused OUT row has reached a cut at which all its original native
data, both head lanes and staged receipt are final; its next two
reactions are one authorized captured-bit OUT with a phase relocation,
then a marker-only Finish. Replace these by one FinishOUT releasing
that same bit and relocating directly to the same Ready/Halt target.
Under an explicitly supplied core atom permitting that release and
relocation together, the completed graph, actual output, original
receipt and authority are unchanged. There is no output-ahead-of-
completion cut.

Proof. The OUT changes no native data, and the following Finish changes
only the controller attachment/publication status. No new row can
run between them. Their composite therefore has exactly the same
native projection and final controller target as the contracted
reaction. The captured authorized bit is released once in either
case, and no receipt is constructed or repeated by the contraction.
Before FinishOUT neither that output nor its row is completed; after
it both are. This proves (OR.2) and removes precisely Proposition
2.4's intermediate cut. For raw OUT effects overwriting the original
latch, the required atom releases the bound old permitted bit. It is
not automatically an application of native OUT to the now-current
latch. Its current core authority remains an explicit premise. ∎

FinishOUT does not move real input acceptance to Finish. Delaying
actual IN would require a separate paid waiting/binding protocol;
an already supplied external bit is not a free retained register.
Alternatively one may retain the pending OUT term in (OR.1) with an
explicit consumer contract. Fusion is sufficient, not necessary.

**Lemma 5.4 (what old-role transport supplies).** In a stage schedule
which never removes old cell/spine occurrences and only uses the
original adjacent head moves, every old cell role at a fixed bounded
offset from a moved head remains reachable after that handoff by a
bounded actual walk. Separate read and append lanes are necessary
when applying this fact to the meter.

Proof. [UAS] Definition 2.2 gives next-cell walk $\mathsf{PRL}$ and
previous-cell walk $\mathsf{PPL}$. Before a lane moves, its actual
origin/head link reaches its old anchor. After displacement
$\Delta\in\{-1,0,1\}$, the old role at offset $a$ is reached from
the new anchor by $a-\Delta$ adjacent walks. An append handoff has
$\Delta=1$ and the same inverse route. A fixed finite original
stencil has a finite maximum offset, independent of growing tape
length. Unary bit rewrites and parent-first births below the old tail
preserve those old spine paths. Initial activation instead uses its
actual fixed origin/template; it has no fictitious old head.

For example let meter read be at $a$ and append at its adjacent $b$.
A core MR of read can move it to $b$ while the receipt makes $b$ a
receipt and appends $c$. Lane 0 is then $a\to b$, lane 1 is
$b\to c$; the old $b$ is reached backwards from lane 1. The receipt
must preserve the core-updated read mark at $b$. Distinct
$O_0/H_0$ and $O_1/H_1$ slots retain these facts even if old
roles coincide. Written-order evaluation on each alias class is
therefore required before final assignments. The walks prove
reachability under the stated schedule; they grant no new authority
and certify no unchecked raw CTL schedule. ∎

**Proposition 5.5 (the precise stable interface consequence).** Fix a
declared class $\mathcal E$ of original finite experiment trees, with
their original lawful input domains and acquired-response branching.
Let $Q_{\mathcal E}$ be the restriction of the unchanged original
$Q$ to those trees. Suppose an explicitly initialized fine system
satisfies Theorem 5.2's local premises for every original row and
actual finite protocol used in those trees, and admits no other
stable-to-stable fragment. Interpret each original action by its
complete fragment and project its completed successor by $\pi$ to
the actual original record. Let $\widehat Q_{\mathcal E}$ be the
partial response map defined by that completion interpretation,
including the unchanged original empty terminal readout. Then at
stable cuts

$$
\widehat Q_{\mathcal E}(x)=Q_{\mathcal E}(\pi(x)),
\qquad
\widehat Q_{\mathcal E}(x)=\widehat Q_{\mathcal E}(y)
\ \Longleftrightarrow\
Q_{\mathcal E}(\pi(x))=Q_{\mathcal E}(\pi(y)).
$$

This is an equality of completion-defined response maps for the
declared experiments; it is not an all-cut original-task equality.

Proof. A disabled original action has no admission, so both partial
responses have the same original failure/domain status. An enabled
one has the same actual original label, wire occurrence and complete
projected successor by Theorem 5.2. Contracting a completed fragment
gives that same admitted original action by hypothesis. Induction
over finite experiment trees gives both directions, including their
adaptive choices from original acquired responses. At the empty
experiment retain only the actual original type/output; at halt
neither interpretation admits a further action. The two response
maps are therefore equal pointwise, and their equality kernels agree
on this completion interpretation. In particular the forward
implication follows by replacing each completion response by its
equal original response; the reverse implication follows by the
same pointwise equalities in the other direction. Taking
$\mathcal E$ to be all original experiments requires all their
row/protocol premises, not only the exhibited local cases. ∎

by this volume. Nor is its mathematical completion interpretation a

free work. The proposition does not transport original primitive

why that stronger assertion fails for predecessor-only transport.
