## 2. Integrality, the involution, and inherited attainment rigidity

**Proposition 2.1 (actual-coordinate congruences).** Every nonempty actual word satisfies

$$
E_W\equiv\zeta\pmod{2\mathbb E},\qquad
F_W\equiv1\pmod{2\mathbb E},\qquad
E_W\equiv\zeta^m\pmod{3\mathbb E}.
\tag{2.1}
$$

There are unique integers $r(W),j(W)$ such that

$$
F_W=r(W)+j(W)v,\qquad r(W)-j(W)\text{ is odd}.
\tag{2.2}
$$

For the empty word put $r(1)=j(1)=0$. For arbitrary quaternion coordinates, $r(E+F\delta)$ will mean $\operatorname{Re}F$ when that notation is used; it need not then be integral.

**Proof.** At length one the coordinates are $(\zeta,\pm1)$. Modulo $2\mathbb E$, (1.2) sends the residues $(\zeta,1)$ to

$$
(\zeta^2+1,\ \zeta+\bar\zeta)=(\zeta,1).
$$

Modulo $3\mathbb E$ its first coordinate is multiplied by $\zeta$, proving the third assertion by induction from $E=1$. Finally write $F=1+2(x+y\zeta)$; since $2\zeta=1+v$,

$$
F=(1+2x+y)+yv.
$$

This proves both integrality and the parity statement. Uniqueness follows from the linear independence of $1,v$ over $\mathbb Q$. Also $2\mathbb E\subset\mathbb Z[v]$, an inclusion needed for the contextual reductions below. $\square$

For two actual words $P,R$ of the same positive length, the free additive basis $1,\zeta$ gives $2\mathbb E\cap3\mathbb E=6\mathbb E$. Therefore

$$
E_P-E_R=6X,\qquad F_P-F_R=2Y,\qquad X,Y\in\mathbb E,
$$

and

$$
N(P-R)=12\bigl(3N_E(X)+N_E(Y)\bigr).
\tag{2.3}
$$

For distinct actual words this is positive, using conditional injection. Since $N_E(x+y\zeta)\equiv(x-y)^2\pmod3$, the factor in parentheses is $0$ or $1$ modulo three.

For $m\ge1$ define the inherited minimum

$$
\mu_m=\min_{\substack{W,V\in\mathcal T_m\\W\ne V}}
\frac{N(W-V)}{12}.
\tag{2.4}
$$

There are finitely many actual words and at least two at these lengths, so this is a positive integer. It cannot equal two. We do not define $\mu_0$. A **norm-48 attaining pair** is an unordered pair of distinct actual tails of the same length with $N(H(W-V))=48$. Since $H=A$ has norm four, this is precisely $N(W-V)=12$, or attainment of $\mu_m=1$.

**Proposition 2.2 (inherited criterion, with its proof).** Let $W^\#$ be obtained by reversing a string and swapping $A\leftrightarrow B$. Then

$$
W^\#=E_W-\bar F_W\delta,\qquad
r(W^\#)=-r(W),\qquad W-W^\#=2r(W)\delta.
\tag{2.5}
$$

Norm 48 occurs at tail length $m\ge1$ if and only if an actual $W\in\mathcal T_m$ has $r(W)=\pm1$. Every attaining pair is $\{W,W^\#\}$; it has exactly one member with real coordinate $+1$ and one with real coordinate $-1$, so its positive member and partner are unique. Its numerator difference is $H(W-W^\#)=\pm2H\delta$.

**Proof.** Define $\Phi(E+F\delta)=E-\bar F\delta$. For $X=E+F\delta$, $Y=G+J\delta$, both $\Phi(XY)$ and $\Phi(Y)\Phi(X)$ have first coordinate $EG-3F\bar J$ and second coordinate $-\bar E\bar J-\bar F G$. Thus $\Phi$ reverses multiplication. It fixes $\zeta$, swaps $A,B$, and squares to the identity, proving (2.5). If $r(W)=\pm1$, the difference has norm $4N(\delta)=12$, and is nonzero.

Conversely suppose $N(W-V)=12$. Equation (2.3) forces $X=0$ and $N_E(Y)=1$. Thus $E_W=E_V$ and $F_W-F_V=2\eta$ for an Eisenstein unit $\eta$. Equality of the two norms (1.3) gives $N_E(F_W)=N_E(F_W-2\eta)$, or

$$
\operatorname{Re}(F_W\bar\eta)=1.

## 3. The endpoint residue inside arbitrary actual contexts

**Theorem 3.1 (endpoint congruence).** For every nonempty actual word, the residue of $F_W$ in $\mathbb Z[v]/4\mathbb Z[v]$ is determined by its first and last algebraic tail letters:

| First letter | Last letter | $F_W\pmod{4\mathbb Z[v]}$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $A$ | $A$ | $1$ |
| $B$ | $B$ | $-1$ |
| $A$ | $B$ | $-v$ |
| $B$ | $A$ | $v$ |

Consequently $r(W)=1$ requires $AA$ endpoints and $F_W=1+4tv$ for some $t\in\mathbb Z$. The case $r(W)=-1$ requires $BB$ endpoints and $F_W=-1+4tv$. Different endpoints exclude norm-48 participation at every length where they occur.

**Proof.** The multiplication law and $\zeta^2=\zeta-1$ give the literal identities

$$
\begin{gathered}
A^2=A-4,\qquad B^2=B-4,\\
AB=(\zeta+2)-v\delta,\qquad
BA=(\zeta+2)+v\delta,\\
ABA=A+4v,\qquad BAB=B+4v.
\end{gathered}
\tag{3.1}
$$

For example the first coordinates of $AB$ and $BA$ are $\zeta^2+3=\zeta+2$, and their second coordinates are $-\zeta+\bar\zeta=-v$ and $\zeta-\bar\zeta=v$. Multiplying once more gives $ABA=(\zeta+4v)+\delta$ and $BAB=(\zeta+4v)-\delta$, which proves the last two identities. The first two also follow from the reduced trace one and norm four of each letter.

Consider the local reductions

$$
AA\longrightarrow A,\quad BB\longrightarrow B,\quad
ABA\longrightarrow A,\quad BAB\longrightarrow B.
\tag{3.2}
$$

They must be justified inside arbitrary actual contexts $P,Q$, not merely as isolated words. Either context may be empty. For the first two, old product minus new product is $-4PQ$. The concatenation $PQ$ is actual or empty, so its $F$ coordinate belongs to $\mathbb Z[v]$ by Proposition 2.1 or is zero. These reductions therefore preserve $F$ modulo $4\mathbb Z[v]$.

For either of the last two reductions the difference is $4PvQ$. Because $v-1=2(\zeta-1)\in2\mathbb E$, closure of $\mathcal K$ gives

$$
PvQ-PQ=P(v-1)Q\in2\mathcal K.
$$

Thus $F_{PvQ}-F_{PQ}\in2\mathbb E\subset\mathbb Z[v]$. The actual or empty product $PQ$ already has $F_{PQ}\in\mathbb Z[v]$, so $F_{PvQ}\in\mathbb Z[v]$ as well. This argument includes a single empty context and both empty contexts; in the latter case $PvQ=v$ has $F$ coordinate zero directly. Hence the last two reductions also preserve the required residue in all allowed contexts.

Each reduction preserves the two endpoint letters and decreases the positive word length; none makes a word empty. It must terminate. A terminal word with no equal adjacent letters is alternating. If its length were at least three it would contain $ABA$ or $BAB$, so the only terminal words are $A,B,AB,BA$. The preserved endpoints determine which one occurs. Their $F$ coordinates are $1,-1,-v,v$, proving the table, including the one-letter cases.

Taking real parts gives residues $1,-1,0,0$ modulo four. Thus real part $+1$ can occur only at $AA$ endpoints and real part $-1$ only at $BB$ endpoints. In either same-endpoint case the imaginary coefficient is divisible by four, yielding the stated exact forms when the real part is fixed. Proposition 2.2 completes the exclusion assertion. $\square$

These are necessary endpoint tests, not a reachability criterion for arbitrary coordinate points. They also do not assert that a word with $AA$ endpoints has real part one. For instance (3.1) gives $A^3=A^2-4A=-3A-4$, so $r(AAA)=-3$ despite those endpoints. The length-five shell example below makes the stronger failure of sufficiency explicit.

## 4. The zero fibre as a direct corollary

**Corollary 4.1 (fixed words and parity).** An actual word has $r(W)=0$ if and only if its ordered string is fixed by $\#$. No positive odd length has such a word. At length $2k$ there are exactly $2^k$ of them, namely

$$
W=S S^\#\qquad(S\in\mathcal T_k).
\tag{4.1}
$$

The case $k=0$ is the one empty word and does not define $\mu_0$.

**Proof.** By (2.5), $r(W)=0$ is equivalent to equality of the products $W,W^\#$, since $\delta\ne0$. Conditional actual injection converts this to equality of strings. Fixed strings must have complementary letters at positions $i$ and $m+1-i$. An odd middle position would equal its own swapped letter, which is impossible. At even length choose the first $k$ letters freely; the rest must be their reverse-and-swap. Conversely this construction is fixed by $\#$. There are exactly $2^k$ choices by injection. The empty case follows separately. This classification is a direct corollary of the inherited involution and injection, not an independently new mathematical ingredient. $\square$

## 5. Exact half-length bilinear formulas on actual words

Fix $k\ge1$, and index all matrices in this section by the actual words $P,R\in\mathcal T_k$. Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Omega(P,R)&=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(F_P\bar E_R-E_P\bar F_R\bigr),\\
G(P,R)&=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(E_P\bar E_R+3F_P\bar F_R\bigr),\\
K_\zeta(P,R)&=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(F_P\bar\zeta\bar E_R-E_P\zeta\bar F_R\bigr),\\
L_+(P,R)&=K_\zeta(P,R)+G(P,R),\\
L_-(P,R)&=K_\zeta(P,R)-G(P,R).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
$$

Their indexing set is exactly $\mathcal T_k$, not the set of solutions of a norm equation.

**Theorem 5.1 (exhaustive parametrization and signs).** Every even word of length $2k$ has a unique representation $W=P R^\#$, with

$$
r(W)=\Omega(P,R),\qquad W^\#=R P^\#.
\tag{5.2}
$$

Every odd word of length $2k+1$ has a unique representation with its middle letter retained, and

$$
\begin{aligned}
r(P A R^\#)&=L_+(P,R),& (P A R^\#)^\#&=R B P^\#,\\
r(P B R^\#)&=L_-(P,R),& (P B R^\#)^\#&=R A P^\#.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.3}
$$

Even-length norm 48 is attained exactly when the block with $P$ beginning $A$ and $R$ beginning $B$ contains an entry $\Omega(P,R)=1$. Odd-length norm 48 is attained exactly when one of $L_+(P,R),L_-(P,R)$ equals one at a position in that same block. These formulas represent each unordered attaining pair exactly once by its positive member. The negative member has $P$ beginning $B$, $R$ beginning $A$ in its own representation, with the transposition and middle-letter exchange displayed above.

**Proof.** Since $R^\#=E_R-\bar F_R\delta$, multiplication gives

$$
F_{P R^\#}=F_P\bar E_R-E_P\bar F_R.
$$

Also $\zeta R^\#=\zeta E_R-\zeta\bar F_R\delta$, so

$$
F_{P\zeta R^\#}=F_P\bar\zeta\bar E_R-E_P\zeta\bar F_R.
$$

Finally

$$
\delta R^\#=3F_R+\bar E_R\delta,
\qquad F_{P\delta R^\#}=E_P\bar E_R+3F_P\bar F_R.
$$

Taking real parts and using $A=\zeta+\delta$, $B=\zeta-\delta$ proves (5.2)–(5.3). These are bilinear identities, not estimates.

Split any even string after its first $k$ letters as $PQ$. Its last half determines the unique $R=Q^\#$, because $\#$ is a bijection of actual $k$-letter strings. An odd string splits uniquely as $P L Q$ with $L\in\{A,B\}$ and again $R=Q^\#$. Thus every actual word, and no additional coordinate point, is represented. The endpoint letters of each represented word are the first letter of $P$ and the swap of the first letter of $R$. Theorem 3.1 consequently puts every positive witness precisely in the stated $(A,B)$ block; a negative witness belongs to the $(B,A)$ block. Proposition 2.2 gives the equivalence with attainment, the exact partner, and the unique positive member. Unique midpoint splitting then proves the stated uniqueness of representation. $\square$

**Theorem 5.2 (integral matrix structure).** All entries of (5.1) are integers. The matrices $\Omega,K_\zeta$ are alternating, $G$ is a symmetric positive semidefinite Gram matrix arising from a positive definite form on four coordinates, and

$$
L_+^{\mathsf T}=-L_-.
\tag{5.4}
$$

Every one of these five matrices has rank at most four, over both $\mathbb Q$ and $\mathbb R$. The diagonal entries are

G(P,P)=4^k,\quad L_+(P,P)=4^k,\quad L_-(P,P)=-4^k.

Moreover $\Omega(P,R)=0$ if and only if $P=R$. No entry of $G,L_+,L_-$ is zero. An entry of $K_\zeta$ between opposite first-letter classes is also nonzero; no full classification of its zeros within a first-letter class is asserted.

**Proof.** Polarization of (1.1) gives

$$
N(P-R)=N(P)+N(R)-2G(P,R)=2\cdot4^k-2G(P,R).
\tag{5.6}

Equation (2.3), also when $P=R$, supplies a nonnegative integer

$$
q(P,R)=N(P-R)/12,\qquad
G(P,R)=4^k-6q(P,R).
\tag{5.7}

Thus $G$ is integral. The entries $\Omega,L_+,L_-$ are real $F$ coordinates of actual nonempty words by Theorem 5.1, hence integers by Proposition 2.1. Consequently $K_\zeta=L_+-G$ is integral too.

Write

$$
E_P=e_P+s_Pv,\qquad F_P=r_P+j_Pv,
\qquad x_P=(e_P,s_P,r_P,j_P).
$$

Here $e_P,s_P\in\tfrac12\mathbb Z$ and $r_P,j_P\in\mathbb Z$. Direct expansion of the real parts in (5.1) gives

For the last formula, use $\bar\zeta=(1-v)/2$, $\zeta=(1+v)/2$: the coefficient of $v$ in $F_P\bar E_R$ is $j_Pe_R-r_Ps_R$, and that in $E_P\bar F_R$ is $s_Pr_R-e_Pj_R$. Multiplication by $\bar\zeta$ and by $\zeta$ produces the signs in (5.8).

\tag{5.9}

Thus no $G$ entry is zero, including off-diagonal entries. Every $L_\pm$ entry is the real coordinate of an odd actual word, which cannot vanish by Corollary 4.1. The remaining nonzero assertion for $K_\zeta$ follows from the parity of the block congruences proved next. $\square$

## 6. The all-odd-length middle-letter obstruction

**Theorem 6.1 (modulus twelve, including coincident halves).** For every $k\ge1$ and every $P,R\in\mathcal T_k$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_+(P,R)-L_-(P,R)
 &=2G(P,R)\\
 &=2\cdot4^k-N(P-R)\\
 &=2\cdot4^k-12q(P,R)
 \equiv8\pmod{12}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.1}
$$

Consequently at most one of the two actual words $P A R^\#$ and $P B R^\#$ can have $r\in\{1,-1\}$. At every odd length at least three, two words differing only in the middle letter cannot both participate in norm-48 pairs.

**Proof.** The first equality is the definition of $L_\pm$, and the next two are (5.6)–(5.7). For $k\ge1$, $4^k\equiv4\pmod6$, so twice this power is $8$ modulo twelve. If both real coordinates were in $\{1,-1\}$, their difference would be one of $0,2,-2$, whose residues modulo twelve are $0,2,10$, never eight. Proposition 2.2 identifies these coordinates with participation in attaining pairs, proving the assertion.

No division by $P-R$ or assumption that it is nonzero was made. If $P=R$, then $q=0$, $K_\zeta=0$, $G=4^k$, and $L_\pm=\pm4^k$; neither word participates. If $P\ne R$, conditional injection makes $q$ positive.
