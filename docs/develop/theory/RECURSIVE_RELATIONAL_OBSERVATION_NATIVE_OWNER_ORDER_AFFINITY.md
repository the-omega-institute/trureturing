## 2. Full actual owners and conditional domination

**Definition 2.1 (credited exact carrier).** A complete native template
on $n$ original blocks selects at every $(i,q)\in[n]\times W_d$ a
constant $\Gamma_q(x_i)=z\in G_q$, or an equation

$$
\Gamma_q(x_i)=\delta_{i,q}(r_{x_j}(a)),
\qquad a\in W_d,\quad \delta_{i,q}:\mathcal A\to G_q.
\tag{OOA.6}
$$

All equations hold on the same tuple; donors may be self, off-target,
shared or cyclic. Its solution set is $C_\Theta\subseteq S_d^n$.
An admissible subrelation has such witnesses for every target.
[NDR, Theorem 2.2][NDR] supplies containing complete templates without
changing nonnegative mass maxima or cover optima.

Use the exact [RRN, Definition 5.1 and Theorem 5.2][RRN] carrier.
A seed $c$ has nonempty domain $T_c\subseteq\mathcal A$ and a distinct
actual owner port $o_c=(i_c,q_c)$. Each port prescribes a constant
factor or $B_{i,q}(s_c)$ for one seed, and at its owner

$$
h(B_{o_c}(a))=a\qquad(a\in T_c).
\tag{OOA.7}
$$

Repeated occurrences use the same variable. A seed assignment either
reconstructs a unique actual $x_i(s)\in S_d$ at every block or fails
actual-tree compatibility. For arbitrary nonnegative whole-block
weights $w_i$, let $\Phi_i(s)=w_i(x_i(s))$ when block $i$ reconstructs,
and zero otherwise. Each full $w_i$ occurs once. The credited two-way
correspondence gives one lift per actual tuple and

$$
\sum_{x\in C_\Theta}\prod_iw_i(x_i)
=\sum_{s\in\prod_cT_c}\prod_i\Phi_i(s).
\tag{OOA.8}
$$

[CBN, §§2–6][CBN] supplies the actual constructor constraints and
equality-aware once-owned weights. No pairwise factorization of $w_i$
is assumed. Seeds in this representation are analytical variables,
never extra acquired or exported observations.

**Definition 2.2 (owner tuples and retained relation).** Let $O_i$
be the full tuple of all seeds owned by $i$, with domain $\Omega_i$.
An empty tuple has its single empty value. Thus
$\mathcal S=\prod_i\Omega_i$ is exactly the original seed product,
with each seed appearing once. Let $I_i$ be the set of distinct outside
owners with seeds prescribed at block $i$, and write $k_i=|I_i|$.
For $j\in I_i$, $B_{i,j}$ is the tuple of all locally prescribed
seeds owned by that same $j$. For $A\subseteq I_i$ put
$B_{i,A}=(B_{i,j})_{j\in A}$; it is a projection of $(O_j)_{j\in A}$.
Let $R\subseteq\mathcal S$ be any retained positive-weight subrelation
of actual solutions, and set

$$
Z_R=\sum_{s\in R}\prod_i\Phi_i(s).
\tag{OOA.9}
$$

No rectangularity or independence of $R$, its seeds or its Gibbs law
is assumed. Empty domains or $R=\varnothing$ give zero mass and are
handled before logarithms.

**Assumption 2.3 (full conditional rows).** For every $i$ and
$A\subseteq I_i$, choose a strictly positive normalized row
$Q_{i,A}(o_i\mid b_A)$ on the entire $\Omega_i$ for every full
context $b_A$, including unrealizable contexts. Put

$$
\gamma_k(A)=\frac{|A|!(k-|A|)!}{(k+1)!},\qquad
G_i(s)=\prod_{A\subseteq I_i}
 Q_{i,A}(O_i(s)\mid B_{i,A}(s))^{\gamma_{k_i}(A)}.
\tag{OOA.10}
$$

Here the coefficient depends on $A$ only through its cardinality.
The coefficients sum to one. Suppose $C_i>0$ and
$\Phi_i(s)\le C_iG_i(s)$ on $R$, and define there
$u_i(s)=\Phi_i(s)/(C_iG_i(s))\in(0,1]$.

For reusable local checks use exactly [COK, Definitions 2.2–2.3][COK]'s
finite decorations and support-aware outer relations. Such a relation
requires the full local vector to reconstruct a positive-weight block
and, for each incoming owner group, one positive-weight actual owner
block realizing all that group's owner factors together. Different
groups' witnesses need not extend globally. For heterogeneous weights,
use the support of the actual outside owner's $w_j$ in that test;
using $\operatorname{supp}w_i$ instead is not licensed. [COK,
Proposition 2.4][COK] supplies coverage of positive global assignments.

## 3. Joint order-law affinity and the exact common-law defect

**Definition 3.1 (normalized order laws).** For a permutation $\pi$ of
the original $n$ blocks, let $A_i(\pi)\subseteq I_i$ be the incoming
owners preceding $i$. On the full $\mathcal S$ define

$$
q_\pi(s)=\prod_iQ_{i,A_i(\pi)}(O_i(s)\mid B_{i,A_i(\pi)}(s)),
\quad
H(s)=\prod_{\pi\in\mathfrak S_n}q_\pi(s)^{1/n!},
\quad a_R=\sum_{s\in R}H(s).
\tag{OOA.11}
$$

These are proof distributions on owner tuples. They are not a law of
acquisition or an independent-source model.

**Theorem 3.2 (guarded affinity reduction, retaining both mass directions).**
Every $q_\pi$ is a strictly positive probability on $\mathcal S$.
Pointwise $H=\prod_iG_i$. If $R$ is nonempty, $0<a_R\le1$, and

$$
Z_R=\left(\prod_iC_i\right)
       \sum_{s\in R}H(s)\prod_iu_i(s)
\le\left(\prod_iC_i\right)a_R.
\tag{OOA.12}
$$

Keeping $R,H$ and the once-owned slacks gives the equality in both
directions. Dropping slacks or enlarging $R$ gives an upper bound only.

Proof. Generate the full owner tuples in order $\pi$. Every context
is a function of earlier tuples and its row sums to one. Summing in
reverse order gives $\sum_sq_\pi(s)=1$. In a uniform permutation,
the relative order of $i$ and its $k_i$ distinct incoming owners is
uniform. Exactly $A$ precede $i$ with probability $\gamma_{k_i}(A)$.
Averaging $\log q_\pi(s)$ therefore gives $\log H(s)=\sum_i\log G_i(s)$.
Substitution proves the equality in (OOA.12), using each $\Phi_i$ once.
Pointwise geometric/arithmetic mean gives
$H(s)\le(n!)^{-1}\sum_\pi q_\pi(s)$, so $\sum_{\mathcal S}H\le1$.
Strict positivity proves $a_R>0$ for nonempty $R$. $\square$

**Theorem 3.3 (global relative entropy and zero-loss completion).** For
any probability $P$ on a nonempty $R$, set
$P_*(s)=\mathbf1_R(s)H(s)/a_R$ and define the COK chain loss

$$
\Delta(P)=\mathbb E_\pi\sum_i
 I_P(O_i;O_{<i,\pi}\mid B_{i,A_i(\pi)}).
$$

Then exactly

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_\pi D_2(P\Vert q_\pi)
&=\Delta(P)+\sum_{i,A}\gamma_{k_i}(A)
 D_2(P(O_i\mid B_{i,A})\Vert Q_{i,A}\mid P(B_{i,A}))\\
&=D_2(P\Vert P_*)-\log_2a_R.
\end{aligned}
\tag{OOA.13}
$$

For the actual Gibbs law $P(s)=\mathbf1_R(s)\prod_i\Phi_i(s)/Z_R$,

$$
\log_2Z_R=\sum_i\log_2C_i+\log_2a_R
 -D_2(P\Vert P_*)-\sum_i\mathbb E_P\log_2(1/u_i).
\tag{OOA.14}
$$

In particular $a_R=1$ if and only if $R=\mathcal S$ and all order
laws $q_\pi$ coincide. This is completion with zero analytical defect
for the specified positive kernels, not completion of arbitrary locally
feasible marginals or hard-zero native laws.

Proof. Expanding the left side of (OOA.13) gives
$-H(P)-\mathbb E_P\log_2H$. Conditional cross entropy writes
$-\mathbb E_P\log Q_{i,A}$ as conditional entropy plus conditional
relative entropy. The chain rule, using that $B_{i,A_i(\pi)}$ is a
projection of $O_{<i,\pi}$, makes the remaining entropy difference
exactly $\Delta(P)$. This is [COK, Theorem 3.2][COK]'s common-law
loss, now expressed by global normalized laws. The definition of $P_*$
gives the second equality. Gibbs' identity and
$\prod_i\Phi_i=(\prod_iC_i)H\prod_iu_i$ give (OOA.14).

If $a_R=1$, positivity of $H$ excludes every proper subset $R$ of
$\mathcal S$. Equality in the summed geometric/arithmetic mean then
forces equality pointwise in that inequality: every $q_\pi(s)$ is the
same for each fixed $s$. The converse follows from normalization.
All terms use the same $P$; no unrelated local maximizing laws have
been combined. $\square$

## 4. Actual-owner diamonds and exact order agreement

**Definition 4.1 (dependency graph and two orders).** Form a simple
undirected graph $G$ on original blocks, with $ij$ an edge precisely
when a seed owned by either endpoint is prescribed at the other.
This includes prescribed occurrences whose table happens to be constant;
self occurrences add no edge. For $e=ij$ put
$W_e=N_G[i]\cup N_G[j]$. Given
$P\subseteq W_e\setminus\{i,j\}$ and full owner values
$u\in\prod_{v\in P}\Omega_v$, define

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_e^+(a,b\mid u)
&=Q_{i,I_i\cap P}(a\mid B_{i,I_i\cap P}(u))\,
  Q_{j,I_j\cap(P\cup\{i\})}(b\mid B_{j,I_j\cap(P\cup\{i\})}(u,a)),\\
K_e^-(a,b\mid u)
&=Q_{j,I_j\cap P}(b\mid B_{j,I_j\cap P}(u))\,
  Q_{i,I_i\cap(P\cup\{j\})}(a\mid B_{i,I_i\cap(P\cup\{j\})}(u,b)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{OOA.15}
$$

Irrelevant components are ignored; repeated seed references select the
same component of their actual full owner tuple. Both are normalized
joint laws on $\Omega_i\times\Omega_j$. Define a uniform edge loss
$\delta_e\in[0,1]$ by the requirement

$$
\sum_{a,b}\sqrt{K_e^+(a,b\mid u)K_e^-(a,b\mid u)}\le1-\delta_e
\quad\text{for every }P,u.
\tag{OOA.16}
$$

The test includes unrealizable contexts. It is a stronger, finitely
testable premise than a statement only under an unknown Gibbs marginal.

**Proposition 4.2 (exact diamond criterion).** All $q_\pi$ coincide
if and only if $K_e^+=K_e^-$ for every edge, preceding subset and full
context in Definition 4.1. For nonedges the corresponding equality
holds automatically.

Proof. Exchanging two consecutive blocks in a permutation changes only
their two rows. Their product is precisely (OOA.15), using the earlier
neighbors' values. For nonneighbors neither context changes and the
rows commute. If all diamonds agree, adjacent transpositions connect
all permutations and prove sufficiency.

Conversely fix $e,P,u$ and choose a permutation putting exactly $P$
first, then $i,j$, then all other blocks. Compare with the permutation
reversing the two adjacent endpoints. Summing the common later rows
in reverse order leaves the same strictly positive prefix density
times respectively $K_e^+$ and $K_e^-$. Equality of the two full
laws and cancellation of that density gives the required diamond at
the arbitrary $u$. Strict positivity is essential to this cancellation.
$\square$

## 5. Quantitative joint contraction by paired permutations

**Theorem 5.1 (disjoint-neighborhood owner-swap rule).** In any finite
actual network satisfying Assumption 2.3, let $T$ be any set of edges
whose full $W_e$ are pairwise disjoint, and suppose (OOA.16) holds.
For every retained $R$, including arbitrary guards, compatibility zeros
and correlations,

$$
a_R\le\prod_{e\in T}\left(1-\frac{2\delta_e}{|W_e|}\right),
\qquad
Z_R\le\left(\prod_iC_i\right)
          \prod_{e\in T}\left(1-\frac{2\delta_e}{|W_e|}\right).
\tag{OOA.17}
$$

The first inequality uses the definition of $a_R$ even when $R$ is
empty. No independence of actual seeds, solutions or block weights is
required.

Proof. Let $\pi$ be a uniform permutation of the original blocks.
Call $e=ij$ eligible if $i,j$ are consecutive in its restriction to
$W_e$. On $w=|W_e|$ labels there are $2(w-1)!$ orders with this
property, so eligibility has probability $2/w$. The relative orders on
disjoint sets $W_e$ are independent: after prescribing them, the number
of full permutations is $n!/\prod_e|W_e|!$, independently of the
prescribed orders. This is independence of auxiliary relative orders.

Swap the positions of both endpoints of every eligible $e\in T$.
Because the neighborhoods are disjoint, the operations have disjoint
endpoints, preserve every eligibility event, and define an involution
$\tau$ on permutations. Thus $\tau$ preserves their uniform law.
No neighbor of either endpoint lies strictly between eligible endpoints.
No other kernel row changes its earlier incoming-owner set: a vertex
between the endpoints is a nonneighbor of both, and a vertex outside
their interval sees both on the same sides as before.

For each eligible pair move its later endpoint just after its earlier
endpoint, crossing only nonneighbors. Each crossed adjacent swap leaves
every kernel row unchanged. These moves can make all eligible pairs
consecutive at once. A move cannot split a previously completed pair:
it removes its own distinct endpoint and inserts after its own other
endpoint. It also leaves the restriction to every other selected $W_f$
unchanged. In this grouped order $q_\pi$ and $q_{\tau\pi}$ have the
same sequential list of single blocks and pairs; only each eligible
pair's internal order is reversed. Contexts at a later group contain
the same full earlier owner values on both sides.

In $\sqrt{q_\pi q_{\tau\pi}}$ a single block contributes its
normalized row, and an eligible pair contributes
$\sqrt{K_e^+K_e^-}$ with the common preceding context. Reverse summation
over this list, applying (OOA.16) uniformly in that context, proves

$$
\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\sqrt{q_\pi(s)q_{\tau\pi}(s)}
\le\prod_{\substack{e\in T\\e\text{ eligible in }\pi}}(1-\delta_e).
\tag{OOA.18}
$$

This summation never factors a native solution law. It bounds full
normalized analytical rows on their shared variables.

For each $s$, permutation invariance gives
$\mathbb E_\pi\log\sqrt{q_\pi(s)q_{\tau\pi}(s)}=\log H(s)$.
Geometric/arithmetic mean yields
$H(s)\le\mathbb E_\pi\sqrt{q_\pi(s)q_{\tau\pi}(s)}$.
Sum on $R$, enlarge this nonnegative sum to $\mathcal S$, and apply
(OOA.18). Independence of the eligibility indicators gives

$$
a_R\le\mathbb E_\pi\prod_{e\text{ eligible}}(1-\delta_e)
=\prod_{e\in T}\left(1-\frac{2\delta_e}{|W_e|}\right).
$$

Theorem 3.2 supplies the mass inequality. $\square$

**Corollary 5.2 (degree-counted extensive loss).** Suppose $G$ has
maximum degree at most an integer $\Delta\ge1$. Mark $t$ vertices
high, and suppose each high vertex is incident to an edge satisfying
(OOA.16) with $\delta_e\ge\delta$ for a fixed $0<\delta<1$. If
$C_i\le c_0$ at low vertices and $C_i\le c_1$ at high vertices,
where $0<c_0\le c_1$, put $B=2\Delta^2(\Delta+1)$. Then

$$
Z_R\le c_0^{n-t}c_1^t(1-\delta/\Delta)^{t/(2B)}.
\tag{OOA.19}
$$

Proof. Select one certified edge incident to each high vertex, retaining
only distinct edges. At least $t/2$ remain. For any edge $ij$,
$|N[i]\cup N[j]|\le2\Delta$, since $i,j$ belong to both closed
neighborhoods. For a fixed vertex $v$, at most
$\Delta(\Delta+1)$ edges have neighborhoods containing $v$: one
endpoint must be in $N[v]$, and its degree is at most $\Delta$.
An edge neighborhood therefore meets those of at most $B$ candidate
edges, including itself. Greedy selection leaves at least $t/(2B)$
edges with disjoint neighborhoods. Each has
$2\delta_e/|W_e|\ge\delta/\Delta$. Apply (OOA.17).
Since $0<1-\delta/\Delta<1$, monotonicity in the number selected
gives the displayed real exponent. $\square$

## 6. A specified finite sound, incomplete all-size certificate

**Definition 6.1 (complete bounded-incidence neighborhood list).** Fix
$d,L\ge1$. Prescribed incidence is the number of original blocks
having a port assigned to a seed, including constant assigned tables;
multiple occurrences within a block count once. Enumerate the complete
COK decoration list $\mathcal E_d$ with its canonical ordering and
support-aware relations $R_\eta(\mu)$, for a rational probability on
the full $S_d$. Write $A_{d,L,n}(\mu)$ for the maximum mass over
all valid owned-seed networks on $n$ original blocks with prescribed
incidence at most $L$, and put
$\beta_{d,L}(\mu)=\sup_{n\ge1}A_{d,L,n}(\mu)^{1/n}$.
This is the credited SIR restricted pressure, with full actual guards
and joint block weights. Let

$$
m=m_d,\qquad \Delta=mL,\qquad B=2\Delta^2(\Delta+1).
\tag{OOA.20}
$$

Every actual incidence-$L$ network has dependency degree at most
$m+m(L-1)=\Delta$: there are at most $m$ incoming owners, and at
most $m$ owned seeds, each used in at most $L-1$ other blocks.

Use the following finite outer list of rooted radius-two patterns.
Enumerate simple rooted graphs on at most $1+\Delta^2$ vertices,
all within distance two of the root and of maximum visible degree at
most $\Delta$. Decorate every vertex by one element of $\mathcal E_d$.
Match local seed occurrences to actual owner-port names, domains and
owner tables, identifying repeated references. At vertices of distance
zero or one, every incoming owner group has its owner among the listed
neighbors. Every edge incident to such a vertex is witnessed by an
assigned seed in at least one direction. Its listed star is declared
complete; no further incident edge is allowed there. At distance two,
incoming groups may instead have formal unlisted owner names; identify
these names in every possible way consistent with distinct actual owner
ports and group identities. Allow further unlisted consumers of boundary
owned seeds without imposing their completion. Check every visible seed
incidence is at most $L$, and every internal reference agrees with its
owner decoration and is an edge of the displayed graph. Keep all
internally consistent patterns, without a
global realizability or positive-mass extension test.

This is an explicit finite enumeration: there are boundedly many vertices,
at most $m$ local seeds and groups at each vertex, finite ports/domains/
tables, and finitely many equality patterns on their external names.
Take least encodings under vertex and seed renaming, using the COK
orders for replies, words and factors. A boundary formal name supplies
only the owner-port/domain/table data already in its incoming group;
no kernel at an unlisted vertex is needed. Every actual rooted
radius-two neighborhood occurs in this outer list, with all root and
neighbor incidences intact. No converse completion statement is used.

For an edge at the root, its entire $W_e$ is listed, and full owner
domains at these vertices are known. Thus all tests (OOA.15)–(OOA.16)
for that edge are determined by the pattern, even when its boundary
has unlisted neighbors. The kernels of the two endpoints use only their
complete listed incoming groups.

**Definition 6.2 (the finite predicate $V_{\rm swap}$).** Input is
$d,L$, rational probability $\mu$ on the entire canonical $S_d$,
rational $C>0$, and a finite string supplying the following data.
Malformed indices, missing/duplicate entries and failed tests reject.

1. Partition all types with $R_\eta(\mu)\ne\varnothing$ into low
   and high. Types with empty relation are marked zero. Supply positive
   rational normalized full rows $Q_{\eta,A}$ for every nonzero type,
   every subset and every full context. Supply rational
   $0<c_0\le c_1$ and $0<\delta<1$.
2. At each nonzero type and each $s\in R_\eta(\mu)$, with
   $r=(k+1)!$, check the exact rational COK inequality

$$
\Phi_\eta(s)^r\le c_b^r\prod_{A\subseteq[k]}
 Q_{\eta,A}(O\mid B_A)^{|A|!(k-|A|)!},
\quad b=0\text{ for low},\ b=1\text{ for high}.
\tag{OOA.21}
$$

3. Recompute the complete finite pattern list of Definition 6.1. Patterns
   with a zero vertex need no swap witness. In every remaining pattern
   whose root is high, require a supplied incident edge and, for every
   $P,u,a,b$ in Definition 4.1, rational $h_{P,u,a,b}\ge0$ with

$$
h_{P,u,a,b}^{\,2}\ge K_e^+(a,b\mid u)K_e^-(a,b\mid u),
\qquad \sum_{a,b}h_{P,u,a,b}\le1-\delta\quad(P,u\text{ fixed}).
\tag{OOA.22}
$$

4. Finally check

$$
C\ge c_0,\qquad C^{2B}\ge c_1^{2B}(1-\delta/\Delta).
\tag{OOA.23}
$$

All arrays use the finite canonical orders just specified. There is no
search for an unspecified extension, no oracle for global feasibility,
and no implicit square-root comparison. The specification defines a
mathematical predicate; it installs no repository judge or tool.

**Theorem 6.3 (totality and all-size soundness).** $V_{\rm swap}$ is
total computable. Acceptance implies, simultaneously for every $n$ and
every original owned-seed network of prescribed incidence at most $L$,

$$
Z(\mathcal N;\mu,\ldots,\mu)\le C^n,
\qquad \beta_{d,L}(\mu)\le C.
\tag{OOA.24}
$$

Proof. Each enumeration and every rational power or comparison in the
definition is finite. If a network has positive mass, all its vertex
types have nonempty support-aware relations, by COK coverage. Its actual

## 8. Original all-competitor consumer and paid witness

**Theorem 8.1 (quantitative inequality for every original code).** Fix
$d\le D$ and any analytical probability $\mu$ on the full $S_d$.
Restrict the same arbitrary correct original complete code to the
perfect right-subtree scaffold with $n=2^{D-d}$ actual canonical blocks.
For each positive-mass restricted name fiber $B_e$, choose complete
witnesses, perform the exact RRN owner lift, and retain the lift of

$$
\mu^{\otimes n}(B_e)\le
\left(\prod_iC_{e,i}\right)
\prod_{f\in T_e}\left(1-\frac{2\delta_{e,f}}{|W_{e,f}|}\right),
\qquad
1\le\sum_e
\left(\prod_iC_{e,i}\right)
\prod_{f\in T_e}\left(1-\frac{2\delta_{e,f}}{|W_{e,f}|}\right).
\tag{OOA.33}
$$

The sum can be over positive-mass names only. It is a bound on every
original competitor with its own correlated fibers and arbitrary
off-target late queries, without independently assigning child codes.

Proof. Let the public frontier words be $a_i$ of length $D-d$.
Every tuple in $S_d^n$ gives an actual source in $\mathcal F_D$.
The request $Ra_iq$, $q\in W_d$, has length at most $D$. At a fixed

and wrapper cones and is constant, or enters one unique actual donor
block with an arbitrary finite suffix. FMR canonical all-word
simulation turns that suffix into a function of one boundary reply in
that same donor block, or a constant. Equal chosen replies therefore
give identical predictors and, by correctness for all $k$, equal whole
target trajectories and equal $\Gamma$.

Consequently the unchanged restricted fiber is admissible, as in
[NPC, Theorem 7.1][NPC]. NDR saturation and RRN's one-lift identity
provide $R_e$ without replacing it by independent projections. Theorem
5.1 bounds its same product mass. The restricted fibers partition a
probability-one slice; discard zero-mass fibers and sum their inequalities.
The analytical product law supplies no independent-seed premise, source
prior, request or extra observation to a reader. $\square$

## 9. Remaining completion and optimization obligations

**Open problem 9.1 (the unchanged effective global bridge).** Define
$A_n^{(d)}(w)$ as the maximum full-template mass with whole-block
weight $w$, and $\beta_d(w)=\sup_{n\ge1}A_n^{(d)}(w)^{1/n}$.
[NPC, Theorems 5.3 and 6.2][NPC] supplies

$$
\sigma_d=\inf_n\frac{\log_2K_d(n)}n
=-\log_2\min_{\mu\in\Delta(S_d)}\beta_d(\mu),\qquad
\lambda=\sup_d2^{-d}\sigma_d,
\quad 0\le\lambda-2^{-d}\sigma_d\le2^{-d}\phi,
\tag{OOA.38}
$$

with $U_0=1,U_{h+1}=1+U_h^2$ and
$\phi=\lim_h2^{-h}\log_2U_h\le1$, credited to FMR.
The original goal still requires a total sound cap verifier with
optimized accepted rational endpoints cofinal in every $\sigma_d$,
or a uniform effective length error

$$
\sigma_d\ge\frac{\log_2K_d(n)}n-\eta_d(n),\qquad
\eta_d(n)\longrightarrow0
\tag{OOA.39}
$$

with effective control. Neither is proved here. Soundness of
$V_{\rm swap}$ is distinct from such cofinality. No theorem says that
near-optimal kernels have positive-density certified defects in all
high neighborhoods. Zero-defect regions require another completion or
loss argument; even order agreement leaves the exact retained relation
and pointwise slacks in (OOA.12).

The plain envelope used for the reusable local checks in
Assumption 2.3 is exactly that of
[NWO, Definitions 1.3–1.7][NWO]: the same $d,S_d,W_d$, complete
COK decorations, simultaneous positive-support owner-group tests
and coefficients $\gamma_k(A)$. NWO Theorems 3.2–3.3 prove
exact-support continuity, global lower semicontinuity and

$$
a_d=\min_{\mu\in\Delta(S_d)}\Omega_d(\mu)
   =\inf_{\nu\in\Delta(S_d)\cap\mathbb Q^{S_d}}\Omega_d(\nu)
   \ge b_d:=\min_{\mu\in\Delta(S_d)}\beta_d(\mu).
$$

Its Theorems 5.3 and 6.2 give an effective support-disjunctive
semialgebraic epigraph and a computable positive algebraic $a_d$.
Theorem 7.2 supplies every rational $C>a_d$ at some rational
probability with complete positive rational COK rows. These are
also all-low certificates of Convention 6.4: set $c_0=c_1=C$
and choose any rational $0<\delta<1$ in Definition 6.2.
Support-preserving approximation therefore settles this
finite-envelope infimum; filling zeros is unnecessary and global
continuity across supports fails in general (NWO Corollary 4.5).

Equality $a_d=b_d$ at general depth remains unproved (NWO Open
problem 8.4). Nor does NWO supply approximation preserving high-type
coverage and a positive swap margin under support enlargement,
optimized completeness of $V_{\rm swap}$, or control of the SIR
incidence-transfer loss. Those enhanced-certificate and global
pressure obligations are separate from the solved plain-envelope
rational approximation.

The strict cycle correction is not an optimized unrestricted pressure
improvement, as Proposition 7.3 proves with an actual mass-one
competitor. It does supply a genuine extensive joint loss and a bound
on every original code via (OOA.33), while preserving the precise
unclosed obligation of [GDB, Definition 11.1][GDB] and [COK, Open
problem 9.3][COK]. No best numerical endpoint is asserted here; exact
general $M_1(D)$, sharp $\lambda$, effective two-sided convergence and
the wider minimal-relations/mutual-recovery programme remain open.
