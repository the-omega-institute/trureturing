# Common configuration optimality with a supported nonendpoint requires nonnative acquired residuals

**Mathematical status.** This volume gives ordinary mathematical proofs. Public prose suppliers remain fallible inputs under their stated hypotheses; no Lean kernel verification, frozen status or independent approval is claimed.

## 1. One source, complete laws and the new restriction

For an installed prior with positive mass on at least one depth $k\ge3$, this volume gives a necessary condition for the original common p-conf and suspended-conf problem. It concerns the complete p law obtained by averaging an individual suspended configuration's **actual alpha successors**. Even when the individual p laws are nonnative, all those acquired successor averages cannot remain mixtures of constant-parameter native stopped laws at an exact common optimum.

The quantitative theorem is uniform over all finite regular stationary tables with native acquired residuals and a supported nonendpoint. The original-observer exact-optimum consequence permits arbitrary finite kernels, transient configurations and zero or unit emissions. No unrestricted positive gap, exact construction, vanishing family or fixed-resource optimum is established.

**Definition 1.1 (The original experiment).** Retain the source, control and complete-tail prediction task of [ST, Sections 1–3; PAID, Definitions 1.1–1.3]. The parameters are $m=2,d=1,\ell=2,n=4$, with $F_0=0,F_1=1,F_{j+2}=F_{j+1}+F_j$. One actual depth $K$ is drawn before all Reads from one fixed finite or countable probability prior $\mu$, with $\mu(1),\mu(2)>0$. Conditional on that same $K=k$, all seed and payload letters are independent, with alpha probability

$$
r_k=\frac{F_{k+1}}{F_{k+3}},\qquad
a:=r_1=\frac13,\qquad b:=r_2=\frac25,
\qquad r_k\in[3/8,5/13]\quad(k\ge3).
\tag{1.1}
$$

Both letters of every rejected equal seed pair remain paid. The parser accepts $\alpha\beta$ as seed 0 and $\beta\alpha$ as seed 1. Both accepted seeds, every marker triple and every positive finite rejection and return history remain in the domain. At payload p, alpha completes marker 0 and beta suspends; at suspension, alpha returns to p and beta completes marker 1. The third record is written before its latch. Fourth completion retains all held records and enables only the matching original Stop, with unique delivery. Pending and delivered cuts permit no Read. There is no reset, fresh depth, additional observation, future-event conditioning or added controller port.

The original finite control $C_0$ includes parser, finite marker tree, bare and held records, selectors, write/latch flags, permissions, completion, pendingStop and deliveredStop. An observer has one fixed finite COMPLETE configuration set, source-independent random initialization and fixed time-homogeneous source-independent letter-update kernels. All persistent randomness, program and output indices, sampler workspace and control fields are counted. An exact posterior, probability-vector register, uncounted tape, archive, clock or source-correlated seed is unavailable.

Put $\lambda_r=r(1-r)$ and

$$
w_{j,0}=(\beta\alpha)^j\alpha,\qquad
w_{j,1}=(\beta\alpha)^j\beta\beta\qquad(j\ge0).
$$

The complete raw p carrier consists of these finite words and the legal infinite noncompletion word. The suspended carrier consists of beta, the words $\alpha w_{j,c}$ and its legal infinite noncompletion word. For a mathematical comparison parameter $r\in I:=[a,b]$, define

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{p,r}(w_{j,0})&=r\lambda_r^j,&
P_{p,r}(w_{j,1})&=(1-r)^2\lambda_r^j,\\
P_{\beta,r}(\beta)&=1-r,&
P_{\beta,r}(\alpha w_{j,c})&=rP_{p,r}(w_{j,c}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.2}
$$

These laws have total mass one and zero noncompletion mass. The interval $I$ is only a comparison family; it introduces no actual depths or source operations.

For each actual control $c$, the original renderer $I_c$ inserts all future control, record, permission, completion, Stop and delivery events into the raw Read word. Reading back the letters is its inverse. These complete-carrier bijections preserve TV and commute with deleting the next operation together with its deterministic event block. In particular, an already acquired suspended beta is not inserted into its future a second time. At a positive history $h$, let $A(h),B(h)$ count all acquired alpha and beta Reads, including rejected seed pairs and partial parses. Independence conditional on the same $K$ gives the positive-history likelihood $r_k^{A(h)}(1-r_k)^{B(h)}$. Finite-prefix conditioning leaves the unread letters independent with that same parameter, so the complete target at an active fourth cut is

$$
T_h^\mu=(I_{C_0(h)})_*\sum_k\nu_h(k)P_{s,r_k},\qquad
\nu_h(k)=\frac{\mu(k)r_k^{A(h)}(1-r_k)^{B(h)}}
{\sum_i\mu(i)r_i^{A(h)}(1-r_i)^{B(h)}}.
$$

The denominator is positive and the sum is finite or countably summable. No future completion is conditioned on. Posterior counts and probabilities are analysis quantities, never runtime inputs.

Every individual decoded law must be generated exactly by that configuration's own emissions and the same acquired-letter update kernels. In full notation, for every legal next operation and complete residual event, $D_z(oE)=q_z(o)\sum_{z'}P_o(z,z')D_{z'}(E)$ with the installed acquired kernel $P_o$ itself. At zero predicted probability this identity has zero left side, while the actual update remains installed. This obligation is independent of any marginalized conditioning defect. At a positive history $h$, let $\rho_h$ be the observer-randomness row, $D_z$ its complete decoded laws and $T_h^\mu$ the actual target. Define

$$
e_{\rm law}(h)=\operatorname{TV}\!\left(\sum_z\rho_h(z)D_z,T_h^\mu\right),\qquad
e_{\rm conf}(h)=\sum_z\rho_h(z)\operatorname{TV}(D_z,T_h^\mu).
\tag{1.3}
$$

The respective risks $R_{j,s}$ are suprema over all positive original histories at phase $s\in\{p,\beta\}$. They are not actual-history-average losses. Full $\mathcal H_p$ includes every finite fourth-segment return, whereas $\mathcal H_3$ contains only the first fourth p cut. The supplied separate complete-tail minima [CLIP, Definition 1.3; PAID, Definition 1.3] are

$$
\rho_p=\frac{1116529}{22781250},\qquad
\rho_\beta=\frac{239}{6750}.
\tag{1.4}
$$

The supplied terminal-projection values $13/266,9/266$ are different quantities and are not substituted for (1.4). The supplied actual three-depth endpoint-tag counterexample concerns that generator's configuration risk; it proves neither a universal sharper lower bound nor a marginalized-law failure [CLIP, Citation 1.5].

For a legal next operation $o$, let $q_h(o)=\sum_z\rho_h(z)q_z(o)$ and $\bar D_h=\sum_z\rho_h(z)D_z$. On a positive predicted cylinder, $\operatorname{res}_o$ conditions on that operation and deletes it together with its original deterministic event block. Define

$$
\delta(h,o)=
\begin{cases}
\operatorname{TV}(\operatorname{res}_o\bar D_h,\bar D_{ho}),&q_h(o)>0,\\
0,&q_h(o)=0.
\end{cases}
$$

The zero case defines no conditional law; the actual successor and its risk remain in the domain. The fourth-cut and all-operation defect suprema, $\Delta_4,\Delta_{\rm all}$, remain distinct. Pending Stop has deterministic residual and delivered Stop has empty supremum. An achievable tuple $(R_{{\rm law},p},R_{{\rm conf},p},R_{{\rm law},\beta},R_{{\rm conf},\beta},\Delta_4,\Delta_{\rm all})$ comes from one observer, installed source, prior and counted resource allocation. Separate attainability is not joint attainment. No defect budget or zero-defect recovery is assumed here.

**Definition 1.2 (Native acquired residual).** Let $X,Y$ be finite p and suspended carriers on one held-record fibre, with actual noncompleting kernels $B:X\to Y$ and $A:Y\to X$. Write their own-generated complete raw laws as $Q_x,W_y$, and their synthetic alpha probabilities as $u_x,v_y$. Thus

$$
Q_x=u_x\delta_\alpha+(1-u_x)\beta\sum_yB_{xy}W_y,
\qquad
W_y=(1-v_y)\delta_\beta+v_y\alpha J_y,
\qquad J_y:=\sum_xA_{yx}Q_x.
\tag{1.5}
$$

Here $\delta_w$ is the Dirac law at the complete raw word $w$, and prefixing a letter to a law means its pushforward by concatenation. $J_y$ is the law of the actual alpha-successor configuration, averaged only over that one acquired update. It is defined even if $v_y=0$. It is not the observer-randomness-marginalized forecast after conditioning on a synthetic alpha.

Let

$$
\mathcal N_p(I)=
\left\{\int_I P_{p,r}\,\nu(dr):\nu\text{ is a Borel probability measure on }I\right\}.
\tag{1.6}
$$

A table has native acquired residuals if every positive-row suspended label has $J_y\in\mathcal N_p(I)$. This imposes no native-mixture condition on the individual $Q_x$, no constancy of $v$, no redraw kernel and no equality of different $J_y$.

The measures in (1.6), stationary rows and matrix powers below are mathematical analysis objects. Neither a continuous mixture measure nor a row vector is made readable by the finite running observer.

**Theorem 1.3 (Original exact-optimum consequence).** Suppose additionally $\mu(\{k\ge3\})>0$. If an original finite COMPLETE observer attains both complete-tail configuration minima,

$$
R_{{\rm conf},p}=\rho_p,\qquad
R_{{\rm conf},\beta}=\rho_\beta,
\tag{1.7}
$$

then every common stationary support extracted on the original seed-1, marker-100 fibre contains a positive suspended label $y$ with

$$
J_y\notin\mathcal N_p(I).
\tag{1.8}
$$

That label is reachable after a finite positive original history. No uniform bound on that history's paid length or probability is asserted. Equivalently, requiring every reachable suspended configuration's actual alpha-successor average on that fibre to be native precludes (1.7). Arbitrary transient behavior, zero/unit emissions and noncompletion mass are allowed before the endpoint equalities are applied. The proof is completed in Section 5.

## 2. The original-domain bridge and regular tables

**Mathematical citation 2.1 (Common acquired row).** [PAID, Lemma 14.1] applies literally to the experiment of Definition 1.1 and the suffix

$$
S=\beta\alpha\mid\beta\beta\alpha\alpha.
\tag{2.1}
$$

It supplies finite carriers, the actual $B,A$, and probability rows $\pi,\tau$ with

$$
\pi B=\tau,\qquad \tau A=\pi,
\tag{2.2}
$$

such that, for every supported depth, the same rows satisfy

$$
\sum_x\pi_x\operatorname{TV}(Q_x,P_{p,r_k})\le R_{{\rm conf},p},\qquad
\sum_y\tau_y\operatorname{TV}(W_y,P_{\beta,r_k})\le R_{{\rm conf},\beta}.
\tag{2.3}
$$

The law-order counterparts also hold. Positive-row labels are reachable original configurations; their laws are their exact original decoders pulled back through their own $I_c$. Deleting zero-row labels preserves both kernels and every retained law, since (2.2) prohibits transitions from positive-row labels to zero-row labels. Transient original configurations are not deleted from the original history domain.

The correspondence does not change source, operation, loss or resource semantics. For completeness, its ordinary justification is as follows. At the paid seed-pair boundary, let $R_\alpha,R_\beta$ be the finite updates for rejected equal pairs. Choose periods $d_\alpha,d_\beta$ for which $R_\alpha^{d_\alpha n}$ and $R_\beta^{d_\beta n}$ converge. For each supported $r=r_k$, use histories $(\alpha\alpha)^{m_n}(\beta\beta)^{t_n}S$, with

$$
m_n=d_\alpha\left\lfloor nr/d_\alpha\right\rfloor,\qquad
t_n=d_\beta\left\lfloor n(1-r)/d_\beta\right\rfloor.
$$

Writing $E_\alpha,E_\beta$ for those two matrix limits, $\eta_0$ for the initial row and $P_S$ for the fixed acquired suffix update, their observer rows converge to the one source-independent row $\zeta=\eta_0E_\alpha E_\beta P_S$. Their likelihood ratio at any competing depth is the fixed positive suffix ratio times $\exp[-2n\operatorname{KL}(\operatorname{Ber}(r)\Vert\operatorname{Ber}(r_i))+O(1)]$. The rounding term is uniformly bounded on $I$; so is the suffix ratio, since its likelihood is $r^3(1-r)^3$. The source parameters are distinct: $r_{k+1}=(1-r_k)/(2-r_k)$ is a strict contraction on $I$ with only the irrational fixed point $(3-\sqrt5)/2$, so its rational orbit cannot repeat. Multiplication by $\mu(i)/\mu(k)$ therefore gives a summable dominating sequence and pointwise convergence to zero for every $i\ne k$. This proves posterior concentration for countable as well as finite priors.

Appending a fixed number $j$ of acquired returns, with or without a further beta, preserves that domination. The limiting rows are $\zeta(BA)^j$ and $\zeta(BA)^jB$, with the pure supported targets. Configuration loss is linear in the finite row and 1-Lipschitz in the target; law loss is continuous in both. Cesaro averaging gives (2.2)–(2.3) on one row for every supported depth. If a stationary coordinate is positive, some finite-return limiting coordinate is positive, and a sufficiently late finite positive-history approximant is positive there too. This proves reachability. No limiting row or posterior is installed in the observer, and no cost or hard defect budget is preserved by this analysis extraction.

**Definition 2.2 (Regular table risks).** A regular table satisfies (1.5), (2.2), positive retained rows and $u_x,v_y\in[a,b]$. Every synthetic return has probability at most $4/15$, so the complete laws are normalized, retain their legal infinite outcomes with mass zero, and require no conditioning on eventual completion. Set

$$
\bar Q=\pi Q,\qquad \bar W=\tau W,
$$

$$
\mathcal R_p=\sup_{k:\mu(k)>0}\sum_x\pi_x\operatorname{TV}(Q_x,P_{p,r_k}),\qquad
\mathcal R_\beta^{\rm law}=\sup_{k:\mu(k)>0}\operatorname{TV}(\bar W,P_{\beta,r_k}),
$$

$$
e=\max\{\mathcal R_p-\rho_p,\mathcal R_\beta^{\rm law}-\rho_\beta\}\ge0.
\tag{2.4}
$$

Nonnegativity follows already from the two endpoint targets and the triangle inequality. Table risks are analysis comparisons, not new source experiments. For extracted tables they are dominated by the corresponding original risks. A stationary source-independent latch realization gives the same fourth-phase risk bounds at all positive original histories by countable target convexity; its own resources must be counted separately [PAID, Lemma 2.1.1].

Use the complete events

$$
E_p=\{w_{0,1},w_{1,1},w_{2,1}\},\qquad
E_\beta=\{\beta,\alpha w_{0,1}\}.
$$

Their native masses are

$$
f(r)=(1-r)^2(1+\lambda_r+\lambda_r^2),\qquad
h(r)=1-r+r(1-r)^2,
$$

$$
f(a)=\frac{412}{729},\quad f(b)=\frac{7299}{15625},\quad
h(a)=\frac{22}{27},\quad h(b)=\frac{93}{125}.
\tag{2.5}
$$

These are the supplied endpoint-positive difference events [ST, Section 2.2]. Indeed, the second-to-first endpoint ratios for $w_{j,0}$ and $w_{j,1}$ are $(6/5)(27/25)^j$ and $(81/100)(27/25)^j$; only the latter, for $j=0,1,2$, is below one. At suspension the ratio for beta is $9/10$, that for $\alpha w_{j,0}$ is $(36/25)(27/25)^j$, and that for $\alpha w_{j,1}$ is $(243/250)(27/25)^j$, below one only for $j=0$. Since both laws are normalized, the two positive-difference events realize their respective complete endpoint TV distances. Let

$$
C_p=\frac{f(a)+f(b)}2=\frac{11758471}{22781250},\qquad
C_\beta=\frac{h(a)+h(b)}2=\frac{5261}{6750},
\qquad \kappa=\frac{h(a)-h(b)}{f(a)-f(b)}=\frac{806625}{1116529}<\frac34.
\tag{2.6}
$$

Endpoint risks bounded by (2.4), together with convexity at p, imply

$$
|\bar Q(E_p)-C_p|\le e,\qquad
|\bar W(E_\beta)-C_\beta|\le e.
\tag{2.7}
$$

Indeed, projection to an event realizing an endpoint distance $2\rho$ places its predicted mass between the endpoint midpoint minus $e$ and that midpoint plus $e$. No individual configuration is assumed to be a minimax center.

## 3. Native acquired residuals force classwise pure mean laws

**Lemma 3.1 (Residual rigidity on the actual return classes).** In a regular table with native acquired residuals, each closed irreducible class of the actual p-return kernel $C=BA$ has one parameter $r\in I$ such that every associated suspended residual is $J_y=P_{p,r}$ and the class-conditional p mean law is $P_{p,r}$.

Proof. A positive stationary finite row decomposes into closed irreducible classes. One way to see the required closedness is to order the strongly connected components of the finite transition graph: stationarity forces zero flow across the outward cut of a component with no incoming flow, and induction removes all intercomponent edges on positive support. Fix one class $X_0$. Let $Y_0$ be its positive $B$ successors. Closedness of $BA$ forces every $A$ row from $Y_0$ back into $X_0$. Distinct closed p classes cannot share a suspended successor, since its stochastic $A$ row would then have to be supported on two disjoint classes. The kernel $AB$ on $Y_0$ is irreducible: take an $A$ step into $X_0$, a $BA$ path within $X_0$, and a final $B$ step to any specified $Y_0$ label.

Restrict all matrices to these classes and put

$$
U=\operatorname{diag}(1-u),\qquad V=\operatorname{diag}(v),\qquad
H=UBVA,\qquad T=AUBV.
\tag{3.1}
$$

$BA$ is the actual p-return matrix. $H$ is its synthetic survival matrix. $T$ describes the same synthetic survival viewed from the actual alpha-successor averages; it is not an acquired transition matrix. The positive-entry graph of $T$ is exactly that of $AB$, since $1-u_x>0$ and $v_y>0$.

From exact individual generation,

$$
Q_x(w_{j,0})=(H^ju)_x,\qquad
J_y(w_{j,0})=(T^jAu)_y.
\tag{3.2}
$$

The second identity follows from $AH^j=T^jA$. Let $p_T$ be the characteristic polynomial of $T$. Cayley–Hamilton gives, for every $j\ge0$ and every native representation $\nu_y$ of $J_y$,

$$
0=\int_I r\lambda_r^j p_T(\lambda_r)\,\nu_y(dr).
$$

Taking the linear combination whose coefficients are those of $p_T$ gives $\int_I r p_T(\lambda_r)^2\nu_y(dr)=0$. Thus all these measures are supported on the same finite set of roots. The map $r\mapsto\lambda_r$ is strictly increasing on $I$, so that set has at most $|Y_0|$ members. Let $c_r(y)$ be their nonnegative probability-weight columns.

Applying $T$ to (3.2) and comparing successive $j$ gives

$$
\sum_r r\lambda_r^j(Tc_r-\lambda_rc_r)=0\quad(j\ge0).
$$

The Vandermonde matrix at distinct $\lambda_r$ is invertible, hence $Tc_r=\lambda_rc_r$ for each root. A nonzero nonnegative eigenvector of the irreducible $T$ is strictly positive: a zero coordinate and $T^nc_r=\lambda_r^nc_r$ would force every reachable coordinate to be zero. Two strictly positive eigenvectors cannot have different eigenvalues. At a maximum of their coordinate ratio the eigenvalue inequality is one way, and at a minimum it is the reverse. Since $r\mapsto\lambda_r$ is injective, only one parameter remains. Row normalization makes its weight column identically one, giving $J_y=P_{p,r}$ for all $y\in Y_0$.

Normalize the stationary rows within this class to $\pi^0,\tau^0$. They retain $\pi^0B=\tau^0$ and $\tau^0A=\pi^0$, so

$$
\sum_x\pi_x^0Q_x=\sum_y\tau_y^0J_y=P_{p,r}.
\tag{3.3}
$$

This proves the lemma. It does not assert $Q_x=P_{p,r}$ individually. The recurrence and positive-eigenvector method is mature and is used in [NATIVE, Lemma 4.2 and Theorem 5.1] for a different hypothesis, native individual p laws. Here it is applied to the actual residual operator $T$ and establishes (3.3) without that hypothesis. $\square$

## 4. The joint calibration inequality

**Theorem 4.1 (One acquired-flow constraint on both complete event masses).** Under Lemma 3.1, index the actual closed classes by $c$, with stationary weights $\omega_c$ and parameters $r_c$. Then

$$
\bar W(E_\beta)
\ge h(b)+\kappa\bigl(\bar Q(E_p)-f(b)\bigr)
+\frac1{10}\sum_c\omega_c(r_c-a)(b-r_c).
\tag{4.1}
$$

In particular simultaneous exact endpoint event calibration forces every $r_c$ to be an endpoint. The assertion permits nonconstant, genuinely interior suspended emissions and arbitrary finite shape. All coordinates in (4.1) belong to the same table and the same unweighted acquired circulation.

Proof. Fix a normalized class and abbreviate its parameter by $r$. Put $s=\tau^0v$. Equation (3.3) gives $\pi^0u=r$. Its complete return-cylinder mass is $r(1-r)$, while exact generation gives that same mass as $\pi^0UBV\mathbf1$. On the **unweighted actual edge law** $\pi_x^0B_{xy}$, therefore,

$$
\mathbb E u=r,\qquad \mathbb E v=s,\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(u,v)=(1-r)(s-r).
\tag{4.2}
$$

Because $(u-a)(b-v)\ge0$, its expectation gives

$$
\operatorname{Cov}(u,v)\le(r-a)(b-s),\qquad
s\le s_+(r):=r+\frac{(r-a)(b-r)}{1-a}.
\tag{4.3}
$$

This bound uses the actual acquired marginals, not the emission-tilted row. Each $J_y=P_{p,r}$, so the class mean suspended event mass is $1-s+s(1-r)^2$. Hence it is at least

$$
h_-(r):=1-s_+(r)r(2-r).
\tag{4.4}
$$

The needed source-specific polynomial comparison is

$$
h_-(r)-h(b)-\kappa(f(r)-f(b))
=(r-a)(b-r)\,q\!\left(\frac{r-a}{b-a}\right),
\tag{4.5}
$$

where, in the degree-four Bernstein basis,

$$
q(t)=\sum_{j=0}^4 b_j\binom4j t^j(1-t)^{4-j},\qquad
(b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4)=
\left(
\frac{1479745}{6699174},
\frac{13024307}{66991740},
\frac{56604893}{334958700},
\frac{16134223}{111652900},
\frac{3373749}{27913225}
\right).
\tag{4.6}
$$

For direct verification, the power-basis coefficients of $q$ are

$$
\left(
\frac{1479745}{6699174},
-\frac{1773143}{16747935},
\frac{12037}{1925050},
-\frac{239}{577515},
\frac{239}{16747935}
\right).
$$

Substitution of $r=1/3+t/15$ in the two sides of (4.5) and multiplication by their positive denominators verifies the polynomial identity. Every coefficient in (4.6) exceeds $1/10$; their differences from $1/10$ are, respectively,

$$
\frac{2024569}{16747935},\quad
\frac{6325133}{66991740},\quad
\frac{23109023}{334958700},\quad
\frac{4968933}{111652900},\quad
\frac{1164853}{55826450}.
$$

The Bernstein basis is nonnegative and sums to one on $[0,1]$. Thus $q(t)>1/10$ there. Combining (4.3)–(4.6), then summing over the actual class weights, proves (4.1), since $\bar Q(E_p)=\sum_c\omega_cf(r_c)$. Exact calibration and $C_\beta=h(b)+\kappa(C_p-f(b))$ force the nonnegative last sum to vanish. Every positive-weight class parameter is then $a$ or $b$. $\square$

The covariance identity alone is elementary. The new constraint is its composition with residual rigidity and the evaluated polynomial (4.5) for these complete source words. It cannot be obtained by setting one scalar suspended emission in a stationary product formula: $v$ may vary, and $UBVA$ remains the weighted matrix throughout.

## 5. A uniform regular-class gap and the original consequence

Set

$$
\delta_p=2\rho_p=\frac{1116529}{11390625},\qquad
\eta=\frac{14219478376}{318644812890625}>0.
\tag{5.1}
$$

For every original supported $r_0\in[3/8,5/13]$, the supplied complete word $w_{3,1}$ has mass $r_0^3(1-r_0)^5\ge b^3(1-b)^5+\eta$. This also follows directly: the derivative has sign $3-8r$, so its minimum on that interval is its value at $5/13$, and subtraction at $b$ gives (5.1). The value at $a$ is smaller than the value at $b$. The countable coordinate triangle identity therefore gives

$$
\operatorname{TV}(P_{p,a},P_{p,r_0})+
\operatorname{TV}(P_{p,b},P_{p,r_0})\ge\delta_p+\eta.
\tag{5.2}
$$

This source protrusion is supplied mathematics [NATIVE, Section 6; INTERIOR] and is not counted as new content.

**Theorem 5.1 (Evaluated residual-native regular-class gap).** If a regular table has native acquired residuals and the installed prior has a supported nonendpoint, then the p-conf/suspended-law table excess of (2.4) satisfies

$$
e\ge\frac{\eta}{9456}>\frac{\eta}{10000}.
\tag{5.3}
$$

The same bound holds for the larger excess obtained by replacing suspended-law with suspended-conf. It is uniform over all finite label counts, arbitrary stochastic entries and all allowed finite or countable priors satisfying the stated support condition.

Proof. Write $d(r)=(r-a)(b-r)$. From (2.7), (4.1) and $\kappa<3/4$,

$$
\sum_c\omega_cd(r_c)\le10(1+\kappa)e\le\frac{35}{2}e.
\tag{5.4}
$$

Round each $r_c$ to its nearest endpoint $\widehat r_c$. Then

$$
|r_c-\widehat r_c|\le\frac{2d(r_c)}{b-a}=30d(r_c).
\tag{5.5}
$$

Native complete p laws satisfy $\operatorname{TV}(P_{p,r},P_{p,t})\le3|r-t|$ on $I$. Couple successive source letters by common uniforms until the first disagreement. The expected number of letters to completion under either native law is at most $2/(1-6/25)=50/19<3$; summing the disagreement probability $|r-t|$ over the still-matching prefixes proves the bound. Infinite outcomes remain in the carrier and have mass zero. Thus

$$
L:=\sum_c\omega_c\operatorname{TV}(P_{p,r_c},P_{p,\widehat r_c})
\le1575e.
\tag{5.6}
$$

Classwise convexity and (3.3), for every supported $t$, give

$$
\sum_c\omega_c\operatorname{TV}(P_{p,r_c},P_{p,t})\le\mathcal R_p.
\tag{5.7}
$$

Let $\alpha$ be the total rounded weight at $a$ and $\beta=1-\alpha$ that at $b$. Apply (5.6)–(5.7) at both endpoints. Since the endpoint distance is $\delta_p$,

$$
\alpha,\beta\ge\frac12-\frac{e+L}{\delta_p}
\ge\frac12-\frac{1576e}{\delta_p}.
\tag{5.8}
$$

Choose any supported nonendpoint $r_0$. Equations (5.2), (5.6)–(5.8) imply

$$
\mathcal R_p
\ge\left(\frac12-\frac{1576e}{\delta_p}\right)(\delta_p+\eta)-1575e.
\tag{5.9}
$$

If the bracket is nonnegative, multiply (5.2) by it and use the two weight bounds. If it is negative, the rounded risk is nonnegative and hence still at least that negative right-hand term. This justifies (5.9) in both cases.

The rational constants satisfy $0<\eta<\delta_p$. Since $\mathcal R_p\le\rho_p+e$ and $\rho_p=\delta_p/2$, (5.9) gives

$$
e\ge\eta/2-3152e-1575e,\qquad
4728e\ge\eta/2.
$$

This proves (5.3). The rounding and coupling estimates are only proof steps; the new exclusion is of the residual-native class, rather than a new oscillation neighborhood or a coefficient revision of a supplied class. $\square$

Proof of Theorem 1.3. Apply the exact original-domain common row of Citation 2.1. For any probability law $D$ and two endpoint laws $P_a,P_b$ on the complete countable carrier,

$$
\operatorname{TV}(D,P_a)+\operatorname{TV}(D,P_b)
-\operatorname{TV}(P_a,P_b)
=\sum_w\operatorname{dist}\bigl(D(w),[P_a(w)\wedge P_b(w),P_a(w)\vee P_b(w)]\bigr).
\tag{5.10}
$$

Apply (5.10) configuration by configuration and average on the same stationary row. Each endpoint configuration loss is at most its exact radius by (1.7), (2.3). Every nonnegative coordinate slack on positive support is therefore zero, at both phases. In particular, $Q_x(\alpha)=u_x\in[a,b]$ and $W_y(\beta)=1-v_y\in[1-b,1-a]$. The original extracted support is consequently regular, without changing any emission, kernel or decoder. Its original noncompletion mass is also zero by the zero endpoint coordinate.

If every retained $J_y$ were native, Theorem 5.1 would give positive excess, whereas (2.3) and convexity give $e=0$. This contradiction proves (1.8). Reachability is part of Citation 2.1. All original positive histories, transient states and possible zero/unit emissions outside this stationary support remain in the original domain; the argument uses an inclusion of reachable states, not a replacement of that domain. $\square$

**Corollary 5.2 (Explicitly restricted vanishing-sequence statement).** Suppose a sequence of original observers has all reachable p and suspended emissions on the designated fibre in $[a,b]$, and all its reachable suspended alpha-successor averages there are native. Proof. Extract the unchanged common support of each observer. The two hypotheses pass to its reachable positive-row subset, and its table risks are dominated by the original configuration risks. Apply Theorem 5.1. $\square$

## 6. The residual hypothesis is strictly different from native individual laws

**Example 6.1 (A rational nonconstant-emission residual-native table).** This example establishes a predicate distinction, not a near-optimal witness. Take two disjoint two-label blocks, with parameters $r=9/25$ and $r=19/50$. Inside a block use p and suspended labels $0,1$, put $B=I$, and give every $A$ row the probability vector

$$
\pi^r=\left(\frac{b-r}{b-a},\frac{r-a}{b-a}\right).
$$

Set $u_0=a,u_1=b$ and

$$
d=(r-a)(b-r),\qquad
v_i=r+\frac{d}{10(1-r)}+\frac{u_i-r}{10}.
\tag{6.1}
$$

Use block weights $1/2,1/2$ and retain the block forever. The four suspended emissions, in block order, are

$$
\frac{143}{400},\quad \frac{437}{1200},\quad
\frac{291}{775},\quad \frac{1777}{4650}.
$$

They are all strictly between $a,b$ and are not constant. Globally $B=I$ is not a redraw, and $A$ is a block-diagonal redraw within each class, rather than one row-independent kernel on the whole support.

The actual stationary rows are $\pi=\tau=(3/10,1/5,3/20,7/20)$; (2.2) holds exactly. In each block, $\mathbb E u=r$, $\operatorname{Var}(u)=d$ and

$$
\mathbb E(1-u)v=r(1-r),\qquad
\mathbb E(1-u)(1-v)=(1-r)^2.
$$

Since every $A$ row is $\pi^r$, the class mean generated law solves

$$
\bar Q^r=r\delta_\alpha+(1-r)^2\delta_{\beta\beta}
+r(1-r)\beta\alpha\bar Q^r.
$$

Expansion and geometric normalization identify its entire law as $P_{p,r}$. Consequently every $J_y$ in that class is $P_{p,r}$: all acquired residuals are native.
