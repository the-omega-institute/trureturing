# AURIC FIB actual-word purity and restricted arithmetic

## 45. Quaternion coordinates for the actual chronological word language

**Definition 45.1 (Inherited recovery problem and arithmetic order).**
Continue [AURIC FIB approximate online recovery memory](AURIC_FIB_APPROXIMATE_ONLINE_RECOVERY_MEMORY.md),
whose Definitions 2.1–2.4, 18.1 and 35.1 fix the actual instrument,
independent standard-Borel seeds, complete causal peak width, arbitrary
terminal full-$H$ CPTP decoder, unhalved all-reference error,
$\varepsilon_{\rm op}$ and $\eta_n$. Its $F_h$, $\ell(h,g)$ and
$d_n$ retain their phase-resolved meanings from §§33 and 38–44.
Section numbers continue that volume. All assertions here are ordinary
mathematics in this model; the analysis carriers are not additional
physical registers.

Use quaternion units $I_q,J_q,K_q$ with
$I_qJ_q=K_q$, $I_q^2=J_q^2=K_q^2=-1$ and complex representation

$$
\rho(I_q)=\begin{pmatrix}i&0\\0&-i\end{pmatrix},\quad
\rho(J_q)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
\rho(K_q)=\begin{pmatrix}0&i\\i&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Put $u=K_q$, $v=\sqrt3 I_q$ and work in the rational quaternion
algebra $D=\mathbb Q\langle u,v\rangle$ with
$u^2=-1$, $v^2=-3$, $uv=-vu$. Define

$$
\zeta=(1+v)/2,\quad \mathbb E=\mathbb Z[\zeta],\quad
\mathcal O=\mathbb E+\mathbb Eu,\quad \delta=(1+\zeta)u,\quad
H=\zeta+\delta,\quad H'=\zeta-\delta,\quad
a=H/2,\quad a'=H'/2.
\tag{45.1}
$$

Conjugation on $\mathbb E$ sends $\zeta$ to $1-\zeta$ and its norm is
$N_E(x+y\zeta)=x^2+xy+y^2$. Quaternion conjugation gives
$N(\alpha+\beta u)=N_E(\alpha)+N_E(\beta)$.

**Theorem 45.2 (Exact source letters, signs and parity lifts).** In the
source orthonormal basis $(f_1,f_2)$, the actual letters satisfy
$g_0=(-i)\rho(a)$, $g_1=\rho(aI_q)$, and
$I_qaI_q^{-1}=a'$. For chronological $h=(h_1,\ldots,h_n)$ set
$b_k=h_{n+1-k}$, $\sigma_k=b_1+\cdots+b_k\pmod2$, and $p=\sigma_n$.
Then

$$
G_h=(-i)^{n-p}\rho(c_hI_q^p),\quad
c_h=a\,a_{\sigma_1}\cdots a_{\sigma_{n-1}},\quad
C_h=2^nc_h=H H_{\sigma_1}\cdots H_{\sigma_{n-1}},
\tag{45.2}
$$

where $a_0=a$, $a_1=a'$, $H_0=H$, $H_1=H'$. For each fixed $p$
the $2^{n-1}$ actual histories correspond bijectively to all ordered
tails of length $n-1$ in $H,H'$, each retaining probability $2^{-n}$.
The two parity lifts together give all $2^n$ histories. At $n=1$ the
tail is empty.

Proof. The quaternion
$a=(1+\sqrt3I_q-\sqrt3J_q+3K_q)/4$ substituted into $\rho$ gives
exactly the matrix $g_0$ in source (2.1) after multiplication by $-i$.
Also $J=i\rho(I_q)$, so $g_1=g_0J=\rho(aI_q)$.
Conjugating by $I_q$ fixes $\zeta$ and changes $u$ to $-u$, proving
the assertion about $a'$. The product is ordered from left to right
by the reversed bits $b_k$. Pushing the $I_q$ factors to the right
therefore uses precisely the displayed prefix parities of these reversed
bits. If $s_1$ is the integer number of one bits, the original scalar
$(-i)^{n-s_1}$ combines with
$I_q^{s_1}=(-1)^{(s_1-p)/2}I_q^p$ to give $(-i)^{n-p}I_q^p$.
Thus the signs from $I_q^2=-1$ are included. Conversely set
$\sigma_0=0$, $\sigma_n=p$ and
$b_k=\sigma_{k-1}\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}\sigma_k$, then reverse.
This proves bijection, chronology and the probability statement. Only
the common same-horizon factor $\lambda^{-n}$ cancels in
$F_g^\dagger F_h$; the parity-dependent factor in (45.2) is retained.
$\square$

**Proposition 45.3 (The actual thin tree law).** In the projective
quaternion group put
$R_0=(-1+uv)/2$, $D_0=-\zeta$, and $j=[v]$.
Then $R_0^3=D_0^3=1$, $a=R_0D_0$, $a'=R_0^{-1}D_0$.
The subgroup generated by $R_0,D_0$ is $C_3*C_3$ and its tree is the
$3$-regular tree. Relative to the vertex $x$ fixed by $D_0$, the actual
length-$n$ law occupies exactly $2^{n-1}$ vertices at distance $2n$,
each with probability $2^{1-n}$. The full sphere there has
$3\cdot2^{2n-1}$ vertices. Thus the actual support is the fraction
$1/(3\cdot2^n)$ of that sphere, and is not its uniform shell law.

Proof. All algebraic identities follow directly from (45.1).
For the tree assertion use the tree of homothety classes of
$\mathbb Z_2$-lattices in $\mathbb Q_2^2$, adjacent when representatives
have index two; each vertex has three neighbors. Choose
$z\in\mathbb Z_2$ with $z^2=-7$, $z\equiv1\pmod4$, and represent

$$
u=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
v=\begin{pmatrix}z&2\\2&-z\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{45.3}
$$

These matrices satisfy the required relations. The reductions of
$1,\zeta,u,\zeta u$ are a basis of $M_2(\mathbb F_2)$, so the order
acts integrally at the standard vertex $x$. The reduction of $D_0$
has order three and cycles its three neighbors. The integral $2R_0$
has determinant four and nonzero rank-one reduction $I+u$, hence
elementary valuations $(0,2)$ and $d(x,R_0x)=2$. The orbit
$x,R_0x,R_0^2x$ has a central vertex $y$ adjacent to $x$, and $R_0$
cycles the three neighbors of $y$. The development of the edge $(x,y)$
under the abstract free product $C_3*C_3$ is a tree; its map to the
lattice tree is bijective on every neighbor set. It is therefore a
covering between trees, hence an isomorphism. The projective lattice
action is faithful, giving the stated free product without extra relations.

Conjugation by $j$ fixes $D_0$ and inverts $R_0$; $j$ fixes $x$ and
has projective order two. Its determinant square class is three, whereas
the norm-one subgroup has square class one, so it lies outside that
subgroup. In (45.2) each tail is the reduced alternating word
$R_0D_0R_0^{\pm1}D_0\cdots R_0^{\pm1}D_0$, with the first
$R_0$ fixed. The free-product tree gives distinct endpoints at distance
$2n$ for its $2^{n-1}$ choices. The terminal $j^p$ fixes $x$:
the parity lifts give the same vertex and distinct projective elements.
Each history has mass $2^{-n}$, proving the vertex masses. Counting
the full sphere gives the claimed ratio. In particular the actual
relative generator $[a]^{-1}[av]=j$ has order two; it cannot become an
order-three relative generator by simultaneous conjugation or swapping.
Complete nonbacktracking-sector or full-shell estimates therefore require
a separate restricted-language comparison before use here. $\square$

## 46. A ramified ideal and the uniform numerator norm 48

**Theorem 46.1 (All-word ideal separation).** The set $\mathcal O$ is
an order with positive integral multiplicative norm. The subgroup
$\mathfrak p=v\mathcal O=\mathcal Ov$ is a two-sided ideal of additive
index nine, $\mathfrak p^2=3\mathcal O$, and
$\mathcal O/\mathfrak p\simeq\mathbb F_9$. Its minimum positive norm
is three. For every pair of distinct same-parity actual length-$n$
words, when such pairs exist,

$$
N(C_h-C_g)\in48\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad N(C_h-C_g)\ge48.
\tag{46.1}
$$

Proof. The relation $u\alpha=\bar\alpha u$ for $\alpha\in\mathbb E$ gives
$(\alpha+\beta u)(\gamma+\eta u)
=(\alpha\gamma-\beta\bar\eta)+(\alpha\eta+\beta\bar\gamma)u$.
This proves closure. Quaternion conjugation proves norm multiplicativity;
its summands $N_E(x+y\zeta)=(x+y/2)^2+3y^2/4$ are nonnegative
integers, zero only at zero. Thus the minimum positive norm of
$\mathcal O$ is one. Conjugation by $v$ fixes $\mathbb E$ and negates $u$,
normalizing $\mathcal O$ and proving $v\mathcal O=\mathcal Ov$ and
two-sidedness. Multiplication by $v$ on each Eisenstein summand has
matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&-2\\2&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
of determinant three, so its full additive index is nine, not three.
Since $v^2=-3$, the square ideal is $3\mathcal O$.
Reduction modulo $v$ sets $\zeta=-1$ in characteristic three and
gives $\mathbb F_3[u]/(u^2+1)=\mathbb F_9$.
Norm multiplication gives the minimum three in $\mathfrak p$ and
twelve in $2\mathfrak p$.

The identity $v(1-\zeta)=1+\zeta$ gives
$H-H'=2(1+\zeta)u\in2\mathfrak p$, and $N(H)=N(H')=4$.
Telescoping the difference of any two equal-length ordered tails in
$H,H'$ puts that difference in the two-sided ideal $2\mathfrak p$.
The common left factor $H$ in (45.2) then gives
$C_h-C_g=H\Delta$ with $\Delta\in2\mathfrak p$.
Distinctness follows from the source same-length residue proof in
Theorem 5.1 (or Proposition 45.3); hence $\Delta\ne0$.
Writing $\Delta=2vT$, $0\ne T\in\mathcal O$, yields
$N(C_h-C_g)=4\cdot12N(T)\ge48$.
This is an all-length proof, not an extrapolation of finite products,
and does not assert reachability of equality at every length. $\square$

**Proposition 46.2 (Stronger separation and available small balls).** For
distinct same-parity actual words,
$\|F_h-F_g\|_F^2\ge96\,4^{-n}$; opposite parity has squared distance
exactly four. In particular the weaker uniform pair separation
$\|F_h-F_g\|_F\ge2\sqrt2\,2^{-n}$ holds for all distinct words
at $n\ge1$. Define arbitrary-center matrix concentration
$\mathcal B_n(r)=\sup_Z2^{-n}\#\{h:\|F_h-Z\|_F\le r\}$.
For all $n\ge0$, $r\ge0$,

$$
\mathcal B_n(r)\le\min\{1,\max\{2^{-n},\sqrt2r\}\}.
\tag{46.2}
$$

At any actual-word center, radii $0\le r<2\sqrt2\,2^{-n}$ contain
exactly that word. At $r=e^{-\alpha n}$, (46.2) supplies only the
exponent $\min(\alpha,\ln2)$, not $\min(3\alpha,\ln2)$.

Proof. At fixed parity the scalar and right-$I_q$ factors in (45.2)
agree, and $\|\rho(q)\|_F^2=2N(q)$, so (46.1) proves the first
bound. At opposite parity $F_g^\dagger F_h$ has determinant $-1$
and purely imaginary trace, giving squared distance four. For (46.2)
condition on any $n-k$ consecutive end letters. Unitary left or right
multiplication transports the event to an arbitrary-center length-$k$
ball without changing radius or conditional fair weights. If
$2r<2\sqrt2\,2^{-k}$ it contains at most one word, giving mass
at most $2^{-k}$. Choose the largest $k\le n$ with
$r<\sqrt2\,2^{-k}$. If $k<n$, maximality gives
$2^{-k}\le\sqrt2r$; if $k=n$, use $2^{-n}$.
If no positive such $k$ exists, the bound by one suffices. The $n=0$
case also gives one. At word centers the all-word separation gives the
stated strict radius conclusion directly. $\square$

## 47. Exact restricted-tail recurrence and norm-48 rigidity

**Definition 47.1 (Reachable tails and their minimum).** Write
$A=H$, $B=H'$ as tail letters only. Let $\mathcal T_m$ be their actual
ordered products of length $m$, with $\mathcal T_0=\{1\}$.
Since $\delta^2=-3$ and $\delta e=\bar e\delta$ for $e\in\mathbb E$,
write every tail uniquely as $W=E_W+F_W\delta$, $E_W,F_W\in\mathbb E$.
For $m\ge1$ put

$$
\mu_m=\min_{\substack{W,V\in\mathcal T_m\\W\ne V}}
 \frac{N(W-V)}{12}.
\tag{47.1}
$$

**Theorem 47.2 (Reachability recursion and gap congruence).** The exact
state set $\{(E_W,F_W):W\in\mathcal T_m\}$ is generated from $(1,0)$
by $m$ successive choices $\epsilon=\pm1$ in

$$
(E,F)\longmapsto(E\zeta-3\epsilon F,
                     \epsilon E+F\bar\zeta).
\tag{47.2}
$$

It obeys $N_E(E)+3N_E(F)=4^m$. For $m\ge1$ all reachable states
satisfy $E\equiv\zeta\pmod{2\mathbb E}$, $F\equiv1\pmod{2\mathbb E}$,
$E\equiv\zeta^m\pmod{3\mathbb E}$. For distinct equal-length tails write
$E_W-E_V=6X$, $F_W-F_V=2Y$. Then

$$
N(H(W-V))=48(3N_E(X)+N_E(Y)),\qquad
3N_E(X)+N_E(Y)\equiv0\text{ or }1\pmod3.
\tag{47.3}
$$

Thus $\mu_m$ is a positive integer, cannot equal two, and absence of
norm-48 numerator pairs forces numerator norm at least 144.

Proof. Multiply $E+F\delta$ on the right by $\zeta+\epsilon\delta$
to get (47.2); hence this recurrence is exactly the ordered language,
not a relaxation. Norm multiplication proves the conserved norm.
At $m=1$ the state is $(\zeta,\pm1)$. Modulo two the next state
is $(\zeta^2+1,\zeta+\bar\zeta)=(\zeta,1)$; modulo three the
first coordinate is multiplied by $\zeta$. Differences therefore have
the displayed divisibilities. Expanding their norm and multiplying by
$N(H)=4$ gives (47.3). For $x+y\zeta$ the norm is
$(x-y)^2\pmod3$, proving the last congruence and excluding factor two.
Noncollision is supplied by the same actual-word injection as in
Theorem 46.1. The $m=0$ singleton is separate. $\square$

**Theorem 47.3 (Exact criterion and rigidity).** Reverse a tail word
and swap $A\leftrightarrow B$ to obtain $W^\#$. For every $m\ge1$,
norm 48 occurs among $H(W-V)$ for $W,V\in\mathcal T_m$ if and only
if some reachable $F_W$ has $\operatorname{Re}F_W=\pm1$.
Every attaining pair has $V=W^\#$, and
$H(W-V)=\pm2H\delta$. The criterion concerns actual reachable tails.

Proof. The anti-automorphism
$\Phi(E+F\delta)=E-\bar F\delta$ fixes $\zeta$, swaps $A,B$,
and reverses multiplication. Hence $W^\#=E_W-\bar F_W\delta$
is an actual tail and $W-W^\#=2\operatorname{Re}(F_W)\delta$.
Since $N(\delta)=3$, real part $\pm1$ proves sufficiency.

Conversely norm 48 in (47.3) forces $X=0$ and $N_E(Y)=1$.
Thus $E_W=E_V$, $F_W-F_V=2\eta$ for a unit $\eta\in\mathbb E$.
Equality of the two tail norms gives
$\operatorname{Re}(F_W\bar\eta)=1$.
An Eisenstein integer of real part one is $1+kv$ with integer $k$,
so $F_W=\eta(1+kv)$. Because $v\equiv1\pmod{2\mathbb E}$ and
$F_W\equiv1\pmod{2\mathbb E}$, odd $k$ is impossible; even $k$ forces
$\eta\equiv1\pmod{2\mathbb E}$. Of the six units
$\pm1,\pm\zeta,\pm(\zeta-1)$ only $\pm1$ have this residue.
Consequently $\operatorname{Re}F_W=\pm1$ and
$F_V=-\bar F_W$. Equality of $E$ and the injection of actual tails
identify $V=W^\#$. This proves necessity and all pair rigidity,
without assuming any norm-shell point is reachable. $\square$

## 48. From restricted arithmetic minima to the full qutrit gap

**Theorem 48.1 (Exact $\mu$-to-$d$ bridge from horizon three).** For
every $n\ge3$,

$$
d_n=\frac{16\mu_{n-1}}{4^n}\ge\frac{16}{4^n},\qquad
d_n=\frac{16}{4^n}\ \text{or}\ d_n\ge\frac{48}{4^n}.
\tag{48.1}
$$

The endpoints are $d_1=d_2=2/3$. In particular (48.1) starts at
$n=3$, not $n=2$. It strengthens the weaker bound (34.2) in the
preceding volume without asserting equality at every horizon.

Proof. In one parity class let
$z=\operatorname{Tr}(F_g^\dagger F_h)$. Equation (45.2) gives

$$
z=2-\frac{N(C_h-C_g)}{4^n},\qquad
1-\frac{|\operatorname{Tr}(Q_g^\dagger Q_h)|}{3}
=1-\frac{|1+z|}{3}.
\tag{48.2}
$$

For $z\ge-1$ the gap is $N(C_h-C_g)/(3\cdot4^n)$.
For $-2\le z<-1$ it is $(4+z)/3\ge2/3$.
At opposite parity the relative determinant is $-1$, its trace is
$iy$ with real $|y|\le2$, and the full gap is
$1-\sqrt{1+y^2}/3\ge1-\sqrt5/3>1/4$.
At $n=3$ the actual chronological pair $000,101$ has $z=5/4$
and gap $1/4$. Appending the same physical letter left-multiplies
both unitaries and preserves their relative product. Therefore such a
same-parity pair exists at every $n\ge3$, and the minimum same-parity
numerator norm is at most $(3/4)4^n$. Its $z$ is at least $5/4$.
The other trace branches cannot minimize. At fixed parity every actual
tail is available by Theorem 45.2, and its minimum numerator norm is
$4\min N(W-V)=48\mu_{n-1}$. This proves the exact formula and
Theorem 47.2 proves its dichotomy.

At $n=1$ the sole relative matrix is $J$ with trace zero, giving
$d_1=2/3$. At $n=2$ the two same-parity pairs have numerator norm
48, hence $z=-1$ and gap one. Each opposite-parity pair differs at one
position and has relative matrix conjugate to $J$, with trace zero
and gap $2/3$. Thus $d_2=2/3$, while the same-parity expression
$16\mu_1/4^2$ would be one. $\square$

**Corollary 48.2 (Stronger necessary plateau and first-saving bounds).**
For $n\ge3$, $N=2^n$, every successful width-$W$ protocol satisfies

$$
\varepsilon\ge\frac{32\mu_{n-1}}{N^2}(1-W/N)_+,\qquad
W\ge N-\frac{\varepsilon N^3}{32\mu_{n-1}},\qquad
\eta_n\ge\frac{32\mu_{n-1}}{N^3}.
\tag{48.3}
$$

Hence $0\le\varepsilon<32\mu_{n-1}/N^3$ forces the minimum
online and offline widths $W_n(\varepsilon)=M_n(\varepsilon)=N$.
In particular $32/N^3$ is a uniform sufficient plateau threshold for
$n\ge3$, superseding (34.4). This is about minimum feasible widths;
successful padded controllers may have more than $N$ states.

Proof. Substitute Theorem 48.1 into the already established
common-decoder separation (33.4), whose proof also applies to every
offline terminal kernel. Integer widths below $N$ have $N-W\ge1$.
Exact history supplies the width-$N$ upper endpoint. The first-saving
bound follows from Definition 35.1. For $n=1,2$, the separate
$d_n=2/3$ instead gives strict sufficient threshold $4/(3N)$.
No equality or optimality of any positive threshold is inferred.
$\square$

## 49. Finite attainable minima and a norm-shell counterexample

**Proposition 49.1 (Complete length-five certificate and horizon six).**
The exact length-five tail set has the following grouped coordinates.
In a row with two independent signs, all four choices occur; in a row
with one sign, both choices occur.

| $E_W$ | All $F_W$ at this $E_W$ | Number of tails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $-20+17\zeta$ | $\pm15$ | 2 |
| $-20+29\zeta$ | $\pm11$ | 2 |
| $-14+5\zeta$ | $\pm12\pm7v$ | 4 |
| $-14+17\zeta$ | $\pm16\pm v$ | 4 |
| $4-31\zeta$ | $\pm3\pm4v$ | 4 |
| $4+17\zeta$ | $\pm13\pm4v$ | 4 |
| $10-31\zeta$ | $\pm4\pm5v$ | 4 |
| $10+5\zeta$ | $\pm16\pm3v$ | 4 |
| $28-31\zeta$ | $\pm7$ | 2 |
| $28+5\zeta$ | $\pm5$ | 2 |

It follows that $\mu_5=3$ and $d_6=3/256$, refuting the equality
$d_n=16/4^n$ at every $n\ge3$.

Proof. The table is obtained by five applications of (47.2) starting
at $(1,0)$, choosing $+1$ for $A$ and $-1$ for $B$.
To specify the finite calculation completely, each displayed coefficient
$x+y\zeta$ is the integer pair $(x,y)$, multiplication is
$(x,y)(z,t)=(xz-yt,xt+yz+yt)$, and conjugation is
$(x,y)\mapsto(x+y,-y)$. Substituting these rules in (47.2), retaining
both children at every step, gives exactly the ten displayed rows and
their 32 distinct states. No row has real part of $F$ equal to $\pm1$.
Theorem 47.3 excludes numerator norm 48; (47.3) excludes norm 96.
For a realizing pair the recurrence gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
ABAAB&=(-14+17\zeta)+(16-v)\delta,\\
BAABA&=(-14+17\zeta)+(16+v)\delta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{49.1}
$$

Their tail difference is $-2v\delta$ with norm 36, so their
numerator difference after the left factor $H$ has norm 144.
This proves $\mu_5=3$ by a complete lower certificate and a witness,
not by the witness alone. The chronological parity-zero histories
$h=110110$, $g=011011$ have these two tails by (45.2). Their relative
$T$-trace is $503/256$ and the full qutrit trace is $759/256$.
Thus their gap is $1-(759/256)/3=3/256$, and Theorem 48.1 proves
it is the global minimum over both parity classes. The parity-one lifts
$010110,111011$ give the same relative traces. In particular
$\eta_6\ge3/8192$, so the minimum widths at error below $3/8192$
are 64. This remains a necessary first-saving bound. $\square$

**Proposition 49.2 (Necessary norm and congruences do not give reachability).**
The two formal tail points

$$
E_*=28-31\zeta,\qquad F_+=1+4v,\qquad F_-=-1+4v
\tag{49.2}
$$

both satisfy all the norm and congruence conditions in Theorem 47.2
for $m=5$, but neither belongs to $\mathcal T_5$. Their tail difference
has norm twelve and their putative numerator difference has norm 48.

Proof. Direct Eisenstein norms give $N_E(E_*)=877$ and
$N_E(F_\pm)=49$, so $N_E(E_*)+3N_E(F_\pm)=1024=4^5$.
Also $E_*\equiv\zeta\pmod{2\mathbb E}$,
$E_*\equiv\zeta^5\pmod{3\mathbb E}$, and $F_\pm\equiv1\pmod{2\mathbb E}$.
Their $F$ difference is two. At this $E_*$ the complete actual table
in Proposition 49.1 has only $F=\pm7$. The two proposed points are
therefore nonactual despite satisfying the shell conditions. This proves
that a norm shell with those congruences would falsely predict a
norm-48 attainment at $n=6$. $\square$

**Proposition 49.3 (Bounded attainments and the pure-power limitation).**
For $1\le m\le7$ the exact values are

$$
(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_7)=(1,1,1,1,3,1,1).
\tag{49.3}
$$

Together with the two separate endpoints this gives
$(d_1,\ldots,d_8)=(2/3,2/3,1/4,1/16,1/64,3/256,1/1024,1/4096)$.

Proof. Norm 48 witnesses for $m=1,2,3,4,6,7$ are respectively
$(A,B)$, $(AA,BB)$, $(ABA,BAB)$, $(AABA,BABB)$,
$(ABAABA,BABBAB)$, and $(AABBABA,BABAABB)$.
Substitution in (47.2) gives $N(W-V)=12$ for each pair, while
Theorem 46.1 supplies the lower bound. The remaining $m=5$ value is
Proposition 49.1. Theorem 48.1 then gives the listed gaps.
These are bounded statements through $m=7$, not a formula for arbitrary
$m$ or a classification of all attainment lengths.

For comparison the actual pure powers satisfy $H^2-H+4=0$ and
the same relation for $H'$. If $U_0=0$, $U_1=1$ and
$U_{k+1}=U_k-4U_{k-1}$, induction gives, for $k\ge1$,
$H^k=U_kH-4U_{k-1}$ and
$H^k-H'^k=U_k(H-H')$.
The determinant $D_k=U_{k+1}U_{k-1}-U_k^2$ has $D_1=-1$ and
$D_{k+1}=4D_k$, hence $D_k=-4^{k-1}$. These elementary identities
apply to pure powers only and do not determine the mixed-word minima.
$\square$

## 50. The unresolved restricted counting and sharp saving problem

**Definition 50.1 (Remaining arithmetic and operational questions).**
The exact set of lengths $m$ admitting a reachable
$\operatorname{Re}F_W=\pm1$ in (47.2), and the sequence $\mu_m$ at
arbitrary lengths, remain undetermined. Theorem 47.3 is an exact
criterion, not a solution of that reachability problem. A common
physical append preserves relative trace and gap but multiplies the
numerator difference norm by four; it cannot propagate attainment at
the shrinking lower scale $16/4^n$ to every later horizon.

The sharp first-saving error $\eta_n$ also remains undetermined beyond
the stated endpoints and necessary bounds. It depends on optimal
common-decoder geometry, not only closest-pair separation. The
near-full matching comparison retains different indices
$\lceil m/2\rceil$ and $m$, and general smaller-width controllers
retain causal residual compatibility at every layer.

Finally the original joint horizon/positive-error problem in Question
32.2 and Definition 44.3 remains unresolved. Its concrete missing bridge
is a quantitative actual restricted-word counting or causal distortion
estimate at exponential accuracy, with multiplicities, phase and a
single underlying controller law preserved. A full-shell lattice law,
Haar replacement or the necessary norm/congruence conditions do not
supply it, by Propositions 45.3 and 49.2. Finite-support walk laws are
also singular to nonatomic Haar measure at every finite time: for a
finite support $S$ choose $y$ outside the finite set $S^{-1}Sx$;
the translated supports $Sx,Sy$ are disjoint. Thus their uniform
Dobrushin overlap is zero, and an $L^2$ spectral gap alone does not
give a positive uniform total-variation overlap for this walk. This
obstructs that hypothesis transfer, not all possible stability methods.

The arithmetic proofs use the explicit source matrices, quaternion
identities, actual same-length injection and the restricted recurrence.
The lattice-tree and finite-dimensional recovery tools are suppliers
inside these model-specific deductions. All arithmetic assertions above follow from the displayed generators
and recurrences. Their scope is the declared finite-dimensional model;
they give neither physical realization, a full joint memory law nor a
sharp first-saving asymptotic.
