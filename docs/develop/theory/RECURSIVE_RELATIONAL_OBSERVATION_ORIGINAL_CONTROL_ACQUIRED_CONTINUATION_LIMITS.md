## 5. General affine survival deficiency and its scalar sharpness

**Theorem 5.1 (general affine lower bound).** For every $c,Z$ as above,
$d,L>0$ and $0<a<b<c-d$, every common specimen-only processor has
worst complete-output error on
$x\in[a,b]$, $\beta\in\{0,\pi\}$, $m\in\{0,1\}$ at least

$$
E_{\rm aff}(a,b,d)=
\frac{d\bigl(\sqrt{b+d}-\sqrt{a+d}\bigr)^2}{2(a+d)(b+d)}.
\tag{AC.11}
$$

This is exactly the best uniform approximation error of
$F(x)=x/(x+d)$ by unrestricted real affine functions on $[a,b]$.
That scalar statement does not assert an attaining complete instrument,
or even physical attainability of every unrestricted affine function.
The twelve histories at heights $a,b,x_*=\sqrt{(a+d)(b+d)}-d$ already
force the lower bound.

Proof. The completed-live output event pulls back under $\Phi$ to an
effect $0\leq E\leq I_V$. Independent fixed inputs are absorbed into
this effect. By (AC.7),
$Q(x)=\operatorname{tr}(E\bar\rho_x)$ is affine. If every individual
complete-output error is at most $\epsilon$, testing this event and
averaging gives $|Q(x)-F(x)|\leq\epsilon$ at each height in scope.
All acquisition aborts and failures contribute to this unconditional
effect; a newly normalized accepted subfamily would not obey this argument.

Put $A=a+d$, $B=b+d$, and let $\ell$ be the chord of $F$. Since
$F'=d/(x+d)^2$ and $F''=-2d/(x+d)^3<0$, its chord has slope $d/(AB)$
and its largest gap occurs at $x_*=\sqrt{AB}-d\in(a,b)$. Direct
substitution gives

$$
g=F(x_*)-\ell(x_*)=
\frac{d(\sqrt B-\sqrt A)^2}{AB}.
\tag{AC.12}
$$

Write $x_*=\lambda a+(1-\lambda)b$, $0<\lambda<1$.
Affineness of $Q$ and $\ell$ gives
$g=(F-Q)(x_*)+\lambda(Q-F)(a)+(1-\lambda)(Q-F)(b)\leq2\epsilon$.
This proves the bound using three heights. Conversely $0\leq F-\ell\leq g$
on the interval, so $\ell+g/2$ has uniform error $g/2$. The three-height
lower proof applies to every affine approximant, proving scalar sharpness.
$\square$

## 6. Exact full-output mixture discrepancy and rational experiment bound

**Theorem 6.1 (complete marked-output defect).** Choose $0<h<a$ and
$a+h+d<c$. For every $L>0$ define, for $u\geq0$,

$$
J(u)=\frac{u h^2}{(a+u)((a+u)^2-h^2)},\qquad
D(a,h,d)=\max_{0\leq u\leq d}J(u).
\tag{AC.15}
$$

Then the actual input mixtures satisfy
$\bar\rho_a=(\bar\rho_{a-h}+\bar\rho_{a+h})/2$, whereas the prescribed
complete output mixtures satisfy exactly

$$
\operatorname{dist}\left(\bar\Omega_a,
 \tfrac12(\bar\Omega_{a-h}+\bar\Omega_{a+h})\right)=D(a,h,d)>0.
\tag{AC.16}
$$

Consequently every common specimen-only processor has worst complete-output
error at least $D(a,h,d)/2$ on these twelve actual histories. The equality
in (AC.16) computes the entire ideal output defect, including the quantum
live block and marked exact-time densities. It does not compute the optimal
full-instrument simulator.

Proof. The input equality is (AC.7). At displacement $u=vt$, elementary
fraction subtraction gives

$$
S_a(t)-\tfrac12(S_{a-h}(t)+S_{a+h}(t))=J(u).
\tag{AC.17}
$$

In (AC.10) the affine terms cancel, so the terminal live-block difference
is $J(d)(2cP_-+ZP_2)$, positive with trace $J(d)$. Differentiating
(AC.17) shows that the total failure-density difference is $-vJ'(vt)$.
Each marked component is its fixed nonnegative weight from (AC.9).
Since the weights sum to one, the complete half-norm is exactly

$$
\tfrac12\left[J(d)+\int_0^d|J'(u)|\,du\right].
\tag{AC.18}
$$

To evaluate it, put $t=a+u$. The sign of $J'(u)$ is the sign of
$N(t)=-2t^3+3at^2-ah^2$. Here $N(a)=a(a^2-h^2)>0$,
$N'(t)=-6t(t-a)<0$ for $t>a$, and $N(3a/2)=-ah^2<0$.
Thus $J$ increases to one positive maximum and then decreases, with
$J(0)=0$. Its variation on $[0,d]$ is $2D-J(d)$, including the case
where the interval ends before the maximum. This proves (AC.16).

Let $\eta=\Phi(\bar\rho_a)$; by input equality it is also the actual
processed outer mixture. Put
$\bar e_x=\tfrac14\sum_{\beta,m}e_{x,\beta,m}$ for the four nuisance
choices. Convexity and the triangle inequality give

$$
D\leq\bar e_a+\tfrac12(\bar e_{a-h}+\bar e_{a+h})
\leq2\max_{x,\beta,m}e_{x,\beta,m}.
\tag{AC.19}
$$

This uses one common map on the actual mixtures, without postselection or
an additional observed mixture label. $\square$

\tag{AC.22}

## 7. Necessary contribution of an actually acquired joint input

**Theorem 7.1 (joint-assistance inequality).** For the twelve histories
of Theorem 6.1 let $\sigma_{x,\beta,m}$ be any actual normalized complete
accessible joint input on one common algebra. Include jointly the specimen
and all readable source-bearing archives, reference shares, programme,
calibration, clock, controller, selection/update inputs and environments.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_0&=\tfrac14\sum_{\beta,m}\sigma_{a,\beta,m},\\
\Gamma_1&=\tfrac18\sum_{\beta,m}
 (\sigma_{a-h,\beta,m}+\sigma_{a+h,\beta,m}),\\
\kappa&=\tfrac12\|\Gamma_0-\Gamma_1\|_{1,\rm cq}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{AC.25}
$$

The nuisance sums are over $\beta\in\{0,\pi\}$ and $m\in\{0,1\}$.
If one common processor achieves complete-output error at most $\epsilon$
on all twelve source histories, then necessarily

$$
\boxed{\kappa+2\epsilon\geq D(a,h,d).}
\tag{AC.26}
$$

For (AC.20), exact continuation therefore requires $\kappa\geq1/11$.
This is a necessary relation on an actually realized joint state, not
a sufficient observer condition, an acquisition protocol or a memory
dimension bound.

Proof. Contractivity bounds the distance between processed $\Gamma_0$
and $\Gamma_1$ by $\kappa$. Each processed mixture is within

## 8. Sharp survival-bit deficiency for an original return excursion

**Definition 8.1 (two actual source families and the requested bit).** Fix
$c=1/4$, $Z=1/2$, $a=1/16$, $b=c-a=3/16$. Both height families
$\rho_{x,\beta,m}$, $x\in\{a,b\}$, $\beta\in\mathbb R$,
$m\in\mathbb Z$ have the six-unit common-preparation realization of §3.
Their arrival live probabilities are both $2/9$, independent of nuisance
parameters. No height or nuisance label is delivered.

For $0<d<a$ the positive return request is

$$
k_{x,+}(t)=
\begin{cases}x+dt,&0\leq t\leq1,\\x+d(2-t),&1\leq t\leq2,
\end{cases}
\qquad \chi(t)-\chi(0)=0.
\tag{AC.28}
$$

The negative mirror replaces $d$ by $-d$. Both requests are legal at
both heights and return to the same actual height and real phase. The
target survival bit is whether the entire canonical excursion finishes
live. A binary simulator may use any effect $0\leq E\leq I_V$;
its error is the supremum over the stated two full nuisance families of
the absolute survival-probability error. Full-instrument error dominates
this bit error. Attainment of the binary task grants no correct quantum
successor or marked failure archive.

**Proposition 8.2 (actual excursion survival).** Write $t_0=d/a\in(0,1)$.
For the positive excursion the two survival probabilities are

$$
u=\frac{3-t_0}{3(1+t_0)}\quad(x=a),\qquad
v=\frac{3(1-t_0)}{3+t_0}\quad(x=b).
\tag{AC.29}
$$

The negative mirror swaps them. For each fixed actual $x$, the terminal
no-failure operator on the entire live space is $\sqrt{S_{x,\pm}}I$.

Proof. Integrating the credited hazard on the upward and downward unit
legs gives charges $\log((x+d)/x)$ and
$\log((c-x)/(c-x-d))$. Hence

$$
S_{x,+}=\frac{x}{x+d}\frac{c-x-d}{c-x}.
\tag{AC.30}
$$

Substituting $x=a,3a$ gives (AC.29); reflection $x\mapsto c-x$ gives
the negative mirror. End probabilities equal start probabilities and
all transported increments vanish. The diagonal ratio formula (OC.19)
therefore gives $K=\sqrt S I$, also on arbitrary entangled references
when the operation is actually supplied. $\square$

**Theorem 8.3 (exact $23/434$ binary minimax).** Take $d=15/256$, so
$t_0=15/16$ and the common support margin is $a-d=1/256$. For the
positive excursion $u=11/31$, $v=1/21$. The infimum worst-input
survival-bit simulation error over all common specimen-only processors is
exactly

$$
\epsilon_{\rm bit}=\frac{23}{434}.
\tag{AC.31}
$$

Consequently every such simulation of the complete prescribed excursion
has complete-output error at least $23/434$ on some actual input. This
does not assert sharpness for that complete output task.

Proof. The four-history averages at the two heights are

$$
\rho_L=\operatorname{diag}(1/8,3/8,1/2),\qquad
\rho_H=\operatorname{diag}(3/8,1/8,1/2),\qquad
\rho_L\leq3\rho_H.
\tag{AC.32}
$$

For any simulator effect put $U=\operatorname{tr}(E\rho_L)$ and
$V_0=\operatorname{tr}(E\rho_H)$. Positivity gives $U\leq3V_0$.
If the worst pure-input bit error is $\epsilon$, nuisance averaging
gives $u-\epsilon\leq U\leq3V_0\leq3(v+\epsilon)$. Thus

$$
\epsilon\geq(u-3v)/4=23/434.
\tag{AC.33}
$$

For sharpness use the valid effect $E=(524/651)P_-$. On every nuisance
input it gives $U=131/434$ and $V_0=131/1302$. The two absolute errors
are both $23/434$. Complementing the effect makes a complete binary
measurement, proving the exact infimum. It need not preserve any specimen
state. No calibrated implementation of the unknown-height excursion is
thereby constructed. $\square$

## 9. Fixed-acceptance decision frontier for the same unknown height

**Theorem 9.1 (relaxed height-decision frontier).** For the two full
families of Definition 8.1, let a common protocol return $L,H$ or
inconclusive. Require conclusive probability at least $q$ on every input,
where $0<q\leq1$. Its worst-input unnormalized wrong-decision probability
has exact minimum

$$
E(q)=\begin{cases}q/4,&0<q\leq1/2,\\q/2-1/8,&1/2\leq q\leq1.
\end{cases}
\tag{AC.34}
$$

The exact minimum worst-input conditional error, conditioned only on that
input's positive conclusive event, is $E(q)/q$. In particular it never
falls below $1/4$. These optima concern a decision record with unrestricted
disturbance, not an exact live continuation.

Proof. Any such common finite protocol induces three POVM effects on its
original input. Average over the four actual nuisance choices and equal
height priors. Only the effects' diagonals in (AC.32) contribute. The two
bottom directions have total average mass $1/2$ and cheapest wrong
fraction $1/4$; the middle direction has average mass $1/2$ and wrong
fraction $1/2$ for either label. If $s$ is average acceptance and $s_2$
its middle contribution, then $s_2\geq\max(0,s-1/2)$ and average wrong
mass is at least $s/4+s_2/4\geq E(s)$.

Since $s\geq q$ and $E$ is increasing, worst wrong mass is at least
$E(q)$. Worst conditional error is at least the ratio of average wrong
mass to average acceptance: it is a weighted average of the actual
conditional errors, with positive acceptance weights. Since $E(s)/s$
is nondecreasing, that ratio is at least $E(q)/q$.

For $q\leq1/2$ take $E_L=2qP_-$, $E_H=2qP_+$. For $q\geq1/2$
put $w=2q-1$ and take
$E_L=P_-+(w/2)P_2$, $E_H=P_++(w/2)P_2$.
The remaining effect is inconclusive. These effects are positive and sum
to at most identity. On every pure nuisance input they have acceptance
exactly $q$ and wrong mass exactly $E(q)$, proving both attainments.
$\square$

This is a concrete application of mature fixed-inconclusive-rate
discrimination, not a new general discrimination principle. As a guard
consumer, the unit upward ramp of displacement $c/2$ has domain $(0,c/2)$:
it is legal at $a$ and illegal at $b$. An exact total handler would need
an error-free executed/rejected decision. Theorem 9.1 excludes obtaining
that decision with positive uniformly guaranteed acceptance from these
inputs alone. An approximate guess is never permission to execute that
separating request. The excursions of §8 instead lie in a common promised
legal menu and need no unknown-height guard.

## 10. Conditional same-source return records and support margin

**Definition 10.1 (additional source-bound actuator).** Suppose an actual
service executes (AC.28) or its mirror on the same source at its unknown
actual height, using the calibrated canonical instrument. Its source-bound

to be a common channel on $V$ and history-independent blanks. Neither

For clarity its complete two-unit operation is specified, including

$$

K_t=\sqrt{S(t)}\operatorname{diag}_{i=0}^4
 \sqrt{p_i(k(t))/p_i(x)},\\
\gamma_i(t)&=\lambda(t)-(\log p_i(k(t)))',\qquad
J_i(t)=\sqrt{\gamma_i(t)}|f\rangle\langle i|K_t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{AC.35}

**Proposition 10.2 (conditional live-record curve).** Under this additional
service, choose an independent fair direction coin and execute exactly one
excursion. On live return guess $L$ for positive, $H$ for negative. For

\qquad

\frac{9(1-t_0^2)}{2(9-5t_0^2)}.
\tag{AC.36}
$$

\left(\frac{S_{x,+}}2|+\rangle\langle+|
      +\frac{S_{x,-}}2|-\rangle\langle-|\right).
\tag{AC.37}
$$

The sign kets in the last factor label the classical coin, distinct from
the specimen basis. No source label is accessible to the controller.
Conditional on fixed $x$ and a successful sign the entire input state
$\rho_{xR}$ is preserved. The sign is source-correlated; no factorization
of the overall record and reference mixture is asserted.

Proof. Proposition 8.2 gives the exact scalar success map for each actual

(AC.35), with all prior fields retained, so this is a full same-source

For $d=15/256$ the values are

$$
q=131/651,\qquad v/2=1/42,\qquad
\Pr(\text{wrong}\mid\text{live})=31/262<1/4.
\tag{AC.38}
$$

At that acceptance the internal decision-only wrong-mass minimum is

Theorem 8.3 quantifies the failure of internally replacing that service

Writing the margin as $\eta=a-d=a(1-t_0)$ makes the boundary explicit.

## 15. Sharp survival-pair envelope over all common return paths

**Theorem 15.1 (all-return envelope).** For every path in Definition 14.3
put $q_a=e^{-\Lambda_a}$ and $q_b=e^{-\Lambda_b}$, with
$\Lambda_x=\int_0^T\lambda_x$. Define

$$
H=\frac{b-a}{2b},\qquad
f(v)=\frac{(b-a)+2av}{2b-(b-a)v},\qquad 0\leq v\leq1.
\tag{AC.47}
$$

Then

$$
q_a\leq f(q_b),\qquad q_b\leq f(q_a).
\tag{AC.48}
$$

Both upper envelopes are exact: for each $0<v\leq1$ a positive
triangle has $(q_a,q_b)=(f(v),v)$, and its negative mirror has the
swapped pair. At $v=1$ the triangle is the zero-amplitude wait.
The endpoint $v=0$ is only a limit and is not an admissible finite path.

Proof. For $-a<s<a$ set

$$
h_x(s)=\frac{c}{(x+s)(c-x-s)}.
$$

Since $z(0)=z(T)=0$, the AC chain rule gives zero integral for
$z'/(x+z)$ and for $z'/(c-x-z)$. Splitting $z'$ into positive
and negative parts and using these equalities yields

$$
\Lambda_x=\frac12\int_0^T|z'(t)|h_x(z(t))\,dt.
\tag{AC.49}
$$

This closed-path identity holds even with infinitely many reversals.
Let $A=\Lambda_a$, $C=\Lambda_b$ and $d=\max z\in[0,a)$.
For $0\leq t<a$ define

$$
F(t)=\log\frac{(a+t)b}{a(b-t)},\qquad
G(t)=\log\frac{(b+t)a}{b(a-t)}.
\tag{AC.50}
$$

Their derivatives are $h_a(t)$ and $h_b(t)$. The function $G$ is an
increasing bijection from $[0,a)$ onto $[0,\infty)$. The ratio

$$
\begin{aligned}
r(s)&=\frac{h_a(s)}{h_b(s)}
 =\frac{(b+s)(a-s)}{(a+s)(b-s)},\\
(\log r)'(s)&=\frac{2b}{b^2-s^2}-\frac{2a}{a^2-s^2}<0
\qquad(-a<s<a)
\end{aligned}
\tag{AC.51}
$$

is strictly decreasing: after multiplication by the positive
denominators, the sign is that of $(a-b)(ab+s^2)$.
Consequently $g(s)=h_a(s)-r(d)h_b(s)$ is nonnegative on the whole
visited range $s\leq d$.

Put $\Phi(t)=\int_0^t g(s)\,ds$. The AC chain rule and (AC.49)
identify $A-r(d)C$ with half the total variation of $\Phi\circ z$.
Split the time interval at a time when $z=d$. Each of the two parts
has variation at least $\Phi(d)-\Phi(0)$, so

$$
A-r(d)C\geq F(d)-r(d)G(d).
\tag{AC.52}
$$

The same variation argument for a primitive of $h_b$ gives
$C\geq G(d)$. All primitives used here are continuously differentiable
on a compact interval containing the visited range; the AC chain rule
therefore applies without a monotone-piece decomposition.

Define $\mathcal K(C)=F(G^{-1}(C))$ on $[0,\infty)$.
Its derivative is $r(G^{-1}(C))$, which is positive and decreasing.
Thus $\mathcal K$ is increasing and concave. Its tangent at $G(d)$
majorizes it, including the right tangent when $d=0$:

$$
\mathcal K(C)\leq F(d)+r(d)(C-G(d))\leq A.
\tag{AC.53}
$$

Exponentiate. Solving $v=e^{-G(d)}$ and substituting into $e^{-F(d)}$
gives

$$
d(v)=\frac{ab(1-v)}{b+av},\qquad e^{-F(d(v))}=f(v).
\tag{AC.54}
$$

Therefore $q_a\leq f(q_b)$. Reflection $z\mapsto-z$ interchanges
$\Lambda_a$ and $\Lambda_b$, proving the other inequality.

For sharpness use $z(t)=d(v)t$ on $[0,1]$ and
$z(t)=d(v)(2-t)$ on $[1,2]$. Its two charges are exactly
$F(d(v)),G(d(v))$, by the credited monotone survival formula.
For $v>0$, $d(v)<a$, so it is a strict legal original path in both
regularity alternatives. Its mirror swaps the pair. Finally

$$
f(v)-v=\frac{(b-a)(1-v)^2}{2b-(b-a)v}\geq0,
\qquad
a-d(v)=\frac{acv}{b+av}>0.
\tag{AC.55}
$$

Thus the positive triangle's larger likelihood is at $a$, and the
limiting wall $d=a$ is excluded. $\square$

## 16. Wrong-live mass, normalization and finite nonattainment

**Definition 16.1 (classical record-only calibration score).** Only for
this score, give $X=a,b$ equal prior. The old nuisance parameters and
joint states remain arbitrary; return survival does not depend on them.
On every completed live branch a decoder outputs a guess
$\widehat X\in\{a,b\}$ using $U$ and the live flag alone.
Independent decoding randomness can be retained in $U$. There is no
extra specimen measurement, readable archive or silently discarded
completed-live record.

Let $\mathsf L$ mean completion of the entire return while live, and put

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=\Pr(\mathsf L)
  =\frac12\sum_u\nu(u)(q_a(u)+q_b(u)),\\
B_*&=\inf_{\widehat X}\Pr(\mathsf L,\widehat X\ne X)
  =\frac12\sum_u\nu(u)\min\{q_a(u),q_b(u)\},\\
R_*&=B_*/P,\qquad P_0=H/2=\frac{b-a}{4b}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{AC.56}
$$

The decoder chooses the larger unnormalized live prior weight at each
seed, proving the formula for $B_*$. Here $P>0$ automatically.
These quantities are measured from the actually reached live cut.
$B_*$ is an unconditional wrong-live mass, whereas $R_*$ conditions
on $\mathsf L$. Neither is the trace distance of a complete instrument,
and a prior in a score is not an acquired source label.

All failed and rejected sectors retain their actual outputs in
(AC.45) and Definition 14.4. They are not successful guesses, retries
or missing probability. In particular, an extra postselection cannot
redefine $P$ while suppressing its discarded sectors. The theorem below
concerns the commonly permitted executions already specified.

**Theorem 16.2 (universal finite-mixture lower bound and strictness).**
Every finite dictionary in this class satisfies

$$
B_*\geq\max\left\{0,\frac{P-P_0}{2-H}\right\}.
\tag{AC.57}
$$

If $0<P<1$, its $B_*$ is strictly above the right side. At $P=1$
all survival probabilities equal one and $B_*=1/2$.

Proof. At a seed write $M=\max(q_a,q_b)$ and $m=\min(q_a,q_b)$.
Theorem 15.1 gives $M\leq f(m)$. Direct differentiation gives

$$
f'(v)=\frac{c^2}{(2b-(b-a)v)^2},\qquad
f''(v)=\frac{2(b-a)c^2}{(2b-(b-a)v)^3}>0.
\tag{AC.58}
$$

Since $f(0)=H$ and $f(1)=1$, its graph lies below the endpoint chord,
strictly at every $0<v<1$. Hence

$$
M+m\leq H+(2-H)m,
\qquad P\leq P_0+(2-H)B_*.
\tag{AC.59}
$$

The average is over the full original seed law $\nu$, not its
survival-renormalized law. This proves (AC.57).
Every seed has $m>0$ by Proposition 14.5, so $B_*>0$.
If $P<1$, at least one positive-weight seed has $m<1$ and its chord
inequality is strict. Therefore $P<P_0+(2-H)B_*$.
For $P>P_0$ this makes the positive lower bound strict; for
$P\leq P_0$ positivity makes the zero lower bound strict.
If $P=1$, averaging numbers at most one forces every $q_x(u)=1$,
and (AC.56) gives $B_*=1/2$. $\square$

## 17. Explicit legal finite families approaching every infimum

**Theorem 17.1 (sharp infimum at every prescribed live yield).**
For every fixed $0<P\leq1$, the infimum of $B_*$ over finite
public mixtures with yield exactly $P$ is

$$
\inf B_*(P)=\max\left\{0,\frac{P-P_0}{2-H}\right\}.
\tag{AC.60}
$$

It is not attained for $0<P<1$. The approaching families can have
the same completed-live yield $P$ at each actual height and the same
wrong-live mass at each height. Four public seed values suffice; each
live path uses at most three triangles and waits.

Proof. Choose $v_n=2^{-n}$, and set

$$
d_n=\frac{ab(1-v_n)}{b+av_n},\qquad
u_n=f(v_n),\qquad P_n=\frac{u_n+v_n}{2}.
\tag{AC.61}
$$

A fair public direction chooses the positive or negative triangle of
amplitude $d_n$. On a live return guess $a$ for positive and $b$ for
negative. Equation (AC.55) makes this Bayes optimal. At either height
the yield is $P_n$ and the wrong-live mass is $v_n/2$.
Since $f$ is strictly increasing, $P_n$ decreases to $P_0$.
Every finite $d_n<a$.

First suppose $P_0<P\leq1$. For all sufficiently large $n$,
$P_n<P$. Mix a wait with the fair-direction protocol, using

$$
w_n=\frac{P-P_n}{1-P_n}
\tag{AC.62}
$$

as the wait probability. On the wait retain a fair guess. The four
seed values are wait-and-guess-$a$, wait-and-guess-$b$, positive and
negative, with probabilities $w_n/2,w_n/2,(1-w_n)/2,(1-w_n)/2$;
zero-weight values may be removed. Both heights have yield exactly $P$
and wrong-live mass

$$
B_n=\frac{w_n+(1-w_n)v_n}{2}
\longrightarrow\frac{P-P_0}{2(1-P_0)}
 =\frac{P-P_0}{2-H}.
\tag{AC.63}
$$

The equal-likelihood wait guesses are optimal ties. For $P=1$ only
the wait is needed.

Now suppose $0<P\leq P_0$. Following the first fair-direction
triangle, and only while still live, append a positive triangle of
amplitude $e$ and its negative mirror of the same amplitude.
At either height their combined multiplicative survival is

$$
\tau(e)=\frac{(a-e)(b-e)}{(a+e)(b+e)},\qquad 0\leq e<a.
\tag{AC.64}
$$

This follows by multiplying the two monotone-leg factors of each
triangle. The function is continuous, strictly decreasing from one
to zero: its logarithmic derivative in the interior is
$-1/(a-e)-1/(b-e)-1/(a+e)-1/(b+e)<0$.
Consequently there is a unique legal $e_n$ with
$\tau(e_n)=\tau_n:=P/P_n\in(0,1)$. Explicitly,

$$
e_n=
\frac{2ab(1-\tau_n)}
 {c(1+\tau_n)+
  \sqrt{c^2(1+\tau_n)^2-4ab(1-\tau_n)^2}}.
\tag{AC.65}
$$

The discriminant is positive; (AC.65) is the smaller root of
$(1-\tau_n)e^2-c(1+\tau_n)e+ab(1-\tau_n)=0$.
The monotonicity argument proves that this root belongs to $(0,a)$.
The damping pair has equal likelihood at the two heights, so it
does not change the optimal direction guess. Its completed yield is
exactly $P$ at each height, with wrong-live mass

$$
B_n=\frac{P}{P_n}\frac{v_n}{2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{AC.66}
$$

Every failure stops the sequence immediately; the remaining triangles
are then unvisited. Each finite path is a common strict original
AC/PC1 path, and Proposition 14.5 gives all its joint failure outputs.
The lower bound and nonattainment are Theorem 16.2. $\square$

**Corollary 17.2 (worst-height conditional-error infimum).** Add the
requirement
$\Pr(\mathsf L\mid X=a)=\Pr(\mathsf L\mid X=b)=P>0$.
Over this class, allowing any record-only decoder, the infimum of
the worst-height conditional error is

$$
\inf\max_{x\in\{a,b\}}
 \Pr(\widehat X\ne x\mid\mathsf L,X=x)
 =
\max\left\{0,\frac{P-P_0}{(2-H)P}\right\}.
\tag{AC.67}
$$

For $0<P<1$ it is not attained; for $P=1$ a wait with fair retained
guess attains $1/2$.

Proof. For any decoder let $b_x=\Pr(\mathsf L,\widehat X\ne x\mid X=x)$.
Its equal-prior wrong mass is $(b_a+b_b)/2\geq B_*$.
Under the common-yield requirement the average conditional error is
$(b_a+b_b)/(2P)$, so the maximum is at least $B_*/P$.
The two constructions in Theorem 17.1 have $b_a=b_b=B_n$ and
equal yield, proving the infimum. The strict lower bound for finite
mixtures proves nonattainment. Conditioning on a height-specific live
event is legitimate since its probability is $P>0$.
Without the common-yield requirement, $B_*/P$ is the equal-prior
live Bayes error; it is not an asserted sharp worst-height formula.
$\square$

likelihood at both heights. Thus this record cannot certify which height

authorize an exact executed-versus-unexecuted handler for the unit
positive ramp of displacement $c/2$, whose domain $(0,c/2)$ contains
$a$ and excludes $b$.

Proof. The two likelihoods are $\nu(u)q_a(u)>0$ and
$\nu(u)q_b(u)>0$. Any randomized conclusive label with positive
probability at this live seed also occurs under the competing height;
zero-error conclusiveness forces zero probability for both labels.
No finite record produces an exclusive height event. $\square$

The same conclusion survives an unrestricted terminal specimen effect
under the full nuisance family, although such a read is outside the
score's operation class. At each height the four actual vectors with
$(\beta,m)\in\{0,\pi\}\times\{0,1\}$ span $V$: their sums and
differences isolate $|2\rangle$, $|+\rangle$ and $|-\rangle$,
all with nonzero coefficients. At a fixed live seed an error-free
label-$a$ positive effect must have zero expectation on all four
height-$b$ vectors. Positivity makes it annihilate their span $V$,
so it cannot identify height $a$ either; interchange the heights for
the other label. This is the credited positive-effect/support
principle instantiated on the acquired record, rather than a new
general discrimination theorem.

After a live return the actual height, old real phase, signed winding
and conditional joint specimen/reference state are unchanged.
Any separately supplied and permitted matching tail known legal at
both candidate heights can be selected using the actual seed and updates.
Its chronological real lift is
$\chi(T+u)=\chi(T)+\chi_{\rm tail}(u)-\chi_{\rm tail}(0)$;
the old lift is not read. Complete canonical failures retain the
first failure and absorbing successor. A finite-error height guess
never grants permission for the separating ramp. This is a restricted
consumer of the existing lawful menu, not a total-domain guard or
height-selected full observer.
