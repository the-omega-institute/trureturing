The serving table, its original labeled entries and every installed record remain fixed. The domain is the entire finite ordered repeated-partner one-image fiber.

Native objects, complete targets, prefix attainment and stationary path principles retain the ownership of Native cut-birth provenance and literal past-row recovery (NBP), Native prescribed-code acquisition on the original one-image fiber (PCA), and Finite-reader compatibility and lawful binary-code synthesis (FCS). ## 1. Original finite trees and the entire actual fiber

**Definition 1.1 (source, image and native reading).** Let

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal T&::=\alpha\mid\beta\mid\langle\mathcal T,\mathcal T\rangle,\\
\rho\alpha&=\beta,\qquad
\rho\beta=E:=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,\qquad
\rho\langle s,t\rangle=\langle\rho s,\rho t\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\tag{BPS.1}
$$

These are finite nonempty ordered trees with literal brackets and leaf labels. Write $\mathcal W=\{L,R\}^{*}$, including $\varepsilon$, and $\mathcal A=\{\alpha,\beta,\mathsf{branch},\mathsf{absent}\}$. Every finite word is a legal query. The reply $r_t(v)$ is the reached constructor, or absent when an earlier leaf blocks descent. Every nonempty continuation from a leaf is absent. For every reached occurrence $p$,

$$
r_t(pw)=r_{t|_p}(w).
\tag{BPS.2}
$$

Native true/false name alpha/beta, and false/true address letters name $L/R$. The exported labels $0,1$ below keep their original separate meaning. Different occurrences of equal subtrees are not identified.

**Lemma 1.2 (inherited image grammar and finite inverse).** Put $\mathcal I=\rho[\mathcal T]$ and $\mathcal B_I=\mathcal I\setminus\{\beta\}$. The disjoint image grammar and inverse are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I&::=\beta\mid E\mid\langle\mathcal I,\mathcal I\rangle,\\
\delta(\beta)&=\alpha,\qquad\delta(E)=\beta,\\
\delta\langle y,z\rangle&=\langle\delta y,\delta z\rangle
\quad(y,z\in\mathcal I).
\end{aligned}
\tag{BPS.3}
$$

Alpha is not an image; beta is its only leaf; $\mathcal B_I$ consists precisely of branch images. Every subtree of an image is alpha or an image. Both $\mathcal B_I$ and its complement among branch trees are infinite.

Proof. This is the image supplier used in NBP Proposition 9.3 and FCS Proposition 1.4. Each source constructor gives the displayed image case. The cases cannot overlap: alpha is never produced at a root, so $E$, whose right child is alpha, is not a pair of images. Recursion on finite terms proves $\delta\rho=\mathrm{id}$ and $\rho\delta=\mathrm{id}$ on their domains. Thus $\rho$ is injective. The subtree assertion follows inductively from the children $\beta,\alpha$ of $E$ and the two image children of a general image pair. Define

$$
B_0=\beta,\qquad B_{h+1}=\langle B_h,B_h\rangle.
\tag{BPS.4}
$$

For positive $h$ these are distinct branch images, with all leaves at depth $h$. The trees $\langle\alpha,B_h\rangle$ are distinct nonimage branches, since their left child is not an image and they are not $E$. These supply both infinitudes. The inverse constructs mathematical witnesses and is no physical inverse port. $\square$

Every two different blocks assert whole-subtree disequality. All exterior brackets and concrete leaves remain; a blocked cut has its concrete strict-prefix blocking leaf. Order reached holes lexicographically and name each block by the zero-based position of its first hole. Restriction recomputes these names, which need not be consecutive.

## 3. The fixed serving apparatus and paid common-start preparation

The root, cut, origin and version remain fixed, with no reset, copying, rerooting, arbitrary edit, source replacement or later update. Authentication selects no member inside $\mathcal U_*$. The complete current row is lawfully acquired. Its acquired tree, class values, acquisition archive and trace are inaccessible to preparation.

Preparation has lawful pre-cut permission to query that same register, which stays stationary through preparation, handoff and serving. Installation, native service and continuity, row acquisition and representation, authentication, pre-cut permission, actual bit export, complete access isolation, code-only handoff and faithful lawful new entry are supplied and separately paid. An absorbing preparation stop supplies no resume action; serving entry is a separately supplied invocation. Inaccessible material need not be physically erased. There is no equality, image, provenance, inverse, generation or other source port. These are the paid common-start and isolation premises of NBP Assumption 14.1, with PCA's original-entry and FCS's serving-table conventions.

**Definition 3.2 (immutable serving table).** The explicit input $T$ consists of a finite installed set $K_T=Q_T\sqcup H_T$, literal words $a_T:Q_T\to\mathcal W$, all four successors $n_T:Q_T\times\mathcal A\to K_T$, and fixed complete row outputs $o_T:H_T\to\mathcal R_C$. Every installed state counts, including unused Reads, unused Halts and different Halts with equal outputs. Duplicate words, shared states, convergence, cycles and early Halts are allowed. The input gives every record, including words used only from a wrong or divergent entry. Nothing in this volume changes $T$, $e_0,e_1$, any serving literal, any of its four successors, its partition, outputs, duplicates, unused states or cycles.

**Definition 3.3 (bounded SOME-preparer).** A preparer has a nonempty finite installed set $K_P=Q_P\sqcup H_P$, one common source-independent start $s_P\in K_P$, one fixed literal $a_P(q)$ per Read, four fixed successors $n_P(q,r)\in K_P$, and an original bit $o_P(h)\in\{0,1\}$ per Halt. The current state is its entire mutable memory. A Read costs one actual native call, including a repeat, absent query, leaf continuation, forced reply or $\varepsilon$. A Halt replaces the live control by its complete bit latch and makes no call. No independent live code register survives beside it. Distinct Halts bearing the same bit and all unused states still count.

On actual $u$, preparation must halt with bit $\sigma(u)$. The paid handoff exports exactly that bit, hides all preparation material and enters the unchanged serving state $e_{\sigma(u)}$ on the same stationary $t_u$. The bit is recoverable from that complete entry at entry; it is not separately accessible later. For $N,P,L\in\mathbb N_0$, success means

$$
\begin{gathered}
|K_P|\le N,\qquad \sigma[\mathcal U_*]=\{0,1\},\\
\text{preparation halts within }P\text{ calls on every }u,\\
T\text{ from }e_{\sigma(u)}\text{ halts with the entire }F(u)
\text{ within }L\text{ calls on every }u.
\end{gathered}
\tag{BPS.9}
$$

Only the selected entry must succeed on a source. The unselected entry may be wrong, exceed $L$ or diverge. SAME budgets mean these original upper budgets, with no added states, calls or changed serving resources. SOME means no independently prescribed pointwise code must be preserved.

**Lemma 3.4 (stationary paths).** On one fixed source a terminating phase cannot revisit a Read state. Different-source paths may nevertheless have cycles in their union graph.

Proof. The fixed literal at a state has a fixed stationary reply. Returning to that state repeats its entire deterministic future, preventing termination. This fixes the source and says nothing about edges selected by different sources. It is NBP Proposition 12.5 and PCA Lemma 2.3. It requires no unfolding into history states. $\square$

## 4. Exact four-type capacities

**Definition 4.1 (four disjoint types and cap six).** Partition all $\mathcal T$ into

$$
\mathsf A=\{\alpha\},\quad \mathsf H=\{\beta\},\quad
\mathsf I=\mathcal B_I,\quad
\mathsf O=\mathcal T\setminus(\mathsf A\cup\mathsf H\cup\mathsf I).
\tag{BPS.10}
$$

The last type consists of the other branch trees. A pair has type $\mathsf I$ exactly for ordered child-type pairs in

$$
\mathcal C_I=\{(\mathsf H,\mathsf A),
(\mathsf H,\mathsf H),(\mathsf H,\mathsf I),
(\mathsf I,\mathsf H),(\mathsf I,\mathsf I)\}.
\tag{BPS.11}
$$

Every other ordered child-type pair has type $\mathsf O$; a pair is never $\mathsf A$ or $\mathsf H$. Order is literal: $(\mathsf A,\mathsf H)$ is a nonimage case. For any set $X$ define $[X]_6=\min(6,|X|)$, using six for infinite sets. Write $u\oplus v=\min(6,u+v)$ and $u\otimes v=\min(6,uv)$ on $\{0,\ldots,6\}$. Unrestricted type capacities are $(1,1,6,6)$.

**Lemma 4.2 (exact capped sums and products).** Disjoint union and Cartesian product capacities are obtained by $\oplus$ and $\otimes$. Moreover a cell of capacity at least $j\le6$ has $j$ actual different finite witnesses.

Proof. For a disjoint union with total size below six, each summand has its exact finite size; if total size is at least six, selecting six members across the disjoint summands certifies saturation. For a product, a zero factor makes it empty. If both nonempty factors have finite size below six, ordinary multiplication is exact until it reaches six. If one factor already has six different members, pairing them with one member of the other gives six different ordered pairs. These cases prove the formulas and witness assertion, also for singleton and finite cells. Lemma 1.2 gives the unrestricted capacities. $\square$

## 5. One trie for all indexed observations of both apparatuses

**Definition 5.1 (fixed indices and common suffix arrangement).** Set

$$
q_T=|Q_T|,\qquad
b=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{|a_T(q)|-3:q\in Q_T,\ |a_T(q)|\ge3\}\bigr).
\tag{BPS.12}
$$

For an arbitrary preparer literal assignment, successful or otherwise, give every installed Read in either apparatus whose word has length at least three a separate index $i$. This includes duplicate words and unused states. Write the word uniquely as $p_iw_i$ with $|p_i|=3$. Its state identity, phase, occurrence $p_i$ and class role $d_i=[p_i]$ remain fixed data. Let $m$ be the number of indices and $k=|Q_P|$. Then

$$
m\le q_T+k\le q_T+N\quad\text{when }|K_P|\le N.
\tag{BPS.13}
$$

Form one finite prefix trie of all suffixes $w_i$ together, across all class roles. A node carries every index ending at that position. Either child is an explicit trie subtree or the symbol $\#$ when no suffix enters it. A relative empty suffix marks the root. Each index occurs exactly once, even when many indices share a position. The role data are not used to merge indices.

**Definition 5.2 (complete summary of a subtree).** If an explicit subtree has index set $J$, for every $r\in\mathcal A^J$ and type $s\in\{\mathsf A,\mathsf H,\mathsf I,\mathsf O\}$ let

$$
f(s,r)=\bigl[\{y\in s:\text{the replies at all indexed relative words equal }r\}\bigr]_6.
\tag{BPS.14}
$$

The summary includes its identified index set. At $\#$ this set is empty and the summary is $(1,1,6,6)$ for the empty vector. When $m=0$, the whole arrangement is $\#$ with these unrestricted root capacities. An absent child in the trie means unobserved finite subtrees are unrestricted; it does not mean an absent child in an actual branch tree.

**Lemma 5.3 (exact recurrence and substitution).** At an explicit root let $K$ be its ending indices, $J_L,J_R$ the disjoint child index sets, and restrict $r$ accordingly. The type-$\mathsf A$ count is one exactly when every ending index requests alpha and every strict-descendant index requests absent; otherwise it is zero. The type-$\mathsf H$ rule replaces alpha by beta. For a branch all ending indices must request branch. Subject to that condition, its type-$\mathsf I$ entry is

$$
\bigoplus_{(s,t)\in\mathcal C_I}
 f_L(s,r|_{J_L})\otimes f_R(t,r|_{J_R}),
\tag{BPS.15}
$$

and its type-$\mathsf O$ entry uses all ordered type pairs outside $\mathcal C_I$. A failed branch condition gives zero. Equal summaries on the same index set may be substituted inside any surrounding trie without changing its root summary.

Proof. A root leaf is a single tree, with exactly the stated root and continuation replies. A branch has its unique ordered pair of child trees, whose indexed conditions are independent because the child index sets are disjoint. Child types partition the possibilities, so the summands are disjoint; (BPS.11) is exactly image membership. Lemma 4.2 therefore gives the exact capped counts. Induction on the finite trie proves (BPS.14) and the recurrence. Each parent uses only the identified child summary and restricted requested vector, so equal-summary replacement propagates inductively to the root. Duplicate indices requesting inconsistent replies correctly give zero. This includes early leaves, absent continuations and markers at the root. $\square$

## 6. Oriented unary actions and stabilization

**Lemma 6.1 (the two unary formulas).** Consider an unmarked unary trie node: it has one explicit observed child and one $\#$ child. Its entire index set $J$ lies strictly below the root. Fix a requested vector $r$ on $J$ and let the observed child's four counts be $c=(a,h,i,o)$. Here $a,h\in\{0,1\}$. Define

$$
e=\mathbf1_{\{r\text{ is all absent}\}},\qquad
S=(a+h+i+o>0),\qquad G=(h+i>0).
\tag{BPS.16}
$$

With the observed child on the left or right respectively the parent summary vector is

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_L(c)&=(e,e,6\mathbf1_G,6\mathbf1_S),\\
U_R(c)&=(e,e,\min(6,a+6\mathbf1_G),6\mathbf1_S).
\end{aligned}
\tag{BPS.17}
$$

The leaf terms $e,e$ apply even when $c$ is the zero vector.

Proof. All indices are proper descendants, so each of the two root leaves realizes precisely the all-absent vector, independently of whether any child tree realizes that vector. For a left-observed branch, the $E$ case contributes $h$ and the general image-pair cases contribute the capped product of $h+i$ with the unrestricted number of right images, whose capacity is six. If $h+i>0$ that product already saturates; otherwise both contributions vanish. For a right-observed branch, $E$ contributes $a$, while the general image-pair contribution again saturates exactly when $h+i>0$. The special and general cases are disjoint. This proves the two image entries, with their orientation retained.

For a nonimage branch, no observed-child realization means zero possibilities. If any realization exists, pair it with six distinct unrestricted $\mathsf O$ siblings on the unobserved side. Each resulting ordered pair is nonimage by (BPS.11), is different, and realizes the same vector. Thus its capacity is exactly six. Lemma 5.3 proves that these cases account for every parent tree. $\square$

**Remark 6.2 (singleton image cells).** In the right-observed case $a=1$ and $h=i=0$ gives image capacity exactly one, from $E$. For example an image whose right-root reply is alpha must equal $E$. Replacing this count by six would allow two different whole class values to occupy a singleton. Likewise $e=1,c=0$ has parent counts $(1,1,0,0)$: the two all-absent root leaves must not be discarded. Neither image cells nor all response cells are assumed infinite.

**Lemma 6.3 (every long unary chain has its bottom-two action).** A consecutive run of unmarked unary nodes of length at least two has exactly the summary action of its bottom two nodes, with their original child orientations and attachment, simultaneously for every requested vector.

Proof. Throughout this run $J,r$ and therefore $e$ are unchanged. Suppose first $e=0$. After the bottom node the leaf entries vanish and the nonimage entry is $6\mathbf1_S$. The image entry is positive exactly when $G$ holds or when the bottom orientation is right and $a>0$. After the second node, either orientation gives

$$
\left(0,0,
6\mathbf1_{\{G\ \lor\ (\text{bottom is right and }a>0)\}},
6\mathbf1_S\right).
\tag{BPS.18}
$$

If $S$ fails, all entries here vanish; otherwise the last entry is six. Both $U_L$ and $U_R$ fix this vector. If $e=1$, the first node has both leaf entries one even for zero child vector. The next node therefore has a positive beta-child contribution to general images, and a nonempty child realization. Its vector is $(1,1,6,6)$, fixed by both orientations. Thus all nodes above the bottom two are redundant for this full summary action. In particular deletion down to one node is not justified by this proof: the zero-child all-absent case and the one-image $E$ case change at the second node. The possible actions are contained in $\mathrm{id},U_L,U_R,U_L\circ U_L,U_L\circ U_R$; equality among some actions is harmless. $\square$

## 7. Pinning the serving words and counting essential paths

**Theorem 7.1 (effective linear suffix contraction).** If $m\ge1$, there is a finite syntactic contraction of the common trie which preserves its complete root summary, retains every index and role, leaves all serving suffixes literally fixed, individually weakly shortens every preparer suffix, and puts every indexed endpoint at depth at most $b+6m$.

Proof. Freeze every explicit trie node at depth at most $b$, not just serving endpoints. An essential node is one carrying an index or having two explicit children. Every trie leaf carries an index. If the trie has $\lambda$ leaves, $u$ unary nodes and $v$ binary nodes, counting its edges gives

$$
u+2v=\lambda+u+v-1,\qquad v=\lambda-1.
\tag{BPS.19}
$$

There are at most $m$ marked nodes and $\lambda\le m$. Hence there are at most $2m-1$ essential nodes, even if a marked node is also binary.

Below the frozen prefix, every nonessential node is unmarked unary. In each maximal consecutive run longer than two, replace the subtree at its first node by the subtree at its second-last node. Thus retain the bottom two nodes and their descendant subtree. Lemma 6.3 preserves that subtree's summary, and Lemma 5.3 propagates equality to the root. The incoming edge at the replacement position retains its original left or right orientation; the retained bottom nodes keep their own ordered child orientations. No orientation is reversed and no extra node is inserted. Deleted nodes carry no indices and have only unobserved side children, so no index or branch point is lost.

Each replacement root is below depth $b$. Consequently frozen nodes never move and all serving endpoints, whose original depth is at most $b$, keep their exact words. Descendants that move only shorten, and a descendant moved under a below-$b$ replacement remains below $b$. Essential topology persists. These finite contractions leave at most two unary nodes in each remaining below-prefix run.

Consider an endpoint now at depth $d>b$ and its tail from depth $b+1$ to that endpoint. Let $E'$ be the number of essential nodes on the tail. The endpoint itself is marked, so there are at most $E'$ unary runs preceding these essential nodes, including an initial tail run. Each contributes at most two nodes. Therefore

$$
d-b\le E'+2E'\le3(2m-1)\le6m.
\tag{BPS.20}
$$

Endpoints within the frozen prefix already satisfy the bound. The construction is finite on an explicit trie and needs no successful execution, source bound or code certificate. $\square$

## 8. Exact joint realization on the original domain

**Definition 8.1 (the attained joint relation).** For an arrangement $A$ of preparer literals with its fixed graph data, define
