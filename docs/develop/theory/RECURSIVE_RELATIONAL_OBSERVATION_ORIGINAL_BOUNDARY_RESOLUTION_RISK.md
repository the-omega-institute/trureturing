**Lemma 5.1 (Finite integer-value bijection, including forced zeros).** Legal raw words of length $L\ge0$ take each value

## 7. Ordinary raw Hausdorff distance and failed transfers

**Theorem 7.1 (Exact spectrum criterion on arbitrary nonempty sets).** Define

$$
\rho(x,y)=\begin{cases}0,&x=y,\\2^{-j},&j\text{ is the first differing raw coordinate}.
\end{cases}
$$

For nonempty $A,B\subseteq K$ use the ordinary Hausdorff formula

$$
d_{H,\rho}(A,B)=\max\left\{
\sup_{x\in A}\inf_{y\in B}\rho(x,y),
\sup_{y\in B}\inf_{x\in A}\rho(x,y)\right\}.
\tag{41}
$$

Then, for EVERY integer $L\ge0$,

$$
P_L(A)=P_L(B)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 d_{H,\rho}(A,B)\le2^{-L}.
\tag{42}
$$

This Hausdorff function is a pseudometric on arbitrary nonempty subsets and a metric on nonempty compact subsets.

Proof. Equal spectra give each point an actual partner in the other set with the same $L$-prefix, at distance at most $2^{-L}$. Infima and suprema in both directions give the bound. Conversely, for $L\ge1$, a prefix occurring in A but not B supplies $x\in A$ whose distance from every point of B is at least $2^{-(L-1)}>2^{-L}$. This contradicts (41), without assuming that an infimum is attained. Reverse the roles for the other inclusion. For $L=0$ every nonempty spectrum is the empty word and $\rho\le1$.

The raw metric is an ultrametric: agreement of two pairs through a prefix forces the third pair to agree through the shorter shared prefix. The usual sup-inf construction is symmetric and satisfies the triangle inequality by choosing arbitrarily close partners for each infimum and then letting the extra error tend to zero. Replacing either set by its closure does not change any point-to-set infimum or the resulting directed suprema, by continuity of distance. Hence it is a pseudometric on arbitrary sets. If A and B are compact, they are closed; zero Hausdorff distance puts every point of either in the other's closure, hence in the other set. This proves the metric claim. $\square$

**Theorem 7.2 (All three exact raw Hausdorff values).** For EVERY finite allowed $N$,

$$
\begin{aligned}

&=d_{H,\rho}(U_A(N),F_\lambda)=2^{-d},\\
d_{H,\rho}(U_B(N),F_\lambda)&=2^{-(d+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{43}
$$

All are positive and tend to zero as $N$ grows. At dyadic resolution the exact admissible stages are (38).

Proof. Equation (39) and Theorem 7.1 give the two upper bounds involving A at level $d$, and the B/fibre upper bound at level $d+1$. The actual terminal owner $z_A\in U_A$ has distance exactly $2^{-d}$ from $F_\lambda$ by Lemma 6.2. Every B point has its $(d+1)$-prefix in $S_{d+1}$, while $z_A$ has neither such prefix, so its distance from every B point is at least $2^{-d}$. The point $u_\lambda\in U_B$ matches its first $d$ digits and attains that distance. This proves both lower bounds involving A. The actual initial owner $z_B\in U_B$ has distance $2^{-(d+1)}$ from the fibre, proving the remaining lower bound. All match the upper bounds. Compactness concerns the actual sets with their owners; no closure identification is substituted. These raw values differ from the projected value $\alpha^{d+2}$ in (32). $\square$

**Proposition 7.3 (Closure and fibre counterexamples).** Neither equal phase images nor zero raw Hausdorff distance establishes equality of arbitrary owned sets.

Proof. $K\setminus\{0^\infty\}$ is dense in $K$: words with one isolated one arbitrarily far after an initial zero prefix approach $0^\infty$. Its raw Hausdorff distance from $K$ is zero although the sets differ. More directly,

$$
\pi(\{u_\lambda\})=\pi(\{v_\lambda\})=\pi(F_\lambda)=\{\lambda\},
$$

$$
d_{H,\rho}(F_\lambda,\{u_\lambda\})
=d_{H,\rho}(F_\lambda,\{v_\lambda\})=1,
\tag{44}
$$

because the two lifts differ at coordinate zero. Deleting a lift is invisible to phase geometry and destroys the claimed raw approximation.

Even the closed cylinder $C_0$ is not the full preimage of its closed phase arc: it owns $u_\lambda$ at $\lambda$ and excludes $v_\lambda$. Arbitrarily assigned partial ownership need not be closed either. **Theorem 7.4 (Raw common-output radius and exact closed-ball boundary).** On the fixed public pair, admit finite descriptions of nonempty compact raw subsets $C\subseteq K$, including the two eventually periodic descriptions of $F_\lambda$. Put $\delta=2^{-d}$. Then

## 13. Expected length loss on the declared output domains

**Definition 13.1 (Completed actions, risk and common laws).** Fix the SAME public pair of Definition 8.1, including its known $N$, and write

## 16. Exact alpha sampling with a paid fair-bit source

**Proposition 16.1 (An unexecuted finite-comparison sampler).** Explicitly install a source-independent finite sampler program, exact finite integer arithmetic for $\alpha$, and a sequential iid fair-bit supply independent of A/B, with its program/configuration, actual backend, binding, transport and continuing rights paid. There is an exact Bernoulli$(\alpha)$ sampler that terminates almost surely, uses expected TWO fair bits and has finite expected specified local polynomial work and storage. It has no finite worst-case bit/work/space cap and no all-tapes termination guarantee.

Algorithm. Read fair bits $b_1,b_2,\ldots$ sequentially. After $k$ bits retain their binary integer $V_k$ and the dyadic interval

$$
I_k=[V_k/2^k,(V_k+1)/2^k].
\tag{88}
$$

If the upper endpoint is below $\alpha$, return TRUE; if the lower endpoint is above $\alpha$, return FALSE; otherwise read the next bit. TRUE selects $l_B$, FALSE selects $l_A$. Each comparison is finite exact integer arithmetic: for $v\ge0$,

$$
\frac v{2^k}\ \lessgtr\ \alpha
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(2v+2^k)^2\ \lessgtr\ 5\,2^{2k}.
\tag{89}
$$

Indeed $\alpha=(\sqrt5-1)/2$; add one after doubling, clear the positive denominator and square the resulting nonnegative quantities. Squaring therefore preserves the order. Apply the same test to $v+1$ for the upper endpoint. Irrationality of $\alpha$ excludes equality with either dyadic endpoint. Shifts, registers, comparisons, copies and writes are real paid operations; no expansion of $\alpha$ or real oracle is accessed. This algorithm is specified only as literal symbolic text.

Proof of law and termination. Mathematically the iid fair sequence defines a uniform $U\in[0,1]$, whose depth-$k$ prefix cylinders each have probability $2^{-k}$. Its nested intervals (88) contain $U$ and have diameter tending to zero. If $U<\alpha$, eventually their upper endpoint is below $\alpha$, and the algorithm returns TRUE. If $U>\alpha$, eventually their lower endpoint is above it, and it returns FALSE. A stop always has the correct comparison sign because its interval contains $U$. The only infinite continuation is the unique nondyadic expansion with $U=\alpha$, a null event. Dyadic endpoint representations also shrink to their common $U$ and eventually stop on the correct side. Consequently TRUE has probability $\alpha$ and FALSE probability $1-\alpha=\alpha^2$.

Let $T$ count fair bits consumed. At EVERY $k\ge0$, exactly one dyadic cell has irrational $\alpha$ in its interior. Its length-$k$ prefix cylinder is exactly $\{T>k\}$: all its ancestor cells also contain $\alpha$, and every other cell has separated by that stage. Hence the exact continuing-cylinder tail and its tail sum are

$$
\Pr\{T>k\}=2^{-k},\qquad
\mathbb E T=\sum_{k\ge0}\Pr\{T>k\}
=\sum_{k\ge0}2^{-k}=2.
\tag{90}
$$

This is an exact symbolic probability proof, not an executed expansion, enumeration or simulation. It proves almost-sure finite stopping while also showing that every finite bit cap is exceeded with positive probability.

Proof of specified local resource bounds. At stage $k$, a fixed number of integer registers, counters and comparison operands have $O(k+1)$ bits. Grade-school squares/comparisons, shifts, binary counter updates, navigation, copies and clearing on a fixed number of sequential binary work tapes cost conservatively $O((k+1)^2)$ bit actions. Through $T$ stages a conservative local allowance is

$$
O(P+I+c+T^3+B_{\rm out})\text{ work},\qquad
O(P+I+c+T+1)\text{ simultaneous local bits},
\tag{91}
$$

before adding other coexisting components and retained outputs. Here $P,I,c$ have their actual installed-program, input/installation and control meanings. For finite $T$, summing the stages bounds the sum of their quadratic costs by a constant times $(T+1)^3$. The tail (90) gives

$$
\mathbb E T^3
=\sum_{k\ge0}\bigl((k+1)^3-k^3\bigr)\Pr\{T>k\}
=\sum_{k\ge0}\bigl((k+1)^3-k^3\bigr)2^{-k}<\infty.
\tag{92}
$$

The tail-sum identity follows by telescoping each finite integer cube and nonnegative summation. The polynomial-weighted geometric series converges, for example by its eventual ratio bounded strictly below one. Thus at each fixed public instance both specified local expected work and expected peak local storage are finite. The output here is one branch symbol; materializing the selected length/record is separately paid. Expected two bits alone does not bound actual fair-bit source provisioning, generation, transport or backend storage. Producer/driver/source state, arithmetic, random backend and retained output all coexist in (65). The null unresolved tape has infinite output loss by Definition 13.1, without changing these nonnegative integrals or the expected risks.

The every-tape boundary is substantive. A fixed finite fair-bit cap produces only dyadic rational output probabilities and cannot realize irrational $\alpha$. More generally, an all-tapes-terminating sequential binary decision tree with finite computation between reads has no infinite branch. If its depths were unbounded, finite branching would produce an infinite path by the finite-branching infinite-path principle. Thus it would have bounded depth and again dyadic output probabilities. This sampler consequently supplies neither an every-tape finite irrational coin nor a finite worst-case digital or physical bound. $\square$

**Proposition 16.2 (What implements the query probability).** The arbitrary-$h$ frontier in Theorem 15.2 first quantifies over allowed common laws. It does not install a generator for an arbitrary real $h$. If $h=a/2^b\in[0,1]$ has an actual paid finite dyadic description, read $b$ fair bits, form their binary integer and compare it with $a$ to select the query branch. There are exactly $a$ selected equal-probability strings, so its probability is $h$. All $b$ bits, input scanning, threshold arithmetic, control and storage are paid. Further independent supplied fair bits implement Proposition 16.1 on the no-query relative attained branch, or one fair endpoint bit for attained log/squared loss. Thus dyadic $h$ has an explicit finite branch-coin implementation, with almost-sure alpha sampling where needed. Exact nondyadic $h$ requires its own specified computable cut/sampler and actual costs; an abstract law, desired risk value or finite formula does not supply that bridge. Arbitrary correlated tape laws likewise have no universal free generator.

## 17. Exact attained-depth relative risk with a finite iid fair-bit cap

**Definition 17.1 (The capped no-information class).** Fix ONE common public input specifying the same original pair and its bound $N,d$. Retain attained-depth outputs $y=\alpha^j$, $j\ge0$ (or proper depths $j\ge1$), relative loss, true terminal commitment and the $+\infty$ failure convention of Definition 13.1. A no-information receiver has the same output or failure on each tape in both worlds, as defined there. Its only randomness is a source-independent sequential iid fair-bit supply with a cap $b\ge0$ on ALL consumed bits, including setup, control, selection and random seeds. Its nonrandom initial state is deterministic given the common input. There is no uncharged seed, random control, backend noise or source advice. Put $M=2^b$ and denote the attained-depth no-information minimax risk by $R_b$; this risk symbol has no precision argument and is distinct from the Reference port $R_\beta(n)$.

**Lemma 17.2 (Endpoint domination and padded finite-cell law).** Every finite-risk capped rule can be clamped to endpoints. Any positive probability on outlying depths strictly increases BOTH risks relative to its clamp. Moreover every completed output law has probabilities that are integer multiples of $1/M$, and every endpoint probability $k/M$, $0\le k\le M$ integer, is attainable by a finite rule.

Proof. Put $z=y/l_A$. If $j<d$, then $z>1$ and the losses $(z-1,z/\alpha-1)$ strictly exceed both coordinates of $l_A$'s loss vector $(0,\alpha)$. If $j>d+1$, then $z<\alpha$ and $(1-z,1-z/\alpha)$ strictly exceed both coordinates of $l_B$'s vector $(\alpha^2,0)$. The other integer depths are exactly $d,d+1$. Clamp $j\le d$ to $d$ and $j\ge d+1$ to $d+1$. This is a common finite deterministic transformation, preserving completion and consuming no randomness; its actual work and output remain paid. Positive outlying mass strictly reduces both finite expected losses under this clamp. Infinite original risks cannot improve a finite optimum.

Fix a full word $w\in\{0,1\}^b$ and use its bits for every fresh random response the receiver requests. The unused infinite tail cannot affect its behavior. If $w$ yielded invalidity or noncompletion, all its extensions would have that failure, on a cylinder of mass $1/M>0$, making risk infinite. Thus every full word yields a finite legal output. A branch stopping after $r\le b$ bits repeats on all $2^{b-r}$ extensions. Every output probability is therefore a full-word count divided by $M$; there are only finitely many outputs. An infinite deterministic loop after a finite transcript also fails on a positive-mass cell and is excluded by finite risk. This covers arbitrary adaptive stopping and arbitrarily long deterministic preparation, without executing an enumeration.

Conversely read $b$ bits as an integer $V\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}$ and output $l_B$ if $V<k$, otherwise $l_A$. Exactly $k$ equally weighted full words select $l_B$. A threshold comparison suffices; no exponential lookup table is needed. The cases $k=0,M$ and $b=0$ are included. $\square$

**Theorem 17.3 (Exact capped minimax, unique law and no ties).** Let

$$
m_b=\lfloor M\alpha\rfloor,\qquad
\theta_b=M\alpha-m_b,\qquad
k_b=\lfloor(M+1)\alpha\rfloor.
\tag{96}
$$

Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_b&=\alpha^3+\frac1M\min\{\alpha\theta_b,\alpha^2(1-\theta_b)\}\\
&=\max\{\alpha^2k_b/M,\alpha(1-k_b/M)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{97}
$$

The unique optimal length law outputs $l_B$ with probability $p_b=k_b/M$ and $l_A$ otherwise. At $b=0$, $k_0=1$ and $R_0=\alpha^2$.

Proof. The quadratic root $\alpha$ is irrational. Indeed if $p/q$ is reduced with $q>0$ and satisfies $p^2+pq-q^2=0$, then $q\mid p^2$ forces $q=1$, inconsistent with $0<\alpha<1$. Thus $0<\theta_b<1$ and no grid probability equals $\alpha$. For an endpoint law with $p=\Pr(l_B)$,

$$
(R_A,R_B)=(\alpha^2p,\alpha(1-p)),\qquad
\alpha^2p-\alpha(1-p)=p-\alpha.
$$

Its maximum $f(p)$ strictly decreases before $\alpha$ and strictly increases after it. The only grid candidates are $m_b/M$ and $(m_b+1)/M$. Their values are respectively

$$
\alpha^3+\alpha\theta_b/M,\qquad
\alpha^3+\alpha^2(1-\theta_b)/M.
$$

The first minus the second is $(\theta_b-\alpha^2)/M$. Thus choose the lower neighbor if $\theta_b<\alpha^2$ and the upper if $\theta_b>\alpha^2$. Equality would imply $(M+1)\alpha=m_b+1$, contradicting irrationality, so the minimizer is unique. Since $(M+1)\alpha=m_b+\theta_b+\alpha$ and $\alpha^2=1-\alpha$, its floor is precisely the selected numerator $k_b$. This proves (97) including the edge neighbors zero and one.

When $b=0$, $M=1$, $m_b=0$ and $\theta_b=\alpha>\alpha^2$, so $k_0=1$ and the deterministic action $l_B$ gives risks $(\alpha^2,0)$. Any minimax capped rule must clamp to the unique endpoint minimizer. Positive mass on outlying depths strictly increases both risks, including the active maximum coordinate of that clamp, so cannot be minimax. Thus the length distribution itself is unique, though full-word assignments and output syntax need not be. $\square$

**Proposition 17.4 (Positive norm gap, honest rates and plateaus).** With $\Delta_b=R_b-\alpha^3$,

$$
\alpha^4\,4^{-b}\le\Delta_b<\alpha^3\,2^{-b},
\qquad R_{b+1}\le R_b,\qquad
\lim_{b\to\infty}R_b=\alpha^3.
\tag{98}
$$

The lower bound is attained at $b=0$. Equality $R_{b+1}=R_b$ holds exactly when $k_{b+1}=2k_b$; otherwise the decrease is strict. No matching exponent or binary-normality claim follows.

Proof. Let $\alpha'=-\phi$ be the conjugate. For any grid $p=k/M\in[0,1]$,

$$
(M\alpha-k)(M\alpha'-k)=k^2+Mk-M^2
$$

is a nonzero integer: a zero factor would make a root rational. Therefore

$$
|p-\alpha|\ge\frac1{M^2(\phi+p)}
\ge\frac1{M^2(\phi+1)}.
$$

The excess $f(p)-\alpha^3$ is $\alpha(\alpha-p)$ below $\alpha$ and $\alpha^2(p-\alpha)$ above it. It is at least $\alpha^2|p-\alpha|$. Since $\phi+1=\alpha^{-2}$, the optimal grid law has excess at least $\alpha^4/M^2>0$. At $b=0$, $\alpha^2-\alpha^3=\alpha^4$ proves equality.

The lines $\alpha\theta$ and $\alpha^2(1-\theta)$ meet only at $\theta=\alpha^2$, with common value $\alpha^3$. Their minimum is strictly smaller elsewhere; the tie is impossible for actual $\theta_b$. This gives the strict upper bound in (98). An extra bit can be ignored, proving monotonicity, and the upper bound proves the limit. The coarse optimal probability $k_b/M$ belongs to the next grid as numerator $2k_b$. Uniqueness of the finer minimizer proves precisely the stated plateau criterion. Infinitely many decreases must be strict, since an eventually constant positive finite-cap gap contradicts the limit. These different upper and lower rates establish neither $\Theta(2^{-b})$ nor $\Theta(4^{-b})$, digit frequencies, normality or a distribution of fractional parts. No fractional part or floor has been evaluated here. $\square$

**Theorem 17.5 (Complete scoped Pareto grid and budget feasibility).** At a fixed cap $b$, the nondominated finite-risk pairs in this no-information attained-depth relative game are exactly

$$
\mathcal P_b=\{(\alpha^2 k/M,\alpha(1-k/M)):
 k\in\mathbb Z,\ 0\le k\le M\}.
\tag{99}
$$

Finite nonnegative risk budgets $(r_A,r_B)$ are feasible exactly when

$$
[\max\{0,1-r_B/\alpha\},\ \min\{1,r_A/\alpha^2\}]
\quad\text{contains some }k/M,\quad k\in\{0,\ldots,M\}.
\tag{100}
$$

A common upper budget $r$ is feasible exactly when $r\ge R_b$. Over all finite caps every $r>\alpha^3$ is feasible, while $r=\alpha^3$ is an unattained infimum and $r<\alpha^3$ is impossible.

Proof. The entire finite-risk set consists of

$$
\left(\frac1M\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^b}L_A(\alpha^{j_w}),
\frac1M\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^b}L_B(\alpha^{j_w})\right)
$$

for finite legal integer depths $j_w$. This describes the set without executing an enumeration. By Lemma 17.2 every law with outlying mass is strictly dominated in both coordinates by its endpoint clamp. Distinct endpoint grid points trade the coordinates strictly: increasing $k$ increases A risk and decreases B risk. None dominates another. An outlying rule dominating an endpoint would have a clamp that also dominates it, contradicting those opposite monotonicities. This proves completeness of (99).

For upper budgets, necessity follows by clamping and solving $\alpha^2 k/M\le r_A$ and $\alpha(1-k/M)\le r_B$; sufficiency is the explicit threshold rule of Lemma 17.2. Empty intervals are infeasible and equality endpoints are retained. Minimizing the common upper budget gives (97). The dyadic endpoint grids over all finite caps are dense in the ideal risk segment, and (98) gives feasibility for every common upper budget strictly above $\alpha^3$. The all-real lower bound (68) excludes smaller budgets. Equality would require the unique ideal attained law $p=\alpha$, which no finite cap supplies. These are feasibility statements for upper budgets; they do not assert every real budget is an exact equal-risk pair, nor optimize communication, random-bit expectation, work or whole storage. $\square$

**Theorem 17.6 (Every-tape completion versus almost-sure completion).** For EACH fixed common public input, a sequential iid fair-bit program with deterministic initial state that completes a legal finite output on EVERY infinite binary tape has some finite bound on consumed bits, even if no cap was specified. Consequently $\alpha^3$ is the unattained relative minimax infimum in this every-tape finite attained-depth class. The almost-sure finite sampler of Proposition 16.1 attains it.

Proof. Build the prefix-closed binary tree of unresolved fresh-bit requests, with completed outputs as leaves. Every reachable deterministic segment must complete or request its next bit in finite time; a permanent deterministic stall would fail on every extension of its finite prefix. If requested-bit depths were unbounded, the finitely branching unresolved tree would have an infinite path by the finite-branching infinite-path principle, equivalently compactness of binary tape space. That tape requests a bit at every depth and never completes, a contradiction. Thus there is a finite depth bound, hence finitely many finite branches. It may depend on the fixed public input, including $N$, and need not be effectively extractable from arbitrary code. Finite branching gives the bound; iid conditional fairness gives dyadic cell weights. The unique ideal attained law from (68) and the endpoint matrix requires $p=\alpha$ irrational, so it is unattainable. Finite-cap programs approach it by (98), proving the infimum.

Proposition 16.1 instead compares shrinking dyadic intervals with the exact quadratic $\alpha$. Every tape representing $U\ne\alpha$ eventually separates and completes; the unique nondyadic alpha tape never does. At every depth $k$ exactly one cell continues, with mass $2^{-k}$, so $\Pr(T>k)=2^{-k}$ and $\mathbb ET=2$. The returned endpoint law is $(\Pr(l_B),\Pr(l_A))=(\alpha,\alpha^2)$, giving risks $(\alpha^3,\alpha^3)$. Its null failure still has pointwise loss $+\infty$, with zero contribution to the nonnegative integral. No finite cap, every-tape completion, worst-case work/space or physical deadline follows. The geometric tail gives finite polynomial moments and hence finite expectations for (91)'s SPECIFIED local work and storage at each fixed input, with actual random-backend costs additional. $\square$

**Proposition 17.7 (The randomness primitive is substantive).** The capped conclusions require iid fair bits as the sole random supply. They do not apply to an arbitrary common correlated law, a primitive irrational coin, or the unrestricted algebraic action domain.

Proof. A fixed deterministic seed adds no entropy. A uniform finite seed of $r$ bits, including a seed deterministically expanded by a pseudorandom generator, contributes those $r$ bits to the cap. Its deterministic expansion cannot change the dyadic full-word probabilities. An arbitrarily weighted finite seed can carry irrational probabilities, but then bounded iid fair bits are no longer its sole generator. World-dependent seeds or source advice are excluded.

Even fair one-bit marginals are insufficient. On a two-symbol source-independent supply set

$$
\Pr(00)=\Pr(11)=\alpha^2,\qquad
\Pr(01)=\Pr(10)=\tfrac12-\alpha^2.
\tag{101}
$$

All four weights are positive because $0<\alpha^2<1/2$. They sum to one, and each coordinate has each marginal probability $1/2$. Output $l_B$ except on $11$. Its probability is $1-\alpha^2=\alpha$, so this bounded two-symbol law attains the ideal risk. It is not iid: $\Pr(11)=\alpha^2$ is irrational and cannot equal $1/4$. Irrational weights reside in the joint primitive law; this gives no bounded iid generator for them. A primitive source-independent Bernoulli$(\alpha)$ coin likewise implements the endpoint law with one primitive choice in a different resource model, with its installation, backend and transport still paid.

Unlimited deterministic computation of finite alpha digits or exact comparisons supplies precision, not entropy. It cannot change bounded-bit cylinder weights. Matching the alpha tape forever produces no completed answer. An atomic uniform real or infinite-branch seed changes the digital primitive. The unrestricted harmonic output $2\alpha^2l_A$ attains risk $\alpha^3$ with zero random bits in its finite algebraic domain, lying strictly between the adjacent attained magnitudes. The completed separating raw query of Section 15 gives zero loss with zero fair bits by acquiring information. Neither alternative contradicts the present no-information attained-depth result. The fair endpoint laws for attained absolute/squared log need probability $1/2$ and remain implementable with one iid fair bit. $\square$

For the capped threshold programs, $k_b$ is a finite exact arithmetic specification: compare nonnegative rationals $x$ with $\alpha$ by the sign of $x^2+x-1$, since the other factor $x+\phi$ is positive. Positive-denominator clearing and integer bisection determine $\lfloor(M+1)\alpha\rfloor$ in finitely many tests, with equality handled; no real oracle, recurrence execution or mathematical test is supplied here. Reading $b$ actual bits, retaining their integer, comparing with the threshold and writing the selected bound expression or record are finite paid operations. Grade-school arithmetic on $O(b+1)$-bit fields gives a conservative $O((b+1)^3)$ threshold-preparation allowance and $O(b+1)$ reused arithmetic storage, in addition to actual program, public input, controls, installation and output. Full boundary records add Proposition 8.3's generation and literal output costs. All old vectors, original driver work, source provisioning/generation, random backend, continuing rights, copies and (65)'s simultaneous sum remain paid. The cap measures random bits, not raw bits, numeric precision, received traffic, total work, latency or storage.

## 18. The exact joint stopped-event and finite-randomness frontier

**Definition 18.1 (Fixed event mass and remaining cells).** Keep the SAME known-$N$ actual pair, attained-depth relative loss, serial/terminal/common-observation premises and cap $b$ on ALL iid fair bits. Receivers may now acquire information. Let $E=H_N$, or let $E=J_N$ under Definition 11.1's exact length-only whole-integer MSB protocol. Put

$$
M=2^b,\qquad v=M\mu(E),\qquad m_E=M-v.
\tag{102}
$$

Within this section $v$ denotes the event-cell count. The symbol $m_E$ counts OFF-EVENT full words; it is distinct from $\beta$'s denominator exponent $m$. The boundary coordinate remains $s$, the common MSB-prefix length remains $k_N$, and the maximum initiated numeric precision remains $M_N$. None is a count of random cells.

**Theorem 18.2 (Necessity for every causal finite-risk cap-$b$ receiver).** For either event, $v$ is an integer with $0\le v\le M$. At fixed event mass $v/M$, EVERY such receiver has

$$
\max\{R_A,R_B\}\ge G_{M,v},\qquad
G_{M,v}:=\frac1M\min_{\substack{k\in\mathbb Z\\0\le k\le m_E}}
\max\{\alpha^2k,\alpha(m_E-k)\}.
\tag{103}
$$

For $m_E>0$ the integer minimizer is uniquely

$$
k^*=\lfloor(m_E+1)\alpha\rfloor,
\qquad
G_{M,v}=\max\{\alpha^2 k^*/M,\alpha(m_E-k^*)/M\}.
\tag{104}
$$

For $m_E=0$, the only off-event allocation is $k=0$ and $G_{M,M}=0$. At $v=0$ the result reduces to $R_b$.

Proof of the full-word reduction. Fix a full length-$b$ word and pad every unused bit position. For each actual world the receiver is deterministic under that word, because all randomness, including setup/control choices, is charged to those bits and the specified services add none. Every allowed tape access receives a bit of this word; its unused infinite tail cannot alter commands, received prefixes, outputs, event occurrence or failure. Thus events and per-world outputs depend on full words, even for adaptive bit consumption and unbounded deterministic preparation.

The proof of Theorem 14.1 makes $E$ the SAME tape event in A and B: for $H_N$ the first high command is chosen from a common preceding history; for $J_N$ eligibility and instruction to receive the crossing bit come from the common preceding history. Both proofs include arbitrary low replies, unrestricted References, output fragments, partial frames, controls and the real first-Step/all-future merger on the complement. They assert equality of events, without equality of replies after crossing. Consequently $E$ is a common union of full-word cylinders, each of iid fair probability $1/M$. Its word count $v$ is an integer and its complement has exactly $m_E$ words. Padding a branch that uses fewer bits simply repeats it on its remaining extensions.

Finite risk in BOTH worlds excludes every failure cell in either: a positive-mass full-word cylinder with invalidity, noncompletion, an output stall or an infinite deterministic loop has infinite risk by Definition 13.1. Each cell therefore yields an actual finite legal attained output in each world. In particular one cannot hide a failure in a null unused tail under a finite cap. On each of the $m_E$ off-event words the supplied causal coupling gives the SAME completed action in both worlds, even when the receiver takes the real first Step and continues. True terminal commitment ensures that later writing cannot use new information to revise an earlier common action.

Apply Lemma 17.2's endpoint clamp to these common off-event actions. If $k$ words clamp to $l_B$ and the other $m_E-k$ to $l_A$, the original off-event losses are at least $\alpha^2 k/M$ in A and $\alpha(m_E-k)/M$ in B. On-event actions need not be common or identifying; their losses are merely nonnegative. Adding those contributions gives (103). This covers ALL causal finite-risk cap-$b$ receivers under the stated interfaces, rather than only a preselected mixture, and executes no word enumeration.

Proof of integer minimization. Suppose $m_E>0$. The B term strictly decreases up to $k=\alpha m_E$, and the A term strictly increases above it, because $\alpha+\alpha^2=1$. Put $q=\lfloor\alpha m_E\rfloor$ and $\vartheta=\alpha m_E-q\in(0,1)$. Only $q$ and $q+1$ can minimize. Their unscaled maxima are

$$
\alpha^3m_E+\alpha\vartheta,\qquad
\alpha^3m_E+\alpha^2(1-\vartheta).
$$

The switch is at $\vartheta=\alpha^2$, which would make $(m_E+1)\alpha=q+1$ an integer and is impossible. The selected numerator is $\lfloor(m_E+1)\alpha\rfloor$, by the same carry argument as Theorem 17.3. It lies between zero and $m_E$ and is unique. If $m_E=0$ the only term is zero; this case requires no fractional-part argument. At $v=0$, $m_E=M$ gives (97). $\square$

**Theorem 18.3 (Paid attainment at every feasible event mass).** For each integer $v\in\{0,\ldots,M\}$, a finite paid cap-$b$ promise receiver on the EXISTING raw Extent menu attains both stopped event masses $\mu(H_N)=\mu(J_N)=v/M$ and risks

$$
(R_A,R_B)=\left(\frac{\alpha^2k^*}{M},
\frac{\alpha(m_E-k^*)}{M}\right),
\tag{105}
$$

where $k^*$ is as in (104), and is zero when $m_E=0$. Hence (103) is the exact fixed-event-mass minimax frontier for either event. More generally, finite budgets $(r_A,r_B)$ at that mass are feasible exactly when some integer $0\le k\le m_E$ has $\alpha^2k/M\le r_A$ and $\alpha(m_E-k)/M\le r_B$.

Proof. Pay for the common public input, the finite search and two candidate floors of Lemma 15.1, threshold arithmetic and bound output descriptions. Read $b$ fair bits as $V\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}$. Designate $V<v$ for the query branch; designate $v\le V<v+k^*$ for $l_B$; designate all remaining words for $l_A$. This finite threshold implementation selects exactly the specified word counts without a lookup table or additional random bits. On a query word initiate Extent$(n_{\rm sep})$, receive and COMPLETE its entire actual MSB frame, compare the whole integer with the two distinct precomputed candidates, and output the corresponding ORIGINAL length. The original targets are unchanged; no new Step, Read or Reference occurs. The branch has zero loss in both worlds. On the other words output the indicated common endpoint with no query.

Exactly $v$ words initiate a high query and receive its $(k_N+1)$-st value bit, since $n_{\rm sep}>N$ and its full value length is greater than $k_N$. Thus both events are precisely the selected query set. Every word completes, including the endpoints $v=0,M$, and the total random-bit use is $b$. The endpoint loss matrix yields (105), attaining (103). Replacing $k^*$ by any permissible $k$ proves sufficiency for the budget criterion; the off-event clamp in Theorem 18.2 proves necessity. This also gives the scoped nondominated risk grid at a fixed event mass. It is a risk-coordinate statement, with all physical and computational costs retained.

To prepare $k^*$, $v$ and $m_E$ are finite integers with $O(b+1)$ bits, and the sign/bisection procedure of Section 17 computes the literal floor specification using finite exact comparisons; no such floor is executed here. The separating search pays (85)–(87), including failed tests and both candidates. Its preparation may occur before random selection, so it is not automatically multiplied by $v/M$. The query branch pays its complete precision request, identifiers, actual rights/checks, echo, sign, length header, ALL value bits including integer part and common prefix, delimiters, separate completion, backend arithmetic, transport, comparison and output. The no-query outputs, all $b$ random deliveries, random backend, local parsing/control/copies/clearing and retained output also cost. Full ordinary records require their own paid conversion and printing; an expression creates no right. The old chronology, driver work, source provisioning/generation and (65)'s maximum of coexisting SUMS remain. $\square$

**Corollary 18.4 (Threshold consequences and actual witness counts).** For a general receiver with event mass $v/M$, EACH world satisfies, for $E=H_N$,

For $E=J_N$, under the exact MSB protocol, EACH world satisfies

$$

The attaining receiver, with no preliminary queries, has the stronger exact witness identities

$$

so its expected actual coordinates are respectively $(v/M)(n_{\rm sep}+a_\beta-m)$ and $(v/M)n_{\rm sep}$, and its corresponding threshold tails are exactly $v/M$.

Proof. The pointwise general implications are $M_N\ge(N+1)\mathbf1_{H_N}$ and $B_N\ge(k_N+1)\mathbf1_{J_N}$, proved in Corollary 14.2 separately in each world. Integrate these nonnegative bounds and use their event inclusions for the tails. Its other branches issue no query, proving (108) and the exact expectations and tails. The conservative thresholds in (106)–(107) need not equal the witness's complete counts. A general event occurrence forces neither identification, full-frame completion nor a correct answer. Partial replies and all pending issued-service debt retain their separate meanings; the witness deliberately completes its entire frame. $\square$

**Proposition 18.5 (Why the full-cap coefficient cannot be reused after conditioning).** In general the false replacement

$$
\max\{R_A,R_B\}\ge R_b(1-\mu(E))
\tag{109}
$$

does not hold. The valid sharp expression is (103), using the remaining-cell count $m_E$.

Proof. Conditional on a nonempty off-event set, its equally weighted full words have probability grid $k/m_E$, with total unconditioned mass $m_E/M$. This need not be the full grid $k/M$, nor a dyadic grid. Its scaled decision problem is exactly (103). The pointwise coefficient $\alpha^3$ in Theorem 14.1 remains valid under every restriction, but a finite-cap coefficient cannot be substituted without solving this remaining grid.

For a concrete symbolic counterexample take $b=2$, $M=4$, $v=1$, $m_E=3$. For $P(x)=x^2+x-1$, strict increase on positive arguments and

$$
P(3/5)=-1/25<0,\qquad P(2/3)=1/9>0
$$

give $3/5<\alpha<2/3<4/5$. Thus $3<5\alpha<10/3<4$ and $k_2=\lfloor5\alpha\rfloor=3$. Since $\alpha>1/2$ implies $3\alpha>1$,

$$
R_2=\max\{3\alpha^2/4,\alpha/4\}=3\alpha^2/4.
$$

Also $2<4\alpha<8/3<3$, so the three-cell optimum has $k^*=\lfloor4\alpha\rfloor=2$. Use the one designated word for a completed paid separating query, two of the three off-event words for $l_B$, and the last for $l_A$. Its risks are $(\alpha^2/2,\alpha/4)$. Because $2\alpha>1$, $2\alpha^2>\alpha$ and their maximum is $\alpha^2/2$. But

$$
R_2(1-v/M)=\frac{9\alpha^2}{16}
>\frac{8\alpha^2}{16}=\frac{\alpha^2}{2},
$$

with positive gap $\alpha^2/16$. This is an actual cap-two paid promise-pair construction for BOTH stopped events, with all four word actions finite. The computations displayed here are symbolic rational/quadratic inequalities, not executed arithmetic or enumeration. Conditionalization changes the remaining grid and invalidates (109). $\square$

For positive off-event mass, exact ideal risk need not be attainable with the finite remaining grid, while unrestricted harmonic outputs and informative queries retain their separate domains.

## 19. Expected loss, tolerance success and the information length omits

**Proposition 19.1 (Explicit failures of stronger interpretations).** Improved expected loss need not give uniform tolerance success above one half, a smaller pointwise error, a recovered owned boundary or an unknown-depth numerical answer.

Proof for tolerance success. For disjoint acceptable-action sets, a common valid action is acceptable in at most one world. Thus EVERY no-information common law satisfies $s_A+s_B\le1$, regardless of its expected risk. Invalid/noncompleted outputs add no success. On either stopped complement the same indicator argument gives $s_A+s_B\le1+\mu(E)$, separately from the positive-prior expected-loss proof. Overlap permits common success one, so it must be checked in the actual output domain.
