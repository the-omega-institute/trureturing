## 2. Actual owned seeds and the common-law loss

**Definition 2.1 (complete native templates and their exact carrier).**
A complete template on $n$ original $S_d$ blocks prescribes at every
$(i,q)\in[n]\times W_d$ a constant in $G_q$ or an equation

$$
\Gamma_q(x_i)=\delta_{i,q}(r_{x_j}(a)),\qquad
j\in[n],\ a\in W_d,\quad\delta_{i,q}:\mathcal A\to G_q.
\tag{OCC.6}
$$

Tables are total; donors, cycles, self queries, off-target queries and
correlations are unrestricted. All equations refer to the same tuple.
The full simultaneous solution set is $C_\Theta\subseteq S_d^n$.

Use the two-way owned-seed language of [RRN, Definition 5.1 and Theorem
5.2][RRN]. Each seed $c$ has a domain $T_c\subseteq\mathcal A$ and
a distinct actual owner port $o_c$. Each port is a constant full factor
or $B_{i,q}(s_c)\in G_q$ for one shared seed. The owner obeys

$$
h(B_{o_c}(a))=a\qquad(a\in T_c).
\tag{OCC.7}
$$

A seed vector is realizable precisely when each block's entire
prescribed factor vector reconstructs an actual $x_i(s)\in S_d$,
necessarily unique. For arbitrary nonnegative whole-block weights,

$$
\Phi_i(s)=
\begin{cases}w_i(x_i(s)),&\text{block }i\text{ reconstructs},\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
\qquad
Z=\sum_s\prod_i\Phi_i(s)
 =\sum_{x\in C_\Theta}\prod_iw_i(x_i).
\tag{OCC.8}
$$

Every actual tuple has one lift, recovered from its owner replies.
Every lawful owned-seed network conversely gives a complete template.
Each original whole-block weight occurs once. No repeated seed becomes
an independent variable. The original number $n$ of weight owners is
unchanged in both directions. [CBN][CBN] supplies actual constructor
reconstruction; [OCB, Theorem 2.2][OCB] supplies its temporal tests.
Seeds and probability laws here are analytical objects, not reader inputs.

**Definition 2.2 (owner partition and incidence).** Let $O_i$ be the
tuple of all seeds owned by original block $i$, with an empty tuple at a
block owning none. These tuples partition the seed coordinates. An
incoming seed is grouped with others from its same actual outside owner,
as in [COK, Definition 2.1][COK]; write the incoming groups at block
$i$ as $B_{i,1},\ldots,B_{i,k_i}$. Prescribed incidence is

$$
\nu(c)=|\{i:\text{some port of original block }i\text{ is assigned to }c\}|.
\tag{OCC.9}
$$

Multiple occurrences in a block count once. This is [SIR, Definition
2.2][SIR]'s convention, including assigned constant tables.

**Definition 2.3 (the chain-rule defect on one law).** For any probability
law $T$ on actual seed solutions let

$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_k(J)&=\frac{|J|!(k-|J|)!}{(k+1)!},\\
\mathcal L(T)&=\sum_i\sum_{J\subseteq[k_i]}
 \gamma_{k_i}(J)H_T(O_i\mid B_{i,J}),\\
\Delta(T)&=\mathcal L(T)-H(T)\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{OCC.10}
$$

All logarithms and entropies have base two. The nonnegative difference
is the permutation-averaged conditional mutual information in [COK,
Theorem 3.2][COK]: apply the entropy chain rule to the entire owner
partition and condition each block only on its incoming preceding owner
groups. This holds for every common law. The stronger COK identity for
$\log_2Z$ uses specifically the Gibbs law
$P(s)=Z^{-1}\prod_i\Phi_i(s)$ of that exact sum. It is not an identity
for an arbitrary compatible completion with matching local marginals.

## 3. Mature conditional specifications and component normalizers

**Definition 3.1 (actual-owner resampling graph).** Let $R$ be a nonempty
finite positive-support relation of actual seed solutions, with owner
partition $(O_i)_{i=1}^n$. A weight $W:R\to(0,\infty)$ can be
the product in (OCC.8), or any specified positive joint weight. Set
$Z=\sum_{s\in R}W(s)$ and $P(s)=W(s)/Z$.

For an integer $r\ge1$, the graph $G_r(R)$ has vertex set $R$ and
joins distinct $s,t$ exactly when

$$
d_O(s,t):=|\{i:O_i(s)\ne O_i(t)\}|\le r.
\tag{OCC.11}
$$

This counts changed actual owner tuples, not changed individual ports,
all block states, or artificial coordinates. A full conditional on an
owner subset $U$ means $T(O_U\mid O_{U^c})$, conditioning on every
outside owner, at every attained outside value.

**Lemma 3.2 (component-normalization principle; mature supplier).** Let
$Q$ be positive at every point of the same $R$. It has exactly the same
full conditional laws as $P$ on every $U\subseteq[n]$ with $|U|\le r$
if and only if $Q(s)/P(s)$ is constant on each connected component
$C$ of $G_r(R)$. Equivalently,

$$
Q=\sum_C q_C P(\,\cdot\mid C),\qquad q_C>0,\quad\sum_Cq_C=1.
\tag{OCC.12}
$$

Write $Z_C=\sum_{s\in C}W(s)$ and $\pi_C=Z_C/Z$. Then

$$
H(Q)+\mathbb E_Q\log_2W
 =\log_2Z-D_2(q\Vert\pi).
\tag{OCC.13}
$$

In particular the specified full conditionals uniquely determine a
positive law on $R$ exactly when $G_r(R)$ is connected.

Proof. If $s,t$ are adjacent, take $U$ containing their changed owners
and of size at most $r$. They have the same outside value, which has
positive probability under both laws. Equality of the full conditional
rows gives

$$
\frac{Q(s)}{Q(t)}=\frac{P(s)}{P(t)}.
$$

Thus $Q/P$ is constant along each edge and hence each component.
Conversely all points with one fixed outside-$U$ value form a clique in
$G_r(R)$. A component-constant ratio cancels in that fiber's conditional
normalization. This gives equality of every specified full conditional,
and normalizing on a component gives (OCC.12).

On $C$, $Q/P=q_C/\pi_C$, so
$D_2(Q\Vert P)=D_2(q\Vert\pi)$. Expanding the left relative entropy
using $\log_2P=\log_2W-\log_2Z$ proves (OCC.13). If there are two
or more components, two different positive component vectors give
different laws with the same conditionals. With one component the ratio
must be one. This proves both uniqueness directions. $\square$

This is the finite path-ratio principle for conditional specifications,
not new abstract mathematics. [Slavkovic–Sullivant, §§1–2 and Theorem
2][SS] treats compatible finite full conditionals with zeros;
[Emerson, §4 Lemma 1][Emerson] explicitly constructs probabilities by
path ratios separately on support components. The proof above states
the actual-owner version and its elementary Gibbs variational identity
without transferring any succinct-circuit complexity claim. Positivity
is required only on $R$; Cartesian support positivity and independence
are not assumed. With zero weights, define $R$ to be the nonempty
positive support first. A zero partition has no Gibbs law and is outside
this lemma.

## 4. A connected native family terminal under individual bijections

**Definition 4.1 (cyclic positive exact-one system).** Fix $m\ge2$ and
indices modulo $m$. Its $3m$ Boolean variables are $x_i,a_i,b_i$.
For each $i$ impose the two ordered positive exact-one-in-three clauses

$$
(x_i,a_i,b_i),\qquad(x_{i+1},a_i,b_i).
\tag{OCC.14}
$$

Each clause has three distinct variables, including at $m=2$.
A Boolean zero is the actual leaf $A$ and a Boolean one is $B$.
Apply precisely [OCB, Theorem 3.3][OCB]'s unique native lift, with
selector states $u_0=A,u_1=B,u_2=C_0$ for true positions $0,1,2$.

Explicitly give each Boolean variable its own original owner block,
all of whose depth-two factors are those of its root leaf seed. Give
each of the $2m$ clauses a selector-owner block with all factors of
$u_c$, whose actual root reply is respectively $A,B,\mathsf{branch}$.
For its position $k$ occupied by Boolean variable $v$, add a connector
block with factor prescriptions

$$
z_\varepsilon=\Gamma_\varepsilon(v),\qquad
z_L=\Gamma_L(B\text{ if }c=k\text{, else }A),\qquad
z_R=\Gamma_R(v).
\tag{OCC.15}
$$

It owns no seed. All occurrences of $v$ query its same actual owner;
all occurrences of a selector query that selector's same actual owner.
For $d>2$, retain these three prescriptions and prescribe every
additional factor from the same actual owner state as its block's root:
$v$ at a variable owner or connector, and $u_c$ at a selector owner.
Call this network $\mathcal T_{m,d}$.

Every table has its original attained range. For completeness, a Boolean
owner's total root decoder maps $A,B$ to their own root factors and
maps branch and absent to the root factor of $A$; these last replies
cannot be fixed points. A selector owner's root decoder fixes the
three stated actual replies and maps absent to the root factor of $A$.
Nonowner off-domain entries may be completed by any attained factor.
These guards give a complete original template by RRN's converse;
no Boolean or selector domain is supplied as an extra source promise.

**Theorem 4.2 (exact actual tuples, counts and incidence).** For every
$m\ge2,d\ge2$, $\mathcal T_{m,d}$ has exactly $11m$ original
blocks and $5m$ seeds, all owned at actual roots. Its seed-block
incidence graph is connected and every seed has incidence at most four.
Its full actual solution set has exactly $2^m+1$ points:

1. Phase one: every $x_i=1$ and every $a_i=b_i=0$; the native lift
   is unique.
2. Phase zero: every $x_i=0$ and, independently as a description of
   the set, each $(a_i,b_i)$ is $(1,0)$ or $(0,1)$; each assignment
   has its unique native lift.

All declared values of every seed occur in actual solutions. Each
original whole-block weight occurs once in its exact weighted sum.

Proof. Subtracting the two clause equations gives $x_i=x_{i+1}$.
The cycle makes all $x_i$ equal. A value one forces both auxiliaries
zero. A value zero permits exactly the two stated choices at each
edge; there is no further clause condition. This gives the Boolean
classification and count.

A Boolean owner is its seed's actual leaf. In a connector the root is
that same leaf and the two possible left factors are distinct:
$\Gamma_L(A)=(3,0)$ and $\Gamma_L(B)=(2,0)$. Thus (OCC.15)
is compatible exactly when that variable is true at the selected clause
position. If compatible, the connector is that variable leaf. The
selector is the unique actual $u_c$. Hence a native solution gives one
satisfying assignment, and any satisfying assignment fills all blocks
uniquely. Additional factors at $d>2$ are already those of these same
leaves or selectors, so neither remove nor add solutions. This proves
both native directions, not just a count of Boolean candidates.

There are $3m$ variable owners, $2m$ selector owners and $6m$
connectors, totaling $11m$ blocks and $5m$ seeds. Each variable
occurs in its owner and its two clause connectors, giving incidence
three. Each selector occurs in its owner and its three connectors,
giving incidence four. The paths through selectors and occurrence
connectors connect $x_i$ to $a_i,b_i$ and $x_{i+1}$; they connect
the whole cycle, and every other vertex is attached. Every Boolean
seed attains both values. Every selector attains position zero in phase
one and positions one and two in phase zero. The unique actual lift and
(OCC.8) count every original weight once. $\square$

**Theorem 4.3 (linear owner barrier and exact resampling components).**
Between any opposite-phase solutions of $\mathcal T_{m,d}$,

$$
d_O(s,t)=4m,\qquad
|\{j:x_j(s)\ne x_j(t)\}|=8m.
\tag{OCC.16}
$$

For two phase-zero solutions whose edge choices differ in $k$ places,
their owner distance is exactly $4k$. Consequently $G_r$ has two
components, precisely the two phases, when $4\le r<4m$; for
$1\le r<4$ every point is isolated. At $r\ge4m$ it is connected.
In particular an update budget $r_m=o(11m)$ cannot connect this
single growing family for all sufficiently large $m$.

Proof. Across phases, $m$ of the $x$ owners change, one auxiliary
owner per edge changes, and both selectors per edge change. Their
owner blocks are distinct, totaling $m+m+2m=4m$. Connector leaves
change at the two occurrences of each $x$ and the two occurrences
of each changing auxiliary, giving $4m$ further changed actual
blocks. The total actual-block distance is $8m$.

Within phase zero, changing one edge choice swaps its two auxiliary
owners and its two selector owners, and changes no other owner.
Different edges affect disjoint such owners, proving the distance $4k$.
The phase-zero edge choices therefore contain their connected
hypercube of four-owner moves. The other phase is a singleton, and
every crossing requires $4m$ owners. The component assertions follow
in all ranges. For $r_m=o(11m)$, eventually $r_m<4m$, so no edge
crosses phases. This last statement is about connectivity, not an
extensive entropy gap at every growing update budget. $\square$

**Theorem 4.4 (individual-bijection terminality).** No nontrivial
individual adjacent temporal test of $\mathcal T_{m,d}$ is a total
bijection between its two distinct seed domains. This remains true after
restricting every domain to its globally attained values. Thus the
individual-test contraction of [GDB, Definition 3.1 and Theorem 3.2][GDB]
performs no nontrivial merge on this witness.

Proof. In a variable or selector owner, every factor is a function of
its one own seed, so adjacent tests are unary tautologies. In a
connector, a distinct-seed test can only compare its Boolean variable
with its clause selector, and its nontrivial condition is exactly

$$
v=\mathbf1_{\{c=k\}},\qquad v\in\{0,1\},\quad c\in\{0,1,2\}.
\tag{OCC.17}
$$

For the root-to-left test this follows directly from the two distinct
leaf left factors. At larger depth, the tests from $L$ to $LL,LR$
give the same condition: a leaf factor extended by $e$ has first
coordinate increased by one for $L$ and two for $R$; hence it
matches the prescribed actual variable suffix exactly when those leaf
labels agree. All remaining adjacent tests use one seed. This also
follows from the exact temporal-summary tests of [OCB, Theorem 2.2][OCB].

Relation (OCC.17) has three allowed pairs on a two-value domain and a
three-value domain. It is not the graph of a total bijection. Theorem
4.2 says all these values occur globally, so attained-value restriction
does not shrink either domain. No distinct Boolean-Boolean or
selector-selector test is present. This proves terminality for the
specified individual-test operation. Joint elimination can discover
additional relations, including $x_i=x_{i+1}$; that operation is not
excluded by the theorem. $\square$

## 5. Full-support block laws and extensive completion ambiguity

**Definition 5.1 (one balanced positive rational block law).** For any
$d\ge2$ let $s_d=|S_d|$ and $L_d=s_d+9$. Give the full $S_d$
the raw weights

$$
w(A)=5,\qquad w(B)=3,\qquad w(C_0)=4,\qquad
w(u)=1\quad(u\notin\{A,B,C_0\}),\qquad \mu_d=w/L_d.
\tag{OCC.18}
$$

This is a strictly positive rational probability on the entire canonical
alphabet, with $L_2=20$. Positivity of these whole-block weights does
not remove native compatibility zeros.

**Theorem 5.2 (exact balanced phase normalizers).** For
$\mathcal T_{m,d}$ under the once-owned homogeneous law $\mu_d$,
both phases have exactly the same mass

$$
T_{m,d}=\frac{(5^8 3^3)^m}{L_d^{11m}},\qquad Z=2T_{m,d}.
\tag{OCC.19}
$$

The actual Gibbs phase bit is fair. Conditional on phase zero, its edge
choices are independent, with probabilities $9/25$ for $(a_i,b_i)=(1,0)$
and $16/25$ for $(0,1)$.

Proof. In phase one the $3m$ variable owners contribute
$A^{2m}B^m$, the $2m$ selectors contribute $A^{2m}$ and the
$6m$ connectors contribute $A^{4m}B^{2m}$. Here powers count
actual block states. The raw product weight is $5^{8m}3^{3m}$.

In phase zero the variable owners still contribute $A^{2m}B^m$,
and the connectors still contribute $A^{4m}B^{2m}$. At edge $i$,
both selectors are $B$ if $a_i=1$, and both are $C_0$ if
$b_i=1$. Summing the original selector weights once at each block
gives

$$
(5^6 3^3)^m(3^2+4^2)^m=(5^8 3^3)^m.
$$

There are $11m$ original normalized block factors, proving (OCC.19).
The same product expansion gives the conditional edge probabilities
$3^2/(3^2+4^2)$ and $4^2/(3^2+4^2)$ and their independence
within this particular conditional law. No independence is assumed
for arbitrary competing relations. $\square$

**Theorem 5.3 (identical local laws and bounded-owner full conditionals).**
Fix $m\ge2,d\ge2$, an integer $\ell\ge1$, an update budget
$1\le r<4m$, and a rational $0<\epsilon<1$. Take $\ell$ disjoint
copies of $\mathcal T_{m,d}$, retaining their $n=11m\ell$ original
blocks and their entire once-owned product weight $W$. Let $P=W/Z$
be its actual Gibbs law. Its phase vector $F\in\{0,1\}^\ell$ is
uniform. Define the positive rational phase law

$$
q=(1-\epsilon)\frac{\delta_{0^\ell}+\delta_{1^\ell}}2
    +\epsilon\operatorname{Unif}(\{0,1\}^\ell),
\qquad Q(\,\cdot\mid F)=P(\,\cdot\mid F),\quad Q_F=q.
\tag{OCC.20}
$$

Then $Q$ is positive on exactly the same complete actual solution set
as $P$. It has the same full conditional law as $P$ for every update
of at most $r$ actual owner tuples. It also has the same marginal on
each entire gadget. Consequently every local joint law used by COK,
including its actual owned tuple and every incoming actual-owner group,
is identical under $P$ and $Q$. Nevertheless, with
$\delta_\ell=\ell-H(q)$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
H(P)-H(Q)&=\delta_\ell,\\
\log_2Z-\bigl(H(Q)+\mathbb E_Q\log_2W\bigr)&=\delta_\ell,\\
\Delta(Q)-\Delta(P)&=\delta_\ell,\\
\delta_\ell&\ge(1-\epsilon)(\ell-1)-h_2(\epsilon).
\end{aligned}
\tag{OCC.21}
$$

For $\epsilon=1/4$ this is at least $3\ell/4-7/4$ on
$11m\ell$ original blocks. For $\ell\ge2$ the gap is strictly
positive; $\ell=1$ gives $Q=P$ and zero gap.

Proof. The copies' actual relation and once-owned weight product factor
across copies, so their Gibbs laws factor. By Theorem 5.2 each phase
has mass $1/2$ and the phase vector is uniform. Every $q(f)>0$,
so $Q$ has the identical positive support and

$$
\frac{Q(s)}{P(s)}=2^\ell q(F(s)).
\tag{OCC.22}
$$

No move of fewer than $4m$ owners changes any gadget phase, by
Theorem 4.3. Thus (OCC.22) is constant on each $G_r$ component.
Lemma 3.2 proves equality of every stated full conditional, including
updates spread over several copies. This argument also covers $r<4$,
when every point is isolated. For $4\le r<4m$, the exact components
are indexed by their phase vectors, since phase-zero choices in each
copy are connected by four-owner moves.

Every one-coordinate phase marginal of $q$ is fair. Given a phase
vector, each gadget has its original Gibbs conditional law, depending
only on its own phase. Averaging therefore gives precisely its full
$P$ marginal. In particular its local block, owned tuple, incoming
groups and full actual outside owners have the same joint distribution
under both laws: all belong to that same gadget. This is simultaneous
compatibility of all the COK laws, not separate abstract local choices.

Conditional entropies of the complete solutions given the phase vector
add across gadgets under both laws. Their expectations agree because
the individual phases have the same marginals. The phase entropy under
$P$ is $\ell$ and under $Q$ is $H(q)$, proving the first equality
of (OCC.21). The same entire-gadget marginals give
$\mathbb E_Q\log_2W=\mathbb E_P\log_2W$, proving the second
equality from the Gibbs identity. Equivalently Lemma 3.2 gives
$D_2(q\Vert\operatorname{Unif})=\ell-H(q)$. The terms in
$\mathcal L$ are the same local entropies under the two laws, so
(OCC.10) gives the third equality.

Introduce the mixture marker for (OCC.20), of entropy $h_2(\epsilon)$.
Its conditional phase entropy is one on the two-point part and $\ell$
on the uniform part. Dropping its nonnegative conditional entropy given
$F$ gives

$$
H(q)\le h_2(\epsilon)+(1-\epsilon)+\epsilon\ell.
$$

This proves the last inequality. Since $h_2(1/4)\le1$, the displayed
coarser rational lower bound follows. For $\ell\ge2$, $q$ is not
uniform when $\epsilon<1$, so strict entropy maximization by the
uniform law gives $\delta_\ell>0$. $\square$

This theorem does not make $Q$ the Gibbs law of the original weights.
It constructs globally realizable positive laws with exactly the stated
identical conditional and local data. The missing global datum is the
original weighted component law, not feasibility of a joint completion.
For the actual $W$, Gibbs variational duality gives
$H(Q)+\mathbb E_Q\log_2W\le\log_2Z$. Substituting this value
as an equality or an upper bound reverses the available implication.
The full network and weights still determine $P$ by definition; summing
their component normalizers recovers it exactly on this family.

## 6. The precise conditional-integration mechanism boundary

$P(s)=Z^{-1}\prod_v\mu(x_v(s))$ on $R_m$. The symbol $\chi$

## 11. Four actual-owner components and their exact normalizers

**Theorem 11.1 (cycle/face quotient and actual-owner connectivity).**
For every $m\ge3$, $R_m$ has dimension $n+1$ over $\mathbb F_2$,
and every $R_h$, $h\in\mathbb F_2^2$, has $2^{n-1}$ points.
For every integer $r\ge3$ with $2r<m$, the graph $G_r(R_m)$ of
Definition 3.1 has exactly four connected components, namely $R_h$.
In particular this holds at the fixed update budget $r=3$ for every
$m\ge7$.

Proof. Let $H$ be the binary vertex-edge incidence matrix. Its
transpose kernel consists of constant vertex vectors: an edge forces
its two endpoint coefficients equal, and the torus graph is connected.
Thus $\operatorname{rank}H=n-1$, and
$R_m=\ker H$ has dimension $2n-(n-1)=n+1$.

Let $f_{x,y}$ be the boundary of the elementary square with corners
$(x,y),(x+1,y),(x+1,y+1),(x,y+1)$. Let $B$ be the span of all
$n$ such edge vectors. They belong to $R_m$. In a dependence
$\sum_{x,y}c_{x,y}f_{x,y}=0$, each edge occurs in its two adjacent
faces, so their coefficients must agree. The dual square torus is
connected. All coefficients are therefore equal, and the only
nonzero dependence is the sum of all faces. Consequently
$\dim B=n-1$.

Every face boundary has $\chi=(0,0)$, including faces crossing a
chosen seam: their crossings occur in pairs. A full horizontal row
cycle has $\chi=(1,0)$; a full vertical column cycle has
$\chi=(0,1)$. Hence $\chi:R_m\to\mathbb F_2^2$ is onto, and its
kernel has dimension $n-1$. It contains $B$, so it equals $B$.
Every $R_h$ is one affine coset of $B$ and has the stated size.

A face flip changes four edges but exactly three owner tuples.
Its southwest vertex owns the bottom and left edges; the southeast
vertex owns the right edge, and the northwest vertex owns the top
edge. These three vertices are distinct for $m\ge3$. A sequence of
face flips therefore connects every coset $R_h$ by three-owner moves.

It remains to exclude every permitted move between different cosets,
including nongeometric moves. For $z\in R_m$, let
$a_x=\sum_y z^x_{x,y}$. Summing its vertex parity equations over
column $x$ cancels all vertical edges and gives $a_x=a_{x-1}$.
Thus $\chi_x(z)=1$ forces at least one changed horizontal edge in
each of the $m$ disjoint horizontal seams, and hence at least $m$
changed edges. Summing over a row gives the corresponding assertion
for $\chi_y(z)=1$ and vertical edges. If $s,t$ differ at at most
$r$ owners, $z=s+t$ has at most $2r$ changed edges, since each owner
owns two. Under $2r<m$, neither crossing parity can be one.
Every edge of $G_r$ stays in one $R_h$. Together with face-flip
connectivity, this proves the component classification. $\square$

**Corollary 11.2 (all four probabilities are known exactly).** Under
Definition 10.3, $P$ is uniform on $R_m$. Its raw component masses,
total mass and component probabilities are

$$
Z_h=2^{n-1}8^{-n}=2^{-2n-1},\qquad
Z=4Z_h=2^{1-2n},\qquad P(R_h)=\frac14.
\tag{OCC.36}
$$

Proof. Every row in the positive support has the same product weight
$8^{-n}$. Apply Theorem 11.1 and sum the four equal masses.
$\square$

Knowing the full conditionals and these normalizers already determines
the mathematical law, by the credited Lemma 3.2. There is no further
law-uniqueness claim. It does not reveal which of the four components
contains an unknown stationary actual source.

## 12. Exactly paid acquisition of the actual component

**Definition 12.1 (one actual source embedding).** Fix public
$m\ge3$, enumerate its $n$ vertices publicly, and put

$$
H_0=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil,\qquad K_0=2^{H_0},\qquad D=H_0+3.
\tag{OCC.37}
$$

In a perfect ordered scaffold of height $H_0$ place $x_v(s)$ at its
first $n$ frontier words $a_v$, each of length $H_0$. Pad every
other frontier position by alpha. Denote the resulting right subtree
by $u_s$ and the original source by $t_s=\langle\alpha,u_s\rangle$.
The map $s\mapsto u_s$ is injective, belongs to $S_D$, and has
height $H_0+2\le D$. Write $\widehat R_m=\{u_s:s\in R_m\}$
and $\widehat R_h=\{u_s:s\in R_h\}$.

Every finite original-root literal on this canonical slice is either
constant or returns exactly one edge bit as alpha or beta. Indeed the
outer root, left cone, scaffold, padding, and each block's three
internal nodes have fixed replies. The only varying positions are
the terminal graph slots, all at original-root length $D$; beyond
these leaves the reply is absent. Querying an edge at its other
endpoint still returns that same bit. No literal returns a parity,
component, subtree, or collection of terminal values.

**Theorem 12.2 (exact acquisition on every execution).** Start with a
constant retained name on the known actual-source slice
$\{t_s:s\in R_m\}$. A deterministic adaptive acquisition using
arbitrary finite original-root literals and required to output
$\chi(s)$ exactly needs at least $2m$ paid Reads on every execution.
There is a procedure using exactly $2m$ Reads on every execution.
Its literals have length $D$, so its total submitted letters are
$2mD$, also minimal. Recovering either one specified bit $\chi_x$
or $\chi_y$ alone has the corresponding exact bounds $m$ Reads
and $mD$ letters. These statements allow the entire common law,
every full conditional and all four normalizers as public data.

Proof. Consider the $m$ full horizontal row cycles and the $m$ full
vertical column cycles. These $2m$ cycles are pairwise edge-disjoint:
different rows have different horizontal edges, different columns
have different vertical edges, and horizontal edges are distinct from
vertical edges. Each is in $R_m$ and toggles one nonzero coordinate
of $\chi$.

Fix any actual input and its terminating adaptive execution. If fewer
than $2m$ distinct edge bits were queried, one of these cycles was
never queried. Toggle it in that actual input. The new edge vector
still lies in $R_m$ and has a different descriptor. Every query of
the fixed execution has the same reply: constants remain constant,
and every queried edge lies outside the toggled cycle. Induction on
the execution shows identical subsequent queries, stopping decision
and output under the deterministic procedure. This contradicts exact
recovery. The argument is for each execution individually, not only
the worst execution. Repeated observations and arbitrary off-target
words do not weaken it, by Definition 12.1.

Read the $m$ actual owner slots for the seam
$\{e^x_{m-1,y}:y\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\}$ and the $m$ owner
slots for $\{e^y_{x,m-1}:x\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\}$.
The two parities are (OCC.35), giving the matching upper bound.
All varying literals have length $D$, so the lower bound on distinct
edge observations also proves the letter lower bound, attained by
these owner words.

For $\chi_x$ alone use only the $m$ pairwise disjoint horizontal
cycles in the indistinguishability argument, and read only its one
horizontal seam. Each such cycle toggles $\chi_x$. The vertical
argument is identical with the coordinate names exchanged. Public
distributional data depend on neither actual input and hence leave
the identical-transcript argument intact. $\square$

**Corollary 12.3 (component count does not bound acquisition).** There
is no finite size-independent budget $f(d,L,r,c)$ that guarantees
exact acquisition of the actual owner component from a constant name
through literal Reads for every such positive-support native fiber,
even when its common law, bounded-owner full conditionals and all
component normalizers are specified exactly. It already fails at
$d=3,L=2,r=3,c=4$.

Proof. For every $m\ge7$, Theorem 11.1 gives precisely those fixed
parameters and four components, Corollary 11.2 gives their normalizers,
and Theorem 12.2 requires $2m$ Reads. This exceeds any proposed finite
value at those fixed parameters. $\square$

This is a descriptor acquisition bound conditional on the constant
named slice. The full-family preparation in §15 pays for its entire
scan; the descriptor is computed from that acquired array and is not
charged a second acquisition. A source-bearing retained name could
already encode $\chi$, so the constant-name premise is material.

## 13. Complete component resolution leaves an extensive common-law loss

**Theorem 13.1 (same local laws in every sector).** For every $m\ge3$
and $h\in\mathbb F_2^2$, put $P_h=P(\,\cdot\mid R_h)$.
At each original block, the full four-seed star under $P_h$ is uniform
on its eight even-parity assignments, exactly as under $P$. With the
same original owner grouping and all original weights retained,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L(P_h)&=\mathcal L(P)=\frac{5n}{3},\\
H(P)&=n+1,&H(P_h)&=n-1,\\
\Delta(P)&=\frac{2n}{3}-1,&
\Delta(P_h)&=\frac{2n}{3}+1,\\
\mathbb E_h\Delta(P_h)&=\Delta(P)+2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{OCC.38}
$$

The expectation uses the actual sector probabilities $1/4$.
The unconditioned value is the credited hard-parity case of
[GDB, Theorem 14.2][TorusGDB]; the conditional value concerns the
Gibbs law of each retained sector under the same product weights.

Proof. At a fixed vertex, its four adjacent face boundaries project
onto the four adjacent-pair flips of its four incident edges. These
span the complete three-dimensional even-parity subspace on the star:
they are the edge-incidence columns of a connected four-cycle, of
rank three. Every face boundary belongs to $B=\ker\chi$ within
$R_m$, by Theorem 11.1. The star projection of $B$ is therefore
the entire even-parity subspace; it cannot be larger because the
vertex equation imposes even parity.

Each $R_h$ is an affine translate of $B$ by an element of $R_m$.
Its star projection is the same full even subspace. A linear map on
a finite affine space has equally sized nonempty fibers, and $P_h$
is uniform on $R_h$. Its star law is thus uniform on those eight
rows. Mixing the four sectors gives exactly the same law under $P$.
This also proves directly that every individual edge is fair in
every sector.

The owned tuple is the east/north pair. The incoming groups are the
distinct west and south bits. A uniform even four-bit star has

$$
H(O_v)=2,\qquad H(O_v\mid B_{v,j})=2\ (j=1,2),\qquad
H(O_v\mid B_{v,1},B_{v,2})=1.
\tag{OCC.39}
$$

Its proper three-bit marginals are uniform; when both incoming bits
are fixed, its outgoing parity is fixed with two equiprobable outgoing
pairs. The $k=2$ coefficients of (OCC.10) are
$1/3,1/6,1/6,1/3$. The local contribution is
$2/3+2/6+2/6+1/3=5/3$. This proves both values of $\mathcal L$.
The cardinalities in Theorem 11.1 give the two global entropies, and
subtracting proves every defect identity in (OCC.38). Conditioning
restricts the original product weight to $R_h$; it substitutes no
separately chosen completion law or independent seed values.
$\square$

**Corollary 13.2 (a precise component-repair premise fails).** Consider
the proposed repair premise that, at fixed local depth, seed incidence,
owner-update budget and bounded number of owner components, supplying
the exact actual component label and every exact component normalizer
always makes the remaining common-law COK defect $o(n)$. Here the
remaining law is the exact conditional Gibbs law, the original owners
and incoming groups remain as prescribed, and no block or whole weight
is removed. This premise is false already at
$d=3,L=2,r=3,c=4$.

Proof. For every $m\ge7$ the four internally $r$-owner-connected
components and their exact weights are known. Complete resolution
conditions on $\chi=h$. Nevertheless for every attained $h$,

$$
\frac{\Delta(P_h)}{n}=\frac23+\frac1n\longrightarrow\frac23.
\tag{OCC.40}
$$

This contradicts the proposed vanishing normalized defect, even when
the source descriptor has been fully paid for. $\square$

The premise just defined is not attributed to $V_{\rm cond}$,
$V_{\rm swap}$, the full recursive grammar or its range extension.
It is stronger than the uniqueness statement in Lemma 3.2 and is not
one of its conclusions. Exact weighted component summation, actual
component acquisition and correlations inside a component are separate
obligations. Unlike a coordinate pin, conditioning on $\chi$ fixes no
individual seed here; none has been deleted from the entropy accounting.

## 14. Four complete names for an explicitly augmented observer task

**Definition 14.1 (the additional descriptor requirement).** On the
known slice $\{t_s:s\in R_m\}$ require the late reader both to
serve every original request of §1 and to recover $\chi(s)$ by the
time it produces its final predictor. It has the same zero-or-one

tables before its reply and every authentic count. The descriptor is

require it. Export fibers may have arbitrary correlations; queries may

**Theorem 14.2 (exact four-name optimum on this augmented slice).**
For every $m\ge3$, every deterministic complete export and reader
satisfying Definition 14.1 obeys

$$
\left|\{E(t_s):s\in R_m\}\right|\ge4.
\tag{OCC.41}
$$

Four names suffice, together with every original requested trajectory.
Consequently this augmented slice task has exact attained alphabet
four. The bound is not an original $M_1(D)$ lower bound.

Proof. Let $Q_h$ be uniform on $R_h$. By Theorem 13.1 every single
edge is fair under every $Q_h$. By Definition 12.1 the reply law of
every fixed finite original-root literal is therefore the same under
all four $Q_h$, whether that word is constant or observes one edge.

Fix one public original request $p_0$, for example $R$. For every
attained name $e$, its chosen literal $a_e$ and the function $f_e$
decoding the descriptor from its reply are fixed. A zero-Read branch
is represented by a fixed formal reply. Extend $f_e$ arbitrarily on
unattained replies. For $h\in\mathbb F_2^2$ put

$$
F_{e,h}=\{s\in R_m:f_e(r_{t_s}(a_e))=h\}.
\tag{OCC.42}
$$

For each $h$, the sets $F_{e,h}$ cover $R_h$: a source belongs to
the successful set for its own actual name. Hence
$1\le\sum_e Q_h(F_{e,h})$. For each fixed $e$, its reply has one
common law under all four $Q_h$, and the four outputs of $f_e$
are disjoint events in that reply alphabet. Thus

$$
\sum_hQ_h(F_{e,h})\le1.
\tag{OCC.43}
$$

Sum first over $h$ and then over names to get $4\le|E[R_m]|$.
No independence of the export and descriptor, no rectangular fiber,
and no restriction to target-prefix queries has been used.

For sufficiency export the four sector names $h=\chi(s)$ after
paid acquisition. The name itself supplies the extra output.
Each original target in the scaffold or padding is constant, and
each nonconstant graph target reads its one actual edge owner and
decodes its leaf factor using (OCC.32). Equation (OCC.5) gives every
subsequently supplied authentic-count reply. Every sector is nonempty,
so all four names are attained. The next section supplies this
acquisition as one terminating full-family preparation. $\square$

The common distribution argument, rather than a bound of four on
the raw reply alphabet, is essential: a single edge has the same
distribution in all sectors, even though its value can be correlated
with a source-dependent export. The proof explicitly handles that
correlation through the successful-set cover.
