# Prescribed binary codes: a nine-state escape from root-only two-call reading

## 1. Finite native trees and complete literal rows

**Definition 1.1 (native objects and literal replies).** A source is a finite
nonempty ordered tree in the free algebra

$$
\mathcal T::=\alpha\mid\beta\mid\langle\mathcal T,\mathcal T\rangle.
$$

Equality retains both the ordered brackets and the two different leaf labels.
In the native Boolean presentation, true is $\alpha$ and false is $\beta$;
the native address letters false and true are $L$ and $R$, respectively.
The substitution is

$$
\rho\alpha=\beta,\qquad
\rho\beta=E:=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,\qquad
\rho\langle s,t\rangle=\langle\rho s,\rho t\rangle.
$$

An address is a finite word in $L,R$, with empty word $\varepsilon$.
The existing occurrences of $t$ form $\operatorname{Pos}(t)$, and $t|_p$
is the subtree at an existing occurrence. The native reply $r_t(p)$ is
$\alpha$ or $\beta$ at that leaf, $\mathsf{branch}$ at a pair, and
$\mathsf{absent}$ at an address outside $\operatorname{Pos}(t)$.
Every finite word is legal. In particular, any nonempty continuation
from either leaf is absent. If $p$ exists, actual descent gives

$$
r_t(pw)=r_{t|_p}(w)\qquad(w\in\{L,R\}^{*}).
\tag{NRE.1}
$$

This identity includes suffixes that cease to exist inside the finite subtree.
Two equal subtrees at different addresses still have different occurrences.
These are the objects of *Native cut-birth provenance and literal past-row
recovery*, Definition 1.1.

**Definition 1.2 (image grammar and its finite inverse).** Write
$\mathcal I=\rho[\mathcal T]$. The native image description, used in that
volume's Proposition 9.3, is

$$
\mathcal I::=\beta\mid E\mid\langle\mathcal I,\mathcal I\rangle.
\tag{NRE.2}
$$

For completeness, substitution on the three source constructors shows
that every image is generated by this grammar. Conversely, each grammar
constructor has a source: $\alpha$ for $\beta$, $\beta$ for $E$, and
the pair of sources for a pair of images. There is no image equal to
$\alpha$. Thus $\beta$ is the only leaf in $\mathcal I$, and the three
grammar alternatives are disjoint: $E$ cannot be a pair of images because
its right child is $\alpha$. Consequently the following inverse is
unambiguous and terminates by recursion on finite trees:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta(\beta)&=\alpha,& \delta(E)&=\beta,\\
\delta\langle y,z\rangle&=\langle\delta y,\delta z\rangle
&& (y,z\in\mathcal I).
\end{aligned}
\tag{NRE.3}
$$

Induction on source constructors gives $\delta\rho=\mathrm{id}$;
induction on the disjoint image grammar gives
$\rho\delta=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal I}$. In particular $\rho$ is injective.
One consequence needed below is

$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{finite value }x& r_x(R)\\ \hline
\alpha\text{ or }\beta&\mathsf{absent}\\
E&\alpha\\
x\in\mathcal I\setminus\{\beta,E\}&\beta\text{ or }\mathsf{branch}
\end{array}
\tag{NRE.4}
$$

Indeed, in the last line $x=\langle y,z\rangle$ with $y,z\in\mathcal I$,
and the root of $z$ is $\beta$ or a branch. These are reused native image
identities, rather than an additional hypothesis about branch fillings.

**Definition 1.3 (whole occurrence row).** For a finite prefix-free cut
$P$, set $H_P(t)=P\cap\operatorname{Pos}(t)$. Replace the entire subtree
at each reached $p\in H_P(t)$ by its occurrence hole $\square_p$.
Keep every remaining bracket and concrete leaf; call the result $C_P(t)$.
The complete row is

$$
R_P(t)=(C_P(t),\pi_P(t)),\qquad
p\sim_{\pi_P(t)}q\ \Longleftrightarrow\ t|_p=t|_q.
\tag{NRE.5}
$$

Distinct classes assert disequality. An unreached member of $P$ has a
concrete blocking leaf at a strict prefix, exposed by the exterior.
Order reached holes lexicographically, with $L<R$. The canonical class
name is the ordinal position of its first reached hole, using zero-based
positions. Thus names need not be consecutive. Whenever holes are removed,
positions and first-occurrence names are computed again on the retained
ordered holes. The symbols used for classes below are aliases for these
names, never additional acquired values. This is the complete-row
convention of the owning volume's Definition 1.2.

## 2. The entire actual fiber and the five stored answers

**Definition 2.1 (fixed domain).** Fix $P=\{L,R\}^3$. The current
exterior $C_*$ is the perfect binary exterior with eight depth-three
occurrence holes, and its complete partition is

$$
\pi_*=\bigl\{\{LLL,LRL\},\{LLR\},\{LRR\},
                 \{RLL,RRL\},\{RLR\},\{RRR\}\bigr\}.
\tag{NRE.6}
$$

Write $R_*=(C_*,\pi_*)$. Use the six distinct class symbols
$D=\{a,c,b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4\}$ with representatives and occurrences

| Class | Representative $p_d$ | All occurrences |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $a$ | $LLL$ | $LLL,LRL$ |
| $c$ | $RLL$ | $RLL,RRL$ |
| $b_1$ | $LLR$ | $LLR$ |
| $b_2$ | $LRR$ | $LRR$ |
| $b_3$ | $RLR$ | $RLR$ |
| $b_4$ | $RRR$ | $RRR$ |

For a tuple $x=(x_a,x_c,x_{b_1},x_{b_2},x_{b_3},x_{b_4})$ of actual
finite values, its literal filling is

$$
\mathscr S(x)=
\left\langle
 \left\langle\langle x_a,x_{b_1}\rangle,
                   \langle x_a,x_{b_2}\rangle\right\rangle,
 \left\langle\langle x_c,x_{b_3}\rangle,
                   \langle x_c,x_{b_4}\rangle\right\rangle
\right\rangle.
\tag{NRE.7}
$$

The domain and required whole answer are exactly

$$
\mathcal U_*:=\{u\in\mathcal T:R_P(\rho u)=R_*\},\qquad F(u):=R_P(u).
\tag{NRE.8}
$$

For each $u$ in this domain, $t=\rho u=\mathscr S(x)$ with
$x_d=t|_{p_d}$ and six pairwise different values. Both occurrences of
$a$, and both of $c$, have the same respective value. Conversely an
injective tuple whose filling is in $\mathcal I$ has the unique actual
finite origin $\delta(\mathscr S(x))$ in $\mathcal U_*$. This is exactly
the full fiber in the owning volume's Definition 9.1; there is no bound
on source size and no further branch-family, history or predecessor promise.

$(q_1,q_2,q_3,q_4)=(LL,LR,RL,RR)$. Put $O_0=P$ and
$O_j=P\setminus\{q_jL,q_jR\}$ for $1\le j\le4$.
Define $R_j=(C_j,\pi_j)$ by the following full exteriors:

$$
\begin{aligned}

                  \langle\square_{LRL},\square_{LRR}\rangle\right\rangle,

                  \langle\square_{RRL},\square_{RRR}\rangle\right\rangle
\right\rangle,\\

                  \langle\square_{RRL},\square_{RRR}\rangle\right\rangle
\right\rangle,\\
C_2={}&\left\langle
 \left\langle\langle\square_{LLL},\square_{LLR}\rangle,\beta\right\rangle,

                  \langle\square_{RRL},\square_{RRR}\rangle\right\rangle

C_3={}&\left\langle

                  \langle\square_{LRL},\square_{LRR}\rangle\right\rangle,

C_4={}&\left\langle
 \left\langle\langle\square_{LLL},\square_{LLR}\rangle,

The complete partitions, in order of first retained occurrence, are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\pi_0={}&\{\{LLL,LRL\},\{LLR\},\{LRR\},
                      \{RLL,RRL\},\{RLR\},\{RRR\}\},\\
\pi_1={}&\{\{LRL\},\{LRR\},\{RLL,RRL\},\{RLR\},\{RRR\}\},\\
\pi_2={}&\{\{LLL\},\{LLR\},\{RLL,RRL\},\{RLR\},\{RRR\}\},\\
\pi_3={}&\{\{LLL,LRL\},\{LLR\},\{LRR\},\{RRL\},\{RRR\}\},\\
\pi_4={}&\{\{LLL,LRL\},\{LLR\},\{LRR\},\{RLL\},\{RLR\}\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{NRE.10}
$$

For explicit canonical names, the following vectors assign to each
retained hole the position of its class's first retained occurrence:

| Row | Ordered reached holes | Canonical name vector | Blocked cuts and their concrete guard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

All different classes in every displayed partition remain disequal.
These are the complete answers of the owning volume's Proposition 9.3.
In $R_1,R_2$ the unremoved occurrence of $a$ remains a hole; in $R_3,R_4$
the unremoved occurrence of $c$ remains a hole. A concrete guard replaces
one pair of occurrences, not an entire repeated class.

## 3. Acquisition, the prescribed bit and the counted runtime

**Assumption 3.1 (two phases and supplied services).** The actual origin
is one $u\in\mathcal U_*$. Exactly one genuine authenticated substitution
has updated that same register to $t=\rho u$, with the same root and cut.
There are no intervening resets, rotations, source replacements, arbitrary
edits or extra updates. Authentication and version binding certify this
relationship without distinguishing $u$ within the fiber.
The complete current row is lawfully acquired on that stationary version.

In the first phase a producer lawfully possesses genuinely acquired finite
current data from this same stationary version: either the lossless tree
$t$, or the complete row $R_*$ and one finite acquired value $x_d$ for
each of the six classes. The complete partition's equality tests are
comparisons of acquired terms. The producer may make finite local
computations on these data. It computes the specified complete binary
code and actually exports it with the complete current row before a
complete access cut. This premise neither grants equality tests on
unknown subtrees nor supplies the predecessor as input.

In the second phase a supplied lawful common entry initializes a common
reader using exactly the complete code, $P,R_*$ and source-independent
controls. These declared inputs must be recoverable from its entry state.
No further source-dependent field is accessible: predecessor data, origin
identity, acquired values, alpha labels, traces, caches, old outputs,
archives, pointers, allocation, control fields, acquisition order or
counts, lengths, meters, timing and update packets convey no extra
distinction. Data determined by the declared payload do not add a
distinction; any other accessible field changes this assumption.
Inaccessibility of discarded material does not require physical erasure.

The same current register stays stationary during reading. Its sole
source port accepts each finite literal address and returns the four
native replies of Definition 1.1. No equality, image, provenance,
generation, inverse or extra subtree port is supplied. Every invocation
is charged, including repeats, absent continuations and addresses used
in prior acquisition. Every origin in $\mathcal U_*$ must terminate with
the complete $F(u)$.

Acquisition, finite acquired-term computation, actual export, access
isolation, common installation, authentication, version binding and lawful
entry are supplied operations with separate prices. In particular a stopped
producer has an absorbing stop; any subsequent reader entry is a separately
supplied invocation. A full traversal can acquire a finite tree, but its
retained trace alone does not provide this narrower handoff. These are
the operational premises of the owning volume's Assumptions 1.3, 8.1,
9.2 and Definition 15.1, specialized to one update. They are hypotheses
of the results below, not deductions from a row equation.

**Definition 3.2 (fixed-code stationary Moore readers).** A reader has
one finite installed disjoint set $\mathcal K=Q\sqcup H$, a fixed
address $\operatorname{addr}(k)\in\{L,R\}^{*}$ for each $k\in Q$,
and fixed functions

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{next}&:Q\times
 \{\alpha,\beta,\mathsf{branch},\mathsf{absent}\}\longrightarrow\mathcal K,\\
\operatorname{out}&:H\longrightarrow\{\text{complete rows at }P\},\\
\iota&:\sigma[\mathcal U_*]\longrightarrow\mathcal K.
\end{aligned}
\tag{NRE.11}
$$

The table is common to every source. The initial state is
$\iota(\sigma(u))$, and the complete prescribed bit is recoverable on
its attained range. At a Read state $k\in Q$, exactly one actual call
at its installed literal is made, and the reply $z$ replaces the state
by $\operatorname{next}(k,z)$. At a Halt $k\in H$, the fixed entire row
$\operatorname{out}(k)$ is returned without another call. Every Read
has all four installed successors, including unattained ones.

The current state is the entire mutable accessible memory after entry.
There is no surviving code register, free output decoder, computed address,
extra program counter, phase, reply register, history, counter, clock, seed,
age, archive or source-dependent table selector. There is no free control
transition between two states. Every installed state counts, including
unused states, duplicate-output halts and states reached only on some
origins. Each running visit counts one invocation. Sharing, repeated
queries, early halts and cycles in a union of different-source paths
are allowed, subject to termination on each actual source.

An arbitrary-literal reader may install any finite native words. A
representative-root-only reader may install only $p_d$ for $d\in D$.
Both use this identical contract and the identical pointwise code
$\sigma$; the producer is not allowed to recode it for the root reader.
This is the fixed-code restriction of the owning volume's Definitions
11.1 and 15.1, with the state upper bound left as a parameter.

The optimized quantities below are installed abstract state cardinality
and worst-case fresh invocation count. First-phase acquisition and local
computation, table installation, fixed whole-output representation,
address construction, storage and transmission, export, authentication,
isolation, entry, transition work and physical service have separate
costs. The finite tree sizes are unbounded across the domain. Neither
the producer's work nor its workspace is bounded by the reader's state
cardinality or call bound.

**Definition 3.3 (the prescribed structure-dependent bit).** For
$u\in\mathcal U_*$ let $h(u)=j$ if $x_{b_j}=\alpha$, and let $h(u)=0$
if none of these four values is $\alpha$. The complete partition makes
the four right-class values different, so this definition is unambiguous.
Prescribe

$$
\sigma(u)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 h(u)\in\{1,2\} \lor\
 \bigl(h(u)=3\ \land\ x_{b_2}=E\bigr)\ \lor\
 \bigl(h(u)=0\ \land\ x_{b_2}\notin\{\beta,E\}\bigr),
\tag{NRE.12}
$$

and set $\sigma(u)=1$ in every remaining case. This is a specified
function of the actual acquired data on the entire original domain.

## 4. A common nine-state arbitrary-literal reader

**Theorem 4.1 (lawful full-fiber realization).** Under Assumption 3.1,
the bit (NRE.12) is computable by a terminating finite computation on the
acquired current data and has attained range $\{0,1\}$. It varies between
actual sources with the same class roots and the same whole target.
There is one reader for this bit with exactly four Read states and five
whole-row Halt states, all actually reachable, correct on every
$u\in\mathcal U_*$ with at most two actual native calls. Its worst-case
call count is attained. Its complete installed table is

| Read state | Actual literal | On $\alpha$ | On $\beta$ | On $\mathsf{branch}$ | On $\mathsf{absent}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $A$ | $LLR$ | $H_1$ | $B$ | $B$ | $B$ |
| $B$ | $LRRR$ | $H_3$ | $H_0$ | $H_0$ | $H_2$ |
| $C$ | $RLR$ | $H_3$ | $D$ | $D$ | $D$ |
| $D$ | $RRR$ | $H_4$ | $H_0$ | $H_0$ | $H_0$ |

Initialization is $\iota(0)=A$, $\iota(1)=C$. Each $H_j$, for
$0\le j\le4$, stores and returns the entire $R_j$ of Definition 2.2.

**Proof.** First make explicit the actual-fiber calculation supplied by
the owning volume's Proposition 9.3. In $\mathscr S(x)$ the root and
both depth-one nodes are branches whose right children are branches.
They cannot be the special block $E$. The image grammar therefore forces
all four depth-two subtrees at $q_j$ to be images. Each is either $E$
or a pair of images. Consequently $x_a,x_c\in\mathcal I$ and either
all six values are images, or one $x_{b_j}=\alpha$ with its left partner
equal to $\beta$. There cannot be two such $j$, since the right classes
are different. In the latter case, every other class value is an image
different from the forced beta and hence a branch image.

Conversely, any injective tuple of these forms has all four $q_j$ blocks
in $\mathcal I$ and therefore has its entire filling in $\mathcal I$.
Its inverse (NRE.3) is an actual finite origin in $\mathcal U_*$. This
proves the exact necessary and sufficient conditions; it introduces no
restriction on the remaining finite branch images. In the all-image
case $h=0$, beta can occupy any one of the six classes or none. In a
positive case $h=j$, the partner is the unique beta class, $b_j$ is
the unique alpha class, and all four remaining classes are arbitrary
distinct branch images avoiding those leaves. These are precisely the
whole root fibers $Z_d,Z_*,\Pi_j$ of the owning Proposition 15.2.

In the zero case the inverse has an old hole at every member of $P$.
Its hole values are $\delta(x_d)$, so injectivity preserves all the
equalities and disequalities; its whole row is $R_0$. In a positive
case the selected $E$ block inverts to the concrete beta leaf at $q_j$.
Each other depth-two block is a pair of images and inverts to a pair.
The old holes are exactly $O_j$, with equality of their predecessor
values equivalent to equality of their current values. Thus

$$
F(u)=R_{h(u)}\qquad(u\in\mathcal U_*).
\tag{NRE.13}
$$

The other occurrence of the selected repeated partner belongs to a
different block and stays an old hole. Its predecessor value can be
alpha even though the newly concrete parent is beta. Definitions
2.2's restricted partitions and concrete guards follow exactly from
this occurrence distinction. Their different reached sets show that
$R_0,\ldots,R_4$ are pairwise different. This recovers the native
ingredients for the present proof and attributes them to Proposition
9.3, rather than counting them as a new classification.

The producer can compute $h$ by inspecting its four acquired right-class
terms for the literal $\alpha$, then compare its acquired $x_{b_2}$ with
the two fixed finite terms $\beta,E$. All these are terminating syntactic
operations on finite acquired terms. When the acquired input is the
lossless tree, local traversal of that already acquired tree at the six
fixed representatives supplies the same terms. No unknown-value test,
predecessor input or additional source invocation is used. Assumption
3.1 supplies actual export of the resulting complete bit and its lawful
entry. The different states $A,C$ recover that bit by the fixed entry
map $A\mapsto0,C\mapsto1$, with no separate bit register after entry.

To see actual attainments and structural variation, put $T_n=\rho^n\alpha$.
Then $T_0=\alpha,T_1=\beta,T_2=E,T_3=\langle E,\beta\rangle$.
All $T_n$ are different: an equality with unequal indices cancels by
injectivity to put $\alpha$ in a positive image, which is impossible.
For $n\ge2$, $T_n$ is a finite branch image. Therefore distinct branch
fillings can always be chosen avoiding any fixed finite list.
Every positive case is attained by assigning its forced leaves and
four such distinct branch images. In particular $h=1$ attains code zero
and $h=4$ attains code one.

More concretely, in class order $(a,c,b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4)$ the two tuples

$$
(T_3,\beta,T_4,T_2,\alpha,T_5),\qquad
(T_3,\beta,T_4,T_6,\alpha,T_5)
\tag{NRE.14}
$$

are injective actual fillings in the positive case $h=3$. Their finite
origins are obtained by $\delta\mathscr S$. They have exactly the same
class roots and the same complete answer $R_3$. Since $T_2=E$ and
$T_6\ne E$, their prescribed bits are zero and one respectively.
Thus no implicit replacement by a root-factored code has occurred.

Now fix an arbitrary original $u\in\mathcal U_*$. By (NRE.1), the
literal $LRRR=p_{b_2}R$ at $B$ reads exactly $r_{x_{b_2}}(R)$.
It is one actual invocation of the original port, including when the
suffix does not exist. The exhaustive cases are as follows.

If $h=1$, the bit is zero, $A$ reads alpha at $b_1$ and returns $H_1$
after one call. If $h=2$, the bit is zero and $b_1$ is non-alpha, so
$A$ enters $B$. Here $x_{b_2}=\alpha$; its $R$ continuation is absent.
The second paid call takes $B$ to $H_2$.

If $h=3$ and $x_{b_2}=E$, the bit is zero. The non-alpha reply at
$A$ leads to $B$, whose actual reply is alpha by (NRE.4), giving $H_3$.
If $h=3$ and $x_{b_2}\ne E$, the bit is one, and $C$ reads alpha
at $b_3$ and gives $H_3$ in one call. This second subcase includes
every other permitted branch shape at $b_2$.

If $h=4$, the bit is one. The non-alpha at $b_3$ takes $C$ to $D$;
the alpha at $b_4$ then gives $H_4$.

Finally let $h=0$. If $x_{b_2}\notin\{\beta,E\}$, the bit is zero,
and the non-alpha at $A$ takes the run to $B$. Since $x_{b_2}$ is an
image different from beta and $E$, (NRE.4) gives beta or branch at its
right child. Either transition returns $H_0$. If
$x_{b_2}\in\{\beta,E\}$, the bit is one, and the two non-alpha
representative replies at $C,D$ give $H_0$. This covers beta at $a$,
at $c$, at any $b_j$, or nowhere, and every allowable finite branch
filling in every zero profile. No internal shape or source-size bound
has entered the argument.

Every returned row is therefore exactly (NRE.13). From $A$ the next
state is a halt or $B$, and from $C$ it is a halt or $D$; both $B,D$
always lead to halts. Thus there are at most two calls. The sixteen
transition entries in the table are all installed constants. The absence
entries at representative roots are unattained, but the absence entry
at $B$ is actually used in the case $h=2$ and is charged.

All states are genuinely reached. Positive cases $h=1,2$ respectively
reach $A,H_1$ and $A,B,H_2$. The first tuple in (NRE.14) reaches
$A,B,H_3$. A zero-case injective tuple
$(\beta,T_4,T_5,T_3,T_6,T_7)$ reaches $A,B,H_0$, since the right
child of $T_3$ is beta. A positive case $h=4$, for example the actual
tuple $(T_3,\beta,T_4,T_5,T_6,\alpha)$, reaches $C,D,H_4$.
This reaches all four Reads and all five Halts and attains two calls.
All nine installed states are counted. Each halt contains precisely
its entire exterior, partition and guards in (NRE.9)--(NRE.10), with
the displayed canonical names; no later decoder supplies those data.

The words at $A,C,D$ have length three and that at $B$ has length four.
Those address and submission costs, the representation of the table
and all five fixed whole outputs, and the operations in Assumption
3.1 remain separate from the nine-state and two-invocation bounds.
The state transitions neither compute addresses nor retain any hidden
phase or workspace. This proves the asserted realization under exactly
the prescribed bit. $\square$

## 5. Four actual origins exclude every two-call root-only reader

**Theorem 5.1 (same-code universal root obstruction).** For the very
same bit (NRE.12), no representative-root-only reader of Definition 3.2
can return $F(u)$ on all of $\mathcal U_*$ in at most two calls, for
any finite number of installed states. The exclusion also holds if
either occurrence root of a repeated class is allowed.

**Proof.** Use the finite orbit $T_n$ and inverse already established.
The following are four fixed current-value tuples, all in class order
$(a,c,b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4)$:

| Origin name | $x_a$ | $x_c$ | $x_{b_1}$ | $x_{b_2}$ | $x_{b_3}$ | $x_{b_4}$ | $h$ | $\sigma$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $v_0$ | $\beta$ | $T_4$ | $T_5$ | $T_3$ | $T_6$ | $T_7$ | $0$ | $0$ |
| $v_1$ | $\beta$ | $T_3$ | $\alpha$ | $T_5$ | $T_6$ | $T_7$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| $v_2$ | $\beta$ | $T_3$ | $T_4$ | $\alpha$ | $T_6$ | $T_7$ | $2$ | $0$ |
| $v_3$ | $T_3$ | $\beta$ | $T_4$ | $T_2$ | $\alpha$ | $T_5$ | $3$ | $0$ |

For each row $i$, let $t_i=\mathscr S(x^{(i)})$ and
$v_i=\delta(t_i)$. These are actual finite sources, as follows.
The six values in each tuple are pairwise distinct by orbit distinctness.
The first tuple has only image values. In the next three, the sole
nonimage is the displayed alpha at $b_1,b_2,b_3$ respectively, and its
left partner is beta. All other entries are images. The exact image
calculation in the proof of Theorem 4.1 therefore puts $t_i$ in
$\mathcal I$, and $\rho v_i=t_i$. Literal filling preserves both
occurrences of $a$ and $c$ with all six-class disequalities, so
$R_P(t_i)=R_*$ and $v_i\in\mathcal U_*$. Explicit inverse expressions
for these origins are

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_0={}&\left\langle
 \left\langle\langle\alpha,T_4\rangle,\langle\alpha,T_2\rangle\right\rangle,
 \left\langle\langle T_3,T_5\rangle,\langle T_3,T_6\rangle\right\rangle
\right\rangle,\\

codes are zero and all public entry fields agree, so every run begins
at exactly the same state $\iota'(0)$. The five different required rows
exclude an initial Halt. Set

$$
G_a=\{z_a,z_1,z_2\},\qquad G_c=\{z_c,z_3,z_4\}.
\tag{NRE.19}
$$

The representative-root matrix gives every possible first query:
$a$ or $c$ separates $G_a$ from $G_c$, and $b_j$ isolates exactly
$z_j$. On every subset of the witnesses each possible root has at most
two actual replies. From a common state, one remaining Read can
therefore supply at most two different whole outputs. An earlier Halt
supplies only one. After the last permitted invocation any attained
successor must be a Halt, since a Read would make another paid call
and no free transition or decoder is available. This justifies the
one-remaining-call bound even with sharing and early stopping.

Suppose the first query isolates $z_j$. On its other reply the remaining
witnesses are the two zeros and the three other positives. They require
four different rows and have at most two calls left. Halting there
cannot be correct. If the second query is a partner, its reply selecting
the group not containing $z_j$ leaves that entire group untouched: it
has one zero and two positives, requiring three different rows with
one call left. If the second query is $b_k$ for $k\ne j$, its
nonisolating reply leaves the two zeros and two positives, again three
different rows with one call left. If it repeats $b_j$, the reply is
constant and leaves four different rows with one call left. These
exhaust the root menu, and each contradicts the preceding bound.
Thus no singleton root can be the first query. Both possible partner
occurrence representatives have exactly the same replies as their
class representatives, so they add no alternative split.

The first query must therefore be $a$ or $c$, with one live branch
containing precisely $G_a$ and the other precisely $G_c$. On $G_a$
there are three different required rows $R_0,R_1,R_2$ and at most two
calls. A Halt cannot serve them. Both partner queries and $b_3,b_4$
are constant on this set; a call at any of them would leave three
rows for one final call. Consequently the second query must be $b_1$
or $b_2$. It isolates its positive witness. On the other reply the
remaining zero and other positive witness have identical replies at
every root except the other right-class root. They still require two
different rows. Thus an actual final call at that other root is
necessary. In particular the code-zero zero-target path on this branch
queries both $b_1$ and $b_2$, in whichever order the competitor chose.

On $G_c$ the three required rows are $R_0,R_3,R_4$. Both partners
and $b_1,b_2$ are constant there. The same two-call argument forces
$b_3$ or $b_4$ second, and the other one third on the nonisolating
reply. The zero-target path on this branch therefore queries both
$b_3$ and $b_4$. Stopping on an isolated positive is allowed throughout;
it does not remove either necessary zero-target continuation.

Thus actual required runs invoke at least five different root-address
classes: the first partner and each of $b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4$. A Read state
has one fixed literal, so these five classes require at least five
different installed Reads. Choosing the other partner occurrence still
uses a literal distinct from every right-class root and cannot reduce
this count. Independently the six actual origins require all five
pairwise distinct entire rows, so there must be at least five different
fixed-output Halts. Reads and Halts are disjoint. This proves the total
lower bound ten.

The proof constrains the competing table itself; it assumes none of
Theorem 8.4's paths, addresses or initialization choices. Histories may
merge, Halts may be shared, states may be duplicated, and cycles may
occur in the union of different-source paths. None changes the necessary
fixed addresses or the number of distinct fixed whole outputs. A repeated
query remains a paid query; an unattained absence edge at a reached root
provides no additional reply. Unused installed states only increase the
count. The six origins refute a full-domain correctness promise and do
not replace that domain. This proves the universal exclusion, including
all faithful code entries and all permitted occurrence representatives.
$\square$
