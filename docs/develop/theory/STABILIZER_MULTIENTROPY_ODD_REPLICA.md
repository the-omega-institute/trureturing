# Stabilizer multi-entropy at every odd replica index

This volume is reference input. Lean declarations and their checked proof terms carry the mathematical truth; nothing in this volume is kernel-verified. The atomizer is `generic-v1`. Existing text is append-only; corrections and additions belong after the final append anchor.

## 1. Scope

For a normalized pure qubit stabilizer state whose qubits are assigned to $\mathtt q$ labelled parties, and for every odd replica index $n\ge3$, the replica partition function $Z_n^{(\mathtt q)}$ is an explicit power of $2$ determined by ranks of matrices over the field $\mathbb F_2(\zeta)$ generated by a primitive $n$-th root of unity $\zeta$: one rank for each nonzero character $t$ of the replica group $(\mathbb Z/n)^{\mathtt q-1}$, namely the rank of $\Lambda_t\Gamma+\Gamma\Lambda_t$, where $\Gamma$ is the adjacency matrix of a graph form of the state and $\Lambda_t$ is a diagonal matrix of powers of $\zeta$ read off from the colouring (Theorem 3.1). When the character takes at most three values on the occupied parties, this rank equals the $\mathbb F_2$-rank of $\Gamma$ masked to pairs of qubits separated by the fibres of the character (Proposition 4.1); this gives an exact decomposition of $S_n^{(\mathtt q)}$ into a sum over partitions of the parties into two or three blocks and a remainder over characters with at least four fibres (Corollary 4.2). The remainder does not reduce to masked ranks: for the four-cycle graph state with its vertices in four distinct parties, every odd $n\ge5$ and every $\mathtt q\ge4$, the masked-rank expression is off by exactly $\tfrac{(n-2)(n-3)}{n^2}\log2$ (Proposition 5.1).

## 2. Notation and conventions

**Convention 2.1 (Parties and colourings).** Fix $\mathtt q\ge2$ and write $[\mathtt q]=\{1,\dots,\mathtt q\}$. A $\mathtt q$-party pure qubit state is a unit vector $\psi\in(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes N}$ together with a colouring $\mathrm{col}:[N]\to[\mathtt q]$; party $c$ consists of the qubits $u$ with $\mathrm{col}(u)=c$, and parties are allowed to be empty. A party is occupied if it contains at least one qubit. A stabilizer state is a common eigenvector, with eigenvalue $1$, of $N$ independent commuting elements of the $N$-qubit Pauli group not containing $-I$.

**Convention 2.2 (Replica multi-entropy).** Let $n\ge2$ and $G=(\mathbb Z/n)^{\mathtt q-1}$. Following Appendix A of Akella, Iizuka and Miyata, the replicas are labelled by $r\in G$; party $c<\mathtt q$ acts on replica labels by $r\mapsto r+\delta_c$, where $\delta_c$ is the $c$-th standard basis vector of $G$, and the last party acts by the identity, $\delta_{\mathtt q}=0$. In the computational basis,

$$
Z_n^{(\mathtt q)}=\sum_{x:G\to\{0,1\}^N}\ \prod_{r\in G}\overline{\psi(x_r)}\,\psi\big(u\mapsto x_{r+\delta_{\mathrm{col}(u)}}(u)\big),
$$

and

$$
S_n^{(\mathtt q)}=\frac{1}{1-n}\,\frac{1}{n^{\mathtt q-2}}\,\log\frac{Z_n^{(\mathtt q)}}{\big(Z_1^{(\mathtt q)}\big)^{n^{\mathtt q-1}}}.
$$

For a normalized state $Z_1^{(\mathtt q)}=1$. Equivalently, $Z_n^{(\mathtt q)}=\langle\psi^{\otimes G}|P|\psi^{\otimes G}\rangle$, where $P=\bigotimes_{u\in[N]}P_u$ and $P_u$ permutes the $|G|$ copies of qubit $u$ by the translation $r\mapsto r+\delta_{\mathrm{col}(u)}$.

**Convention 2.3 (Graph forms).** For a symmetric matrix $\Gamma\in\mathbb F_2^{N\times N}$ with zero diagonal, the graph state is $|\Gamma\rangle=2^{-N/2}\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^N}(-1)^{\sum_{u<v}\Gamma_{uv}x_ux_v}|x\rangle$. Every stabilizer state equals $\lambda\,(U_1\otimes\cdots\otimes U_N)|\Gamma\rangle$ for some such $\Gamma$, single-qubit Clifford unitaries $U_u$ and a scalar $\lambda$ with $|\lambda|=1$ (Van den Nest, Dehaene and De Moor, Theorem 1). Such a $\Gamma$ is called a graph form of $\psi$.

**Convention 2.4 (Characters, fibres and the matrices $M_t$).** Let $n\ge3$ be odd. Fix a primitive $n$-th root of unity $\zeta$ in an algebraic closure of $\mathbb F_2$, and put $F=\mathbb F_2(\zeta)$; it is the field with $2^m$ elements, $m$ the multiplicative order of $2$ modulo $n$. For $t\in G$ let $f_t:[\mathtt q]\to\mathbb Z/n$, $f_t(c)=t\cdot\delta_c$, so $f_t(c)=t_c$ for $c<\mathtt q$ and $f_t(\mathtt q)=0$. Let $\pi_t$ be the partition of $[\mathtt q]$ into the nonempty fibres of $f_t$. Given a colouring, put

$$
\Lambda_t=\operatorname{diag}\big(\zeta^{f_t(\mathrm{col}(u))}\big)_{u\in[N]},\qquad M_t=\Lambda_t\Gamma+\Gamma\Lambda_t,\qquad \rho_t=\operatorname{rank}_FM_t .
$$

Thus $(M_t)_{uv}=\Gamma_{uv}\big(\zeta^{f_t(\mathrm{col}(u))}+\zeta^{f_t(\mathrm{col}(v))}\big)$. The matrix $M_t$ is symmetric with zero diagonal, so it is alternating in characteristic $2$ and $\rho_t$ is even. Since $\Lambda_0=I$, $M_0=0$.

**Convention 2.5 (Set partitions and masked ranks).** $\Pi(\mathtt q)$ is the set of set partitions of $[\mathtt q]$, $|\pi|$ the number of blocks of $\pi$, and $\Pi_k(\mathtt q)$ the partitions with exactly $k$ blocks. For $\pi\in\Pi(\mathtt q)$ the masked adjacency matrix $\Gamma^\pi\in\mathbb F_2^{N\times N}$ is given by $\Gamma^\pi_{uv}=\Gamma_{uv}$ if $\mathrm{col}(u)$ and $\mathrm{col}(v)$ lie in different blocks of $\pi$, and $\Gamma^\pi_{uv}=0$ otherwise, and $r(\pi)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}\Gamma^\pi$. For an integer $a$ and $k\ge0$, $(a)_k=a(a-1)\cdots(a-k+1)$ is the falling factorial, with $(a)_0=1$.

## 3. The rank formula at odd replica index

**Theorem 3.1 (Odd replica rank formula).** Let $n\ge3$ be odd and $\mathtt q\ge2$. For every normalized pure qubit stabilizer state $\psi$ on $N$ qubits, every graph form $\Gamma$ of $\psi$ and every colouring $\mathrm{col}:[N]\to[\mathtt q]$, empty parties allowed, $Z_n^{(\mathtt q)}$ is a positive real number and

$$
Z_n^{(\mathtt q)}=2^{-\frac12\sum_{0\ne t\in G}\operatorname{rank}_F\left(\Lambda_t\Gamma+\Gamma\Lambda_t\right)},\qquad S_n^{(\mathtt q)}=\frac{\log2}{2(n-1)\,n^{\mathtt q-2}}\sum_{0\ne t\in G}\rho_t .
$$

Stance: suspected-novel (§8) for odd $n\ge5$ together with $\mathtt q\ge4$; the cases $\mathtt q\le3$ are literature-attested and the case $n=3$ is repo-derived, as recorded in §8.

**Proof.** *Reduction to a graph state.* Write $\psi=\lambda(U_1\otimes\cdots\otimes U_N)|\Gamma\rangle$ (Convention 2.3). In the form $Z_n^{(\mathtt q)}=\langle\psi^{\otimes G}|P|\psi^{\otimes G}\rangle$ of Convention 2.2, the operator $P$ permutes the copies of each qubit among themselves, so it commutes with $\big(U_1\otimes\cdots\otimes U_N\big)^{\otimes G}$, and the scalar contributes $|\lambda|^{2|G|}=1$. Hence $Z_n^{(\mathtt q)}$ is the same for $\psi$ and for $|\Gamma\rangle$, and it suffices to treat $\psi=|\Gamma\rangle$ with the given $\Gamma$.

*The quadratic form.* Write $\delta_u=\delta_{\mathrm{col}(u)}$ and, for $x:G\to\{0,1\}^N$, let $x_u\in\mathbb F_2^G$ be the function $r\mapsto x_r(u)$. All amplitudes of $|\Gamma\rangle$ are real, and each of the $2|G|$ factors carries the normalisation $2^{-N/2}$, so

$$
Z_n^{(\mathtt q)}=2^{-N|G|}\sum_{x}(-1)^{\Phi(x)},\qquad \Phi(x)=\sum_{u<v}\Gamma_{uv}\sum_{r\in G}\Big(x_r(u)x_r(v)+x_{r+\delta_u}(u)\,x_{r+\delta_v}(v)\Big)\in\mathbb F_2 .
$$

For $a,b\in\mathbb F_2^G$ put $\langle a,b\rangle=\sum_ra(r)b(r)$ and $(T_sa)(r)=a(r+s)$. Re-indexing the second sum by $r\mapsto r-\delta_v$ gives $\Phi(x)=\sum_{u<v}\Gamma_{uv}\big(\langle x_u,x_v\rangle+\langle T_{\delta_u-\delta_v}x_u,x_v\rangle\big)$.

*Fourier coordinates.* For $a\in\mathbb F_2^G$ and $t\in G$ let $\hat a(t)=\sum_ra(r)\zeta^{t\cdot r}\in F$. For $0\ne w\in G$ some coordinate $w_i$ is nonzero, $\zeta^{w_i}\ne1$, and $\sum_{t_i\in\mathbb Z/n}\zeta^{t_iw_i}=(\zeta^{nw_i}-1)/(\zeta^{w_i}-1)=0$; hence $\sum_{t\in G}\zeta^{t\cdot w}$ equals $|G|$ for $w=0$ and $0$ otherwise. Since $|G|=n^{\mathtt q-1}$ is odd, $|G|=1$ in $F$, and this gives the inversion formula $a(r)=\sum_t\hat a(t)\zeta^{-t\cdot r}$ and the Parseval identity $\langle a,b\rangle=\sum_{t\in G}\hat a(t)\,\hat b(-t)$. Moreover $\widehat{T_sa}(t)=\zeta^{-t\cdot s}\hat a(t)$. With $X_u(t)=\hat x_u(t)$ and $\lambda_u=\lambda_u(t)=\zeta^{t\cdot\delta_u}=\zeta^{f_t(\mathrm{col}(u))}$,

$$
\Phi(x)=\sum_{t\in G}\varphi_t,\qquad \varphi_t=\sum_{u<v}\Gamma_{uv}\,\frac{\lambda_u+\lambda_v}{\lambda_u}\,X_u(t)\,X_v(-t),
$$

using $1+\zeta^{-t\cdot(\delta_u-\delta_v)}=1+\lambda_v/\lambda_u$.

*Frobenius orbits.* The group $G$ is partitioned into the orbits of $t\mapsto2t$. The orbit $O$ of $t$ has $k=|O|$ elements, $k$ being the multiplicative order of $2$ modulo the order of $t$. Since the entries of $x_u$ lie in $\mathbb F_2$, $X_u(2t)=X_u(t)^2$, so $X_u(t)^{2^k}=X_u(t)$ and $X_u(t)$ lies in the subfield $K_O\subseteq F$ with $2^k$ elements; likewise $\varphi_{2t}=\varphi_t^2$ and $\varphi_t\in K_O$, so $\sum_{s\in O}\varphi_s=\operatorname{Tr}_{K_O/\mathbb F_2}\varphi_t$. Choose a representative $t_O$ of each orbit. The $\mathbb F_2$-linear map $\mathbb F_2^G\to\prod_OK_O$, $a\mapsto(\hat a(t_O))_O$, is injective, because the representatives determine $\hat a$ on all of $G$ and the inversion formula recovers $a$; both sides have $\mathbb F_2$-dimension $\sum_O|O|=|G|$, so it is a bijection. Applying it to each $x_u$, the sum over $x$ becomes a sum over independent vectors $X^{O}=(X_u(t_O))_u\in K_O^N$, and $\Phi$ is a sum of terms each depending on the coordinates of one orbit, or of one orbit and its negative. The sum over $x$ is therefore a product of the following factors.

*The orbit $\{0\}$.* Here $\lambda_u=1$ for all $u$, so $\varphi_0=0$, and the factor is $2^N=2^{N-\rho_0/2}$.

*Paired orbits.* Let $O\ne-O$, with $t=t_O$, $K=K_O$, and take $-t$ as the representative of $-O$. Then $X=X(t)$ and $X'=X(-t)$ range independently over $K^N$, and the orbits $O$ and $-O$ contribute $\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb F_2}(\varphi_t+\varphi_{-t})$. Since $\lambda_u(-t)=\lambda_u^{-1}$,

$$
\varphi_t+\varphi_{-t}=\sum_{u\ne v}\Gamma_{uv}\,\frac{\lambda_u+\lambda_v}{\lambda_u}\,X_uX'_v=Y^{\mathsf T}M_tX',\qquad Y=\Lambda_t^{-1}X .
$$

As $X\mapsto\Lambda_t^{-1}X$ is a bijection of $K^N$ and the trace form of $K/\mathbb F_2$ is nondegenerate, $\sum_{Y,X'}(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}(Y^{\mathsf T}M_tX')}=|K|^N\cdot\#\{Y:Y^{\mathsf T}M_t=0\}=|K|^{2N-\rho_t}$; the rank over $K$ equals the rank over $F$ because rank is unchanged by field extension. The factor is $2^{2k(N-\rho_t/2)}$, a product over the $2k$ characters of $O\cup-O$.

*Self-paired orbits.* Let $O=-O\ne\{0\}$ with $t=t_O$ and $K=K_O$. Then $-t=2^jt$ for some $0\le j<k$, and $j\ne0$ because $t\ne-t$ for $t\ne0$ when $n$ is odd; $2^{2j}t=t$ forces $k\mid2j$, so $k=2h$ with $j=h$. Let $L\subset K$ be the subfield with $2^h$ elements and $\bar z=z^{2^h}$ the conjugation of $K$ over $L$. Then $X(-t)=\overline{X(t)}$ and $\bar\lambda_u=\lambda_u(-t)=\lambda_u^{-1}$. The orbit contributes $\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb F_2}\varphi_t=\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb F_2}(\varphi_t+\bar\varphi_t)$, and with $X=X(t)$,

$$
\varphi_t+\bar\varphi_t=\sum_{u\ne v}\Gamma_{uv}\,\frac{\lambda_u+\lambda_v}{\lambda_u}\,X_u\bar X_v=X^{\mathsf T}B\bar X,\qquad B=\Lambda_t^{-1}M_t .
$$

The matrix $B$ is Hermitian, $\bar B^{\mathsf T}=B$, since $\overline{B_{vu}}=\Gamma_{uv}(\lambda_u^{-1}+\lambda_v^{-1})\lambda_v=B_{uv}$; its diagonal is zero and $\operatorname{rank}B=\rho_t$. A Hermitian matrix over $K/L$ of rank $\rho$ is congruent to $\operatorname{diag}(I_\rho,0)$: if $B\ne0$, the form $H(X)=X^{\mathsf T}B\bar X\in L$ is not identically zero, because $H(X+cY)=H(X)+c\bar cH(Y)+\operatorname{Tr}_{K/L}(\bar c\,X^{\mathsf T}B\bar Y)$ and the trace form of $K/L$ is nondegenerate; if $H(e)=a\ne0$, choose $c$ with $c\bar c=a^{-1}$, which exists since the norm $z\mapsto z^{2^h+1}$ maps the cyclic group $K^\times$ onto $L^\times$; then $H(ce)=1$, $K^N$ is the direct sum of $Kce$ and its orthogonal complement for $B$, and induction on $N$ applies. The bijective change of variables gives

$$
\sum_{X\in K^N}(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb F_2}H(X)}=|K|^{N-\rho_t}\,g^{\rho_t},\qquad g=\sum_{z\in K}(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb F_2}(z\bar z)}=1+(2^h+1)\sum_{a\in L^\times}(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb F_2}a}=-2^h,
$$

because every $a\in L^\times$ has exactly $2^h+1$ norm preimages and $\sum_{a\in L}(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}a}=0$. Since $\rho_t$ is even, the factor is $2^{k(N-\rho_t)}2^{h\rho_t}=2^{k(N-\rho_t/2)}$, a product over the $k$ characters of $O$.

*Constancy of the rank along orbits.* For every $t$, $M_{2t}$ is obtained from $M_t$ by squaring every entry, since $\lambda_u(2t)=\lambda_u(t)^2$, so $\rho_{2t}=\rho_t$; and $M_{-t}=\Lambda_t^{-1}\Gamma+\Gamma\Lambda_t^{-1}=\Lambda_t^{-1}M_t\Lambda_t^{-1}$, so $\rho_{-t}=\rho_t$. Hence in each of the three cases the factor equals $\prod_{s}2^{N-\rho_s/2}$ over the characters $s$ involved.

*Conclusion.* Multiplying the factors, $\sum_x(-1)^{\Phi(x)}=2^{N|G|-\frac12\sum_{t\in G}\rho_t}$, and with $\rho_0=0$ the normalisation gives $Z_n^{(\mathtt q)}=2^{-\frac12\sum_{t\ne0}\rho_t}>0$. Substituting into Convention 2.2 with $Z_1^{(\mathtt q)}=1$ gives the formula for $S_n^{(\mathtt q)}$. ∎

**Remark 3.2 (Choice of the root of unity and of the graph form).** The right-hand side does not depend on the choice of $\zeta$: another primitive $n$-th root of unity in $F$ is $\zeta^\ell$ with $\gcd(\ell,n)=1$, replacing $\zeta$ by $\zeta^\ell$ replaces $\Lambda_t$ by $\Lambda_{\ell t}$, and $t\mapsto\ell t$ permutes the nonzero elements of $G$. Since the left-hand side depends only on $\psi$ and the colouring, the sum $\sum_{t\ne0}\rho_t$ is the same for all graph forms of $\psi$. The proof shows that the Gauss sum of every self-paired orbit is positive because $\rho_t$ is even, which is why no sign appears. Stance: repo-derived.

**Remark 3.3 (Consistency with two parties).** For $\mathtt q=2$ and a party split $\{X,Y\}$, $M_t=(\zeta^{t}+1)\,\Gamma^{\{X,Y\}}$ for $t\ne0$, and $\Gamma^{\{X,Y\}}$ is block anti-diagonal with off-diagonal block $\Gamma_{XY}$, so $\rho_t=2\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}\Gamma_{XY}$ and Theorem 3.1 gives $S_n^{(2)}=\log2\cdot\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}\Gamma_{XY}$ for every odd $n$. This agrees with the base-$2$ logarithm of the Schmidt rank across the split, $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}\Gamma_{XY}$ (Hein, Eisert and Briegel, Proposition 3, Eq. (43)), and with the independence of the Rényi entropies of a stabilizer state from the Rényi index (Akella, Gadde and Pandey, §3.1). Stance: literature-attested for both comparison facts; the specialisation of Theorem 3.1 is repo-derived.

## 4. Characters with at most three values

**Proposition 4.1 (Three values give masked ranks).** Let $n\ge3$ be odd, $\mathtt q\ge2$, let $\Gamma$ be a graph form of a $\mathtt q$-party pure qubit stabilizer state with colouring $\mathrm{col}$, and let $t\in G$. If $f_t$ takes at most three distinct values on the occupied parties, then $\operatorname{rank}_F(\Lambda_t\Gamma+\Gamma\Lambda_t)=r(\pi_t)$. Stance: suspected-novel (§8).

**Proof.** Write $\lambda_u=\zeta^{f_t(\mathrm{col}(u))}$. Distinct values of $f_t$ give distinct powers of $\zeta$, so $\lambda_u\ne\lambda_v$ exactly when $\mathrm{col}(u)$ and $\mathrm{col}(v)$ lie in different blocks of $\pi_t$. Hence $(M_t)_{uv}=\Gamma^{\pi_t}_{uv}(\lambda_u+\lambda_v)$ for all $u,v$. If the $\lambda_u$ take one value, $M_t=0=\Gamma^{\pi_t}$. If they take two values $a\ne b$, $M_t=(a+b)\Gamma^{\pi_t}$. If they take three values $a,b,c$, let $\beta_a,\beta_b,\beta_c\in F^\times$ be the square roots

$$
\beta_a^2=\frac{(a+b)(a+c)}{b+c},\qquad \beta_b^2=\frac{(b+a)(b+c)}{a+c},\qquad \beta_c^2=\frac{(c+a)(c+b)}{a+b},
$$

which exist and are unique because squaring is an automorphism of the finite field $F$. Then $(\beta_a\beta_b)^2=(a+b)^2$, so $\beta_a\beta_b=a+b$, and similarly for the other two pairs. With $D=\operatorname{diag}(\beta_{\lambda_u})_u$ this gives $M_t=D\,\Gamma^{\pi_t}D$. In every case $M_t$ is congruent to a nonzero multiple of $\Gamma^{\pi_t}$ or equal to it, so $\rho_t=\operatorname{rank}_F\Gamma^{\pi_t}=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}\Gamma^{\pi_t}=r(\pi_t)$, rank being unchanged by field extension. ∎

**Corollary 4.2 (Exact partition decomposition).** Let $n\ge3$ be odd and $\mathtt q\ge2$. For every normalized pure qubit stabilizer state, every graph form $\Gamma$ and every colouring,

$$
S_n^{(\mathtt q)}=\frac{\log2}{2(n-1)\,n^{\mathtt q-2}}\Bigg[\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_2(\mathtt q)}(n-1)\,r(\pi)+\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_3(\mathtt q)}(n-1)(n-2)\,r(\pi)+\sum_{t\in G:\ |\pi_t|\ge4}\rho_t\Bigg].
$$

Each term of the last sum with at most three values of $f_t$ on the occupied parties equals $r(\pi_t)$. If $\min(n,\mathtt q)\le3$, the last sum is empty and

$$
S_n^{(\mathtt q)}=\frac{\log2}{2\,n^{\mathtt q-2}}\Bigg[\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_2(\mathtt q)}r(\pi)+(n-2)\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_3(\mathtt q)}r(\pi)\Bigg].
$$

Stance: suspected-novel (§8) as a decomposition for odd $n\ge5$ together with $\mathtt q\ge4$; for $\min(n,\mathtt q)\le3$ it is not new, the values being literature-attested for $\mathtt q\le3$ and repo-derived for $n=3$, as recorded in §8.

**Proof.** For $\pi\in\Pi_k(\mathtt q)$, the elements $t\in G$ correspond bijectively to the functions $f:[\mathtt q]\to\mathbb Z/n$ with $f(\mathtt q)=0$, and $\pi_t=\pi$ exactly when $f_t$ is constant on the blocks of $\pi$, equal to $0$ on the block containing $\mathtt q$, and takes pairwise distinct nonzero values on the other $k-1$ blocks; there are $(n-1)_{k-1}$ such functions. The character $t=0$ is the only one with $|\pi_t|=1$. For $|\pi_t|\in\{2,3\}$, $f_t$ takes at most three values on all parties, so $\rho_t=r(\pi_t)$ by Proposition 4.1, and grouping these $t$ by $\pi_t$ gives the first two sums with coefficients $(n-1)_1=n-1$ and $(n-1)_2=(n-1)(n-2)$. Substituting into Theorem 3.1 gives the decomposition, and Proposition 4.1 gives the statement about the terms of the last sum. Since $f_t$ takes at most $\min(n,\mathtt q)$ values, $|\pi_t|\le\min(n,\mathtt q)$, so the last sum is empty when $\min(n,\mathtt q)\le3$. ∎

## 5. Failure of the masked-rank expression

For a $\mathtt q$-party stabilizer state with graph form $\Gamma$ and odd $n$, the masked-rank expression is

$$
Z_n^{\mathrm{mask}}=2^{-\frac12\sum_{0\ne t\in G}r(\pi_t)},\qquad S_n^{\mathrm{mask}}=\frac{\log2}{2(n-1)\,n^{\mathtt q-2}}\sum_{0\ne t\in G}r(\pi_t)=\frac{\log2}{2(n-1)\,n^{\mathtt q-2}}\sum_{\substack{\pi\in\Pi(\mathtt q)\\ |\pi|\ge2}}(n-1)_{|\pi|-1}\,r(\pi).
$$

By Corollary 4.2, $Z_n^{(\mathtt q)}=Z_n^{\mathrm{mask}}$ whenever $\min(n,\mathtt q)\le3$.

**Proposition 5.1 (The four-cycle).** Let $n\ge5$ be odd and $\mathtt q\ge4$. Let $\Gamma$ be the adjacency matrix of the four-cycle on qubits $1,2,3,4$ with edges $\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\{3,4\},\{4,1\}$, and let $\mathrm{col}$ place the four qubits in four distinct parties $c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4$. Let

$$
D=\#\{t\in G:\ f_t(c_1),f_t(c_2),f_t(c_3),f_t(c_4)\ \text{pairwise distinct}\}.
$$

Then $\rho_t=4$ and $r(\pi_t)=2$ for each of these $D$ characters, $\rho_t=r(\pi_t)$ for every other $t$, and

$$
-\log_2Z_n^{(\mathtt q)}=-\log_2Z_n^{\mathrm{mask}}+D,\qquad D=\begin{cases}(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)\,n^{\mathtt q-4}, & \mathtt q\in\{c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4\},\\ n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)\,n^{\mathtt q-5}, & \text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
$$

In both cases $D>0$ and

$$
S_n^{(\mathtt q)}-S_n^{\mathrm{mask}}=\frac{(n-2)(n-3)}{n^2}\,\log2 .
$$

In particular the masked-rank expression differs from $Z_n^{(\mathtt q)}$ for every odd $n\ge5$ and every $\mathtt q\ge4$. Stance: suspected-novel (§8).

**Proof.** Let $t$ be one of the $D$ characters and $\lambda_i=\zeta^{f_t(c_i)}$, pairwise distinct. The matrix $M_t$ is the $4\times4$ alternating matrix with $(M_t)_{12}=\lambda_1+\lambda_2$, $(M_t)_{23}=\lambda_2+\lambda_3$, $(M_t)_{34}=\lambda_3+\lambda_4$, $(M_t)_{14}=\lambda_1+\lambda_4$ and $(M_t)_{13}=(M_t)_{24}=0$. Its Pfaffian is

$$
(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)(\lambda_3+\lambda_4)+(\lambda_1+\lambda_4)(\lambda_2+\lambda_3)=(\lambda_1+\lambda_3)(\lambda_2+\lambda_4)\ne0,
$$

and $\det M_t$ is its square, so $\rho_t=4$. All four qubits lie in different blocks of $\pi_t$, so $\Gamma^{\pi_t}=\Gamma$, whose first and third rows coincide and whose first two rows are independent, so $r(\pi_t)=2$. For every other $t$, $f_t$ takes at most three values on the occupied parties $c_1,\dots,c_4$, and $\rho_t=r(\pi_t)$ by Proposition 4.1. By Theorem 3.1, $-\log_2Z_n^{(\mathtt q)}=\tfrac12\sum_{t\ne0}\rho_t=\tfrac12\sum_{t\ne0}r(\pi_t)+\tfrac12\cdot2D$.

To count, recall $f_t(c)=t_c$ for $c<\mathtt q$ and $f_t(\mathtt q)=0$. If $\mathtt q$ is one of the $c_i$, the other three coordinates must take pairwise distinct nonzero values, in $(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)$ ways, and the remaining $\mathtt q-4$ coordinates of $t$ are free. Otherwise $\mathtt q\ge5$, the four coordinates $t_{c_i}$ must be pairwise distinct, in $n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)$ ways, and the remaining $\mathtt q-5$ coordinates are free. Both counts are positive for $n\ge5$, and dividing either by $(n-1)\,n^{\mathtt q-2}$ gives $(n-2)(n-3)/n^2$. By Convention 2.2, $S_n^{(\mathtt q)}-S_n^{\mathrm{mask}}=\frac{\log2}{(n-1)\,n^{\mathtt q-2}}D$, which gives the last formula. ∎

## 6. Boundaries and open questions

**Remark 6.1 (What is not addressed).** All statements concern normalized pure qubit stabilizer states, odd replica indices $n\ge3$ and the replica convention of Convention 2.2. The proof of Theorem 3.1 uses that $|G|$ is odd, both for the inversion formula and for the decomposition of $\mathbb F_2^G$ into Frobenius orbits; the volume makes no statement about even $n$, qudit stabilizer states, mixed states or non-stabilizer states. For characters with four or more values on the occupied parties, $\rho_t$ and $r(\pi_t)$ are not compared in general: Proposition 5.1 exhibits one state with $\rho_t>r(\pi_t)$, and no inequality between $\rho_t$ and $r(\pi_t)$, nor any sign of the total difference $\sum_{t\ne0}(\rho_t-r(\pi_t))$, is asserted for general states. No closed formula for this difference is given beyond the four-cycle.

**Open question 6.2 (Even replica index).** Is there a rank formula of the type of Theorem 3.1 for even $n\ge4$ and $\mathtt q\ge3$, where $\mathbb F_2^G$ is not semisimple?

**Open question 6.3 (Dependence on masked ranks).** For odd $n\ge5$ and $\mathtt q\ge4$, is $S_n^{(\mathtt q)}$ a function of the family $\big(r(\pi)\big)_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathtt q),\,|\pi|\ge2}$, that is, do two stabilizer states with the same masked ranks for all partitions have the same $S_n^{(\mathtt q)}$?

**Open question 6.4 (The correction term).** For odd $n\ge5$ and $\mathtt q\ge4$, which stabilizer states and colourings satisfy $\sum_{t\ne0}\big(\rho_t-r(\pi_t)\big)=0$, and what determines the sign of this sum when it is nonzero?
