# Sparse paving group matchings: strict gaps and complete equal-size obstructions

## 1. Objects and matching quantifiers

**约定 1.1（Ambient group and finite sets）。** Throughout Sections 1–4,
$G$ is an arbitrary torsion-free abelian group, written additively, with
identity $0$. For finite subsets $X,Y\subseteq G$, write

$$
X+Y=\{x+y:x\in X,\ y\in Y\}.
$$

For integers $u\le v$, write
$[u,v]_{\mathbb Z}d=\{td:t\in\mathbb Z,\ u\le t\le v\}$.
If $d\ne0$, torsion-freeness makes these $v-u+1$ elements distinct.
No order, sign, cyclicity or positivity condition on the ground sets is
assumed.

**定义 1.2（Finite matroids and their duals）。** A matroid $P$ on a finite
set $E$ is a family of independent subsets containing the empty set, closed
under taking subsets, and satisfying augmentation: if $I,J$ are independent
and $|I|<|J|$, some $e\in J\setminus I$ makes $I\cup\{e\}$ independent.
Its rank function is

$$
\rho_P(X)=\max\{|I|:I\subseteq X,\ I\text{ independent}\}.
$$

A basis is a maximal independent set; all bases have size
$\rho_P(E)$. Write $\mathcal B(P)$ for the set of bases. A circuit is a
minimal dependent set. The closure of $X$ is
$\operatorname{cl}_P(X)=\{e\in E:\rho_P(X\cup\{e\})=\rho_P(X)\}$.
A flat equals its closure; a hyperplane is a flat of rank
$\rho_P(E)-1$. A circuit-hyperplane is both a circuit and a hyperplane.
A loop has dependent singleton; a coloop belongs to every basis.
The dual $P^*$ has bases $E\setminus Q$, for $Q\in\mathcal B(P)$,
and rank $|E|-\rho_P(E)$.
A rank-$k$ matroid is paving if it has no circuit of cardinality less
than $k$. For $k\ge1$, this is equivalent to independence of every
$(k-1)$-subset; rank-zero paving imposes no condition.
A matroid is sparse paving if it and its dual are paving.

**定义 1.3（The actual target and a specified source basis）。** Let $M,N$
be finite loopless matroids with respective ground sets $A,B\subseteq G$
and the same rank $r\ge1$. Always assume $N$ is sparse paving and
$0\notin B$. A specified $S\in\mathcal B(M)$ is matched to $N$ if there
exist $T\in\mathcal B(N)$ and a bijection $f:S\longrightarrow T$ such
that

$$
s+f(s)\notin A\qquad(s\in S).
$$

The matroid $M$ is matched to $N$ if every $S\in\mathcal B(M)$ is
matched to $N$. A failing basis means a basis with no such bijection to
any actual basis of the specified $N$. This is precisely the ordering-free
form of Aliabadi–Zerbib Definition 1.3 [AZ]: enumerate $S$ in any order
and enumerate $T$ by its images under $f$. Conversely a pair of matched
ordered bases gives this bijection.

The target matroid is fixed independently of the additive group law.
Its rank function and its actual basis family are used throughout.
There is no assumption of representability, a common ground set, or
invariance of matroid independence under translation. The source matroid
need not be paving.

**定义 1.4（Admissible targets and blocked translations）。** For
$s\in S$ and nonempty $X\subseteq S$, put

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_s&=\{b\in B:s+b\notin A\},\\
C_X&=\bigcup_{s\in X}C_s,\\
U_X&=B\setminus C_X=\{b\in B:X+b\subseteq A\},\\
V_X&=U_X\cup\{0\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $C_X$ is a union, whereas $U_X$ is an intersection of blocking
conditions. Since $X\subseteq A$ and $0\notin B$,
$X+V_X\subseteq A$ and $|V_X|=|U_X|+1$.
For $X=\varnothing$ put $C_X=\varnothing$; the additive arguments below
are used only for nonempty $X$.

## 2. Every strict ground-size gap is sufficient

**定理 2.1（Strict-gap theorem）。** For every torsion-free abelian group
$G$ and every pair of finite loopless matroids $M,N$ on finite subsets
$A,B\subseteq G$, respectively, of common rank $r\ge1$, if $N$ and
$N^*$ are paving, $0\notin B$ and $|A|<|B|$, then every
$S\in\mathcal B(M)$ admits a bijection onto some
$T\in\mathcal B(N)$ satisfying $s+f(s)\notin A$ for all $s\in S$.

Proof. Fix any such $S$. We first derive the additive cardinality control
and the independent-representative test used here and at equality.
The additive growth, Rado criterion and sparse paving structure in the
argument are classical intermediate ingredients; the conclusion concerns
the actual independently specified target $N$.

The subgroup $K$ generated by the finite sets under consideration
is a finitely generated torsion-free abelian group. By the structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups,
$K\cong\mathbb Z^k$ for some $k\ge0$. The lexicographic order on
$\mathbb Z^k$ is total and invariant under addition. Transport it to $K$.
All sets and sums in any one application lie in such a subgroup. This is
an auxiliary order for a proof, and assigns no uniform sign to either
ground set. The general orderability result is also [AZ, Theorem 3.8].

For nonempty finite $X,Y\subseteq G$, put $p=|X|$, $q=|Y|$.
Use the compatible order just described to list
$x_0<\cdots<x_{p-1}$ and $y_0<\cdots<y_{q-1}$.
A lattice path from $(0,0)$ to $(p-1,q-1)$ that increases one coordinate
by one at each step visits $p+q-1$ vertices. Their sums $x_i+y_j$ are
strictly increasing, so $|X+Y|\ge p+q-1$.

For nonempty $X\subseteq S$, apply this growth bound to
$X$ and $V_X=U_X\cup\{0\}$ from Definition 1.4. It gives

$$
|A|\ge |X+V_X|\ge |X|+|V_X|-1=|X|+|U_X|.
$$

Subtracting from $|B|$ yields the target-union bounds

$$
|U_X|\le |A|-|X|,\qquad
|C_X|\ge |B|-|A|+|X|. \tag{U}
$$

The zero added to $V_X$ is counted exactly once because $0\notin B$.
The argument applies whether or not $0\in A$, and does not use
[AZ, Lemma 3.14].

The classical sparse paving structure [Bonin, Introduction; AZ, Theorem 3.3]
has the following consequences for $N$; write $b=|B|$.
Every set of size at most $r-1$ is independent, and every
hyperplane has size at most $r$. Every subset of size at least $r+1$
is spanning. A nonspanning subset of size at least $r$ has size exactly
$r$ and is a circuit-hyperplane.

To derive these facts, independence of sets of size at most $r-1$
follows from paving:
a smaller dependent set would contain a circuit of size less than $r$.
For the hyperplane bound, recall the dual rank identity

$$
\rho_{N^*}(D)=|D|-r+\rho_N(B\setminus D).
$$

Here is a derivation from the definition of dual bases. The maximum size
of an independent subset of $D$ in the dual is
$\max_{Q\in\mathcal B(N)}|D\setminus Q|$.
The minimum of $|D\cap Q|$ over these bases is
$r-\rho_N(B\setminus D)$: one obtains it by extending a basis of
$B\setminus D$ to a basis of $N$, and no basis can use more than
$\rho_N(B\setminus D)$ elements of that complement. Substitution gives
the displayed identity.

If $F$ is a hyperplane, its complement $D=B\setminus F$ has dual rank
$|D|-1$. For every proper $D'\subset D$, the set $B\setminus D'$
contains $F$ and an element outside $F$, hence spans $N$. The identity
then gives $\rho_{N^*}(D')=|D'|$. Thus $D$ is a circuit of $N^*$.
Writing $q=b-r$, dual paving gives $|D|\ge q$, so $|F|\le r$.
This includes $q=0$; the asserted bound remains valid.

Every nonspanning subset is contained in a hyperplane: extend a basis
of that subset to an independent set of size $r-1$ and take its closure.
Consequently a set of size at least $r+1$ spans. If $Q$ is nonspanning
and $|Q|\ge r$, its containing hyperplane must have size exactly $r$
and equal $Q$. All proper subsets of $Q$ are independent by paving,
so $Q$ is also a circuit.

For Rado's classical criterion [Rado; AZ, Theorem 3.6], let $P$ be a finite
matroid on $E$, and let $(F_i)_{i\in I}$ be a finite indexed family of
subsets of $E$. There exist distinct $e_i\in F_i$ whose set is
independent in $P$ if and only if

$$
\rho_P\left(\bigcup_{i\in J}F_i\right)\ge |J|
\qquad(J\subseteq I).
$$

Here is the full finite induction argument. Necessity follows by
considering the selected representatives
for $J$. For sufficiency induct on $k=|I|$. The case $k=0$ is empty;
for $k=1$ the rank inequality provides a nonloop in the sole set.
Assume $k\ge2$ and abbreviate $D_J=\bigcup_{i\in J}F_i$.

First suppose some nonempty proper $J\subset I$ is tight:
$\rho_P(D_J)=|J|$. The induction hypothesis gives independent
representatives for $J$, forming a set $Q\subseteq D_J$ of size $|J|$.
Thus $Q$ is a basis of $D_J$ and
$\operatorname{cl}_P(Q)=\operatorname{cl}_P(D_J)$.
Contract $Q$. For clarity, contraction by an independent set has rank

$$
\rho_{P/Q}(Z)=\rho_P(Z\cup Q)-|Q|\qquad(Z\subseteq E\setminus Q).
$$

This follows directly by extending $Q$ to a maximal independent subset
of $Z\cup Q$; independent subsets in the contraction are precisely sets
that can be adjoined to $Q$ independently. For $K\subseteq I\setminus J$,
closure equality and the original rank condition give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{P/Q}(D_K\setminus Q)
&=\rho_P(D_K\cup Q)-|J|\\
&=\rho_P(D_K\cup D_J)-|J|\\
&\ge |K|.
\end{aligned}
$$

Induction in $P/Q$, for the family $(F_i\setminus Q)_{i\notin J}$,
gives representatives independent after contraction. Together with $Q$
they are distinct independent representatives for all of $I$.

If there is no nonempty proper tight $J$, all those $J$ satisfy
$\rho_P(D_J)\ge |J|+1$. Fix $i_0\in I$ and a nonloop
$e\in F_{i_0}$. For every nonempty $K\subseteq I\setminus\{i_0\}$,

$$
\rho_{P/e}(D_K\setminus\{e\})
=\rho_P(D_K\cup\{e\})-1
\ge\rho_P(D_K)-1\ge |K|.
$$

The empty $K$ causes no condition. Induction in $P/e$ supplies the
remaining representatives; adjoining $e$ completes the proof.

Apply this criterion to the family $(C_s)_{s\in S}$ in the actual
matroid $N$. Its independent representatives form an independent set
of size $|S|=r$, hence a basis of $N$. Membership in $C_s$ is exactly
the required exclusion of $s+f(s)$ from $A$. In the reverse direction,
the image of $X$ under a matching is an independent $|X|$-subset of
$C_X$.

Consequently the exact basiswise test is

$$
S\text{ matches }N
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho_N(C_X)\ge |X|\quad(X\subseteq S). \tag{R}
$$

We now combine these ingredients with the strict size hypothesis.
For nonempty $X\subseteq S$, (U) gives $|C_X|\ge |X|+1$.
If $|X|\le r-1$, choose a $|X|$-subset of $C_X$; paving makes
it independent, so $\rho_N(C_X)\ge |X|$.
If $|X|=r$, then $|C_X|\ge r+1$, so the hyperplane bound just
derived gives $\rho_N(C_X)=r$. The empty subset has rank zero.
Criterion (R) supplies the desired bijection to a basis of $N$.
Since $S$ was arbitrary, $M$ is matched to $N$.

**推论 2.2（The exact one-element gap）。** Under the other hypotheses
of Theorem 2.1, $|A|+1=|B|$ implies that $M$ is matched to $N$.

Proof. The equality of cardinalities implies $|A|<|B|$, so Theorem 2.1
applies. In [AZ, Question 2.7], the notation $n$ for rank is $r$ here,
$E(M)=A$ and $E(N)=B$. This proves its strict one-element-gap clause.
The disjunction in that question still has a negative answer, because
its equal-cardinality clause has the supplied counterexamples described
in the interval recovery in Section 4 below.

## 3. Exhaustive classification at equal cardinality

**引理 3.1（Only a full-basis rank obstruction can survive）。** Assume
Definition 1.3 and $|A|=|B|=m$. For any specified basis $S$,

$$
S\text{ fails to match }N
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
C_S\text{ is a circuit-hyperplane of }N\text{ of size }r.
$$

In a failure, $r\ge2$, $m\ge r+1$, $|U_S|=m-r$ and
$S+(U_S\cup\{0\})=A$.

Proof. The target-union bound (U) gives $|C_X|\ge |X|$ for every nonempty $X\subseteq S$.
For $|X|\le r-1$, paving then gives $\rho_N(C_X)\ge |X|$.
By criterion (R) the only possible failure is
$\rho_N(C_S)<r$. Since $|C_S|\ge r$, the sparse paving rank alternatives derived in
Theorem 2.1 identify $C_S$ as a circuit-hyperplane of size $r$. Conversely that hyperplane has
rank $r-1$, violating criterion (R) for $X=S$.

For $r=1$, looplessness makes every nonempty $C_S$ have rank one;
thus failure requires $r\ge2$. If $m=r$, $N$ has an independent
set of size $m$, necessarily $B$, so it is free and cannot have a
nonspanning $r$-subset. Thus $m\ge r+1$ in a failure.
Finally $|U_S|=m-r$ and

$$
m\ge |S+(U_S\cup\{0\})|\ge r+(m-r+1)-1=m.
$$

The containment in $A$ from Definition 1.4 is therefore equality.

**引理 3.2（All translations that keep a block inside a progression）。**
Let $r,m$ be integers with $r\ge1$, $m\ge r$, let $a,d\in G$
with $d\ne0$, and let $j$ be an integer with $0\le j\le m-r$. Put

$$
A=a+[0,m-1]_{\mathbb Z}d,\qquad
S=a+[j,j+r-1]_{\mathbb Z}d.
$$

Then the full set of translations in $G$ satisfying $S+b\subseteq A$
is

$$
W_j=[-j,m-r-j]_{\mathbb Z}d.
$$

Proof. If $S+b\subseteq A$, its first element gives
$a+jd+b=a+td$ for an integer $0\le t<m$, hence
$b=kd$ for some integer $k$. The first and last elements of $S+b$
have indices $j+k$ and $j+r-1+k$ in $A$.
Torsion-freeness makes all integer indices unique, so
$-j\le k\le m-r-j$.
Conversely these inequalities put every index of $S+kd$ between
$0$ and $m-1$. In particular $0\in W_j$ and
$|W_j\setminus\{0\}|=m-r$.

**定理 3.3（Complete obstruction for a specified source basis）。**
For every pair $M,N$ satisfying Definition 1.3 and
$|A|=|B|=m$, and for every specified $S\in\mathcal B(M)$,
$S$ fails to match $N$ if and only if $r\ge2$, $m\ge r+1$, and
there exist $a,d\in G$, $d\ne0$, and an integer $0\le j\le m-r$
with all the following properties:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=\{a+td:0\le t<m\},\\
S&=\{a+(j+t)d:0\le t<r\},\\
U&=\{td:-j\le t\le m-r-j,\ t\ne0\}\subseteq B,\\
H&=B\setminus U\text{ is a circuit-hyperplane of }N.
\end{aligned}
$$

All indices here are integers. In every such obstruction,
$U=U_S$, $H=C_S$, $|U|=m-r$, $|H|=r$ and $\rho_N(H)=r-1$.

Proof. Suppose $S$ fails. Lemma 3.1 gives $r\ge2$, $m\ge r+1$,
$|U_S|=m-r$ and

$$
|S+(U_S\cup\{0\})|=|S|+|U_S\cup\{0\}|-1=m.
$$

Both summands have at least two elements. Here we need the classical
equality rigidity for the ordered sumset bound, with its full argument.
For nonempty finite $X,Y$ of sizes $p,q\ge2$, use a compatible order
on their finitely generated subgroup as in Theorem 2.1, and list
$x_0<\cdots<x_{p-1}$ and $y_0<\cdots<y_{q-1}$.
If $|X+Y|=p+q-1$, every increasing lattice path from $(0,0)$ to
$(p-1,q-1)$ lists the whole sumset in increasing order. Fix
$0\le i<p-1$ and $0\le k<q-1$. Choose two paths with the same
initial part ending at $(i,k)$ and the same final part starting at
$(i+1,k+1)$, one passing through $(i+1,k)$ and the other through
$(i,k+1)$. The intermediate sums occupy the same position in the
increasing list of the entire sumset. Hence

$$
x_{i+1}+y_k=x_i+y_{k+1},\qquad
x_{i+1}-x_i=y_{k+1}-y_k.
$$

All consecutive gaps in both lists equal a common positive $d\ne0$.
Thus $X=x_0+[0,p-1]_{\mathbb Z}d$ and
$Y=y_0+[0,q-1]_{\mathbb Z}d$ in $G$ itself. Conversely two such
progressions have sumset
$x_0+y_0+[0,p+q-2]_{\mathbb Z}d$: every index in this interval is
a sum of indices in the two shorter intervals, and torsion-freeness
makes its $p+q-1$ elements distinct. This proves both directions of
the classical equality case as an intermediate step.

Applying it to $S$ and $U_S\cup\{0\}$ gives
$s_0,v_0,d\in G$, $d\ne0$, such that

$$
S=s_0+[0,r-1]_{\mathbb Z}d,\qquad
U_S\cup\{0\}=v_0+[0,m-r]_{\mathbb Z}d.
$$

Since the second progression contains $0$, there is a unique
$j\in[0,m-r]_{\mathbb Z}$ with $v_0=-jd$.
Set $a=s_0-jd$. Adding the two progressions gives
$A=a+[0,m-1]_{\mathbb Z}d$, and
$S=a+[j,j+r-1]_{\mathbb Z}d$.
Removing $0$ from the second progression gives the stated $U=U_S$.
Its complement $H=C_S$ is the circuit-hyperplane of Lemma 3.1.
This proves every required condition without an assumption on signs
or the rest of the bases of $M$.

Conversely suppose the displayed conditions hold. Lemma 3.2 identifies
the full blocking-translation set as $W_j=U\cup\{0\}$.
Because $U\subseteq B$ and $0\notin B$, intersection with $B$ gives
$U_S=U$ and hence $C_S=H$.
The circuit-hyperplane $H$ has rank $r-1$ in the specified $N$.
Criterion (R) rules out an independent system of $r$ representatives,
so no bijection to any target basis exists. This also proves the
additional identities claimed in the theorem.

**推论 3.4（Exact existential formulation for two matroids）。** Under
Definition 1.3 and $|A|=|B|=m$,
$M$ is not matched to $N$ if and only if $r\ge2$, $m\ge r+1$,
and there exist $a,d\in G$, $d\ne0$, and an integer
$0\le j\le m-r$ such that

$$
\begin{gathered}
A=a+[0,m-1]_{\mathbb Z}d,\qquad
S_j=a+[j,j+r-1]_{\mathbb Z}d\in\mathcal B(M),\\
U_j=([-j,m-r-j]_{\mathbb Z}d)\setminus\{0\}\subseteq B,\qquad
B\setminus U_j\text{ is a circuit-hyperplane of }N.
\end{gathered}
$$

For a fixed progression representation of $A$ with $r\ge2$ and $m>r$,
the entire family of failing bases is exactly

$$
\left\{S_j:
0\le j\le m-r,\quad S_j\in\mathcal B(M),\quad
U_j\subseteq B,\quad
B\setminus U_j\text{ is a circuit-hyperplane of }N\right\}.
$$

Proof. Negating the universal quantifier over source bases in
Definition 1.3 gives the existence of one failing $S$.
Apply Theorem 3.3 to it, or conversely to the displayed $S_j$.
There is no requirement that every basis of $M$ be a block.

To justify the assertion for a fixed progression representation,
order the subgroup generated by the finitely many parameters of any
two such representations. In an increasing enumeration of a
progression of size $m\ge2$, consecutive gaps equal its positive
step. Hence two representations of $A$ have the same step up to
reversal and the same endpoints. Reversal replaces
$(a,d,j)$ by $(a+(m-1)d,-d,m-r-j)$, leaving $S_j$ and $U_j$
as sets unchanged. Thus the necessity in Theorem 3.3 holds for the
fixed representation as well, and its sufficiency already did.

**推论 3.5（Immediate positive cases at equality）。** Under
Definition 1.3 and $|A|=|B|$, $M$ is matched to $N$ if $A$ is not
an arithmetic progression, or if $N$ has no circuit-hyperplanes.
In particular it is matched to every uniform target of the same rank.

Proof. Either hypothesis excludes the corresponding necessary
condition in Theorem 3.3. A uniform rank-$r$ matroid has every
$r$-subset as a basis and therefore has no circuit-hyperplane:
a circuit-hyperplane in rank $r$ has size $r$, since deleting an
element makes it independent of rank $r-1$.

## 4. Degeneracies, supplied families, and other obstruction shapes

**命题 4.1（Rank one, free grounds, and coloops）。** Under
Definition 1.3 with $|A|\le |B|$ the following statements hold.

1. If $r=1$, every source basis is matched.
2. If $|A|=|B|=r$, both matroids are free and are matched.
3. If $r\ge2$ and $q=|B|-r\ge2$, $N$ has no coloops.
4. If $r\ge2$ and $q=1$, $N$ is either uniform, or it has exactly
   one coloop $e$ and exactly one circuit-hyperplane $B\setminus\{e\}$.
   In the latter case its bases are precisely the $r$-subsets containing
   $e$. At equality $|A|=|B|=r+1$, failure occurs exactly for the
   endpoint blocks in Theorem 3.3 for which $U_j=\{e\}$.

Proof. For (1), $S$ is a singleton and the target-union bound (U) makes $C_S$
nonempty; every element is a nonloop. For (2), a rank-$r$ matroid on
$r$ elements has its ground as its sole basis; matching follows from
criterion (R) and the target-union bound (U), since every subset of $B$ is independent.
For (3), paving of $N^*$ of rank at least two excludes dual loops.
A loop in the dual is exactly a coloop in $N$ by the complementary
basis definition.

For (4), $N^*$ has rank one. Let $L$ be its set of loops and
$D=B\setminus L$ its nonempty set of nonloops. Its bases are exactly
the singletons in $D$, so the primal bases are precisely
$B\setminus\{d\}$ for $d\in D$. Thus $L$ is the set of primal
coloops, $D$ is the unique primal circuit, and every proper subset
of $D$ lies in a primal basis. Paving of $N$ gives $|D|\ge r$;
since $|B|=r+1$, $|L|\le1$. If $L$ is empty the matroid is uniform.
If $L=\{e\}$ then $|D|=r$, $D$ has rank $r-1$, and adding $e$
spans; it is the unique circuit-hyperplane.
In Theorem 3.3, $m-r=1$ makes $j$ either $0$ or $1$;
$U_0=\{d\}$ and $U_1=\{-d\}$. The assertion follows.
There is consequently no implicit coloopless restriction in either
main theorem. Coloops of the source have no special role, because the
argument applies separately to every one of its actual bases.

The supplied construction and its interval instance [Family] illustrate
Theorem 3.3 as follows. Let $E$ be a finite set of size $r+q$, let $H\subset E$ have size $r$, and
let $r\ge2$, $q\ge1$. The family

$$
\mathcal Q=\{Q\subseteq E:|Q|=r,\ Q\ne H\}
$$

is the basis family of a loopless sparse paving rank-$r$ matroid,
and $H$ is a circuit-hyperplane. This is the one-deleted-base
construction supplied in [Family]. Its properties follow directly from
the supplied exchange and extension arguments. The family is nonempty
because $E\setminus H$ is nonempty.
For basis exchange, fix $Q,R\in\mathcal Q$ and $x\in Q\setminus R$.
If $|R\setminus Q|=1$, exchanging with its unique element gives $R$.
If $|R\setminus Q|\ge2$, the candidates
$(Q\setminus\{x\})\cup\{y\}$ for $y\in R\setminus Q$ are distinct;
at most one equals $H$, so an allowed exchange exists.
This is the basis-axiom characterization of a matroid.

Every $I\subseteq E$ with $|I|<r$ extends to an $r$-subset $Q$.
If $Q=H$, choose $h\in H\setminus I$ and $e\in E\setminus H$;
replacing $h$ by $e$ gives an allowed basis still containing $I$.
Thus all sets of size at most $r-1$ are independent, proving paving
and looplessness. Complementation shows the dual bases are the
$q$-subsets other than $E\setminus H$. The same extension argument
makes every set of size below $q$ independent in the dual, including
$q=1$, when this statement concerns only the empty set.
Finally $H$ is dependent with all proper subsets independent.
For any $e\notin H$, replacing one element of $H$ by $e$ gives a
basis inside $H\cup\{e\}$. Hence $H$ has rank $r-1$ and is a
hyperplane as well as a circuit.

For the supplied punctured-interval instance, let $n,s\ge2$,
$G=\mathbb Z$, $E=\{1,\ldots,n+s\}$ and
$H=\{s+1,\ldots,s+n\}$. Let $M=N$ have as bases all $n$-subsets
of $E$ other than $H$. The basis $S=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ fails to match
$N$. This is the family already supplied in [Family].

In the supplied construction take $r=n$, $q=s$. The initial block $S$ differs
from $H$, and so is a basis. In Theorem 3.3 take

$$
m=n+s,\qquad a=1,\qquad d=1,\qquad j=0,\qquad
U=\{1,\ldots,s\}.
$$

Then $E\setminus U=H$, giving precisely the obstruction.
Directly, an admissible target for $x\in S$ must satisfy
$x+b>n+s$, hence $b\in H$. All $n$ distinct targets would therefore
form the nonbasis $H$.
The member $n=s=2$ keeps the negative answer to the disjunctive
[AZ, Question 2.7]. Theorem 2.1 proves the strict-gap branch and does
not turn the whole question into an affirmative assertion.

**命题 4.2（An interior block, zero in the source, and other bases）。**
A failing interior block can coexist with a matched nonblock basis,
even when $0\in A$.

Proof. Take $A=\{0,1,2,3\}$, $M$ uniform of rank two on $A$,
$B=\{-1,1,4,5\}$, and let $N$ have all two-element bases except
$H=\{4,5\}$. The supplied construction in Section 4 makes $N$ loopless and sparse paving.
For $a=0$, $d=1$, $m=4$, $r=2$, $j=1$,
$S=\{1,2\}$ and $U=\{-1,1\}$ meet Theorem 3.3, so $S$ fails.
The nonblock basis $\{0,3\}$ does match: send $0$ to $4$ and $3$
to $1$. Both sums equal $4\notin A$, and $\{1,4\}$ is an actual
basis of $N$. Thus failure of $M$ to be matched does not mean that
all its bases fail, or that all its bases are consecutive blocks.

**命题 4.3（Corank one and a coloop obstruction）。** A corank-one
target with a coloop can have an endpoint obstruction.

Proof. Take
$A=\{1,2,3\}$ with uniform rank-two source,
$B=\{1,4,5\}$, and target bases $\{1,4\},\{1,5\}$.
Here $1$ is a coloop, $H=\{4,5\}$ is a circuit-hyperplane,
$S=\{1,2\}$ and $U=\{1\}$. The supplied construction in Section 4, or the direct description
in Proposition 4.1, verifies sparse paving. This is the $j=0$
corank-one obstruction. Reversing the progression gives the equivalent
$j=1$ description with step $-1$. No assumption of corank at least
two is part of Theorem 3.3.

**命题 4.4（A noncyclic ambient group and independent ground sets）。**
An obstruction can occur in a noncyclic ambient group with disjoint
source and target grounds, and target elements outside the source
progression coset.

Proof. Take $G=\mathbb Z^2$, $a=(0,1)$, $d=(1,0)$, $m=4$, $r=2$,
$j=1$, and

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=\{(t,1):0\le t\le3\},& S&=\{(1,1),(2,1)\},\\
U&=\{(-1,0),(1,0)\},& H&=\{(0,3),(0,4)\},\qquad B=U\cup H.
\end{aligned}
$$

Choose the uniform rank-two source and the one-deleted-base target
with hole $H$. Theorem 3.3 applies. In particular the ambient group
need not be cyclic, $A$ need not lie in the subgroup generated by $d$,
and $B$ need not be contained in the affine progression containing $A$.
A progression obstruction forces $A$ into one coset of an infinite
cyclic subgroup; it imposes no such restriction on the whole ambient
group or on $H$.

**命题 4.5（Sign and endpoint symmetry）。** The obstruction conditions
are invariant under

$$
(a,d,j)\longmapsto(a+(m-1)d,-d,m-r-j).
$$

They allow both endpoints $j=0,m-r$ and every interior integer.
Applying any injective group homomorphism to all elements preserves
the sum exclusions and the obstruction, with the matroids transported
by the resulting ground-set bijections.

Proof. Reversing the indices leaves $A$ and $S$ unchanged. Substituting
$t\mapsto-t$ in $[-j,m-r-j]_{\mathbb Z}d$ leaves $U$ unchanged
under the displayed transformation. For a homomorphism $\phi$,
$\phi(s+b)=\phi(s)+\phi(b)$, and injectivity makes
$s+b\in A$ equivalent to $\phi(s)+\phi(b)\in\phi(A)$.
Transport preserves every independent set and therefore every basis,
circuit and hyperplane. The negative identity map handles simultaneous
sign reversal; no positivity premise enters either theorem.

## 5. Exact failure boundaries and the additive source defect

**命题 5.1（The extra unit in the cited additive lemma is false）。**
[AZ, Lemma 3.14] states that nonempty finite $P,Q$ in a group with
$P,Q,P+Q\subseteq X\subseteq G\setminus\{0\}$ imply
$|X|\ge |P|+|Q|+1$. This implication is false.

Proof. Take $G=\mathbb Z$,

$$
P=Q=\{1,2,3\},\qquad X=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}.
$$

Then $P+Q=\{2,3,4,5,6\}$, so all hypotheses hold, but the
asserted conclusion is $6\ge7$.
More generally $P=[1,p]_{\mathbb Z}$ and $Q=[1,q]_{\mathbb Z}$
with $X=[1,p+q]_{\mathbb Z}$ give equality $|X|=|P|+|Q|$.

The precise bookkeeping defect is visible in the proof of that lemma.
For $P_0=P\cup\{0\}$ and $Q_0=Q\cup\{0\}$ one has

$$
P_0+Q_0=\{0\}\cup P\cup Q\cup(P+Q),
$$

with a disjoint added $\{0\}$. The proof's identity omits this
singleton. A bound $|P_0+Q_0|\ge |P_0|+|Q_0|-1$
therefore gives $|X|\ge |P|+|Q|$, not the extra unit.
[AK, Lemma 2.8] repeats the same statement and omitted-singleton
identity. The same counterexample applies at its stated hypotheses.
Neither claim is an ingredient of the derivation of (U) in Theorem 2.1.
This counterexample concerns those exact lemmas; it is not a claim
that every theorem citing either paper is false.

**命题 5.2（The auxiliary self-matching claim in the sequel）。**
[AK, Claim 3.3] is used with a group matching $f$ on a ground set,
a source basis $S$ and a nonbasis image $T=f(S)$. It asserts the
existence of $x\in E\setminus T$ and $s\in S$ with $s+x\notin E$.
This assertion is false under the loopless sparse paving, zero-excluding
self-matching hypotheses.

Proof. In the $n=s=2$ member of the supplied interval family in Section 4, take
$E=\{1,2,3,4\}$, forbidden basis $H=\{3,4\}$,
$S=\{1,2\}$, and the group matching

$$
f(1)=4,\quad f(2)=3,\quad f(3)=2,\quad f(4)=1.
$$

All four sums are $5\notin E$; $T=H$ is not a basis.
But $E\setminus T=\{1,2\}$ and
$S+(E\setminus T)=\{2,3,4\}\subseteq E$.
Thus this claim fails under the very sparse paving, loopless,
zero-excluding self-matching conditions in which it is used.
This delimits that proof step and does not assert a separate
classification for arbitrary paving targets.

**命题 5.3（Dual paving cannot simply be dropped）。** The strict-gap
conclusion of Theorem 2.1 can fail if dual paving is omitted, even over
$\mathbb Z$ with a one-element gap.

Proof. Let $G=\mathbb Z$, $A=\{1,2,3,4\}$, and $M$ be uniform of rank two
on $A$. Put $B=\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ and define the target to be the
rank-two partition matroid allowing at most one element from each
of $\{1,2\}$ and $\{3,4,5\}$. It is loopless and paving.
For $S=\{1,2\}$ one has $C_S=\{3,4,5\}$ of rank one,
so $S$ has no matched target basis despite $|B|=|A|+1$ and
$0\notin B$. The set $\{3,4,5\}$ is a hyperplane of size three;
its complement is a two-element circuit in the dual, whose rank
is three. Thus the hyperplane-size bound used in Theorem 2.1 fails without
dual paving.

**命题 5.4（Torsion and the exclusion of zero）。** The strict-gap
conclusion fails in general finite abelian groups. Even over
$\mathbb Z$, allowing zero in the target ground can invalidate the
strict one-element-gap conclusion, and at equality failure can already
occur in rank one on singleton grounds.

Proof. In $G=\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z$, let
$A=\{0,2,4,6\}$, take the uniform rank-two source, and put
$B=\{1,2,3,4,6\}$. Let $N$ have all two-element bases except
$H=\{1,3\}$. The supplied construction in Section 4 verifies looplessness and sparse paving.
For $S=\{0,2\}$, every even target keeps both sums in $A$, while
every odd target leaves $A$; thus $C_S=H$ has rank one.
Here $0\notin B$ and $|B|=|A|+1$, but $S$ fails to match.
This counterexample does not concern prime-order groups specifically.

The zero exclusion is also a real condition. Even over $\mathbb Z$,
let $A=\{1,2,3\}$, take a uniform rank-two source, and put
$B=\{0,1,3,4\}$ with the one-deleted-base target whose
circuit-hyperplane is $\{3,4\}$. For $S=\{1,2\}$ the targets
$0,1$ are blocked and $C_S=\{3,4\}$ has rank one. Thus a strict
one-element gap does not suffice if $0\in B$ is allowed.
At equality already $r=m=1$, $A=\{1\}$, $B=\{0\}$ fails.

## 7. Prime-cyclic grounds: strict gaps and the actual-basis obstruction

**约定 7.1（The full prime domain）。** Throughout Sections 7–11,
$p$ is any prime and $G=(\mathbb F_p,+)$. The finite loopless matroids
$M,N$ have independently specified grounds $A,B\subseteq\mathbb F_p$
and common rank $r$, with

$$
1\le r\le |A|\le |B|<p,\qquad 0\notin B,
$$

and both $N$ and its dual are paving. Rank-zero duals are allowed,
with the convention of Definition 1.2. A specified source basis is
$S\in\mathcal B(M)$. Matching means a bijection from $S$ onto an
actual basis of this fixed $N$, with every sum outside $A$, exactly
as in Definition 1.3. In particular $0$ may belong to $A$, and neither
ground is assigned an order or a sign. For $s\in S$ and nonempty
$X\subseteq S$, use the sets $C_s,C_X,U_X,V_X$ of Definition 1.4,
now interpreted in $\mathbb F_p$. For $d\in\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0\}$
and integers $u\le v$ with $v-u<p$, write

$$
I_d[u,v]=\{td\in\mathbb F_p:t\in\mathbb Z,\ u\le t\le v\}.
$$

This interval has $v-u+1$ elements. Its indices are integers reduced
modulo $p$ in the displayed set; no lifting of sums to the integers
is implicit. An arithmetic progression of length $k<p$ means
$a+I_d[0,k-1]$ for some nonzero $d$. A semi-progression means a set
from which one element can be deleted to leave a progression, as in
[AZ, Section 3.5].

**定理 7.2（Every strict gap, and the exact prime obstruction）。**
Under Convention 7.1, every strict inequality $|A|<|B|$ makes $M$
matched to $N$. If $|A|=|B|=m$, then for every specified basis $S$,

$$
S\text{ fails to match }N
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
C_S\text{ is a circuit-hyperplane of the actual }N.
\tag{P-O}
$$

In any such failure,

$$
r\ge2,\qquad m\ge r+1,\qquad
|C_S|=r,\qquad |U_S|=m-r,\qquad
S+(U_S\cup\{0\})=A.
\tag{P-E}
$$

Proof. The additive input is the classical Cauchy–Davenport theorem,
in the precise prime-group form [BDM, Theorem 1.1]: for nonempty
$X,Y\subseteq\mathbb F_p$,
$|X+Y|\ge\min\{p,|X|+|Y|-1\}$. Since
$X+V_X\subseteq A$ and $|A|<p$, applying it with
$Y=V_X=U_X\cup\{0\}$ gives

$$
|X|+|U_X|\le |A|,\qquad
|C_X|\ge |B|-|A|+|X|\qquad(\varnothing\ne X\subseteq S).
\tag{P-U}
$$

Indeed the alternative lower bound $p$ is impossible for a sumset
contained in $A$; $0\notin B$ ensures $|V_X|=|U_X|+1$.
This uses no exclusion of zero from $A$.

The independent-representative criterion is the classical Rado theorem
[AZ, Theorem 3.6], applied to the sets $(C_s)_{s\in S}$ in $N$:

$$
S\text{ matches }N
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho_N(C_X)\ge |X|\quad(X\subseteq S).
\tag{P-R}
$$

An independent representative set has $r$ distinct elements, so it is
an actual basis, rather than a basis of a relaxation. The sparse paving
facts proved in Theorem 2.1 are independent of its ambient-group
assumption: subsets of size at most $r-1$ are independent, hyperplanes
have size at most $r$, and a nonspanning subset of size at least $r$
is exactly an $r$-element circuit-hyperplane. Their proof uses only
paving and the dual rank identity. These are also the supplied
`small_indep`, `dual_paving_card_bound` and `nonspanning_structure`
arguments in [Native]; no prime-group structure is needed for them.

If $|A|<|B|$, (P-U) gives $|C_X|\ge |X|+1$ for every nonempty
$X\subseteq S$. For $|X|<r$, paving supplies an independent
$|X|$-subset of $C_X$; for $|X|=r$, the set $C_X$ has at least
$r+1$ elements and therefore spans. The empty subset has rank zero.
Thus (P-R) proves the strict-gap assertion.

At equality, (P-U) gives $|C_X|\ge |X|$. Every proper subset of
$S$ therefore passes (P-R), so failure is equivalent to
$\rho_N(C_S)<r$. Since $|C_S|\ge r$, the sparse paving facts
identify precisely a circuit-hyperplane of size $r$. Conversely such
a hyperplane has rank $r-1$ and fails (P-R) at $X=S$.
This is the supplied sparse paving obstruction mechanism, now with
the prime additive estimate: the group-independent Hall and
one-representative repair argument in [Native, `sparse_hall_obstruction`]
has the same conclusion. Its published `basiswise_obstruction` theorem
has a torsion-free hypothesis; it is not itself applied to
$\mathbb F_p$ here. The Hall/Rado mechanism, dual rank argument and
repair are credited supply.

Looplessness rules out $r=1$ because a singleton circuit would be a
loop. If $m=r$, the target is free and has no circuit-hyperplane;
hence failure requires $m\ge r+1$. Complementary cardinalities give
$|U_S|=m-r$. Finally Cauchy–Davenport and the containment in $A$
give

$$
m\ge |S+(U_S\cup\{0\})|
\ge \min\{p,r+(m-r+1)-1\}=m.
$$

The containment is consequently equality, proving (P-E).

## 8. Complete arithmetic classification below saturation

**定理 8.1（Every specified failing basis when $m\le p-2$）。**
Assume Convention 7.1 and $|A|=|B|=m\le p-2$. A specified
$S\in\mathcal B(M)$ fails to match $N$ if and only if
$r\ge2$, $q=m-r\ge1$, and there exist
$a\in\mathbb F_p$, $d\in\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0\}$ and an
integer $0\le j\le q$ such that

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=a+I_d[0,m-1],\\
S&=a+I_d[j,j+r-1],\\
U&=I_d[-j,q-j]\setminus\{0\}\subseteq B,\\
H&=B\setminus U\text{ is a circuit-hyperplane of }N.
\end{aligned}
\tag{P-A}
$$

In each such configuration $U=U_S$, $H=C_S$,
$|U|=q$, $|H|=r$ and $\rho_N(H)=r-1$. The individual
admissible sets, not just their union, are exactly

$$
C_{a+(j+i)d}
 =B\setminus I_d[-j-i,m-1-j-i]
\qquad(0\le i<r).
\tag{P-C}
$$

Proof. Suppose $S$ fails. Theorem 7.2 gives $r\ge2$, $q\ge1$
and, for $V=U_S\cup\{0\}$,

$$
S+V=A,\qquad |S|=r,\qquad |V|=q+1,\qquad
|S+V|=m=|S|+|V|-1\le p-2.
\tag{P-V}
$$

Invoke the classical Vosper critical-pair theorem in the version
actually supplied by [BDM, Theorem 1.3]. The pair $(S,V)$ is
nonempty and critical, both sets have at least two elements, and
$|S|+|V|=m+1\le p-1$. Thus neither the whole-group outcome,
nor the outcome $|S|+|V|=p$, nor a singleton outcome is possible.
The remaining outcome says that $S$ and $V$ are arithmetic
progressions with the same nonzero difference $d$. Write

$$
S=s_0+I_d[0,r-1],\qquad V=v_0+I_d[0,q].
$$

Since $0\in V$ and $q<p$, a unique integer $j\in[0,q]$
satisfies $v_0=-jd$. Put $a=s_0-jd$. Every integer from $0$
to $r+q-1=m-1$ is the sum of an index in $[0,r-1]$ and one
in $[0,q]$. Hence, in the field itself,

$$
A=S+V=a+I_d[0,m-1],\qquad
S=a+I_d[j,j+r-1].
$$

These sums give $m$ distinct residues since $m<p$. Removing zero
from $V$ gives $U_S=I_d[-j,q-j]\setminus\{0\}$.
Its complement $C_S$ in $B$ is the actual circuit-hyperplane
of (P-O), proving necessity of every condition in (P-A).

For sufficiency, first determine all blocking translations for the
displayed $A,S$, including translations outside $B$. They are

$$
\{b\in\mathbb F_p:S+b\subseteq A\}=I_d[-j,q-j].
\tag{P-W}
$$

To prove this identity without discarding wraparound, suppose
$S+b\subseteq A$. The first point of $S+b$ is uniquely
$a+td$ for an integer $0\le t\le m-1$, so
$b=(t-j)d$. If $t>q=m-r$, then
$1\le m-t\le r-1$. The point at index $m-t$ of this
translated block is $a+md$. It is outside $A$, because the
$m+1$ residues $a,a+d,\ldots,a+md$ are distinct for $m<p$.
This is a contradiction. Therefore $t\le q$, giving
$-j\le t-j\le q-j$. Conversely a translation with these
indices puts all $r$ block indices between $0$ and $m-1$.
This proves (P-W), including all possible cyclic translations.

The set on the right of (P-W) contains zero exactly at index zero;
its other $q$ elements form $U$. Because $0\notin B$ and
$U\subseteq B$, intersecting (P-W) with $B$ gives $U_S=U$,
and consequently $C_S=B\setminus U=H$. Since $H$ is an actual
circuit-hyperplane, (P-O) proves failure. For each individual
$s=a+(j+i)d$, the equality
$-s+A=I_d[-j-i,m-1-j-i]$ proves (P-C).
Taking complements and unions recovers exactly $U_S=U$ and
$C_S=H$, so no additional admissibility condition is omitted.

**推论 8.2（All failing bases and the nonprogression consequence）。**
Under the hypotheses of Theorem 8.1 the entire family of failing
source bases is

$$
\left\{
 a+I_d[j,j+r-1]:
 \begin{array}{l}
 r\ge2,\ q=m-r\ge1,\ a\in\mathbb F_p,\ d\ne0,\ 0\le j\le q,\\
 A=a+I_d[0,m-1],\ a+I_d[j,j+r-1]\in\mathcal B(M),\\
 U=I_d[-j,q-j]\setminus\{0\}\subseteq B,\\
 B\setminus U\text{ is a circuit-hyperplane of }N
 \end{array}
\right\}.
\tag{P-F}
$$

Here $j$ is an integer and $a,d$ range over the field; repeated
representations denote the same source set. Thus $M$ is not matched
to $N$ exactly when this family is nonempty. In particular $M$ is
matched to $N$ if $A$ is not a progression. This includes every
semi-progression that is not a progression, and therefore includes
the neither-progression-nor-semi-progression case in this range.

Proof. The necessity and sufficiency in Theorem 8.1 apply separately
to every actual source basis; taking precisely those bases gives
(P-F). The statement does not require any uniqueness of the
progression representation of a cyclic set. If $A$ is not a
progression, the required representation of $A$ cannot occur.

## 9. Saturated grounds and the degenerate boundaries

**定理 9.1（The complete saturated criterion and forced assignment）。**
Assume Convention 7.1 and $|A|=|B|=p-1$. There is a unique
$\alpha\in\mathbb F_p$ such that

$$
A=\mathbb F_p\setminus\{\alpha\},\qquad
B=\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0\}.
$$

For every specified basis $S$, the unique assignment satisfying the
sum exclusion, even before a target basis is required, is

$$
f_\alpha(s)=\alpha-s\qquad(s\in S).
\tag{P-S}
$$

It is injective, takes values in $B$, and has image
$T_\alpha=\alpha-S$. Thus

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_s&=\{\alpha-s\},& C_S&=\alpha-S,&
U_S&=B\setminus(\alpha-S),\\
S\text{ matches }N
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \alpha-S\in\mathcal B(N),\\
S\text{ fails to match }N
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\alpha-S\text{ is a circuit-hyperplane of }N.
\end{aligned}
\tag{P-T}
$$

Equivalently, all failing source bases are exactly

$$
\{\alpha-H:H\text{ is a circuit-hyperplane of }N,
                     \ \alpha-H\in\mathcal B(M)\}.
\tag{P-H}
$$

Proof. The two ground descriptions follow from their cardinalities
and the exclusion of zero from $B$. The condition $s+b\notin A$
is precisely $s+b=\alpha$, so it forces $b=\alpha-s$.
Since $s\in A$, this target is nonzero. Distinct source elements
have distinct forced targets. Every admissible map therefore equals
(P-S), and it matches an actual target basis exactly when its image
is a basis. Every $r$-subset in a sparse paving matroid is either
a basis or a circuit-hyperplane [AZ, Theorem 3.3], giving (P-T).
Reflection $x\mapsto\alpha-x$ is an involution, so reflecting each
actual target circuit-hyperplane gives precisely (P-H).
This proves both directions and uniqueness for every rank, including
$r=1$ and $r=p-1$.

For comparison with (P-E), the full set of blocking translations is

$$
\{b:S+b\subseteq A\}=\mathbb F_p\setminus(\alpha-S).
$$

It contains zero, and its intersection with $B$ is $U_S$.
In a failure its cardinality is $p-r$; adjoining zero to $U_S$
recovers this set. Cauchy–Davenport gives
$|S+(U_S\cup\{0\})|\ge r+(p-r)-1=p-1$, so containment
in $A$ is again equality. The critical-pair cardinalities here are
$|S|+|U_S\cup\{0\}|=p$. This is exactly the separate
$|X|+|Y|=p$, $|X+Y|=p-1$ alternative in [BDM, Theorem 1.3],
which does not force the progression conclusion used in Section 8.

**推论 9.2（Ranks, small primes, full grounds and coloops）。**
In the full domain of Convention 7.1 the following boundaries hold.

1. If $r=1$, every source basis matches. If $r=|A|$, its sole
   source basis matches every prescribed actual basis of $N$.
   In particular a rank-zero dual of $N$ forces
   $r=|A|=|B|$ and both matroids are free.
2. For $p=2$ and $p=3$, every allowed pair is matched.
3. Put $q_B=|B|-r$. If $q_B\ge2$, $N$ has no coloops.
   If $q_B=1$ and $r\ge2$, either $N$ is uniform or it has a
   unique coloop $e$ and a unique circuit-hyperplane
   $H=B\setminus\{e\}$. In the latter case its bases are
   precisely the $r$-subsets containing $e$.
4. In this nonuniform corank-one case, at equality
   $m=r+1\le p-2$, a specified source basis fails exactly when
   (P-A) holds with $j=0$ or $j=1$ and respectively
   $\{d\}=\{e\}$ or $\{-d\}=\{e\}$.
   At saturation $m=r+1=p-1$, the exact criterion is instead
   $S=A\setminus\{\alpha-e\}$.
5. Every uniform target is matched when $|A|\le |B|<p$.
   At saturation every source ground is a progression: for any
   nonzero $d$ it equals $\alpha+d+I_d[0,p-2]$.

Proof. For $r=1$, (P-U) makes the admissible set nonempty and
looplessness makes each of its elements a basis. If $r=|A|$,
fix any actual basis $T$ of $N$. The prime-group matching property
[AZ, Theorem 1.2], applied to the equally sized sets $A,T$ with
$0\notin T$, supplies a matching onto $T$. This is an application
of the classical group result, not a new group matching theorem.
If $q_B=0$, the inequalities $r\le |A|\le |B|=r$ force equality,
and a matroid of full ground rank is free.

For $p=2$, the inequalities force $|A|=|B|=r=1$.
For $p=3$, either $r=1$, or $r=2$ forces
$|A|=|B|=2=r$. These cases have already been proved affirmative;
no prime or rank has been removed from either classification.

The coloop facts are the group-independent argument of Proposition
4.1. Explicitly, if $q_B\ge2$, paving of the dual forbids loops
in the dual, hence forbids primal coloops. If $q_B=1$, let $L$
be the loops of the rank-one dual and $D=B\setminus L$ its
nonempty nonloop set. Its bases are the singletons in $D$,
so primal bases are exactly $B\setminus\{d\}$ for $d\in D$.
The set $D$ is the unique primal circuit and $L$ the primal
coloops. Paving gives $|D|\ge r$; as $|B|=r+1$, either
$L=\varnothing$ and $N$ is uniform, or $L=\{e\}$ and
$D=B\setminus\{e\}$ is the unique circuit-hyperplane.

For nonsaturated equality, $q=m-r=1$ leaves just $j=0,1$
in (P-A), with $U=\{d\}$ and $U=\{-d\}$ respectively;
the complement is $H$ exactly when $U=\{e\}$.
At saturation, (P-T) requires
$\alpha-S=B\setminus\{e\}$. Reflection takes $B$ onto $A$
and $e$ onto $\alpha-e$, giving the stated equivalent criterion.
Every set mentioned here is still required to be the specified
actual source basis. Source coloops require no extra restriction.

Uniform targets have no circuit-hyperplanes, so (P-O) excludes
failures at equality; strict gaps follow from Theorem 7.2.
Finally the $p-1$ nonzero multiples of any $d\ne0$ are all the
nonzero elements of the field, proving the last progression formula.
The nonprogression consequence of Corollary 8.2 therefore addresses
exactly the nonsaturated range in which it can have instances.

## 10. The original prime question and symbolic negative families

**命题 10.1（Nonsaturated progression failures for every prime $p\ge5$）。**
For every prime $p\ge5$ set

$$
A=\{1,2,3\},\qquad S=\{1,2\},\qquad
B=\{1,-1,-2\},\qquad H=\{-1,-2\}\subseteq\mathbb F_p.
$$

Let $M$ be uniform of rank two on $A$ and let $N$ have as bases
all two-element subsets of $B$ other than $H$.
Then $M,N$ satisfy Convention 7.1, $A$ is a progression,
$|A|=|B|=3\le p-2$, and the specified basis $S$ fails to match
the actual $N$. Here the unique coloop of $N$ is $1$.

Proof. The residues $1,2,3$ are distinct, as are $1,-1,-2$,
and none of the latter is zero, because $p\ge5$.
The one-deleted-base construction supplied in Section 4 and [Family]
applies with $r=2$, $q=1$ and this ground $B$ and hole $H$.
It gives a loopless rank-two matroid, paving together with its dual,
and makes $H$ a circuit-hyperplane; its bases are
$\{1,-1\}$ and $\{1,-2\}$. Thus $1$ is a coloop, consistently
with Corollary 9.2. This uses the finite-set construction itself,
not a reduction of an integer matroid modulo $p$.

In (P-A) take $a=1$, $d=1$, $j=0$, $r=2$, $m=3$.
The blocking set is $U=\{1\}$ and its complement in $B$ is $H$.
Theorem 8.1 proves failure. More explicitly the admissible sets are

$$
C_1=\{-1,-2\},\qquad C_2=\{-2\}.
$$

Indeed $1+1=2$, $2+1=3$ and $2+(-1)=1$ belong to $A$,
whereas $1+(-1)=0$, $2+(-2)=0$ and $1+(-2)=-1$
are outside $A$. The last exclusion holds for every $p\ge5$.
Every admissible injective image must therefore be the deleted
basis $H$. All assertions are symbolic in $p$.

**命题 10.2（Saturated self-matching failures for every prime $p\ge5$）。**
For every prime $p\ge5$ put

$$
E=\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0\},\qquad
S=\{1,2\},\qquad H=\{-1,-2\}.
$$

Let $M=N$ be the rank-two matroid on $E$ whose bases are all
two-element subsets other than $H$. It is loopless and sparse
paving, its ground is a progression, it has no coloops, and
$S\in\mathcal B(M)$ fails to match $N$.
The actual basis $S'=\{1,-1\}$ of the same $M$ does match $N$.

Proof. Apply the supplied one-deleted-base construction with
$r=2$ and $q=p-3\ge2$. It gives looplessness and both paving
properties. The dual has rank at least two, so $N$ has no coloops.
The sets $S$ and $H$ are distinct: $1\notin H$ for $p\ge5$.
Thus $S$ is an actual source basis. Theorem 9.1 with $\alpha=0$
forces the target image $-S=H$, which is a nonbasis.
The ground is the progression $1+I_1[0,p-2]$.
The set $S'$ is a basis because $1\notin H$, and its forced
image is $-S'=S'$, an actual basis; sending each element to its
negative gives both sums zero outside $E$. This also distinguishes
failure of a specified basis from failure of every source basis.

**定理 10.3（Disposition of Question 6.1 and [AZ, Question 2.8]）。**
Under Convention 7.1, every strict gap matches, with no progression
condition. Equal-size failures for $m\le p-2$ are exactly (P-A),
basis by basis, and for $m=p-1$ are exactly (P-T).
Consequently the one-element-gap clause [AZ, Question 2.8(1)] is
affirmative for every prime, even without its progression premise.
The universal equality assertion in [AZ, Question 2.8(2)] is false
for every prime $p\ge5$, already on progression source grounds;
for $p=2,3$ every allowed instance is affirmative.
For a particular equality instance, being a progression or a
semi-progression alone does not prescribe a negative answer: its
answer is exactly the basiswise classification above.

Proof. Theorem 7.2 applies to every strict gap, including
$|B|=|A|+1$. Theorems 8.1 and 9.1 cover the two exhaustive
possibilities for equal size $m<p$. Propositions 10.1 and 10.2
supply progression equality failures for every $p\ge5$ in the two
regimes. Corollary 9.2 handles $p=2,3$ and gives affirmative
equality instances, including all uniform targets. These are the
original common positive rank and actual-target quantifiers of
[AZ, Definition 1.3]: $n=r$, $E(M)=A$, $E(N)=B$.
The strict clause and the equality clause therefore retain their
separate truth values. This answers precisely Question 6.1.
The hypotheses still require dual paving and a prime-order group;
the larger-hyperplane problem of Question 6.2 and the composite-order
case of Proposition 5.4 are not consequences of these assertions.
