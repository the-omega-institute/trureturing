# Sparse paving group matchings: strict gaps and complete equal-size obstructions

## 1. Objects and matching quantifiers

**约定 1.1（Ambient group and finite sets）。** Throughout Sections 1–6,
$G$ is an arbitrary torsion-free abelian group, written additively, with
identity $0$. For finite subsets $X,Y\subseteq G$, write

$$
X+Y=\{x+y:x\in X,\ y\in Y\}.
$$

For integers $u\le v$, write
$[u,v]_{\mathbb Z}d=\{td:t\in\mathbb Z,\ u\le t\le v\}$.
If $d\ne0$, torsion-freeness makes these $v-u+1$ elements distinct.
No order, sign, cyclicity or positivity condition on the ground sets is
assumed.

**定义 1.2（Finite matroids and their duals）。** A matroid $P$ on a finite
set $E$ is a family of independent subsets containing the empty set, closed
under taking subsets, and satisfying augmentation: if $I,J$ are independent
and $|I|<|J|$, some $e\in J\setminus I$ makes $I\cup\{e\}$ independent.
Its rank function is

$$
\rho_P(X)=\max\{|I|:I\subseteq X,\ I\text{ independent}\}.
$$

A basis is a maximal independent set; all bases have size
$\rho_P(E)$. Write $\mathcal B(P)$ for the set of bases. A circuit is a
minimal dependent set. The closure of $X$ is
$\operatorname{cl}_P(X)=\{e\in E:\rho_P(X\cup\{e\})=\rho_P(X)\}$.
A flat equals its closure; a hyperplane is a flat of rank
$\rho_P(E)-1$. A circuit-hyperplane is both a circuit and a hyperplane.
A loop has dependent singleton; a coloop belongs to every basis.
The dual $P^*$ has bases $E\setminus Q$, for $Q\in\mathcal B(P)$,
and rank $|E|-\rho_P(E)$.
A rank-$k$ matroid is paving if it has no circuit of cardinality less
than $k$. For $k\ge1$, this is equivalent to independence of every
$(k-1)$-subset; rank-zero paving imposes no condition.
A matroid is sparse paving if it and its dual are paving.

**定义 1.3（The actual target and a specified source basis）。** Let $M,N$
be finite loopless matroids with respective ground sets $A,B\subseteq G$
and the same rank $r\ge1$. Always assume $N$ is sparse paving and
$0\notin B$. A specified $S\in\mathcal B(M)$ is matched to $N$ if there
exist $T\in\mathcal B(N)$ and a bijection $f:S\longrightarrow T$ such
that

$$
s+f(s)\notin A\qquad(s\in S).
$$

The matroid $M$ is matched to $N$ if every $S\in\mathcal B(M)$ is
matched to $N$. A failing basis means a basis with no such bijection to
any actual basis of the specified $N$. This is precisely the ordering-free
form of Aliabadi–Zerbib Definition 1.3 [AZ]: enumerate $S$ in any order
and enumerate $T$ by its images under $f$. Conversely a pair of matched
ordered bases gives this bijection.

The target matroid is fixed independently of the additive group law.
Its rank function and its actual basis family are used throughout.
There is no assumption of representability, a common ground set, or
invariance of matroid independence under translation. The source matroid
need not be paving.

**定义 1.4（Admissible targets and blocked translations）。** For
$s\in S$ and nonempty $X\subseteq S$, put

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_s&=\{b\in B:s+b\notin A\},\\
C_X&=\bigcup_{s\in X}C_s,\\
U_X&=B\setminus C_X=\{b\in B:X+b\subseteq A\},\\
V_X&=U_X\cup\{0\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $C_X$ is a union, whereas $U_X$ is an intersection of blocking
conditions. Since $X\subseteq A$ and $0\notin B$,
$X+V_X\subseteq A$ and $|V_X|=|U_X|+1$.
For $X=\varnothing$ put $C_X=\varnothing$; the additive arguments below
are used only for nonempty $X$.

## 2. Classical additive growth and its equality case

The ingredients in this section are the classical ordered sumset bound
and its equality rigidity. The proofs are included to specify exactly
which hypotheses are used. They do not use the stronger inequality
asserted in [AZ, Lemma 3.14].

**引理 2.1（An auxiliary compatible order）。** Any subgroup generated by
finitely many elements of $G$ admits a translation-invariant total order.

Proof. Such a subgroup $K$ is a finitely generated torsion-free abelian
group. By the structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups,
$K\cong\mathbb Z^k$ for some $k\ge0$. The lexicographic order on
$\mathbb Z^k$ is total and invariant under addition. Transport it to $K$.
All sets and sums in any one application lie in such a subgroup. This is
an auxiliary order for a proof, and assigns no uniform sign to either
ground set. The general orderability result is also [AZ, Theorem 3.8].

**引理 2.2（Sumset bound and rigidity）。** Let $X,Y$ be nonempty finite
subsets of $G$, of cardinalities $p,q$. Then

$$
|X+Y|\ge p+q-1.
$$

If $p,q\ge2$, equality holds if and only if there are $x,y,d\in G$,
$d\ne0$, with

$$
X=x+[0,p-1]_{\mathbb Z}d,\qquad
Y=y+[0,q-1]_{\mathbb Z}d.
$$

Proof. Use Lemma 2.1 on the subgroup generated by $X\cup Y$ and list
$x_0<\cdots<x_{p-1}$ and $y_0<\cdots<y_{q-1}$.
A lattice path from $(0,0)$ to $(p-1,q-1)$ that increases one coordinate
by one at each step visits $p+q-1$ vertices. Their sums $x_i+y_j$ are
strictly increasing, giving the bound.

Suppose equality holds and $p,q\ge2$. Every such path lists the whole
sumset in increasing order. Fix $0\le i<p-1$, $0\le j<q-1$.
Choose two paths with the same initial part ending at $(i,j)$ and the
same final part starting at $(i+1,j+1)$, one passing through $(i+1,j)$
and the other through $(i,j+1)$. The intermediate sums occupy the same
position in the increasing list of the whole sumset. Therefore

$$
x_{i+1}+y_j=x_i+y_{j+1},\qquad
x_{i+1}-x_i=y_{j+1}-y_j.
$$

All successive gaps in both lists are a common positive $d\ne0$.
This proves the two progression descriptions in $G$ itself. Conversely,
these descriptions give
$X+Y=x+y+[0,p+q-2]_{\mathbb Z}d$: every integer in the indicated
interval is a sum of indices in the two shorter intervals, and
torsion-freeness gives exactly $p+q-1$ distinct elements.

**引理 2.3（The target-union bound）。** Under Definition 1.3, for every
nonempty $X\subseteq S$,

$$
|U_X|\le |A|-|X|,\qquad
|C_X|\ge |B|-|A|+|X|.
$$

Proof. Definition 1.4 and Lemma 2.2 give

$$
|A|\ge |X+V_X|\ge |X|+|V_X|-1=|X|+|U_X|.
$$

Subtract from $|B|$ to obtain the second inequality. The added element
$0$ is counted exactly once in $V_X$; it is not counted in $U_X$.
The argument applies whether or not $0\in A$.

## 3. Matroid rank and independent representatives

The matroid facts here are classical; the sparse paving characterization
is stated in [Bonin, Introduction] and [AZ, Theorem 3.3]. The
independent-representative criterion is Rado's theorem, stated in
[AZ, Theorem 3.6]. We give its finite matroid proof rather than replacing
the target by a linear realization.

**引理 3.1（Hyperplane size and the sparse paving rank alternatives）。**
Let $N$ be a rank-$r$ sparse paving matroid on $B$, where $b=|B|$ and
$r\ge1$. Every set of size at most $r-1$ is independent, and every
hyperplane has size at most $r$. Every subset of size at least $r+1$
is spanning. A nonspanning subset of size at least $r$ has size exactly
$r$ and is a circuit-hyperplane.

Proof. Independence of sets of size at most $r-1$ follows from paving:
a smaller dependent set would contain a circuit of size less than $r$.
For the hyperplane bound, recall the dual rank identity

$$
\rho_{N^*}(D)=|D|-r+\rho_N(B\setminus D).
$$

Here is a derivation from the definition of dual bases. The maximum size
of an independent subset of $D$ in the dual is
$\max_{Q\in\mathcal B(N)}|D\setminus Q|$.
The minimum of $|D\cap Q|$ over these bases is
$r-\rho_N(B\setminus D)$: one obtains it by extending a basis of
$B\setminus D$ to a basis of $N$, and no basis can use more than
$\rho_N(B\setminus D)$ elements of that complement. Substitution gives
the displayed identity.

If $F$ is a hyperplane, its complement $D=B\setminus F$ has dual rank
$|D|-1$. For every proper $D'\subset D$, the set $B\setminus D'$
contains $F$ and an element outside $F$, hence spans $N$. The identity
then gives $\rho_{N^*}(D')=|D'|$. Thus $D$ is a circuit of $N^*$.
Writing $q=b-r$, dual paving gives $|D|\ge q$, so $|F|\le r$.
This includes $q=0$; the asserted bound remains valid.

Every nonspanning subset is contained in a hyperplane: extend a basis
of that subset to an independent set of size $r-1$ and take its closure.
Consequently a set of size at least $r+1$ spans. If $Q$ is nonspanning
and $|Q|\ge r$, its containing hyperplane must have size exactly $r$
and equal $Q$. All proper subsets of $Q$ are independent by paving,
so $Q$ is also a circuit.

**引理 3.2（Rado's finite matroid criterion）。** Let $P$ be a finite
matroid on $E$, and let $(F_i)_{i\in I}$ be a finite indexed family of
subsets of $E$. There exist distinct $e_i\in F_i$ whose set is
independent in $P$ if and only if

$$
\rho_P\left(\bigcup_{i\in J}F_i\right)\ge |J|
\qquad(J\subseteq I).
$$

Proof. Necessity follows by considering the selected representatives
for $J$. For sufficiency induct on $k=|I|$. The case $k=0$ is empty;
for $k=1$ the rank inequality provides a nonloop in the sole set.
Assume $k\ge2$ and abbreviate $D_J=\bigcup_{i\in J}F_i$.

First suppose some nonempty proper $J\subset I$ is tight:
$\rho_P(D_J)=|J|$. The induction hypothesis gives independent
representatives for $J$, forming a set $Q\subseteq D_J$ of size $|J|$.
Thus $Q$ is a basis of $D_J$ and
$\operatorname{cl}_P(Q)=\operatorname{cl}_P(D_J)$.
Contract $Q$. For clarity, contraction by an independent set has rank

$$
\rho_{P/Q}(Z)=\rho_P(Z\cup Q)-|Q|\qquad(Z\subseteq E\setminus Q).
$$

This follows directly by extending $Q$ to a maximal independent subset
of $Z\cup Q$; independent subsets in the contraction are precisely sets
that can be adjoined to $Q$ independently. For $K\subseteq I\setminus J$,
closure equality and the original rank condition give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{P/Q}(D_K\setminus Q)
&=\rho_P(D_K\cup Q)-|J|\\
&=\rho_P(D_K\cup D_J)-|J|\\
&\ge |K|.
\end{aligned}
$$

Induction in $P/Q$, for the family $(F_i\setminus Q)_{i\notin J}$,
gives representatives independent after contraction. Together with $Q$
they are distinct independent representatives for all of $I$.

If there is no nonempty proper tight $J$, all those $J$ satisfy
$\rho_P(D_J)\ge |J|+1$. Fix $i_0\in I$ and a nonloop
$e\in F_{i_0}$. For every nonempty $K\subseteq I\setminus\{i_0\}$,

$$
\rho_{P/e}(D_K\setminus\{e\})
=\rho_P(D_K\cup\{e\})-1
\ge\rho_P(D_K)-1\ge |K|.
$$

The empty $K$ causes no condition. Induction in $P/e$ supplies the
remaining representatives; adjoining $e$ completes the proof.

**命题 3.3（Exact basiswise matching test）。** For a specified basis
$S$ in Definition 1.3,

$$
S\text{ matches }N
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho_N(C_X)\ge |X|\text{ for every }X\subseteq S.
$$

Proof. Apply Lemma 3.2 to the family $(C_s)_{s\in S}$ in the actual
matroid $N$. Its independent representatives form an independent set
of size $|S|=r$, hence a basis of $N$. Membership in $C_s$ is exactly
the required exclusion of $s+f(s)$ from $A$. In the reverse direction,
the image of $X$ under a matching is an independent $|X|$-subset of
$C_X$.

## 4. Every strict ground-size gap is sufficient

**定理 4.1（Strict-gap theorem）。** For every torsion-free abelian group
$G$ and every pair of finite loopless matroids $M,N$ on finite subsets
$A,B\subseteq G$, respectively, of common rank $r\ge1$, if $N$ and
$N^*$ are paving, $0\notin B$ and $|A|<|B|$, then every
$S\in\mathcal B(M)$ admits a bijection onto some
$T\in\mathcal B(N)$ satisfying $s+f(s)\notin A$ for all $s\in S$.

Proof. Fix any such $S$. For nonempty $X\subseteq S$, Lemma 2.3 gives
$|C_X|\ge |X|+1$.
If $|X|\le r-1$, choose a $|X|$-subset of $C_X$; it is independent
by Lemma 3.1, so $\rho_N(C_X)\ge |X|$.
If $|X|=r$, then $|C_X|\ge r+1$ and Lemma 3.1 gives
$\rho_N(C_X)=r$.
The empty subset has rank zero. Proposition 3.3 supplies the desired
bijection to a basis of $N$. Since $S$ was arbitrary, $M$ is matched
to $N$.

**推论 4.2（The exact one-element gap）。** Under the other hypotheses
of Theorem 4.1, $|A|+1=|B|$ implies that $M$ is matched to $N$.

Proof. The equality of cardinalities implies $|A|<|B|$, so Theorem 4.1
applies. In [AZ, Question 2.7], the notation $n$ for rank is $r$ here,
$E(M)=A$ and $E(N)=B$. This proves its strict one-element-gap clause.
The disjunction in that question still has a negative answer, because
its equal-cardinality clause has the supplied counterexamples described
in Proposition 6.3 below.

## 5. Exhaustive classification at equal cardinality

**引理 5.1（Only a full-basis rank obstruction can survive）。** Assume
Definition 1.3 and $|A|=|B|=m$. For any specified basis $S$,

$$
S\text{ fails to match }N
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
C_S\text{ is a circuit-hyperplane of }N\text{ of size }r.
$$

In a failure, $r\ge2$, $m\ge r+1$, $|U_S|=m-r$ and
$S+(U_S\cup\{0\})=A$.

Proof. Lemma 2.3 gives $|C_X|\ge |X|$ for every nonempty $X\subseteq S$.
For $|X|\le r-1$, paving then gives $\rho_N(C_X)\ge |X|$.
By Proposition 3.3 the only possible failure is
$\rho_N(C_S)<r$. Since $|C_S|\ge r$, Lemma 3.1 identifies $C_S$
as a circuit-hyperplane of size $r$. Conversely that hyperplane has
rank $r-1$, violating Proposition 3.3 for $X=S$.

For $r=1$, looplessness makes every nonempty $C_S$ have rank one;
thus failure requires $r\ge2$. If $m=r$, $N$ has an independent
set of size $m$, necessarily $B$, so it is free and cannot have a
nonspanning $r$-subset. Thus $m\ge r+1$ in a failure.
Finally $|U_S|=m-r$ and

$$
m\ge |S+(U_S\cup\{0\})|\ge r+(m-r+1)-1=m.
$$

The containment in $A$ from Definition 1.4 is therefore equality.

**引理 5.2（All translations that keep a block inside a progression）。**
Let $r\ge1$, $m\ge r$, $d\ne0$ and $0\le j\le m-r$ be integers,
and put

$$
A=a+[0,m-1]_{\mathbb Z}d,\qquad
S=a+[j,j+r-1]_{\mathbb Z}d.
$$

Then the full set of translations in $G$ satisfying $S+b\subseteq A$
is

$$
W_j=[-j,m-r-j]_{\mathbb Z}d.
$$

Proof. If $S+b\subseteq A$, its first element gives
$a+jd+b=a+td$ for an integer $0\le t<m$, hence
$b=kd$ for some integer $k$. The first and last elements of $S+b$
have indices $j+k$ and $j+r-1+k$ in $A$.
Torsion-freeness makes all integer indices unique, so
$-j\le k\le m-r-j$.
Conversely these inequalities put every index of $S+kd$ between
$0$ and $m-1$. In particular $0\in W_j$ and
$|W_j\setminus\{0\}|=m-r$.

**定理 5.3（Complete obstruction for a specified source basis）。**
For every pair $M,N$ satisfying Definition 1.3 and
$|A|=|B|=m$, and for every specified $S\in\mathcal B(M)$,
$S$ fails to match $N$ if and only if $r\ge2$, $m\ge r+1$, and
there exist $a,d\in G$, $d\ne0$, and an integer $0\le j\le m-r$
with all the following properties:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=\{a+td:0\le t<m\},\\
S&=\{a+(j+t)d:0\le t<r\},\\
U&=\{td:-j\le t\le m-r-j,\ t\ne0\}\subseteq B,\\
H&=B\setminus U\text{ is a circuit-hyperplane of }N.
\end{aligned}
$$

All indices here are integers. In every such obstruction,
$U=U_S$, $H=C_S$, $|U|=m-r$, $|H|=r$ and $\rho_N(H)=r-1$.

Proof. Suppose $S$ fails. Lemma 5.1 gives $r\ge2$, $m\ge r+1$,
$|U_S|=m-r$ and

$$
|S+(U_S\cup\{0\})|=|S|+|U_S\cup\{0\}|-1=m.
$$

Both summands have at least two elements. Lemma 2.2 therefore gives
$s_0,v_0,d\in G$, $d\ne0$, such that

$$
S=s_0+[0,r-1]_{\mathbb Z}d,\qquad
U_S\cup\{0\}=v_0+[0,m-r]_{\mathbb Z}d.
$$

Since the second progression contains $0$, there is a unique
$j\in[0,m-r]_{\mathbb Z}$ with $v_0=-jd$.
Set $a=s_0-jd$. Adding the two progressions gives
$A=a+[0,m-1]_{\mathbb Z}d$, and
$S=a+[j,j+r-1]_{\mathbb Z}d$.
Removing $0$ from the second progression gives the stated $U=U_S$.
Its complement $H=C_S$ is the circuit-hyperplane of Lemma 5.1.
This proves every required condition without an assumption on signs
or the rest of the bases of $M$.

Conversely suppose the displayed conditions hold. Lemma 5.2 identifies
the full blocking-translation set as $W_j=U\cup\{0\}$.
Because $U\subseteq B$ and $0\notin B$, intersection with $B$ gives
$U_S=U$ and hence $C_S=H$.
The circuit-hyperplane $H$ has rank $r-1$ in the specified $N$.
Proposition 3.3 rules out an independent system of $r$ representatives,
so no bijection to any target basis exists. This also proves the
additional identities claimed in the theorem.

**推论 5.4（Exact existential formulation for two matroids）。** Under
Definition 1.3 and $|A|=|B|=m$,
$M$ is not matched to $N$ if and only if $r\ge2$, $m\ge r+1$,
and there exist $a,d\in G$, $d\ne0$, and an integer
$0\le j\le m-r$ such that

$$
\begin{gathered}
A=a+[0,m-1]_{\mathbb Z}d,\qquad
S_j=a+[j,j+r-1]_{\mathbb Z}d\in\mathcal B(M),\\
U_j=([-j,m-r-j]_{\mathbb Z}d)\setminus\{0\}\subseteq B,\qquad
B\setminus U_j\text{ is a circuit-hyperplane of }N.
\end{gathered}
$$

For a fixed progression representation of $A$ with $r\ge2$ and $m>r$,
the entire family of failing bases is exactly

$$
\left\{S_j:
0\le j\le m-r,\quad S_j\in\mathcal B(M),\quad
U_j\subseteq B,\quad
B\setminus U_j\text{ is a circuit-hyperplane of }N\right\}.
$$

Proof. Negating the universal quantifier over source bases in
Definition 1.3 gives the existence of one failing $S$.
Apply Theorem 5.3 to it, or conversely to the displayed $S_j$.
There is no requirement that every basis of $M$ be a block.

To justify the assertion for a fixed progression representation,
order the subgroup generated by the finitely many parameters of any
two such representations. In an increasing enumeration of a
progression of size $m\ge2$, consecutive gaps equal its positive
step. Hence two representations of $A$ have the same step up to
reversal and the same endpoints. Reversal replaces
$(a,d,j)$ by $(a+(m-1)d,-d,m-r-j)$, leaving $S_j$ and $U_j$
as sets unchanged. Thus the necessity in Theorem 5.3 holds for the
fixed representation as well, and its sufficiency already did.

**推论 5.5（Immediate positive cases at equality）。** Under
Definition 1.3 and $|A|=|B|$, $M$ is matched to $N$ if $A$ is not
an arithmetic progression, or if $N$ has no circuit-hyperplanes.
In particular it is matched to every uniform target of the same rank.

Proof. Either hypothesis excludes the corresponding necessary
condition in Theorem 5.3. A uniform rank-$r$ matroid has every
$r$-subset as a basis and therefore has no circuit-hyperplane:
a circuit-hyperplane in rank $r$ has size $r$, since deleting an
element makes it independent of rank $r-1$.

## 6. Degeneracies, supplied families, and other obstruction shapes

**命题 6.1（Rank one, free grounds, and coloops）。** Under
Definition 1.3 with $|A|\le |B|$ the following statements hold.

1. If $r=1$, every source basis is matched.
2. If $|A|=|B|=r$, both matroids are free and are matched.
3. If $r\ge2$ and $q=|B|-r\ge2$, $N$ has no coloops.
4. If $r\ge2$ and $q=1$, $N$ is either uniform, or it has exactly
   one coloop $e$ and exactly one circuit-hyperplane $B\setminus\{e\}$.
   In the latter case its bases are precisely the $r$-subsets containing
   $e$. At equality $|A|=|B|=r+1$, failure occurs exactly for the
   endpoint blocks in Theorem 5.3 for which $U_j=\{e\}$.

Proof. For (1), $S$ is a singleton and Lemma 2.3 makes $C_S$
nonempty; every element is a nonloop. For (2), a rank-$r$ matroid on
$r$ elements has its ground as its sole basis; matching follows from
Proposition 3.3 and Lemma 2.3, since every subset of $B$ is independent.
For (3), paving of $N^*$ of rank at least two excludes dual loops.
A loop in the dual is exactly a coloop in $N$ by the complementary
basis definition.

For (4), $N^*$ has rank one. Let $L$ be its set of loops and
$D=B\setminus L$ its nonempty set of nonloops. Its bases are exactly
the singletons in $D$, so the primal bases are precisely
$B\setminus\{d\}$ for $d\in D$. Thus $L$ is the set of primal
coloops, $D$ is the unique primal circuit, and every proper subset
of $D$ lies in a primal basis. Paving of $N$ gives $|D|\ge r$;
since $|B|=r+1$, $|L|\le1$. If $L$ is empty the matroid is uniform.
If $L=\{e\}$ then $|D|=r$, $D$ has rank $r-1$, and adding $e$
spans; it is the unique circuit-hyperplane.
In Theorem 5.3, $m-r=1$ makes $j$ either $0$ or $1$;
$U_0=\{d\}$ and $U_1=\{-d\}$. The assertion follows.
There is consequently no implicit coloopless restriction in either
main theorem. Coloops of the source have no special role, because the
argument applies separately to every one of its actual bases.

**引理 6.2（The supplied one-deleted-base construction）。** Let $E$
be a finite set of size $r+q$, let $H\subset E$ have size $r$, and
let $r\ge2$, $q\ge1$. The family

$$
\mathcal Q=\{Q\subseteq E:|Q|=r,\ Q\ne H\}
$$

is the basis family of a loopless sparse paving rank-$r$ matroid,
and $H$ is a circuit-hyperplane. This is the one-deleted-base
construction supplied in [Family], not an additional construction claim.

Proof. The family is nonempty because $E\setminus H$ is nonempty.
For basis exchange, fix $Q,R\in\mathcal Q$ and $x\in Q\setminus R$.
If $|R\setminus Q|=1$, exchanging with its unique element gives $R$.
If $|R\setminus Q|\ge2$, the candidates
$(Q\setminus\{x\})\cup\{y\}$ for $y\in R\setminus Q$ are distinct;
at most one equals $H$, so an allowed exchange exists.
This is the basis-axiom characterization of a matroid.

Every $I\subseteq E$ with $|I|<r$ extends to an $r$-subset $Q$.
If $Q=H$, choose $h\in H\setminus I$ and $e\in E\setminus H$;
replacing $h$ by $e$ gives an allowed basis still containing $I$.
Thus all sets of size at most $r-1$ are independent, proving paving
and looplessness. Complementation shows the dual bases are the
$q$-subsets other than $E\setminus H$. The same extension argument
makes every set of size below $q$ independent in the dual, including
$q=1$, when this statement concerns only the empty set.
Finally $H$ is dependent with all proper subsets independent.
For any $e\notin H$, replacing one element of $H$ by $e$ gives a
basis inside $H\cup\{e\}$. Hence $H$ has rank $r-1$ and is a
hyperplane as well as a circuit.

**命题 6.3（Recovery of the supplied punctured-interval family）。** Let
$n,s\ge2$, $G=\mathbb Z$, $E=\{1,\ldots,n+s\}$ and
$H=\{s+1,\ldots,s+n\}$. Let $M=N$ have as bases all $n$-subsets
of $E$ other than $H$. The basis $S=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ fails to match
$N$. This is the family already supplied in [Family].

Proof. In Lemma 6.2 use $r=n$, $q=s$. The initial block $S$ differs
from $H$, and so is a basis. In Theorem 5.3 take

$$
m=n+s,\qquad a=1,\qquad d=1,\qquad j=0,\qquad
U=\{1,\ldots,s\}.
$$

Then $E\setminus U=H$, giving precisely the obstruction.
Directly, an admissible target for $x\in S$ must satisfy
$x+b>n+s$, hence $b\in H$. All $n$ distinct targets would therefore
form the nonbasis $H$.
The member $n=s=2$ keeps the negative answer to the disjunctive
[AZ, Question 2.7]. Theorem 4.1 proves the strict-gap branch and does
not turn the whole question into an affirmative assertion.

**例 6.4（An interior block, zero in the source, and other bases）。**
Take $A=\{0,1,2,3\}$, $M$ uniform of rank two on $A$,
$B=\{-1,1,4,5\}$, and let $N$ have all two-element bases except
$H=\{4,5\}$. Lemma 6.2 makes $N$ loopless and sparse paving.
For $a=0$, $d=1$, $m=4$, $r=2$, $j=1$,
$S=\{1,2\}$ and $U=\{-1,1\}$ meet Theorem 5.3, so $S$ fails.
The nonblock basis $\{0,3\}$ does match: send $0$ to $4$ and $3$
to $1$. Both sums equal $4\notin A$, and $\{1,4\}$ is an actual
basis of $N$. Thus failure of $M$ to be matched does not mean that
all its bases fail, or that all its bases are consecutive blocks.

**例 6.5（Corank one and a coloop obstruction）。** Take
$A=\{1,2,3\}$ with uniform rank-two source,
$B=\{1,4,5\}$, and target bases $\{1,4\},\{1,5\}$.
Here $1$ is a coloop, $H=\{4,5\}$ is a circuit-hyperplane,
$S=\{1,2\}$ and $U=\{1\}$. Lemma 6.2, or the direct description
in Proposition 6.1, verifies sparse paving. This is the $j=0$
corank-one obstruction. Reversing the progression gives the equivalent
$j=1$ description with step $-1$. No assumption of corank at least
two is part of Theorem 5.3.

**例 6.6（A noncyclic ambient group and independent ground sets）。**
Take $G=\mathbb Z^2$, $a=(0,1)$, $d=(1,0)$, $m=4$, $r=2$,
$j=1$, and

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=\{(t,1):0\le t\le3\},& S&=\{(1,1),(2,1)\},\\
U&=\{(-1,0),(1,0)\},& H&=\{(0,3),(0,4)\},\qquad B=U\cup H.
\end{aligned}
$$

Choose the uniform rank-two source and the one-deleted-base target
with hole $H$. Theorem 5.3 applies. In particular the ambient group
need not be cyclic, $A$ need not lie in the subgroup generated by $d$,
and $B$ need not be contained in the affine progression containing $A$.
A progression obstruction forces $A$ into one coset of an infinite
cyclic subgroup; it imposes no such restriction on the whole ambient
group or on $H$.

**命题 6.7（Sign and endpoint symmetry）。** The obstruction conditions
are invariant under

$$
(a,d,j)\longmapsto(a+(m-1)d,-d,m-r-j).
$$

They allow both endpoints $j=0,m-r$ and every interior integer.
Applying any injective group homomorphism to all elements preserves
the sum exclusions and the obstruction, with the matroids transported
by the resulting ground-set bijections.

Proof. Reversing the indices leaves $A$ and $S$ unchanged. Substituting
$t\mapsto-t$ in $[-j,m-r-j]_{\mathbb Z}d$ leaves $U$ unchanged
under the displayed transformation. For a homomorphism $\phi$,
$\phi(s+b)=\phi(s)+\phi(b)$, and injectivity makes
$s+b\in A$ equivalent to $\phi(s)+\phi(b)\in\phi(A)$.
Transport preserves every independent set and therefore every basis,
circuit and hyperplane. The negative identity map handles simultaneous
sign reversal; no positivity premise enters either theorem.

## 7. Exact failure boundaries and the additive source defect

**反例 7.1（The extra unit in the cited additive lemma is false）。**
[AZ, Lemma 3.14] states that nonempty finite $P,Q$ in a group with
$P,Q,P+Q\subseteq X\subseteq G\setminus\{0\}$ imply
$|X|\ge |P|+|Q|+1$. Take $G=\mathbb Z$,

$$
P=Q=\{1,2,3\},\qquad X=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}.
$$

Then $P+Q=\{2,3,4,5,6\}$, so all hypotheses hold, but the
asserted conclusion is $6\ge7$.
More generally $P=[1,p]_{\mathbb Z}$ and $Q=[1,q]_{\mathbb Z}$
with $X=[1,p+q]_{\mathbb Z}$ give equality $|X|=|P|+|Q|$.

The precise bookkeeping defect is visible in the proof of that lemma.
For $P_0=P\cup\{0\}$ and $Q_0=Q\cup\{0\}$ one has

$$
P_0+Q_0=\{0\}\cup P\cup Q\cup(P+Q),
$$

with a disjoint added $\{0\}$. The proof's identity omits this
singleton. A bound $|P_0+Q_0|\ge |P_0|+|Q_0|-1$
therefore gives $|X|\ge |P|+|Q|$, not the extra unit.
[AK, Lemma 2.8] repeats the same statement and omitted-singleton
identity. The same counterexample applies at its stated hypotheses.
Neither claim is an ingredient of Lemma 2.3.
This counterexample concerns those exact lemmas; it is not a claim
that every theorem citing either paper is false.

**反例 7.2（The auxiliary self-matching claim in the sequel）。**
[AK, Claim 3.3] is used with a group matching $f$ on a ground set,
a source basis $S$ and a nonbasis image $T=f(S)$. It asserts the
existence of $x\in E\setminus T$ and $s\in S$ with $s+x\notin E$.
In the supplied $n=s=2$ member of Proposition 6.3, take
$E=\{1,2,3,4\}$, forbidden basis $H=\{3,4\}$,
$S=\{1,2\}$, and the group matching

$$
f(1)=4,\quad f(2)=3,\quad f(3)=2,\quad f(4)=1.
$$

All four sums are $5\notin E$; $T=H$ is not a basis.
But $E\setminus T=\{1,2\}$ and
$S+(E\setminus T)=\{2,3,4\}\subseteq E$.
Thus this claim fails under the very sparse paving, loopless,
zero-excluding self-matching conditions in which it is used.
This delimits that proof step and does not assert a separate
classification for arbitrary paving targets.

**反例 7.3（Dual paving cannot simply be dropped）。** Let
$G=\mathbb Z$, $A=\{1,2,3,4\}$, and $M$ be uniform of rank two
on $A$. Put $B=\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ and define the target to be the
rank-two partition matroid allowing at most one element from each
of $\{1,2\}$ and $\{3,4,5\}$. It is loopless and paving.
For $S=\{1,2\}$ one has $C_S=\{3,4,5\}$ of rank one,
so $S$ has no matched target basis despite $|B|=|A|+1$ and
$0\notin B$. The set $\{3,4,5\}$ is a hyperplane of size three;
its complement is a two-element circuit in the dual, whose rank
is three. Hence dual paving fails exactly where Lemma 3.1 needs it.

**反例 7.4（Torsion and the exclusion of zero）。** The strict-gap
conclusion fails in general finite abelian groups. In
$G=\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z$, let
$A=\{0,2,4,6\}$, take the uniform rank-two source, and put
$B=\{1,2,3,4,6\}$. Let $N$ have all two-element bases except
$H=\{1,3\}$. Lemma 6.2 verifies looplessness and sparse paving.
For $S=\{0,2\}$, every even target keeps both sums in $A$, while
every odd target leaves $A$; thus $C_S=H$ has rank one.
Here $0\notin B$ and $|B|=|A|+1$, but $S$ fails to match.
This counterexample does not concern prime-order groups specifically.

The zero exclusion is also a real condition. Even over $\mathbb Z$,
let $A=\{1,2,3\}$, take a uniform rank-two source, and put
$B=\{0,1,3,4\}$ with the one-deleted-base target whose
circuit-hyperplane is $\{3,4\}$. For $S=\{1,2\}$ the targets
$0,1$ are blocked and $C_S=\{3,4\}$ has rank one. Thus a strict
one-element gap does not suffice if $0\in B$ is allowed.
At equality already $r=m=1$, $A=\{1\}$, $B=\{0\}$ fails.

## 8. Mathematical sources, correspondence, and remaining questions

The independent-representative theorem, ordered additive growth,
equality rigidity, and elementary sparse paving structure are classical
ingredients. The substantive inference here is their composition in
Theorem 4.1 and the necessary-and-sufficient original-domain
classification in Theorem 5.3 and Corollary 5.4. In particular the
necessity direction classifies every failing specified source basis
for every allowed target, rather than merely producing an interval
counterexample. The supplied one-deleted-base construction and
punctured-interval family are intermediate examples, not new results
of this volume. These distinctions make no assertion of worldwide
originality, priority, or exhaustive literature coverage.

**开放问题 8.1（A precise torsion boundary）。** For a fixed prime $p$,
classify equal-size failures, and the one-element-gap case, for
loopless sparse paving targets in $\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$ with
$|A|\le |B|<p$ and $0\notin B$. The torsion-free proof of Lemma 2.2
cannot be transferred merely by treating cyclic indices as integers.
The needed replacement is a sumset theorem with its exact equality
and wraparound alternatives for $X+(U_X\cup\{0\})\subseteq A$.
Counterexample 7.4 does not decide this prime-order problem.

**开放问题 8.2（Paving targets with larger hyperplanes）。** For
arbitrary loopless paving $N$ without dual paving, give a structural
classification of failures when $|A|\le |B|$ over torsion-free
abelian groups. Proposition 3.3 and Lemma 2.3 remain exact necessary
tools, but a deficient $C_S$ can lie in a hyperplane of size greater
than $r$; Counterexample 7.3 exhibits this phenomenon even at a
one-element gap. The missing inference is a classification of
possible blocked-translation sets jointly with those larger target
hyperplanes, rather than the minimal sumset equality used in
Theorem 5.3. No complete classification in that wider class is asserted.
