## 5. Source-specific transfer of mature compression mathematics

**Convention 5.1 (BRW hypotheses and their precise consumer).** Bluhm,

**Convention 5.2 (KI hypotheses and redundant factors).** Koashi and Imoto,

## 6. Resource domain and the observer boundary

**Definition 6.1 (distinct resource questions).** The resource minimized in
Theorem 4.2 is $\max_\alpha d_\alpha$ for one exact live coding cut. Its
finite four-state certificate establishes the minimum for the entire
continuous family (QC.19). It is different from all of the following:

* The Hilbert dimension of a single register embedding live and stopped
  sectors, which is four in (QC.5).
* The total retained classical–quantum state, including archive, references,
  controller, clock, source identity, permissions and all event payloads.
* The number of bits in a finite-precision archive, or the cardinality of an
  exact time or waveform register. An exact real failure time is not a finite
  classical alphabet.
* Acquisition cost, preparation and calibration cost, coherent gate accuracy,
  waveform description, physical hardware or elapsed laboratory time.
* Coding through an arbitrary infinite classical algebra, a nonnormal
  classical interface, pre-supplied correlated assistance, or an uncounted
  entangled receiver resource. The finite-$n$ lower bound makes no claim
  that any of these alternatives succeeds or fails.
* An ensemble's asymptotic iid compression rate with an allowed vanishing
  error, or an approximate finite-horizon implementation. KI §VIII.E
  (PDF p. 22) concerns independent ensemble signals and asymptotically
  negligible error; it is not the exact one-specimen task (QC.21).

The integer $n$ in (QC.20) can be as large as desired while finite. It is
not assumed independent of an encoder outcome: the label may acquire
input-dependent information. The no-imprinting argument constrains what it
can acquire while permitting the exact common recovery, and the rank
argument still counts its quantum block correctly. A correct parity bit
supplied before encoding is a different input: conditional on parity, the
fixed-height family lies in the two-dimensional span of $e_2$ and $v_+$ or
$v_-$. The supplier's fixed-parity operations already exploit such a promise.
Thus the unassisted hypothesis cannot be silently dropped.

The upper correspondence in Theorem 3.1 allows an actual correlated archive
and keeps it unchanged; the lower bound in Theorem 4.2 denies the code
access to such assistance. These are explicitly different contracts. Three
live quantum dimensions do not imply that the entire actual observer is
three-dimensional, nor that its classical archive is bounded. A finite
quantum dimension supports a continuum of exact states without identifying
those states from one specimen.

**Definition 6.2 (same-input comparison and remaining acquisition gap).**
Theorem 3.1 compares two representations of the same unknown input and the
same actual joint records, controller and supplied continuation. It does not
compare different unlabelled unknown programmes while giving their
parameters to a decoder. Theorem 4.2 likewise requires one common code for
all unknown states; the construction of source witnesses does not hand
their $\beta,m$ to that code. Neither theorem is an unknown-source
identification algorithm.

The complete labelled instrument is therefore a smaller conditional
representation of the specimen's lawful continuation. It is not an acquired
internal observer. In particular, it does not construct the joint record and
calibration services required by
[Transport–Memory Completion](RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_TRANSPORT_MEMORY_COMPLETION.md),
§§2.9–2.12 and 2.18: the same coherent source, actual common port, aligned
times and records, known nonzero amplitudes, known nonzero record overlap

Nor does it supply the actual compatible archive, domains, routes,
permission fields and closed update/selection service required by
[Process Geometry](RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_PROCESS_GEOMETRY.md),
§34, and
[Atlas Stable Core Holonomy](RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_ATLAS_STABLE_CORE_HOLONOMY.md),
§§1–3.

Keeping those fields unchanged in an exact isometry proves preservation
when they are actually supplied; it does not establish their acquisition,
availability or finite cost. If a guard or selector depends on an omitted
source field, the complete observer interface has not been closed.
[Original-Control Record Acquisition](RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_ORIGINAL_CONTROL_RECORD_ACQUISITION.md),
