## 2. Common laws and one attained positive multiplier

**Definition 2.1 (directed fractional cylinder).** Fix finite
$t=e^A-1\ge t_0$. Write $u=\cos\phi$, $v=\sin\phi$,
$h=(1,u/\sqrt\alpha,v/\sqrt\beta)$ and

$$
\begin{gathered}
X=\{(u,v,\xi):u^2+v^2=1,\ 0\le\xi\le1\},\quad
b_x=t\mathfrak c(h)\xi,\\
\Gamma(x)=(1,u,v,u^2,uv,\xi\mathfrak c,
                  \xi\mathfrak c u^2,\xi\mathfrak c uv)^T,\\
B=B(t)=(1,0,0,\alpha,0,\mu/t,\alpha V/t,0)^T,\quad
k(x)=|u|,\quad g_t(x)=|\ell(h)+b_x f(h)|.
\end{gathered}
\tag{FC.3}
$$

Angles are directed modulo $2\pi$; $\phi$ and $\phi+\pi$ are
distinct. One original supported-nonzero low label is a pair
$(h,\xi)$ with positive coefficient weight. Those weights sum to
one but are not acquisition probabilities. A common finite law
$\pi=\sum p_i\delta_{x_i}$ has $p_i\ge0$ and

$K(\pi)=\sum p_i k(x_i)$ and $J_t(\pi)=\sum p_i g_t(x_i)$.
Zero-weight terms are omitted when labels are counted. Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_t(r)&=\max\{J_t(\pi):\textstyle\sum p_i\Gamma(x_i)=B, K(\pi)\ge r\},\\
C_t(\lambda)&=\max\{J_t(\pi)+\lambda K(\pi):
                                  \textstyle\sum p_i\Gamma(x_i)=B\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{FC.4}
$$

These are GC.57 and GC.74 in the same normalization. For reference,
$q=\nu/t$ and
$r_{\max}(t)=\sqrt\alpha\,[q\sqrt{u_F}+
\sqrt{(1-q)(1-qu_F)}]$. The weak request domain is
$0\le r\le r_{\max}(t)$ and $s=s_0r/\sqrt\alpha$.

## 3. Complete contacts and exact fractional groups

**Definition 3.1 (endpoint inventory and signed description).** Put
$\eta_j(\phi)=\ell(h)+j t\mathfrak c(h)f(h)$ for $j=0,1$ and
$d_j(\phi)=d(\phi,j)$. Define the **complete** sheet inventory

$$
Z=\{(\phi,j):\phi\in\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z),\ j=0,1,
                                                    \ d_j(\phi)=0\}.
\tag{FC.6}
$$

Coincident signed-branch descriptions of one sheet point are
deduplicated; the two sheets themselves are distinct. To state the
reused finite inventory explicitly, let

$$
\begin{gathered}
P_y=y_0+y_1u+y_2v+y_3u^2+y_4uv,\quad
Q_y=y_5+y_6u^2+y_7uv,\\
D_c=h_0^2\delta+(b^2/\alpha)u^2+(d^2/\beta)v^2>0,\\
N_c=h_1^2h_2^2\delta+(a^2h_2^2/\alpha)u^2
                     +(c_{\rm src}^2h_1^2/\beta)v^2>0,\\
F_{0,\epsilon,\sigma}=P_y-\epsilon\lambda u-\sigma\ell,\\
F_{1,\epsilon,\sigma}=D_c(P_y-\epsilon\lambda u-\sigma\ell)
                               +N_c(Q_y-\sigma t f),\quad
\epsilon,\sigma\in\{\pm1\}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{FC.7}
$$

Thus $\mathfrak c=N_c/D_c$ and all eight functions in (FC.7) are
nonnegative on the full directed circle. The inventory is their union
of circle zeros, on the indicated sheets. This is the RC Theorem
69.1 interface after changing its multiplier conjugate to $|z|$ to
the present multiplier conjugate to $|u|$:
$\lambda=\lambda_{\rm RC}/\sqrt\alpha$. Its failure quadratic
is represented here by $Q_y=t\widehat n_h^TZ\widehat n_h$,
modulo the circle identity, when using RC's symmetric-matrix dual.

The reused bound is $|Z|\le24$. Its hypotheses can be checked directly
at (FC.5): the four pure tests have degree at most two and the four
saturated tests degree at most four. If one were identically zero,
flipping $\epsilon$ would leave $2\epsilon\lambda u$, multiplied
by $D_c$ on the saturated sheet; this changes sign. Thus none is
identically zero. A nonnegative nonzero degree-$d$ trigonometric
polynomial has at most $d$ distinct circle zeros because each has
even multiplicity in its degree-$2d$ homogeneous half-angle form.
Summing $4\cdot2+4\cdot4$ gives 24, before deduplication. This
recalls a supplier proof, not a new contact or physical-label bound.

For half-angle coordinates $u=(V_h^2-U_h^2)/(U_h^2+V_h^2)$,
$v=2U_hV_h/(U_h^2+V_h^2)$, clear powers two and four of
$U_h^2+V_h^2$. The homogeneous degrees are four and eight.
The missing affine-chart point $V_h=0$, namely $(-1,0)$, is
included. Positive denominators add no roots; repeated roots consume
multiplicity and do not enlarge the distinct-root bound. All weak
height signs and zero-height contacts are retained. RC Proposition
70.2 excludes $u=0$ for $\lambda>0$, but no axis is discarded
by an assumed genericity rule. No nonidentity assertion at
$\lambda=0$ is needed here.

**Definition 3.2 (compatible contact groups).** At a fixed directed
angle whose two endpoints both belong to $Z$, put them into one
two-coordinate group exactly when
$\eta_0(\phi)\eta_1(\phi)\ge0$. Otherwise each present endpoint
is its own singleton group. Denote the resulting partition by
$\mathscr G=\{G_1,\ldots,G_g\}$; $g\le |Z|\le24$.
Each paired group represents the whole segment $0\le\xi\le1$
at that angle, and a singleton represents just its endpoint. Both
coordinates of a paired group remain available independently; the
group is not replaced by one fixed column.

**Theorem 3.3 (complete fractional-contact accounting).** Under
Assumptions 1.1 and 2.2, the zero set of $d$ on $X$ is precisely
the union of the singleton and segment sets in Definition 3.2.
Every law supported on it can be replaced by at most one original
fractional label per occupied group, preserving the complete moment
vector, $K$ and $J_t$. Conversely splitting each fractional contact
uses endpoint coordinates of its one group, preserves those same
data and occupies no additional group. Consequently the minimum
fractional-label count at any fixed such joint data equals the
minimum occupied-group count over all its endpoint representations.

Proof. At a fixed angle, $\Gamma$ is affine in $\xi$, $k$ is
constant, and $\eta_\xi=(1-\xi)\eta_0+\xi\eta_1$. Hence

$$
d(\phi,\xi)=(1-\xi)d_0+\xi d_1+
 (1-\xi)|\eta_0|+\xi|\eta_1|-|\eta_\xi|.
\tag{FC.8}
$$

Every term on the right is nonnegative. If $0<\xi<1$, its
vanishing requires both endpoint gaps to vanish and equality in the
real triangle inequality. The latter is precisely
$\eta_0\eta_1\ge0$, including one or both heights zero. These
conditions also suffice. At an endpoint only its own gap is required
to vanish. Thus no fractional contact outside the listed groups is
lost, even at height zeros or repeated signed descriptions.

For endpoint masses $a_0,a_1\ge0$ in one paired group put
$p=a_0+a_1$ and, if $p>0$, $\xi=a_1/p$. The replacement
has $p\Gamma(\phi,\xi)=a_0\Gamma(\phi,0)+
a_1\Gamma(\phi,1)$ and the same $p|u|$. GC Theorem 15.1 gives
the exact low-height defect

$$
D_{\rm low}=a_0|\eta_0|+a_1|\eta_1|
                    -|a_0\eta_0+a_1\eta_1|.
\tag{FC.9}
$$

It vanishes in a compatible group. For strictly opposite heights and
two positive masses it is $2\min(a_0|\eta_0|,a_1|\eta_1|)$;
the actual TV defect is HALF of (FC.9). A zero mass gives zero
defect, regardless of the other endpoint's height; if $p=0$ the
label is omitted. Opposite-height endpoints therefore stay in two
singleton groups when both are occupied.

Within a paired segment $g_t$ is affine. Any number of fractional
atoms there merge by summing their masses and taking the
mass-weighted average of $\xi$. The same argument applies to a
probability measure on that segment: its mass and first $\xi$
moment determine all these data. There are only finitely many
groups. Conversely an atom of mass $p$ at $\xi$ splits into
$p(1-\xi),p\xi$ at the two endpoints of its group, with zero
pieces omitted. An original contact law with $L$ positive labels
therefore gives an endpoint representation using at most $L$
groups; an endpoint representation using $L$ groups gives an
original law using exactly $L$ labels by merging. Taking the two
minima proves the count equality. This does not equate label count
with angular-node count or with a matrix-mass rank. $\square$

## 4. All optimal laws on the complete multiplier face

**Definition 4.1 (complete endpoint polytope).** Enumerate the entire
inventory $Z$ as $x_1,\ldots,x_N$, where $N\le24$. Set

$$
\begin{gathered}
A=[\Gamma(x_1)\ \cdots\ \Gamma(x_N)],\quad
\kappa=(k(x_1),\ldots,k(x_N))^T,\\
\mathcal P=\{w\in\mathbb R^N:w\ge0,\ Aw=B\},\quad
\gamma(w)=\#\{G\in\mathscr G:\textstyle\sum_{i\in G}w_i>0\}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{FC.10}
$$

There is no full-row-rank assumption. An endpoint contact not used by
a particular optimizer remains a column of $A$. An unused coordinate
may be forced to zero on the entire polytope without being removed
from the complete inventory.

**Theorem 4.2 (all-optima activity and complete-face parametrization).**
Under Assumptions 1.1 and 2.2, $\mathcal P$ is nonempty and compact.
Its points are exactly the endpoint multiplier-optimal laws, and
every fractional multiplier-optimal law splits into it. Moreover

$$
\kappa^T\mathcal P=[r_-,r_+],\qquad
R_t(r)=C_t(\lambda)-\lambda r\quad(r\in[r_-,r_+]).
\tag{FC.11}
$$

For each such request, **every** optimal common law has $K=r$ and
zero integrated $d$; conversely every common law supported on the
complete contact set with moments $B$ and $K=r$ is optimal. Thus
the complete moment, contrast and height data of every original
optimal law at that request, including all alternative atom tables,
are represented by $\mathcal P\cap\{\kappa^Tw=r\}$ and the height-preserving
group merges. In particular

$$
n_t(r)=\min\{\gamma(w):w\in\mathcal P,\ \kappa^Tw=r\}.
\tag{FC.12}
$$

Proof. For any common law the integrated dual gap is
$C_t(\lambda)-J_t-\lambda K\ge0$. A multiplier maximizer exists
by GC Proposition 12.2 and has gap integral zero. Nonnegative gap
therefore vanishes at each positive atom. For the stipulated measure
extension it vanishes almost everywhere; the continuous
zero-integral support principle gives the same contact restriction.
Theorem 3.3 splits and collects the law into finitely many endpoint
coordinates, proving $\mathcal P\ne\varnothing$. Its first row
is $\sum w_i=1$, so $\mathcal P$ is closed and bounded. Each
$w\in\mathcal P$ integrates only contacts and hence has
$J_t+\lambda K=y\cdot B=C_t(\lambda)$. Conversely every
multiplier optimum splits into $\mathcal P$. Its contrast image
is consequently a compact interval, with both extrema attained;
this proves the first equality in (FC.11), including $r_-=r_+$.

For $r$ in that interval choose $w$ with $\kappa^Tw=r$.
It gives height $C_t(\lambda)-\lambda r$. Every competitor for
the original inequality request has $K\ge r$ and obeys

$$
J_t\le C_t(\lambda)-\lambda K
                         \le C_t(\lambda)-\lambda r.
\tag{FC.13}
$$

Equality of its height with the attained value, together with
$\lambda>0$, forces both $K=r$ and zero integrated gap. Theorem
3.3 therefore applies to **each** optimum, not just the first law
chosen. The converse follows by integrating $d=0$. These arguments
use the original inequality $K\ge r$; they do not impose activity
in advance. For $\lambda=0$ that inference would fail.

Necessity in Assumption 1.1 sends every original optimal instrument
to the same complete moment equations and actual height, with
$K\ge r$. Its physical binary contrast is at most
$s_0K/\sqrt\alpha$ and at least the requested $s$; the just-proved
activity makes both equal to $s$. Each positive supported low
label belongs to one listed group. Collecting these labels produces
a point of the slice with no more groups than the original number
of labels. Conversely merge a point of that slice group by group.
GC Proposition 12.2 and Proposition 10.1 realize the resulting
finite law by an original optimal instrument with exactly one low
label per occupied group. This proves (FC.12). The countable or
stipulated measurable classes cannot lower this finite minimum:
their contact laws collect into the same finite group data, while
the finite constructor attains those data. This is a correspondence
of laws and minima, not a bijection of full instruments. $\square$

## 5. Coordinate faces and one-dimensional contrast images

**Definition 5.1 (group faces and skeleton intervals).** For a selection
$Q\subseteq\mathscr G$ write $I_Q=\bigcup_{G\in Q}G$ and

$$
\mathcal P_Q=\{w\in\mathcal P:w_i=0\ (i\notin I_Q)\}.
\tag{FC.14}
$$

It is allowed to be empty. If nonempty, let
$I(Q)=[\min_{\mathcal P_Q}\kappa^Tw,
\max_{\mathcal P_Q}\kappa^Tw]$. An empty face contributes the
empty set. For a nonempty face $F$ of $\mathcal P$, define its
support as the positive-coordinate support of any relative-interior
point, and let $\gamma(F)$ count the groups meeting that support.
Equivalently that support is the union of all positive supports on
$F$. Let $\mathcal V$ and $\mathcal E$ be its vertices and
one-dimensional faces, respectively. For $v\in\mathcal V$ put
$I(v)=\{\kappa^Tv\}$. If an edge $e=[v_0,v_1]$ has endpoints
$v_0,v_1$, put
$I(e)=[\min(\kappa^Tv_0,\kappa^Tv_1),
\max(\kappa^Tv_0,\kappa^Tv_1)]$. Constant-$K$ edges contribute
singletons. For an integer $m\ge0$ define

$$
U_m=\bigcup_{\substack{v\in\mathcal V\\\gamma(v)\le m}}I(v)
\ \cup\!
\bigcup_{\substack{e\in\mathcal E\\\gamma(e)\le m}}I(e).
\tag{FC.15}
$$

A singleton polytope has one vertex and no edges. The support of an
edge means its relative-interior support, not a potentially smaller
support at one endpoint.

**Theorem 5.2 (complete coordinate-face and edge-image criterion).**
Under Assumptions 1.1 and 2.2, for every integer $m\ge0$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\{r\in[r_-,r_+]:n_t(r)\le m\}
&=\bigcup_{\substack{Q\subseteq\mathscr G\\|Q|\le m}}I(Q)
=U_m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{FC.16}
$$

This equality includes empty coordinate faces, singleton polytopes,
redundant moment equations or columns, zero weights, paired groups,
constant-$K$ edges, and a constant-$K$ whole face. In particular,
for $m=7,8$ it decides **all** alternative short original optimal
alphabets on this face from its complete finite data.

Proof. For a slice point $w$, $\gamma(w)\le m$ precisely when it
belongs to some $\mathcal P_Q$ with $|Q|\le m$. By (FC.12),
taking its contrast image proves the first equality. Because all
coordinates of $\mathcal P$ are nonnegative, imposing zeros
outside $I_Q$ is the equality set of the nonnegative functional
$\sum_{i\notin I_Q}w_i$. A nonempty $\mathcal P_Q$ is therefore
a face. Its contrast image is a compact interval by convexity.

The two extrema of a linear functional on a nonempty polytope are
achieved at vertices. If the extrema coincide, any one vertex
already projects onto the entire singleton image. Otherwise use
the connected edge graph of that face to join a minimizing vertex
to a maximizing vertex. Along the successive edges, the projected
path is continuous, so contains every value between these extrema.
Every such vertex or edge belongs to $\mathcal P$, since a face
of a face is a face, and uses only coordinates in $I_Q$. Thus
$I(Q)\subseteq U_m$ when $|Q|\le m$. The edge-graph assertion is
the ordinary finite-polytope theorem, valid in the affine hull: a
generic linear objective has a unique maximizing vertex; from any
other vertex its feasible tangent cone, generated by incident edge
directions, has an increasing edge. Following increasing edges in
the finite graph reaches that vertex. No simple-vertex or
independent-column assumption is involved.

In the reverse direction let $F$ be a vertex or edge with
$\gamma(F)\le m$. Take $Q$ to be its occupied groups. Any
coordinate positive at some point of $F$ is positive at a
relative-interior point: otherwise the coordinate's nonnegative
affine functional would vanish in a relative neighborhood and
hence on $F$. Consequently the whole of $F$ lies in
$\mathcal P_Q$. Its image is contained in $I(Q)$, proving the
reverse inclusion. Empty faces give nothing; the mass-one row
makes $\mathcal P_\varnothing$ empty, so $U_0=\varnothing$.
A singleton face needs no edge, and a constant image needs no
division by a contrast difference. These facts cover the stated
degeneracies directly. $\square$

## 6. Exact eight/nine classification and finite interpolation

**Theorem 6.1 (classification and a realizing edge).** Under the same
assumptions, $1\le n_t(r)\le9$ throughout $[r_-,r_+]$, and

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_t(r)=8&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r\in U_8\setminus U_7,\\
n_t(r)=9&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                              r\in[r_-,r_+]\setminus U_8.
\end{aligned}
\tag{FC.17}
$$

In particular $U_8=[r_-,r_+]$ is equivalent to eight-low compression
on this supplied face; $U_7=[r_-,r_+]$ is the corresponding
seven-low statement. Both $r_-$ and $r_+$ belong to $U_8$.
For a nonconstant edge with endpoint contrasts $K_-<K_+$, every
$r\in[K_-,K_+]$ has the exact endpoint representation

$$
w(r)=\frac{K_+-r}{K_+-K_-}w^-+
                       \frac{r-K_-}{K_+-K_-}w^+.
\tag{FC.18}
$$

Merging occupied groups gives original optimal fractional laws with
at most $\gamma(e)$ labels. If $r\notin U_8$, there is such an
edge strictly bracketing $r$ with exactly nine positive endpoint
coordinates in its relative interior, all in different groups.
Writing their index set as $I$, it satisfies

$$
\operatorname{rank}A_I=8,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}\begin{pmatrix}A_I\\\kappa_I^T\end{pmatrix}=9.
\tag{FC.19}
$$

Proof. The slice $\mathcal P\cap\{\kappa^Tw=r\}$ is a
nonempty compact polytope. At a vertex of this slice, its positive
columns of $\widehat A=[A;\kappa^T]$ are independent. Otherwise
a nonzero kernel vector on that positive support gives two small
opposite feasible perturbations, contradicting extremality. There
are thus at most $\operatorname{rank}\widehat A\le9$ positive
endpoints, and at most nine groups. Merging proves the nine-low
upper bound, consistent with the credited FA bound. Mass one
excludes zero labels. Theorem 5.2 and integer counts now prove
(FC.17). Minimizing or maximizing $K$ on $\mathcal P$ gives a
vertex; the same perturbation argument using $A$ alone bounds its
