## 1. Original task and the credited joint carrier

**Definition 1.2 (source and late-target task).** Let

$$
\mathcal T::=\alpha\mid\beta\mid\langle\mathcal T,\mathcal T\rangle,
\qquad
\operatorname{ht}(\alpha)=\operatorname{ht}(\beta)=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{ht}(\langle u,v\rangle)
=1+\max\{\operatorname{ht}(u),\operatorname{ht}(v)\}.
$$

Pairing is free and ordered. A literal Read at any finite original-root
word in $\{L,R\}^*$ returns its constructor, or absent after a leaf,
in $\mathcal A=\{\alpha,\beta,\mathsf{branch},\mathsf{absent}\}$.
The empty word is legal. Set

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho(\alpha)&=\beta,&
\rho(\beta)&=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,&
\rho(\langle u,v\rangle)&=\langle\rho(u),\rho(v)\rangle,\\
\mathcal F_D&=\{\langle\alpha,v\rangle:\operatorname{ht}(v)\le D\},&
W_D&=\{q\in\{L,R\}^*:|q|\le D-1\},&
\mathcal P_D&=\{Rq:q\in W_D\},
\qquad D\ge1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{WBF.1}
$$

One actual initial $t\in\mathcal F_D$ stays stationary throughout
preparation and late acquisition. Preparation is one deterministic
effective procedure, initially source-blind, depending on public $D$
but not on the later target. It terminates on every source and obtains
source dependence only through charged literal Reads. It exports a
finite complete name $E(t)$. All accessible surviving source distinctions,
including control, selectors, buffers, lengths, allocation and accessible
timing, belong to that name. Separately paid handoff and isolation
services make every other preparation distinction and transcript
inaccessible. These services are assumptions about the interface.

Only after export, a public source-independent $p\in\mathcal P_D$
arrives. The deterministic reader uses $E(t),D,p$ and installed public
tables, and makes zero Reads or one Read of the same stationary source
at any finite original-root word chosen before its reply and before
every supplied count. Its final predictor must return
$r_{\rho^kt}(p)$ for every subsequently supplied authentic $k\ge0$,
without further Reads, simultaneously for every $t$ and $p$.
There is no subtree, equality, source-identity, hidden-age, navigation,
reset, copying or free-advice port. The minimum complete attained alphabet
is $M_1(D)=\min |E[\mathcal F_D]|$. The minimization does not discard
variable shapes or restrict competitors to independent child codes or
on-target queries. Other resources are specified in Section 8.

## 2. The exact product-reference whole-star Finner class

**Definition 2.1 (all allowed data and global compensation).** A
certificate on Definition 1.4 consists of a strictly positive binary
probability law $\nu_a$ for each edge, exponents $\theta_v\ge0$,
strictly positive full four-bit tables
$f_v:\mathbb F_2^{\operatorname{st}(v)}\to(0,\infty)$, and
a scalar $c>0$. All these data may be real. Require

$$
\theta_u+\theta_v\le1\quad(uv\in E),
\qquad
W(s)\le c\left(\prod_{a\in E}\nu_a(s_a)\right)
             \left(\prod_{v\in V}f_v(s_{\operatorname{st}(v)})\right)
\quad(s\in\mathbb F_2^E).
\tag{WBF.7}
$$

The second condition is one global pointwise inequality on the same
actual assignment. It permits unrestricted compensation between
different whole-star tables and the scalar. No separate per-vertex
factor identity is required. Allowing zero entries in finite
nonnegative tables or in reference probabilities would add no feasible
certificate: every edge value and every star pattern extends to an
assignment with $W(s)>0$, whereas a zero entry would make its majorant
zero. Thus strict positivity loses no finite feasible datum of that
kind. Let
$\nu_{\operatorname{st}(v)}(z)=\prod_{a\ni v}\nu_a(z_a)$ and
define the reported upper bound

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_v&=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\left(\sum_z\nu_{\operatorname{st}(v)}(z)
                    f_v(z)^{1/\theta_v}\right)^{\theta_v},
 &\theta_v>0,\\
\displaystyle\max_z f_v(z),&\theta_v=0,
\end{cases}\\
F&=c\prod_{v\in V}N_v.
\end{aligned}
\tag{WBF.8}
$$

The product reference $\nu=\bigotimes_a\nu_a$ is an analytical
probability space. It is not a claim that the actual conditioned
source or the native Gibbs law has independent edge replies.

This class retains every original whole-star scope and permits cyclic
overlap. It has no scope splitting or enlargement, auxiliary variables,
recut, syndrome correction, multiple-rule combination or additional
scaffold/stability bypass. An extension with any of those operations
is a different class. The optimum below quantifies over every datum
allowed in (WBF.7)–(WBF.8), including all product laws, zero exponents,
scalars and global positive majorants.

**Lemma 2.2 (finite product-space Finner inequality).** On a finite
product probability space with strictly positive coordinate laws, let
nonnegative functions $g_i$ have coordinate scopes $S_i$. For
$0<\eta_i\le1$ assume
$\sum_{i:a\in S_i}\eta_i\le1$ at every coordinate $a$. Then

$$
\mathbb E\prod_i g_i\le
\prod_i\big(\mathbb E g_i^{1/\eta_i}\big)^{\eta_i}.
\tag{WBF.9}
$$

Additional functions with exponent zero may be included by replacing
their factors on the right by their global maximum.

Proof. First bound each zero-exponent function pointwise by its
maximum and remove it. For the positive exponents, induct on the number
of coordinates. Constants and empty scopes cause no difficulty. Fix a
coordinate $a$ and hold all others fixed. Hölder with exponents
$1/\eta_i$ for the functions depending on $a$, and a constant-one
factor of reciprocal exponent
$1-\sum_{i:a\in S_i}\eta_i$ if this is positive, gives

$$
\mathbb E_a\prod_i g_i\le
\left(\prod_{i:a\notin S_i}g_i\right)
\prod_{i:a\in S_i}
\left(\mathbb E_a g_i^{1/\eta_i}\right)^{\eta_i}.
\tag{WBF.10}
$$

Replace each involved function by
$\widetilde g_i=(\mathbb E_a g_i^{1/\eta_i})^{\eta_i}$,
whose scope is contained in $S_i\setminus\{a\}$. The same
incidence inequalities hold on all remaining coordinates. Apply the
induction hypothesis with the same $\eta_i$. For every involved
function, $\widetilde g_i^{1/\eta_i}=\mathbb E_a g_i^{1/\eta_i}$,
so the resulting norm is exactly its original full-product norm by
finite summation. For uninvolved functions it is unchanged. This proves
(WBF.9); the no-coordinate case is equality for constants. The
zero-exponent argument also applies to identically zero functions.
$\square$

**Proposition 2.3 (soundness with zero exponents).** Every certificate
of Definition 2.1 satisfies $Z_G\le F$.

Proof. Sum the pointwise inequality (WBF.7) over all common assignments
to obtain $Z_G\le c\mathbb E_\nu\prod_v f_v$. Every edge is in
exactly its two endpoint scopes, so the incidence hypothesis of
Lemma 2.2 is precisely $\theta_u+\theta_v\le1$. Each positive
$\theta_v$ is at most one because its star is nonempty. The lemma,
including its maximum convention, gives (WBF.8). Positivity of every
$\nu_a$ means the maximum is over all sixteen star patterns, with
no hidden null-support exception. $\square$

## 3. A common actual trial law with the required star marginals

**Lemma 3.1 (full even-star projection).** Let

$$
H:\mathbb F_2^E\longrightarrow\mathbb F_2^V,
\qquad (Hs)_v=\sum_{a\ni v}s_a,
\qquad K=\ker H.
\tag{WBF.11}
$$

For every vertex,

$$
K|_{\operatorname{st}(v)}
=\{z\in\mathbb F_2^4:\textstyle\sum_a z_a=0\}.
\tag{WBF.12}
$$

Consequently the uniform law on $K$ has the uniform distribution
on the eight even patterns at that star, and a fair marginal at every
edge.

Proof. Membership in $K$ implies even parity at every star. Conversely,
choose an even subset of the four edges incident to $v$. Pair their
other endpoints. For each pair choose a path in the connected graph
$G-v$ and take the mod-two sum of all chosen paths and selected
incident edges. At $v$ its degree is even. At each other vertex the
path-boundary contribution cancels the contribution from the selected
incident edges. Intersections and cancellations of paths preserve this
mod-two statement, and no chosen path changes an edge incident to $v$.
The result belongs to $K$ with exactly the prescribed star pattern.
The restriction map is linear and onto the displayed subspace. All
fibers of a linear map on a finite vector space have equal size, proving
the uniform image statement. Each individual bit is fair on the eight
even patterns, giving the last assertion. This is the projection in
[GDB, Lemma 21.1][GDB]; the explicit proof identifies the graph
hypothesis actually used here. $\square$

**Lemma 3.2 (one joint trial law).** For $A>B>0$, set

$$
T=8(A^2+B^2),\qquad
r=\frac{A^2-B^2}{A^2+B^2},\qquad
P=r\,\operatorname{Unif}(K)
 +(1-r)\,\operatorname{Unif}(\mathbb F_2^E).
\tag{WBF.13}
$$

This is one probability law, positive on every actual solution in
Convention 1.5, whose marginal on every star is

$$
Q_v(z)=\frac{\Phi_v(z)^2}{T}.
\tag{WBF.14}
$$

Every edge marginal under $P$ is fair. With the declared star orders
the $Q_v$ have the same entropy and the same expectation of
$\log_2\Phi_v$.

Proof. Since $0<r<1$, (WBF.13) is positive on the entire edge cube.
The uniform cube has probability $1/16$ on each star pattern, while
Lemma 3.1 gives probability $1/8$ on every even pattern and zero on
every odd pattern for the kernel law. The mixture therefore gives

$$
Q_v(z)=\begin{cases}
(1+r)/16=A^2/T,&z\text{ even},\\
(1-r)/16=B^2/T,&z\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\tag{WBF.15}
$$

These sixteen probabilities sum to one. Both mixture components have
fair edge marginals. The actual bijection of Convention 1.5 pushes
this single law to native tuples without a choice of separate local
realizations. Parity and the two weights are invariant under the slot
orders, proving the entropy and expectation assertions. $\square$

**Convention 3.3 (role of the trial law).** The law $P$ supplies a
simultaneous variational witness for every star and every shared edge.
It is not postulated to be the normalized native weight $W/Z_G$,
and it need not be that law. Neither $P$ nor its kernel component is
an acquired observer input, installed source advice or additional
retained information. Replacing it by separate, independently chosen
star laws would not justify the expectation of a global majorant.

## 4. Variational lower bound and the sharp attained optimum

**Lemma 4.1 (local norm inequality, including exponent zero).** For a
certificate of Definition 2.1 let

$$
\begin{aligned}
h&=-\sum_z Q_v(z)\log_2 Q_v(z),&
a&=\sum_z Q_v(z)\log_2\Phi_v(z),\\
\ell_e&=\tfrac12\big(\log_2\nu_e(0)+\log_2\nu_e(1)\big).
\end{aligned}
\tag{WBF.16}
$$

The first two quantities do not depend on $v$. For every vertex,

$$
\log_2 N_v\ge
\mathbb E_P\log_2 f_v(s_{\operatorname{st}(v)})
 +\theta_v h+\theta_v\sum_{e\ni v}\ell_e.
\tag{WBF.17}
$$

Also $h\le4$, $\ell_e\le-1$ and $h=\log_2 T-2a$.

Proof. If $\theta_v>0$, write
$M_v=\sum_z\nu_{\operatorname{st}(v)}(z)f_v(z)^{1/\theta_v}$
and define the strictly positive probability
$R_v(z)=\nu_{\operatorname{st}(v)}(z)f_v(z)^{1/\theta_v}/M_v$.
Expanding its relative entropy against $Q_v$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
0\le D_2(Q_v\Vert R_v)
&=-h-\mathbb E_{Q_v}\log_2\nu_{\operatorname{st}(v)}
   -\theta_v^{-1}\mathbb E_{Q_v}\log_2 f_v+\log_2 M_v.
\end{aligned}
\tag{WBF.18}
$$

Relative entropy is nonnegative, for example by applying concavity
of the logarithm to
$\mathbb E_{Q_v}(R_v/Q_v)=1$; every entry here is positive.
The fair edge marginals of Lemma 3.2 yield
$\mathbb E_{Q_v}\log_2\nu_{\operatorname{st}(v)}
=\sum_{e\ni v}\ell_e$. Multiply (WBF.18) by $\theta_v$
and use $\log_2N_v=\theta_v\log_2M_v$ to get (WBF.17).
If $\theta_v=0$, the asserted inequality is just
$\log_2\max_z f_v(z)\ge\mathbb E_{Q_v}\log_2 f_v(z)$.
No limit of a changing exponent or reference law is needed.

The entropy of sixteen states is at most four, by relative entropy
against the uniform law. Since $\nu_e(0)+\nu_e(1)=1$,
$\nu_e(0)\nu_e(1)\le1/4$, giving $\ell_e\le-1$.
Finally (WBF.14) implies
$\log_2Q_v=2\log_2\Phi_v-\log_2T$; taking expectations
proves the last identity. $\square$

**Theorem 4.2 (sharp optimum over the complete defined class).** On
every graph of Definition 1.4 and for every $A>B>0$, the minimum
of the sound upper bound (WBF.8), over all data in Definition 2.1,
is

$$
\min_{\nu,\boldsymbol\theta,c,\boldsymbol f} F
=\big[8(A^2+B^2)\big]^{n/2}=T^{n/2}.
\tag{WBF.19}
$$

The minimum is attained. This includes arbitrary real positive
product bases, zero exponents, scalars, and globally compensating
full-star tables obeying only (WBF.7).

Proof. Fix any feasible certificate and use the one common trial law
$P$ from Lemma 3.2. Taking its expectation of the logarithm of the
global pointwise majorant gives

$$
\log_2c+\sum_v\mathbb E_P\log_2 f_v
 +\sum_{e\in E}\ell_e\ge na.
\tag{WBF.20}
$$

All logarithms are finite. Add (WBF.17) over the vertices and
substitute (WBF.20). For $\Theta=\sum_v\theta_v$ this gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_2F
&\ge na+h\Theta+
  \sum_{uv\in E}(\theta_u+\theta_v-1)\ell_{uv}\\
&\ge na+h\Theta+\sum_{uv\in E}(1-\theta_u-\theta_v)\\
&=na+2n+(h-4)\Theta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{WBF.21}
$$

In the second line each coefficient $\theta_u+\theta_v-1$
is nonpositive and $\ell_{uv}\le-1$, so the inequality has the
displayed direction. The last line uses degree four and $|E|=2n$.
Summing the edge constraints yields $4\Theta\le2n$.
Since $h-4\le0$, it follows that

$$
\log_2F\ge na+2n+(h-4)\frac n2
=n(a+h/2)=\frac n2\log_2T.
\tag{WBF.22}
$$

This lower bound used no local factor identity, no symmetry assumption
on the certificate data, and no independence of the trial law.

For the converse, take

$$
\nu_e(0)=\nu_e(1)=\tfrac12,\qquad
\theta_v=\tfrac12,\qquad c=1,\qquad f_v=4\Phi_v.
\tag{WBF.23}
$$

Every edge constraint is equality. Since there are $2n$ edges,
$\prod_e\nu_e=2^{-2n}$ and $\prod_v4=2^{2n}$; hence
the global majorant is pointwise equality. At a star,

$$
\sum_z 2^{-4}(4\Phi_v(z))^2
=8A^2+8B^2=T.
\tag{WBF.24}
$$

Its norm factor is $T^{1/2}$, so $F=T^{n/2}$. This proves the
opposite inequality and attainment, rather than merely an infimum
approached by singular data. If $A,B$ are rational, all attaining
certificate entries, exponents and the scalar are rational; the
evaluated upper bound can be algebraic. $\square$

**Corollary 4.3 (boundary of the stated hypotheses).** The same
formula holds for $A=B>0$, attained by (WBF.23), with $P$ the
uniform cube law. No assertion here includes $B=0$ or zero reference
probabilities in Definition 2.1.

Proof. At $A=B$, take $r=0$ and repeat the proof; $h=4$ and
$a=\log_2A$, so (WBF.22) remains valid. The original class requires
strictly positive tables and reference probabilities. A hard-zero
extension would have different admissible data and logarithmic
conventions, which are not used in this theorem. $\square$

## 5. Exact extensive excess on a full-support native law

**Definition 5.1 (credited full-alphabet law).** Use [GDB, Definition
12.4][GDB]: on all $123$ members of $S_3$, assign raw weight
$63$ to each of the eight even perfect height-two trees and weight
$1$ to every other state. The total is $8\cdot63+115=619$.
Normalize to the strictly positive law $\mu$ on the entire alphabet.
On the complete native graph solutions this gives
$A=63/619$ and $B=1/619$. The restriction to perfect trees is a
constraint of that native template; no source shape has been deleted
from the task or from the law.

**Theorem 5.2 (exact residual after every allowed optimization).** At
Definition 5.1's law, every graph of Definition 1.4 satisfies

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min F&=\left(\frac{4\sqrt{1985}}{619}\right)^n,\\
Z_G&=\left(\frac{128}{619}\right)^n
       \left[1+\left(\frac{31}{32}\right)^n\right],\\
\log_2\min F-\log_2Z_G
&=n\kappa-\log_2\left[1+\left(\frac{31}{32}\right)^n\right]
>n\kappa-1,\\
\kappa&=\tfrac12\log_2 1985-5>\tfrac13.
\end{aligned}
\tag{WBF.25}
$$

Proof. Substitute $A,B$ into Theorem 4.2:
$T=31760/383161$ and $\sqrt T=4\sqrt{1985}/619$.
The expression for $Z_G$ is precisely the credited partition
(WBF.6). Dividing these positive values and taking logarithms gives
the exact difference. Since $0<(31/32)^n<1$, its final logarithm
is less than one. Finally

$$
1985^3=7821346625>4294967296=2^{32}
\tag{WBF.26}
$$

implies $\tfrac12\log_2 1985-5>1/3$ by taking logarithms
and dividing by six. Thus the strict positive residual does not
depend on a decimal approximation. $\square$

**Proposition 5.3 (unbounded actual counterfamily and exact excluded
premise).** For every integer $n\ge5$, let $G_n$ have vertices
modulo $n$ and edges $\{i,i+1\}$ and $\{i,i+2\}$ for all $i$.
It satisfies Definition 1.4. Consequently there is no function
$\eta(n)\to0$ such that every actual template in this family has
a certificate of Definition 2.1 with

$$
\log_2F-\log_2Z_{G_n}\le n\eta(n).
\tag{WBF.27}
$$

This remains false if only arbitrarily large dyadic lengths are
required.

Proof. For $n\ge5$, the neighbors $i\pm1,i\pm2$ are four
distinct vertices and the displayed unordered edges have no unwanted
duplication. The graph is simple and 4-regular. Its step-one cycle
proves connectedness; deleting any vertex from that cycle leaves a
path through all remaining vertices. Orient $i\to i+1,i+2$ to
obtain two incoming and two outgoing edges as required by the native
carrier. Apply Theorem 5.2 to every $G_n$. Its optimized per-block
excess is greater than $\kappa-1/n$, so cannot be bounded above
by a function tending to zero. The same proof holds along $n=2^j$.
$\square$

The excluded premise is a mandatory uniformly vanishing per-template
loss for this one norm class. The allowed data already optimize every
product reference, exponent and global positive whole-star majorant;
altering only those choices cannot repair (WBF.27). This statement
does not quantify over extensions of the class or over pressure-optimal
laws.

## 6. Separation from the existing whole-scope forest method

**Convention 6.1 (credited forest class).** [GDB, Definition 20.3][GDB]
uses finite rational mixtures of proper selected vertex subsets $F_j$,
with weights $\lambda_j>0$ summing to one. Its positive tables
$\psi_{jv}$ on the original stars satisfy the global identity

$$
\prod_j\left(\prod_{v\in F_j}\psi_{jv}(s_{\operatorname{st}(v)})
        \right)^{\lambda_j}=W(s)
\quad\text{for every common }s.
\tag{WBF.28}
$$

It reports $U=\sum_j\lambda_j\log_2 Z_j$, where
$Z_j=\sum_{s\in\mathbb F_2^E}\prod_{v\in F_j}\psi_{jv}$
retains every original variable, including free variables. In its
forest subclass each $G[F_j]$ is a forest, allowing exact tree
sum-product evaluation of $Z_j$. The full partition sums and selected
submodels in this class are different objects from the single
product-reference norms of Definition 2.1. Both permit global
compensation; neither comparison may replace that condition by
separate per-vertex identities.

**Proposition 6.2 (strict separation on a common actual graph).** On
$K_5$ and for every $A>B>0$, the optimum of the credited forest
class in Convention 6.1 is strictly larger than the logarithm of
Theorem 4.2's optimum. More precisely, with

$$
f(t)=t\log_2\frac{A^{1/t}+B^{1/t}}2\quad(t>0),
\tag{WBF.29}
$$

these optima are respectively

$$
U_{\rm forest}=10+5f(2/5),\qquad
\log_2\min F=10+5f(1/2)<U_{\rm forest}.
\tag{WBF.30}
$$

Proof. [GDB, Theorem 21.2][GDB] already evaluates the optimum
over all globally compensating tables at fixed mixture data as
$2n+\sum_v f(\rho_v)$, where
$\rho_v=\sum_{j:v\in F_j}\lambda_j$. Feasibility for $A>B$
requires $\rho_v>0$. On $K_5$ an induced forest has at most
