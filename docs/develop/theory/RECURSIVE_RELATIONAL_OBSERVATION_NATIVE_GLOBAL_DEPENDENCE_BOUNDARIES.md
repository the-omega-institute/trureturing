# Recursive relational observation: native global dependence boundaries

**Convention 1.1 (scope and mathematical status).** This is ordinary
mathematical reference input, without Lean kernel certification. It uses
the `generic-v1` theory convention. Published text is immutable; additions
and corrections belong after the final append anchor. The native
deductions below concern exact compatibility contraction, specifically
defined analytic premises, and approximation of an individual template's
pressure from finite neighborhoods. Their common carrier retains actual
owner replies and each original joint block weight once.

## 1. Original source, action, observation and resources

**Definition 1.2 (unchanged native task).** Let

$$
\mathcal T::=\alpha\mid\beta\mid\langle\mathcal T,\mathcal T\rangle,
\qquad \operatorname{ht}(\alpha)=\operatorname{ht}(\beta)=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{ht}(\langle u,v\rangle)=1+\max(\operatorname{ht}(u),\operatorname{ht}(v)).
$$

Pairing is free and ordered. Every finite original-root word over $L,R$,
including the empty word, is legal. Its reply $r_t(a)$ is the addressed
constructor, or absent after traversal passes a leaf, in

$$
\mathcal A=\{\alpha,\beta,\mathsf{branch},\mathsf{absent}\}.
$$

Use the homomorphic substitution and original families

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho\alpha&=\beta,&\rho\beta&=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,&
\rho\langle u,v\rangle&=\langle\rho u,\rho v\rangle,\\
\mathcal F_D&=\{\langle\alpha,v\rangle:\operatorname{ht}(v)\le D\},&
W_D&=\{q:|q|\le D-1\},&\mathcal P_D&=\{Rq:q\in W_D\},
\end{aligned}
\tag{GDB.1}
$$

for public $D\ge1$. One actual initial source stays stationary during
preparation and late acquisition; $\rho^kt$ specifies prediction targets.
There is no subtree, equality, source-identity, age, reset, copying,
free-navigation or advice port.

**Assumption 1.3 (complete paid export and late timing).** Preparation
is one deterministic effective procedure, source-blind initially, depending
on $D$ and public installed tables but receiving no later target. It
terminates on every $t\in\mathcal F_D$ and acquires every source
distinction by charged literal Reads. Its finite export $E(t)$ contains
every accessible surviving source distinction: selectors, control,
operands, buffers, lengths, allocation, handles, counters and accessible
timing. Every other accessible field is a function of this complete name
and public data. Separately paid handoff and access-isolation services
remove access to all other preparation dependence, including transcripts,
and provide lawful common entry.

Only after export, a public source-independent $p\in\mathcal P_D$
arrives. A deterministic reader uses $E(t),D,p$ and public tables to
choose zero Reads or one fresh Read of that same stationary source, at
any finite original-root word. The choice precedes its reply and every
supplied count. Its final predictor $Z$ must obey

$$
\operatorname{Dec}(Z,D,p,k)=r_{\rho^kt}(p)
\quad(t\in\mathcal F_D,\ p\in\mathcal P_D)
\tag{GDB.2}
$$

for every subsequently supplied authentic $k\ge0$, with no further
Reads. Define $M_1(D)$ as the minimum complete attained cardinality
$|E[\mathcal F_D]|$. Preparation Read cost must be bounded for an
attaining construction. Preparation work/control and temporary storage,
installed tables, post-request workspace, final predictor size, submitted
address lengths and letters, count representation, handoff, isolation,
entry, authentication and physical time are separate resources. The
services are premises, not deductions about physical operations. These
are the original [FMR, §1][FMR] and [NPC, §1][NPC] conditions.

**Convention 1.4 (credited temporal factor and canonical completion).**
Use [AAR, §§12–13][AAR]. Put $e(\alpha)=0,e(\beta)=1$ and
$s(r)=\#L(r)+2\#R(r)$. At an original branch endpoint set
$\Gamma_q(u)=(0,0)$. Otherwise let the first original leaf along
$q$ have label $\ell$ at $a$, with $q=ar$. Then

$$
\Gamma_q(u)=
\begin{cases}
(2-e(\ell),1-e(\ell)),&r=\varepsilon,\\
(s(r)+2-e(\ell),\mathbf1_{r\text{ ends in }R}),&r\ne\varepsilon.
\end{cases}
\tag{GDB.3}
$$

On attained factors the credited decoder is

$$
\operatorname{Pred}((b,c),k)=
\begin{cases}
\mathsf{branch},&k\ge b,\\
\beta,&b\ge1,\ k=b-1,\\
\alpha,&c=1,\ k=b-2,\\
\mathsf{absent},&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{GDB.4}
$$

Here $c=1$ implies $b\ge2$. This gives $r_{\rho^ku}(q)$ for
every $k\ge0$, including initially absent targets. The complete
trajectory recovers $b$ as its first branch count and $c$ by its alpha
stage. Write $h(z)=\operatorname{Pred}(z,0)$.

Put $C_0=\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle$ and

$$
S_1=\{\alpha,\beta,C_0\},\qquad
S_{d+1}=\{\alpha,\beta\}\sqcup\{\langle x,y\rangle:x,y\in S_d\}.
\tag{GDB.5}
$$

The completion $\pi_d(v)\in S_d$ for $\operatorname{ht}(v)\le d$
fixes $S_d$ and preserves all replies and factors on $W_d$. It keeps
constructors through depth $d-1$ and completes a branch there by $C_0$.
On $S_d$, a length-$d$ reply is alpha if its parent is branch and
absent otherwise; longer words are absent. Thus an arbitrary finite
canonical probe is a function of one boundary reply in the same donor
block or a constant. These are [FMR, Lemma 2.2 and Theorems 2.3–2.5][FMR].

## 2. The exact owned carrier and its consumers

**Definition 2.1 (complete templates and once-owned seed networks).**
Fix $d,n\ge1$, let $V=[n]\times W_d$, and put $G_q=\Gamma_q[S_d]$.
A complete native template chooses at every $(i,q)$ a constant factor
in $G_q$ or an equation

$$
\Gamma_q(x_i)=\delta_{i,q}(r_{x_j}(a)),\qquad
(j,a)\in V,\quad\delta_{i,q}:\mathcal A\longrightarrow G_q.
\tag{GDB.6}
$$

All tables are total and all equations hold on the same actual tuple.
Let $C_\Theta\subseteq S_d^n$ be the full solution set, possibly empty.
Admissible subsets have such witnesses for all targets, without having
to equal their full solution set. [NDR, Theorem 2.2][NDR] supplies
same-coordinate saturation preserving cover optima and nonnegative mass
maxima; arbitrary off-target donors, self donors and cycles remain allowed.

Use exactly [RRN, Definition 5.1 and Theorem 5.2][RRN]. A seed $s_c$
has domain $T_c\subseteq\mathcal A$ and a distinct designated actual
owner port $o_c\in V$. Every port $v=(i,q)$ prescribes a constant
$z_v\in G_q$ or a table $z_v=B_v(s_c)$ with $B_v:T_c\to G_q$.
The owner is assigned to its own seed and obeys

$$
h(B_{o_c}(a))=a\qquad(a\in T_c).
\tag{GDB.7}
$$

Repeated occurrences use one shared variable. For
$s\in\mathcal S=\prod_cT_c$, a block is realizable if its entire
prescribed factor vector equals that of an actual $x_i\in S_d$.
Such $x_i$ is unique. For arbitrary full weights $w_i:S_d\to[0,\infty)$,
set

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_i(s)&=\sum_{u\in S_d}w_i(u)
       \prod_{q\in W_d}\mathbf1_{\{\Gamma_q(u)=z_{i,q}(s)\}},\\
Z(\mathcal N;\boldsymbol w)&=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\prod_{i=1}^n\Phi_i(s).
\end{aligned}
\tag{GDB.8}
$$

Each $\Phi_i$ is zero or the weight of its unique reconstructed block.
The original $w_i$ is used exactly once and need not factor within that
block. Actual owner replies recover every seed on a realizable assignment.
RRN gives complete templates in both directions with exactly the same
actual tuples, one lift per tuple and this same mass. Seeds are analytical
variables, not free acquired or exported readings.

Empty domains give no assignments and zero mass. With no seeds there is
one empty assignment, reconstructing either a constant tuple or none.
Singleton domains are allowed; they may be replaced by constants when
desired. None of the representation assertions requires positive weights.

**Convention 2.2 (exact temporal compatibility and prescribed incidence).**
Reuse [OCB, Theorem 2.2][OCB]. For
$Q_0=\{(0,0),(2,1),(1,0)\}$ and $\ell_L=1,\ell_R=2$, put

$$
U((b,c))=\begin{cases}\star,&(b,c)=(0,0),\\b,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
\quad
D_e((b,c))=\begin{cases}\star,&(b,c)\in Q_0,\\b-\ell_e,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{GDB.9}
$$

The symbol $\star$ is distinct from every integer. A seed assignment is
realizable exactly when

$$
U(z_{i,q}(s))=D_e(z_{i,qe}(s))
\quad(q,qe\in W_d,\ e\in\{L,R\},\ i\in[n]).
\tag{GDB.10}
$$

Ranges, actual owner guards and all these tests remain part of the model.
Between distinct seeds a test is an equality of two unary functions;
within a seed it is a unary condition. Constants remain constants.

Define $I_c$ to be the set of original blocks with at least one port
assigned to $c$, and $\nu(c)=|I_c|$. This counts prescribed incidence,
including an assigned table that happens to be constant, and counts
multiple occurrences within a block once. Let $\mathfrak N_{d,L}(n)$
be the class with $\nu(c)\le L$ for every seed, as in
[SIR, Definition 2.2][SIR]. Blocks, seeds and topology are not bounded
in number by fixing $d,L$.

**Convention 2.3 (unchanged mass, cover and rate objectives).** Write

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_n^{(d)}(w)&=\max_\Theta\sum_{x\in C_\Theta}\prod_iw(x_i),&
\beta_d(w)&=\sup_{n\ge1} A_n^{(d)}(w)^{1/n},\\
\sigma_d&=\inf_{n\ge1}\frac{\log_2K_d(n)}n,&
2^{-\sigma_d}&=\min_{\mu\in\Delta(S_d)}\beta_d(\mu),\\

0&\le\lambda-2^{-d}\sigma_d\le2^{-d}\phi.
\end{aligned}
\tag{GDB.11}
$$

Here $K_d(n)$ is the unrestricted admissible cover number,
$U_0=1,U_{j+1}=1+U_j^2$, and $\phi=\lim_j2^{-j}\log_2U_j\le1$.
These are [FMR, §6][FMR] and [NPC, §§5–7][NPC] suppliers. All logarithms
are base two except where $\ln$ is written. A genuine all-template cap

$$
M_1(D)\ge\left\lceil C^{-2^{D-d}}\right\rceil\quad(D\ge d),
\qquad\lambda\ge-2^{-d}\log_2C.
\tag{GDB.12}
$$

The analytical product law in these sums is not a reader input and does

## 3. Further contraction of temporal compatibility

**Definition 3.1 (selected total-bijective tests).** Suppose all current
seed domains are nonempty. Select any collection of tests (GDB.10)
between distinct seeds such that the entire allowed relation on their
current domains is the graph of a total bijection
$\pi_{cb}:T_c\to T_b$. Neither a partial bijection on an unstated
support nor a relation that becomes bijective only on unknown globally
feasible assignments qualifies. Retain parallel tests separately.

Form the undirected graph of selected tests. In each connected component
$K$, choose a representative existing seed $r$ and a spanning tree.
Compose the tree bijections, using inverses against edge orientations,
to obtain $p_c:T_r\to T_c$ with $p_r=\mathrm{id}$. Define

$$
D_K=\{a\in T_r:
 p_b(a)=\pi_{cb}(p_c(a))\text{ for every selected edge }c\to b
                                     \text{ in }K\}.
\tag{GDB.13}
$$

Tree edges satisfy this identically; the remaining conditions retain
every cycle consistency requirement. Introduce one phase $a_K\in D_K$
owned at the old actual port $o_r$. At every port formerly assigned to
$c\in K$, substitute $B_v(p_c(a_K))$. Keep all original blocks,
constants and weights, and impose actual compatibility on the resulting
full vectors. Former nonrepresentative owner ports become ordinary ports.

**Theorem 3.2 (exact actual-tuple phase contraction).** For every
$d,n\ge1$, every lawful finite owned-seed network and every choice
in Definition 3.1, this replacement is a lawful owned-seed network
on the same original $n$ blocks. The realizable seed assignments before
and after replacement are in bijection, with

$$
s_c=p_c(a_K)\quad(c\in K),
\qquad x_{\rm old}(s)=x_{\rm new}(a).
\tag{GDB.14}
$$

In particular it preserves the identical actual original tuples and

$$
Z(\mathcal N;w_1,\ldots,w_n)
 =Z(\mathcal N^{\rm ph};w_1,\ldots,w_n)
\tag{GDB.15}
$$

for arbitrary nonnegative whole-block weights, including zero weights.
The corresponding finite-sum identity also holds for an arbitrary
correlated tuple weight $W(x)$, and for complex weights. A phase is
the actual reply at its retained owner, not an additional observation.

The finite construction can be repeated whenever a transformed test
between distinct current phases becomes a total bijection on its current
domains. Unless an empty domain is found, after at most $\max(J-1,0)$
nontrivial merging passes, where $J$ is the original seed count, it
terminates with no such individual test. This terminal property does not
assert discovery of all functional dependence in the joint solution set.

Proof. In an original realizable assignment, (GDB.10) implies every
selected edge. Propagation on a component's tree gives uniquely
$s_c=p_c(s_r)$. The other selected edges are precisely the remaining
conditions in (GDB.13), so $a_K=s_r$ belongs to $D_K$.

Conversely, lift a phase assignment by (GDB.14). Its tree edges hold by
construction and its other selected edges hold by (GDB.13). At every
original port its prescribed factor is exactly the old prescribed factor
on this lifted assignment. Consequently every unselected test, unary
condition and constant condition holds on one side exactly when it holds
on the other. The actual reconstruction criterion (GDB.10) gives the
same $x_i$ in both directions. This preserves compatibility zeros;
there is no projection that might combine different lifts.

At the retained owner, $p_r=\mathrm{id}$ and (GDB.7) give
$h(B_{o_r}(p_r(a)))=a$. Owners stay distinct and every substituted
factor remains in its original $G_q$. Thus RRN's exact converse supplies
a complete original native template with exactly this solution set.
The phase is recovered uniquely as that owner's actual reply, proving
the bijection without multiplicity. Summation on identical tuples proves
all the weight assertions, using one $w_i$ per original block.

If $D_K$ is empty, the selected edges themselves are inconsistent.
The original solution set is empty, and the replacement empty owner
domain represents that same empty set by RRN's guard construction.
An initially empty domain is treated in this way directly. With no
seeds, or with no selected edges, the unchanged network already suffices;
incompatible constants need not be detected by contraction. Singleton
domains and singleton phases obey the same proof.

Every relation test examines at most sixteen input pairs, and every
transport and cycle test has at most four representative values.
Each nontrivial pass merges at least two existing variables. Domains and
tables remain finite, so the stated bound on merging passes proves
termination. This is a finite structural transformation, not a uniform
network-size cutoff or a procedure evaluating all-size pressure.
$\square$

**Corollary 3.3 (all-competitor preservation and the incidence boundary).**
After the credited RRN reply-star normalization, restricting unrestricted
native witnesses to the terminal witnesses of Theorem 3.2 preserves
the collection of all complete solution sets at fixed $d,n$. It therefore
preserves integral and fractional covers, every nonnegative mass maximum
and $\beta_d$, with original normalization $n$.

For a contracted component $K$, if assigned occurrences are retained in
the incidence accounting, its phase incidence is exactly

$$
\nu_{\rm new}(K)=\left|\bigcup_{c\in K} I_c\right|
 \le\min\left(n,\sum_{c\in K}\nu_{\rm old}(c)\right)
 \le\min(n,L|K|)
\tag{GDB.16}
$$

when the original incidence is at most $L$. The number of distinct
variables occurring in any original block cannot increase. The same
bounded-incidence class need not be preserved: §4 attains new incidence
$n$ from old incidence two. For a restricted input class, exactness is
therefore an equality with its quotient image retaining the original
owners and provenance, not an equality with $\mathfrak N_{d,L}(n)$.

Proof. Every original template has an exact RRN witness, and Theorem
3.2 produces a terminal witness with the identical solution set.
Conversely every resulting witness is a lawful native witness by that
theorem and RRN's converse. These two containments give equality of the
collections and both directions of every mass and cover comparison.
Each occurrence of a seed in $K$ becomes an occurrence of its phase in
the same original block and no new occurrence is added. This gives the
union identity, its bounds and the per-block assertion. Even a now
constant assigned table is retained in this stated accounting; removing
it can only reduce incidence. The example below proves sharp growth.
$\square$

The selected edges belong to the graph of actual temporal compatibility
left by RRN. They are not the directed reply-dependence skeleton that
RRN already eliminates. Spanning-tree propagation and finite bijection
consistency are mature ingredients; the additional statements here are
the native same-tuple, owner, weight, query and incidence correspondences.

## 4. Incidence-two alternating phases at every positive leaf law

**Theorem 4.1 (actual alternating cycles).** For every $d\ge2$ and
$n\ge2$, index seeds and blocks cyclically. Let
$T_i=\{\alpha,\beta\}$, with owner $(i,\varepsilon)$, and let
$\tau$ exchange alpha and beta. Prescribe

$$
\begin{aligned}
z_{i,\varepsilon}&=\Gamma_\varepsilon(s_i),&
z_{i,L}&=\Gamma_L(\tau(s_{i+1})),\\
z_{i,q}&=\Gamma_q(s_i)&&
 (q\in W_d\setminus\{\varepsilon,L\}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{GDB.17}
$$

These are lawful complete native data with seed incidence exactly two.
If $n=2m$ is even, their actual solution set consists exactly of the
two alternating leaf tuples. If $n$ is odd, it is empty. The selected
root-left compatibility tests are total complement bijections. Their
contraction has a single phase with two values for even $n$, empty
domain for odd $n$, and prescribed incidence $n$ in the even case.

For every nonnegative $\mu:S_d\to[0,\infty)$ with
$a=\mu(\alpha)>0$ and $b=\mu(\beta)>0$, the homogeneous even-cycle
mass and its normalized actual solution law are

$$
Z_n(\mu)=2(ab)^m,\qquad Q_n=\tfrac12(\delta_{x^+}+\delta_{x^-}).
\tag{GDB.18}
$$

Proof. The owner root table maps alpha to $(2,1)$ and beta to $(1,0)$.
At its unused branch and absent inputs choose $(2,1)$; its reply is
alpha, different from either excluded input. This is an explicit total
guard. All other unused decoder entries can be any attained factor of
their target. Thus every prescribed table belongs to the original native
interface, and all targets, including absent suffixes, have equations.

The root factor forces $x_i$ to be the actual leaf $s_i$. The factors
$\Gamma_L(\alpha)=(3,0)$ and $\Gamma_L(\beta)=(2,0)$ are distinct,
so the left equation holds exactly when $s_i=\tau(s_{i+1})$.
All other factors then agree with that same leaf. Complementing around
the cycle is consistent exactly for even length, and then its initial
leaf determines one of two assignments. Each seed occurs in its own
block and its predecessor, proving incidence two. Formula (GDB.9)
makes the root-left test precisely that complement relation. Its cycle
composition is the identity or complement according to parity, proving
the contraction and the incidence assertion.

Both even solutions contain $m$ copies of each leaf, so both have
weight $(ab)^m$, irrespective of the ratio $a/b$. This proves the
mass and the same-law balance. If either leaf weight vanishes, the
even solution set is still the same but its mass is zero; the normalized
law and subsequent positive-mass influence claims are then inapplicable.
$\square$

**Theorem 4.2 (specified local-weight zeros, susceptibility and influence).**
Under Theorem 4.1's positive leaf-weight hypotheses, for even $n=2m\ge4$
perturb only the alpha weight in odd-numbered original blocks by a
multiplier $z$, leaving every other entry of every whole-block weight
fixed. The exact complex partition function is

$$
Z_n(z)=(ab)^m(1+z^m).
\tag{GDB.19}
$$

It vanishes at $z_m=\exp(i\pi/m)$, with
$|z_m-1|=2\sin(\pi/(2m))\le\pi/m=2\pi/n$.
Consequently, for every $\delta>0$, a nonempty actual incidence-two
network has a zero with all independent relative perturbations

$$
w_i(u)=\mu(u)(1+\xi_{i,u}),\qquad |\xi_{i,u}|<\delta.
\tag{GDB.20}
$$

The lengths can be chosen dyadic. If $\mu$ is a probability and each
complex block vector is required to sum to one, the same conclusion
holds after normalization, with arbitrarily small relative perturbations
on every positive coordinate.

For real $t$ the natural-log pressure

$$
f_n(t)=\frac1n\ln\frac{Z_n(e^t)}{(ab)^m}
       =\frac{\ln(1+e^{mt})}{2m}
\tag{GDB.21}
$$

satisfies $f_n''(0)=n/16$ and tends pointwise to $\max(0,t)/2$.
For the actual seed indicators $X_i=\mathbf1_{s_i=\alpha}$, the
zero-diagonal signed influence matrix

$$
\Psi_{ij}=\Pr(X_j=1\mid X_i=1)-\Pr(X_j=1\mid X_i=0)
\quad(i\ne j)
\tag{GDB.22}
$$

has largest eigenvalue $n-1$. Opposite pinnings of one actual seed give
Hamming Wasserstein distance exactly $n-1$ on the common remaining
free set. At seeds at arbitrarily large cycle distance, their conditional
total-variation difference is one.

Proof. Exactly one alternating tuple has alpha at all $m$ odd sites;
the other has beta there. Inspecting their weights gives (GDB.19) and
its zeros. As $m$ grows, their distances to one tend to zero, also
along powers of two. Only the entries specified in (GDB.20) are changed.

For a probability law, divide every perturbed odd block by
$1+a(z-1)$. The partition function is divided by
$[1+a(z-1)]^m$. For $|z-1|\le1/2$ these denominators are nonzero,
and each positive entry changes relatively by at most $2|z-1|$.
The zeros are therefore preserved under arbitrarily small normalized
perturbations. Zero coordinates, if present, remain zero.

Differentiating (GDB.21) proves the curvature, and dividing the elementary
log-sum-exp bounds by $2m$ proves its pointwise limit. Choose parity
signs $\epsilon_i\in\{1,-1\}$. The balanced two-point law gives
$\Psi_{ij}=\epsilon_i\epsilon_j$ off the diagonal, hence
$\Psi=\epsilon\epsilon^{\mathsf T}-I$, with eigenvalues $n-1$
and $-1$ on its orthogonal complement. Pinning any one seed selects
one actual tuple deterministically. The opposite pinnings select tuples
differing at every other seed, so their only coupling has Hamming cost
$n-1$. The last assertion follows from the same deterministic difference.
This is the mature two-phase influence calculation of
[Anari–Liu–Oveis Gharan, Definitions 1.1–1.2 and introduction][ALO];
Theorem 4.1 supplies its actual native realization for every positive
leaf-weight pair. $\square$

## 5. Precisely rejected direct analytic and mixing premises

**Definition 5.1 (the raw uniform premise classes).** For fixed
$d\ge2,L\ge2$, a probability $\mu$ and $\delta>0$, let
$\mathsf U_\delta(\mu)$ assert that every nonempty network in
$\mathfrak N_{d,L}(n)$, for every $n\ge1$, has nonzero partition
function under all perturbations (GDB.20). Empty solution sets are
excluded from this premise.

Define a direct zero-free cap method to have the following mandatory
condition for every accepted nontrivial label $(d,L,\mu,C)$: $\mu$
is a strictly positive rational probability and a sound finite witness
establishes $\mathsf U_\delta(\mu)$ for some rational $\delta>0$.
Its only other branch may accept $C\ge1$. Define direct spectral and
coupling variants by replacing this mandatory premise with a finite
size-independent bound, respectively, on (GDB.22)'s largest eigenvalue
or on the free-set Hamming Wasserstein distance under opposite one-seed
pinnings, for every positive-mass native network in the same class.
These definitions constrain the propositions witnessed, without imposing
a particular internal proof language for their sound witnesses.

**Theorem 5.2 (all-positive-law failure of those mandatory premises).**
For every $d\ge2$, every $L\ge2$, and every probability $\mu$
with $\mu(\alpha),\mu(\beta)>0$, no $\delta>0$ satisfies
$\mathsf U_\delta(\mu)$, and neither raw influence nor raw coupling
has a finite size-independent bound. Hence the methods in Definition
5.1 accept no nontrivial label at any strictly positive law. With only
the stated trivial branch, all their block lower endpoints
$-\log_2C$ are at most zero. At $d=2$ they are not optimizer-cofinal,
since the credited $\sigma_2=\log_2(25/17)>0$.

Proof. Every such law satisfies Theorem 4.1's hypotheses. Choose its
even cycle sufficiently large in Theorem 4.2. It is nonempty, has
incidence two and contradicts any proposed radius or finite bound.
This applies simultaneously to every positive rational optimizer
candidate. Soundness forbids a witness for the false mandatory premise.
The remaining branch and [NCM, Theorem 4.1][NCM] give the endpoint
conclusion. The same depth-two pressure is attained by local products,
so the argument also cannot be repaired by replacing the unrestricted
depth-two pressure with its incidence-$L$ version. $\square$

**Convention 5.3 (exact exclusions and a concrete bypass).** This
theorem concerns the raw hard models with full-support block laws;
positive block laws do not remove compatibility zeros. It is not a
mixing claim after adding a fixed positive floor to every $\Phi_i$.
It does not reject every zero-free path, complex domain, polynomial
approximation, direct mass inequality, or handling of exceptional
low-mass templates. Exact phase contraction handles these particular
cycles, although Corollary 3.3 shows its incidence cost.

The relation $\{\alpha,\beta\}\subseteq S_d$ is itself locally
admissible, so $\beta_d(\mu)\ge a+b$. The alternating family is
not asserted to attain pressure. Its use is to disprove a mandatory
universal premise. In particular [RCE, §§14–15 at the separate pin][RCEnew]
supplies, for arbitrary nonnegative full weights on $S_2$,

$$
\beta_2(w)=J_2(w)=\max\{W-a,W-b,a+b+t\},
\quad W=\sum_{u\in S_2}w(u),\quad
t=\max_{g\in S_2\setminus\{\alpha,\beta\}}w(g).
\tag{GDB.23}
$$

Its original recursive-plus-range grammar accepts exactly the rational
caps at least this value, and has optimized cap $17/25$. At its
full-support attaining law $(8,8,1,\ldots,1)/25$, the raw premises
of Theorem 5.2 still fail. Thus that theorem does not refute the existing
grammar, its support or stability rules, or its optimizer-cofinality at
other depths. RCE's universal two-reply locality theorem concerns the
entire declared one-block support and one own-coordinate experiment;
it does not supply two calls or a global pressure cutoff.

## 6. A nonempty ternary obstruction for the stated all-one interpolation

**Definition 6.1 (actual twisted ternary cycles).** Work at $d=2$.
Let $T=\{\alpha,\beta,\mathsf{branch}\}$ and define
$u(\alpha)=\alpha,u(\beta)=\beta,u(\mathsf{branch})=C_0$.
On a cycle of $n\ge2$ seeds, owned at their respective block roots,
let $\theta_i$ be the identity except at one edge, where it exchanges
alpha and beta and fixes branch. Prescribe

$$
z_{i,\varepsilon}=\Gamma_\varepsilon(u(s_i)),\quad
z_{i,L}=\Gamma_L(u(\theta_i(s_{i+1}))),\quad
z_{i,R}=\Gamma_R(u(s_i)).
\tag{GDB.24}
$$

Use the fixed full-support probability on the entire eleven-state $S_2$

$$
\mu(\alpha)=\mu(\beta)=a=\frac2{13},\qquad
\mu(g)=b=\frac1{13}\quad(g\text{ any of the nine pair states}).
\tag{GDB.25}
$$

Every seed has prescribed block incidence two. The owner table has the
required three fixed replies; map its unused absent input to $(2,1)$.
All nonowner unused entries are completed by attained factors. These
are total complete native tables, not arbitrary graph constraints.

**Theorem 6.2 (exact polynomial and exponentially approaching zeros).**
Definition 6.1's hard solution set is exactly $\{C_0^n\}$ and has
mass $b^n>0$. Let $\Phi_i$ be its actual local potentials from
(GDB.8). For real or complex $v$, define

$$
f_n(v)=\sum_{s\in T^n}\prod_i(\Phi_i(s)+v).
\tag{GDB.26}
$$

With $D=\operatorname{diag}(a,a,b)$, the three-by-three all-one
matrix $J$, and the permutation matrix $P$ exchanging the first two
states, exactly

$$
f_n(v)=\operatorname{tr}((D+vJ)^nP)
      =\operatorname{tr}(Q(v)^n)-a^n,
\qquad Q(v)=\begin{pmatrix}a+2v&v\\2v&b+v\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{GDB.27}
$$

For every $n\ge4$, put $t_n=2a(b/a)^n=4/(13\,2^n)$.
There is a real zero $v_n\in(-t_n,0)$. Consequently the precise
straight-line all-one interpolation

$$
P_n(z)=\sum_{s\in T^n}\prod_i[(1-z)+z\Phi_i(s)]
      =z^nf_n(1/z-1)
\tag{GDB.28}
$$

has a real zero in $(1,(1-t_n)^{-1})$, although $P_n(1)=b^n>0$.
No common disk $|z|<r$ with $r>1$ is zero-free for these nonempty
actual incidence-two inputs.

Proof. For an arbitrary pair of neighboring seed values, inspect the
eleven actual states of $S_2$. A leaf root requires the same leaf
left factor, since the leaf left values $(3,0),(2,0)$ differ from each
other and from every initially present child factor in $Q_0$.
A branch root requires a left factor in $Q_0$; among the three
prescribed left values only $\Gamma_L(C_0)=(2,1)$ qualifies.
The right factor is already that of its own $u(s_i)$. Thus local
reconstruction holds exactly when $s_i=\theta_i(s_{i+1})$, and then
the actual block is $u(s_i)$. The single twist forces the cyclic
seed to be branch. This proves the hard solution and its mass.

The untwisted local matrix is $D+vJ$ and the twisted one is
$(D+vJ)P$, since $JP=J$. The trace of their cyclic product is
the sum in (GDB.26). Both $D$ and $J$ commute with $P$. Its
antisymmetric line contributes $-a^n$; on vectors $(x,x,y)$ the
matrix $D+vJ$ acts by $Q(v)$. This proves both equalities in
(GDB.27), with the same shared seeds and one potential per original
block. No independent replica or uncharged owner is introduced.

For $t=t_n$ and $n\ge4$, one has
$t\le\min((a-b)/2,b/4)$. The matrix $Q(-t)$ is similar to

$$
A_t=\begin{pmatrix}a-2t&-\sqrt2\,t\\-\sqrt2\,t&b-t\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its eigenvalues $\ell_-\le\ell_+$ are positive because
$\ell_-\ge b-(1+\sqrt2)t>0$. Also

$$
(a-t)I-A_t=
\begin{pmatrix}t&\sqrt2\,t\\\sqrt2\,t&a-b\end{pmatrix}
\succeq0,
$$

as its determinant is $t(a-b)-2t^2\ge0$ and its diagonal entries
are nonnegative. Hence $\ell_+\le a-t$, while the Rayleigh
quotient of the second basis vector gives $\ell_-\le b-t\le b$.
It follows that

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_n(-t)&\le(a-t)^n+b^n-a^n\\
       &\le-t a^{n-1}+b^n=-b^n<0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{GDB.29}
$$

Here $(a-t)^n\le a^n-ta^{n-1}$ follows from
$(1-t/a)^n\le1-t/a$. Since $f_n(0)=b^n>0$, the intermediate
value theorem gives $v_n$. Factoring $z$ from each term proves
(GDB.28) for $z\ne0$, hence as a polynomial identity with its
continuous value at zero. Substituting $z=(1+v_n)^{-1}$ gives
the stated interval. Its upper endpoint tends to one. $\square$

**Corollary 6.3 (positive floors with their exact quantifiers).** For
$\eta\ge0$, replace the analytical factors by $\Phi_i+\eta$ and

P_{n,\eta}(z)=z^nf_n(1/z-1+\eta).
\tag{GDB.30}
$$

Whenever $\eta+t_n<1$, it has a real zero in

\left((1-\eta)^{-1},(1-\eta-t_n)^{-1}\right).

$$

For every fixed $r>1$ and $\eta_0>0$, some $0<\eta<\eta_0$

This does not exclude a radius depending on a fixed positive $\eta$,
a different path, or a direct all-size bound.

Proof. Substitute $v_n$ from Theorem 6.2 in
$z=(1-\eta+v_n)^{-1}$ to obtain (GDB.31). First choose a positive

## 7. An actual graph lift with counted incidence three

**Definition 7.1 (connector and edge blocks).** Let $G$ be a finite
simple graph with $v$ vertices, $e\ge1$ edges and no isolated vertices.
Fix a public orientation of each edge and a public cyclic ordering of
the edges incident to each vertex. For every incidence $(u,a)$ introduce
a distinct binary seed $s_{u,a}\in\{\alpha,\beta\}$ and an original
connector block $P_{u,a}$ owning that seed at its actual root. For
$d\ge2$, prescribe all its factors by

$$
z_{P_{u,a},L}=\Gamma_L(s_{u,\operatorname{next}(a)}),\qquad
z_{P_{u,a},q}=\Gamma_q(s_{u,a})\quad(q\in W_d\setminus\{L\}).
\tag{GDB.32}
$$

Use the binary root guard of Theorem 4.1. For each oriented edge
$a=uw$, add an original block $X_a$ with root factor $(0,0)$ and

$$
z_{X_a,Lr}=\Gamma_r(s_{u,a}),\qquad
z_{X_a,Rr}=\Gamma_r(s_{w,a})\qquad(Lr,Rr\in W_d).
\tag{GDB.33}
$$

Every equation is constant or queries the indicated actual owner root;
unused decoder entries are completed in the attained range. All original
targets are specified. There are exactly $2e$ seeds, $2e$ connector
blocks and $e$ edge blocks: $n=3e$ original weight owners. A seed occurs
in its own connector, the cyclic predecessor connector and its edge block.
Its incidence is three at vertices of degree at least two and two at a
degree-one vertex, hence always at most three.

Fix $t=2^{-10}$ and the single full-support block law on all of $S_d$

$$
\begin{aligned}
w_d(\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle)
 &=w_d(\langle\beta,\beta\rangle)=t,\\
w_d(x)&=1\quad\text{at every other }x\in S_d,\\
W_d^{\rm wt}&=|S_d|-2+2t,\qquad\mu_d=w_d/W_d^{\rm wt}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{GDB.34}
$$

The superscript distinguishes this weight total from the word menu $W_d$.
At $d=2$ it is $4609/512$; each diagonal probability is $1/9218$
and each of the other nine probabilities is $512/4609$.

**Theorem 7.2 (exact native graph partition identity).** The network
$\mathcal N_d(G)$ of Definition 7.1 is a complete actual native
competitor in $\mathfrak N_{d,3}(3e)$. It has exactly $2^v$ actual
tuples, one per vertex spin assignment $b:V(G)\to\{0,1\}$, and

$$
Z(\mathcal N_d(G);\mu_d,\ldots,\mu_d)
 =(W_d^{\rm wt})^{-3e}\sum_b t^{m_G(b)},
\tag{GDB.35}
$$

where $m_G(b)$ is the number of monochromatic edges. Both maps of
solutions are bijective. Every original whole-block potential is counted
once. The raw identity holds for arbitrary $t\ge0$, with $0^0=1$;
the fixed positive $t$ gives positive mass and an ordinary logarithm.

Proof. A connector root forces that actual block to be its own seed's
leaf. Injectivity of the two leaf left factors makes (GDB.32) hold
exactly when $s_{u,a}=s_{u,\operatorname{next}(a)}$. Traversing the
public cyclic order forces one common bit at vertex $u$. This includes
the degree-one tautology; no equality oracle or independent-copy
assumption is used. All other connector targets are then correct.

The edge root is branch. Its immediate child factors identify precisely
its two endpoint leaves, and all longer factors in (GDB.33) are their
actual factors. Thus the edge block is exactly the ordered pair of
these leaves. Conversely any vertex assignment fills every connector
and edge block uniquely and satisfies every equation. The actual
connector roots recover all seeds. This proves both bijections and
the solution count, including disconnected graphs.

Every connector has raw weight one. An edge block has raw weight $t$
exactly for a monochromatic edge, otherwise one. Multiplication of the
original $3e$ weights once gives $t^{m_G(b)}$, and normalization gives
(GDB.35). This is an explicitly charged enlargement by $2e$ original
connector blocks, not a lossless recut of arbitrary competitors. It
provides a graph subfamily of actual native networks; a theorem about
all graphs in this subfamily alone would not cover all native templates.
$\square$

## 8. Equal finite neighborhoods with separated individual pressures

**Definition 8.1 (full marked profile and per-template pressure).** Use
the bipartite incidence graph with one vertex for each seed and one for
each original block, joined when a block has a port assigned to that seed.
Its marks contain each domain, every owner designation and actual owner
port, every prescribed port/table, all constants, all repeated occurrences
within a block, and the entire block-weight table. Distinct occurrences
of the same seed within one block are in the same edge mark. Global
unique names are forgotten, but all local labels and references are
preserved under consistent block/seed renaming.

For $R\ge0$, let $p_R(\mathcal N)$ be the distribution of the rooted
radius-$R$ induced marked ball at a uniformly chosen original block.
One may also include the corresponding seed-rooted distribution and the
exact numbers of blocks and seeds. These strengthenings are included in
the theorem below. Write

$$
\mathcal P(\mathcal N)=\frac1n\log_2Z(\mathcal N;\mu_d,\ldots,\mu_d).
\tag{GDB.36}
$$

This is the pressure of an individual finite network, not the all-network
supremum $\log_2\beta_d(\mu_d)$.

**Theorem 8.2 (native finite-neighborhood pressure separation).** For
every fixed $d\ge2$ and every integer $R\ge0$, there are arbitrarily
large pairs $\mathcal A_R,\mathcal B_R\in\mathfrak N_{d,3}(n)$
with the same $n$, seed count, full marked profile $p_R$ and
full-support law (GDB.34), the same number of actual tuples, and

$$
\mathcal P(\mathcal B_R)-\mathcal P(\mathcal A_R)\ge\frac35.
\tag{GDB.37}
$$

Both may be padded by identical constant-alpha blocks to a common
dyadic length $K$, preserving all these profile agreements and a pressure
gap greater than $3/10$ per padded original block.

Proof. We first give the finite graph ingredient with explicit constants.
For every integer $g\ge2$ there are arbitrarily large finite simple
graphs with no isolated vertices, maximum degree at most $1000$, girth
greater than $g$, $e\ge5v$, and

$$
m_G(b)\ge e/5\qquad\text{for every binary coloring }b.
\tag{GDB.38}
$$

Here is an elementary probabilistic derivation, used only inside this
native construction. On even $N\ge\max(6,4g)$ vertices, take the
union of $1000$ independent uniform perfect matchings. It is a loopless
multigraph with $m=500N$ edges counted with multiplicity. For any fixed
cut its expected crossing count is at most
$1000N^2/(4(N-1))\le3m/5$. Expose matching partners one pair at
a time. Conditional completions for two alternative next partners
couple by switching two matching edges, changing the final cut count
by at most two. The Doob martingale therefore has increments of absolute
value at most two over at most $m$ steps. The usual Azuma bound gives

$$
\Pr\{\text{this cut has at least }3m/4\text{ crossing edges}\}
 \le\exp(-9m/3200).
$$

Union over at most $2^N$ cuts gives
$\exp((\ln2-45/32)N)<1/2$. This is the standard bounded-difference
probabilistic method; no new general concentration theorem is asserted.

For each $2\le\ell\le g$, enumerate ordered candidate cycles
with distinct matching-labelled edges and their matching colors.
In a parallel-edge cycle of length two the colors are distinct.
There are at most $N^\ell1000^\ell$ candidates.
Required edges within any one matching must be disjoint;
otherwise their probability is zero. For disjoint prescribed edges,
the successive matching denominators are at least $N/2$ when
$N\ge4g$, so the probability is at most $(2/N)^\ell$. This
also bounds colored parallel-edge cycles at $\ell=2$. Thus the
expected total short-cycle count is at most
$B_g=\sum_{\ell=2}^g2000^\ell$. By Markov's inequality its count
is at most $2B_g$ with probability at least $1/2$. The strict cut
probability bound makes the intersection of these two good events
nonempty. Choose arbitrarily large even $N\ge\max(6,4g,64B_g)$.

Delete one edge from each listed short cycle, using at most
$a\le2B_g\le m/16$ deletions, and remove isolated vertices. The
remaining graph is simple, has the required girth and maximum degree,
and has $e=m-a\ge15m/16\ge5v$, since $v\le N$. Every coloring
leaves at least $m/4-a\ge(m-a)/5=e/5$ monochromatic edges.
Since $v\ge2e/1000$, its size grows without bound with $N$.
All the final properties, including (GDB.38), can be checked on a finite
graph by finite enumeration. Consequently exhaustive search selects a
graph of any required girth and minimum size effectively, without a
useful efficiency assertion.

Choose such $G$ with girth greater than $4R+4$, with its public edge
orientations and cyclic orders. Let $G\sqcup G$ be its disjoint
two-sheet cover and $G\times K_2$ its bipartite double cover. Lift
the orders and orientations from $G$, and put

$$
\mathcal A_R=\mathcal N_d(G\sqcup G),\qquad
\mathcal B_R=\mathcal N_d(G\times K_2).
\tag{GDB.39}
$$

Both graphs have $2v$ vertices and $2e$ edges. Hence both networks
have exactly $n=6e$ original blocks, $4e$ seeds and $2^{2v}$
actual tuples. Their full block laws are identical.

To verify the marked-profile assertion, project each decorated native
incidence graph to that of $G$. In both covers every block and seed
has two preimages. The connector cycles at a vertex preserve the sheet;
only a traversal through an original edge block exchanges sheets in
the bipartite cover. An odd sheet change along a closed walk therefore
projects, after collapsing connector gadgets, to an odd closed walk
in $G$, of no greater length. Such a walk contains an odd cycle.
A discrepancy between induced radius-$R$ balls would give such a
closed walk of length at most $2R+1$: two paths from the root of
length at most $R$, with possibly one edge between their endpoints.
The girth choice excludes it. Map a lifted root and each vertex reached
by a path of length at most $R$ to the endpoint of the same projected
path in the other cover. The exclusion makes this map independent of
the chosen path and preserves every induced edge. Reversing the covers
gives its inverse. All port tables, constants, owner marks and weight
tables are unchanged. Internal connector cycles cause no sheet change
and lift identically. Since each base root has exactly
two preimages, both block-rooted and seed-rooted profile distributions
are exactly equal, including their counts. No spin assignment is made
part of these fixed marks.

Every coloring of $G\sqcup G$ has at least $2e/5$ monochromatic
edges. Its raw spin sum is at most

$$
2^{2v}t^{2e/5}=2^{2v-4e}.
$$

The bipartite double cover has at least two proper colorings, each of
weight one. Its raw sum is at least two. The common normalization
$(W_d^{\rm wt})^{-6e}$ in (GDB.35) cancels in their ratio, so

$$
\mathcal P(\mathcal B_R)-\mathcal P(\mathcal A_R)
 \ge\frac{1-2v+4e}{6e}
 \ge\frac23-\frac{v}{3e}\ge\frac35.
$$

Finally choose $K=2^{\lceil\log_2 n\rceil}$. Pad both networks
by $K-n$ constant-alpha blocks with no seeds. These isolated marked
blocks add exactly the same mixture to their profiles. Their raw weights
are one and their normalized weights are $(W_d^{\rm wt})^{-1}$,
so the mass ratio is unchanged. Since $K<2n$, dividing the logarithmic
ratio by $K$ gives a gap greater than $3/10$. $\square$

**Corollary 8.3 (the precise per-template approximation obstruction).**
Let $U_R(d,3,\mu_d,n,p_R)$ depend only on the displayed full marked
profile and counts. Even allowing noncomputable functions, it is
impossible to have both

$$
\mathcal P(\mathcal N)\le U_R(d,3,\mu_d,n,p_R(\mathcal N))
\tag{GDB.40}
$$

for every actual network, and a uniform approximation condition

$$
U_R(d,3,\mu_d,n,p_R(\mathcal N))-\mathcal P(\mathcal N)
 \le\delta_R+\epsilon_R(n),
\quad\delta_R\longrightarrow0,\quad
\epsilon_R(n)\longrightarrow0\text{ for fixed }R.
\tag{GDB.41}
$$

At every $R$, the excess at some arbitrarily large input is at least
$3/5$. A two-sided estimator from these same inputs has worst error at
least $3/10$. On dyadic padded inputs the corresponding lower bounds
are greater than $3/10$ and $3/20$.

Proof. Both networks in Theorem 8.2 give identical arguments to $U_R$.
Soundness for the larger pressure forces excess at least their gap on
the smaller one. Their sizes are arbitrarily large, so an error tending
to zero in $n$ cannot absorb it. Choose $R$ with $\delta_R$ below
the fixed gap to contradict (GDB.41). For two-sided approximation,
one common real output must have error at least half the pressure
separation. The padded version follows from the same argument.
$\square$

This rules out uniform approximation of every individual template by
these fixed finite-neighborhood data. It permits sound but loose local
uppers, and does not refute approximation of an optimized all-network
supremum, optimizer-cofinal cap hierarchies, all global verifiers,
local-global colored feasibility, or faithful operational recuts.
In particular it does not identify the optimizer of $\beta_d(\mu_d)$.
Optimizing over $\mu$ changes yet another quantifier. No
noncomputability conclusion follows.

**Proposition 8.4 (a global joint statistic for this subfamily).** For
the graph-realized networks define
$N_G(j)=|\{b:m_G(b)=j\}|$ for $0\le j\le e$, and let
$h_G(j)=\log_2N_G(j)$ with $\log_2 0=-\infty$. For $t>0$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\max_j\frac{h_G(j)+j\log_2t}{3e}
&\le\mathcal P(\mathcal N_d(G))+\log_2W_d^{\rm wt}\\
&\le\max_j\frac{h_G(j)+j\log_2t}{3e}
       +\frac{\log_2(e+1)}{3e}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{GDB.42}
$$

Proof. Group the exact same spin assignments in (GDB.35) by $m_G$.
Its raw sum is exactly $\sum_{j=0}^e N_G(j)t^j$, between its
largest summand and $(e+1)$ times that summand. Taking logarithms
and dividing by $3e$ proves both directions. At $t=0$ the exact
raw sum is the proper-coloring count $N_G(0)$ instead; a zero value
requires the extended logarithm convention. $\square$

This elementary finite Laplace comparison is a consumer of the exact
native lift, not a new general entropy theorem. It specifies a joint
relation missing from the demonstrated neighborhood data: simultaneous
realizability and multiplicity of near-proper colorings. In (GDB.38)
the low graph has $N_G(j)=0$ for $j<e/5$, whereas its bipartite
cover has positive $N(0)$. A maximum cut alone does not give the
full histogram entropy. Finite computation of that histogram does not
bound the supremum over all native networks or construct a cofinal
global-statistic hierarchy.
