# FIB harmonic-edge load: predictive quotient, unifilar erasure tails and strict scope

This separate supplier gives ordinary handwritten proofs of a universal comparison between future-output-classification error and both actual one-sided erasure defects. An explicit row-unifilar hypothesis then supplies a law-dependent geometric allowance. An exact full-support twelve-state law proves strict scope beyond supported positive-rectangle-word and Condition K routes, while invisible hidden information persists. The arbitrary-finite attainment/separation problem remains open. This text is reference mathematics, not Lean-kernel verification.

## 1. Original domain and one actual probability law

**Definition 1.1 (Original objective and producer domain).** Retain the complete open-path words and all legal Hamming-one edges:

$$
B_n=\{w\in\{0,1\}^n:w_iw_{i+1}=0\ (1\le i<n)\},\qquad
h(s,t)=\begin{cases}st/(s+t)&s+t>0,\\0&s=t=0,\end{cases}
$$

$$
\Phi_n(\mu)=\sum_{\{w,v\}\in E(\Gamma_n)}h(\mu_w,\mu_v),\qquad
\kappa_n=\max_{\mu\in\mathcal P(B_n)}\Phi_n(\mu),\qquad
c_\star=\lim_n\kappa_n/n.
$$

Endpoint insertion uses $w_0=w_{n+1}=0$, without a first-to-last constraint. Zero masses do not delete words or edges. All-zero words, endpoint ones and every legal seam remain. For every finite $N\ge1$, the entire exact-real producer domain of [Universal, Definition 4.1][U] is

$$
\Theta_N=\{(\lambda,M_0,M_1):\lambda\ge0,\ \lambda\mathbf1=1,\ M_0,M_1\ge0,
A=M_0+M_1,\ A\mathbf1=\mathbf1,\ \lambda A=\lambda,\ \lambda M_1^2\mathbf1=0\}.
\tag{1.1}
$$

Here $\lambda$ is a row and $M_x$ are $N\times N$. Write $M_w=M_{w_1}\cdots M_{w_n}$ and $M_\epsilon=I$. Structural zeros, zero stationary masses, reducible, periodic and noncontractive cases are included. Row unifilarity is an additional hypothesis only in §3.

**Definition 1.2 (Same-law convention and actual P8 fields).** Fix one $\theta\in\Theta_N$. The stationary joint law of [Universal, Proposition 4.2][U] is

$$
J_\theta(H_a=h_a,\ldots,H_{b+1}=h_{b+1},X_a=x_a,\ldots,X_b=x_b)
=\lambda_{h_a}\prod_{i=a}^{b}M_{x_i}(h_i,h_{i+1}),\qquad
P_\theta([w]_a)=\lambda M_w\mathbf1.
\tag{1.2}
$$

Right and left consistency use $A\mathbf1=\mathbf1$ and $\lambda A=\lambda$, respectively. Every $11$ cylinder has zero mass, so the visible marginal $P=P_\theta$ is supported on the two-sided no11 space. All clocks are the original emitted-bit integer coordinates. In each argument, classes, classifiers, posterior versions and expectations belong to this $J_\theta$ and its $P$, rather than separately specified conditional models.

For integers $r,R\ge0$, use exactly [Conditional Response, §2][P8]'s fields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_0&=\sigma(X_j:j\ne0),\\
\mathcal G_L(r)&=\sigma(X_{-r},\ldots,X_{-1},X_j:j\ge1),\\
\mathcal G_R(r)&=\sigma(X_j:j\le-1,X_1,\ldots,X_r),
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
q=\mathbb E_P[X_0\mid\mathcal E_0],\quad
p_L(r)=\mathbb E_P[X_0\mid\mathcal G_L(r)],\quad
p_R(r)=\mathbb E_P[X_0\mid\mathcal G_R(r)],
$$

$$
e_L(r)=\mathbb E_P(q-p_L(r))^2,\quad
e_R(r)=\mathbb E_P(q-p_R(r))^2,\quad
\tau_P(R)=\sum_{r\ge R}(e_L(r)+e_R(r)).
\tag{1.3}
$$

Empty coordinate lists at $r=0$ give no information. Both fields retain their entire opposite half-line. The series is allowed to be infinite. The supplied original risk identity is $d(P)=\mathbb E_P(X_0-q)^2\le c_\star$. [Conditional Response, §1][P8] also supplies $0<1800/12559\le c_\star\le3-2\sqrt2$.

All conditional expectations have $[0,1]$ measurable versions. Zero-total finite fibers have zero weight and require no division. On a neighbor-one event, $X_0=q=0$ and the full loss is zero. A one-sided defect vanishes there if its field retains the forcing neighbor. Both fields retain both neighbors for $r\ge1$; at $r=0$, $\mathcal G_L(0)$ omits $X_{-1}$ and $\mathcal G_R(0)$ omits $X_1$. Thus full forced-zero loss does not imply a blanket radius-zero forced-zero defect.

**Lemma 1.3 (Same-law positive-mass trimming).** Restricting (1.1) to $I_+=\{i:\lambda_i>0\}$ preserves every output cylinder, gives a stationary stochastic presentation, and makes $M_1^2=0$ entrywise. This is the trimming operation of [Renewal, Lemma 8.2][R].

Proof. If $j\notin I_+$, then $0=\lambda_j=\sum_{i\in I_+}\lambda_iA_{ij}$. Nonnegativity forces $A_{ij}=M_x(i,j)=0$ for every retained $i$. The retained set is closed, and every path with positive initial mass stays there. Restriction therefore preserves (1.2), normalization and stationarity. On this set, $\lambda>0$ and $\lambda M_1^2\mathbf1=0$ force every entry of the nonnegative matrix $M_1^2$ to vanish. Consequently every nonzero trimmed word product has positive visible probability and is legal. Trimming is a proved same-law operation; zero-mass presentations remain allowed in the original domain. $\square$

## 2. Universal task-visible screening and classification comparison

**Definition 2.1 (Future-output equivalence).** On the positive-mass states define

$$
i\sim j\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathbf e_iM_w\mathbf1=\mathbf e_jM_w\mathbf1
\quad\text{for every finite binary word }w.
\tag{2.1}
$$

These are equality classes of conditional future output measures, since future cylinders determine those measures. Let $Q_t=[H_t]$, with $K$ classes. Define the optimal current-class error

$$
\epsilon_Q(r)=\inf_m J_\theta(Q_0\ne m(X_{-r:-1}))
=\mathbb E_{J_\theta}\!\left[1-\max_a
J_\theta(Q_0=a\mid X_{-r:-1})\right].
\tag{2.2}
$$

A fixed ordering resolves MAP ties. Stationarity identifies (2.2) with the optimal error for $Q_{r+1}$ given $X_{1:r}$. At radius zero the observed word is empty. This quotient is a function of the selected actual producer; its class count is not a hidden-state minimality assertion.

**Lemma 2.2 (Universal future-output screening).** Under $J_\theta$, the future outputs $X_{t:\infty}$ are conditionally independent of all hidden and visible coordinates before $t$, given $Q_t$.

Proof. The path factorization (1.2) makes their conditional law given $H_t=i$ and the entire past equal to the future output law from $i$. By (2.1) this law depends only on $Q_t$. Averaging within a class proves the conditional independence first on finite past/future cylinders; bounded monotone-class extension proves it for the half-lines. This screening statement does not imply a general marked Markov quotient: in a nonunifilar model, equality of the $xw$ laws constrains successor mixtures and does not identify their individual successor-class weights. $\square$

**Theorem 2.3 (Both actual classification-to-defect comparisons).** For every finite $N$, every $\theta\in\Theta_N$ and every $r,R\ge0$,

$$
e_L(r)\le\epsilon_Q(r),\qquad e_R(r)\le\epsilon_Q(r),\qquad
\tau_P(R)\le2\sum_{r\ge R}\epsilon_Q(r).
\tag{2.3}
$$

No unifilarity, positivity, irreducibility, aperiodicity, synchronization or attainment hypothesis is required. Summability of the class errors is sufficient, not asserted necessary.

Proof. All following expectations are under the same $J_\theta$; output expectations agree with those under $P$.

For the left comparison, write $W=X_{-r:-1}$, $L=X_{-\infty:-r-1}$ and $V=X_{1:\infty}$. Choose

$$
g(Q_0,V)=\mathbb E[X_0\mid Q_0,V].
$$

Lemma 2.2 screens $(X_0,V)$ from $(L,W)$ given $Q_0$. Conditioning further on $V$ therefore gives

$$
q=\mathbb E[g\mid L,W,V],\qquad
p_L(r)=\mathbb E[g\mid W,V].
$$

The second field is contained in the first. Conditional contraction and projection optimality, with a MAP $m_L(W)$ for $Q_0$, give

$$
\begin{aligned}
e_L(r)
&\le\mathbb E(g-p_L(r))^2\\
&\le\mathbb E\bigl(g(Q_0,V)-g(m_L(W),V)\bigr)^2\\
&\le J_\theta(Q_0\ne m_L(W))=\epsilon_Q(r).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.4}
$$

The approximation in the second line is $\sigma(W,V)$-measurable. Its squared error is zero on correct classification and at most one otherwise.

For the right comparison, write $B=X_{-\infty:-1}$, $W=X_{1:r}$ and $V'=X_{r+1:\infty}$. Now choose

$$
g(B,W,Q_{r+1})=\mathbb E[X_0\mid B,W,Q_{r+1}].
$$

Screening at $r+1$ makes $V'$ independent of $(B,W,X_0)$ given $Q_{r+1}$. Thus

$$
q=\mathbb E[g\mid B,W,V'],\qquad p_R(r)=\mathbb E[g\mid B,W].
$$

For a MAP $m_R(W)$ for $Q_{r+1}$, contraction and the $\sigma(B,W)$-measurable approximation $g(B,W,m_R(W))$ give

$$
e_R(r)\le\mathbb E(g-p_R(r))^2
\le\mathbb E\bigl(g(B,W,Q_{r+1})-g(B,W,m_R(W))\bigr)^2
\le\epsilon_Q(r).
\tag{2.5}
$$

Stationarity supplies the last equality of error probabilities; no time reversal is assumed. The whole $V$ in (2.4) and the whole $B$ in (2.5) remain conditioned on.

Finite $Q$ and the standard Borel output spaces permit measurable $[0,1]$ extensions of both $g$ functions on impossible class/exterior pairs. Such extensions make the approximations legitimate, without assigning an auxiliary probability law or dividing by a null fiber. Summing the nonnegative bounds proves the tail comparison, including an infinite right side. $\square$

## 3. Restricted marked quotient and a law-dependent geometric allowance

**Definition 3.1 (Row-unifilar supplier hypothesis).** After Lemma 1.3's same-law trimming, assume each row of each $M_x$ has at most one positive entry. Write

$$
p_i(x)=\sum_jM_x(i,j),\qquad
M_x(i,j)=p_i(x)\mathbf1_{\{j=\delta(i,x)\}}
$$

when $p_i(x)>0$. Successors on zero-probability symbols may be assigned arbitrarily and have zero weight. This hypothesis restricts the present supplier, not $\Theta_N$.

**Proposition 3.2 (Actual marked quotient under unifilarity).** The classes (2.1) have a well-defined row-unifilar marked kernel

$$
T_x(a,b)=p_a(x)\mathbf1_{\{b=\delta(a,x)\}},\qquad
\pi_a=\sum_{i:[i]=a}\lambda_i.
\tag{3.1}
$$

It is the actual pushforward of $(H_t,X_t)$ under $J_\theta$, is stationary with row $\pi$, and preserves every original output cylinder.

Proof. If $i\sim j$, their first-symbol probabilities agree. For every $w$,

$$
p_i(x)\,\mathbf e_{\delta(i,x)}M_w\mathbf1
=\mathbf e_iM_{xw}\mathbf1
=\mathbf e_jM_{xw}\mathbf1
=p_j(x)\,\mathbf e_{\delta(j,x)}M_w\mathbf1.
$$

When the common $p_i(x)$ is positive, division proves equivalent successors. Hence $p_a(x)$ and its successor class are independent of the representative. Conditional on any actual past and $Q_t=a$, every compatible $H_t$ has the same outgoing class/symbol probabilities (3.1). Averaging proves the marked Markov property for this actual pushforward. Stationarity and cylinder preservation follow by pushforward; distinct quotient states have distinct finite-word future distributions by construction. This is the step requiring row unifilarity. $\square$

**Theorem 3.3 (Symbolic allowance with all stationary boundaries).** If $K=1$, then $\epsilon_Q(r)=e_L(r)=e_R(r)=0$ for all $r$; take $\eta_\theta(R)=0$. If $K\ge2$, choose a finite $\ell\ge1$ distinguishing every pair of quotient states by its length-$\ell$ word distribution $P_a(w)=\mathbf e_aT_w\mathbf1$. Put

$$
\rho=\max_{a\ne b}\sum_{|w|=\ell}\sqrt{P_a(w)P_b(w)}<1,\qquad
D=\sum_{a<b}\sqrt{\pi_a\pi_b}\le\frac{K-1}{2}.
\tag{3.2}
$$

Any common upper bound below one may replace this $\rho$. Then

$$
\epsilon_Q(r),\ e_L(r),\ e_R(r)\le D\rho^{\lfloor r/\ell\rfloor},\qquad
\tau_P(R)\le\eta_\theta(R):=
\frac{2D\ell}{1-\rho}\rho^{\lfloor R/\ell\rfloor}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{3.3}
$$

These constants depend on the fixed actual law and its selected presentation. The conclusion covers reducible and periodic stationary presentations, structural zeros and original zero-mass states through Lemma 1.3. It gives no fixed-$N$ uniform rate or efficient distinguishing length.

Proof. There are finitely many distinct pairs, each distinguished by some finite cylinder. Longer word distributions retain every shorter marginal, so one common $\ell$ exists. Cauchy–Schwarz gives affinity at most one, with equality precisely for equal normalized distributions. Thus (3.2) has $\rho<1$.

On ordered distinct quotient pairs define the nonnegative matrix

$$
\mathsf B_{(a,b),(c,d)}
=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}}\sqrt{p_a(x)p_b(x)}
\,\mathbf1_{\{\delta(a,x)=c,\ \delta(b,x)=d,\ c\ne d\}},
\tag{3.4}
$$

ignoring zero-weight symbols. Its row sums are at most one. Each word determines one trajectory from each initial class, and trajectories cannot split after meeting. Consequently

$$
(\mathsf B^r\mathbf1)_{(a,b)}
=\sum_{\substack{|w|=r\\\delta(a,w)\ne\delta(b,w)}}
\sqrt{P_a(w)P_b(w)}.
\tag{3.5}
$$

Undefined paths have zero contribution. This identity follows by expanding the path products; final distinctness is equivalent to never having met. In particular $\mathsf B^\ell\mathbf1\le\rho\mathbf1$. Nonnegativity and $\mathsf B\mathbf1\le\mathbf1$ imply

$$
\mathsf B^r\mathbf1\le\rho^{\lfloor r/\ell\rfloor}\mathbf1.
\tag{3.6}
$$

Indeed write $r=m\ell+t$, $0\le t<\ell$, bound the remainder by $\mathbf1$, and iterate the length-$\ell$ inequality.

For an observed word $w$, let $a_i=\pi_iP_i(w)$ and group initial masses by final class:

$$
b_s=\sum_{\delta(i,w)=s}a_i.
$$

Its unnormalized contribution to final-class MAP error is $\sum_s b_s-\max_s b_s$. If $m$ maximizes $b_s$, then $b_s\le\sqrt{b_mb_s}$ for $s\ne m$, and

$$
\sum_s b_s-\max_s b_s
\le\sum_{s<t}\sqrt{b_sb_t}
\le\sum_{\substack{i<j\\\delta(i,w)\ne\delta(j,w)}}\sqrt{a_ia_j}.
\tag{3.7}
$$

The second inequality uses $\sqrt{\sum_i a_i}\le\sum_i\sqrt{a_i}$ separately in the two disjoint groups. Summing over words, (3.5)–(3.6) and stationarity give

$$
\epsilon_Q(r)\le
\sum_{i<j}\sqrt{\pi_i\pi_j}(\mathsf B^r\mathbf1)_{(i,j)}
\le D\rho^{\lfloor r/\ell\rfloor}.
$$

Finally $D=((\sum_i\sqrt{\pi_i})^2-1)/2\le(K-1)/2$. Apply Theorem 2.3 and sum by $\ell$-sized blocks, overcounting the first partial block harmlessly, to obtain (3.3). The proof uses every quotient pair directly and needs neither communication nor convergence of hidden transition powers.

For $\rho=0$, use $0^0=1$ and $0^m=0$ for $m\ge1$: $\mathsf B^\ell\mathbf1=0$ makes all classification errors and defects vanish for $r\ge\ell$, while (3.3) still bounds the initial finite tail. The $K=1$ case follows immediately from (2.2)–(2.3). $\square$

The pair matrix and word sums are symbolic proof objects. They do not assert a numerical computation, word/pair search, exact synchronization word or hidden minimality. The allowance is proved before any attainment assumption.

## 4. An exact full-support strict-scope witness

**Definition 4.1 (The twelve-state law).** Use $F_s,T_s$, $s\in\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$, and set

$$
p_0=p_3=\tfrac14,\qquad p_1=p_4=\tfrac12,\qquad
p_2=p_5=\tfrac34,\qquad c=\tfrac3{62}.
$$

The only nonzero entries are

$$
M_0(F_s,F_s)=1-p_s,\qquad M_0(T_s,F_s)=1,\qquad
M_1(F_s,T_{s+1})=p_s,
\tag{4.1}
$$

with $\lambda(F_s)=c/p_s$ and $\lambda(T_s)=c$. Indices in (4.1) are modulo six.

**Proposition 4.2 (Lawfulness, full support and exact classes).** This parameter belongs to $\Theta_{12}$, all stationary masses are positive, its hidden chain is irreducible and aperiodic, and every legal output cylinder is positive. Its exact future-output classes are

$$
\{F_s,F_{s+3}\},\qquad\{T_s,T_{s+3}\},\qquad s=0,1,2.
\tag{4.2}
$$

Proof. The free masses are $6/31,3/31,2/31$ repeated twice, totaling $22/31$; tail masses total $9/31$. Every row sums to one. Each event flow $\lambda(F_s)p_s=c$ supplies the incoming mass of $T_{s+1}$; the incoming mass of $F_s$ is $\lambda(F_s)(1-p_s)+c=\lambda(F_s)$. Thus $\lambda A=\lambda$. A one enters $T$, whose one row is zero, so $M_1^2=0$.

The positive $10$ path advances $F_s$ to $F_{s+1}$; these paths connect the cycle and every tail state. Each free state has a positive zero self-loop. From any $F_s$, every no11 word follows a positive path, including leading or final ones and all-zero words. Positive initial free mass gives full legal support on the original clock.

Shift by three preserves every marked probability, proving (4.2)'s within-pair equalities. Distinct free residues have different next-one probabilities $p_s$; distinct tail residues have different second-one probabilities $p_s$; free and tail states differ on the next-one event. There are exactly six classes, denoted $F_z,T_z$ for $z$ modulo three, with $\pi(F_z)=2c/p_z$ and $\pi(T_z)=2c$. This proves the classes, not minimality among all presentations. $\square$

**Proposition 4.3 (Actual summable tail and persistent hidden information).** With $\bar\rho=\sqrt{15}/4<1$,

$$
e_L(r),e_R(r)\le\tfrac52\bar\rho^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor},\qquad
\tau_P(R)\le\frac{10}{1-\bar\rho}\bar\rho^{\lfloor R/2\rfloor}.
\tag{4.3}
$$

Nevertheless, using natural-log entropy and $W=X_{-r:-1}$,

$$
I_{J_\theta}(H_{-r};H_0\mid W)=\ln2+H_{J_\theta}(Q_0\mid W)\ge\ln2
\quad(r\ge0).
\tag{4.4}
$$

Proof. Length-two distributions, in order $00,01,10,11$, are

$$
P_{F_z}=\bigl((1-p_z)^2,(1-p_z)p_z,p_z,0\bigr),\qquad
P_{T_z}=(1-p_z,p_z,0,0).
$$

Free/free pairs differ by at least $1/4$ on $10$, tail/tail pairs on $01$, and free/tail pairs on $10$. Thus their total variation is at least $1/4$. For normalized distributions $u,v$ with affinity $h=\sum_w\sqrt{u_wv_w}$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

$$
\operatorname{TV}(u,v)
=\tfrac12\sum_w|\sqrt{u_w}-\sqrt{v_w}|(\sqrt{u_w}+\sqrt{v_w})
\le\sqrt{1-h^2}.
$$

All affinities are at most $\bar\rho$. Apply Theorem 3.3 with $\ell=2$, $K=6$ and $D\le5/2$ to obtain (4.3).

Unifilarity makes $H_0$ a function of $(H_{-r},W)$. The observation-preserving symmetry $s\mapsto s+3$ preserves this stationary joint law and exchanges the two lifts of each $Q_0$. Conditional on $(Q_0,W)$, the lifts are equiprobable. The entropy chain rule therefore gives (4.4). Its series diverges, although the actual defect series is finite. $\square$

**Theorem 4.4 (Past class, future residue and full erasure).** Let $Z_i$ be the hidden index modulo three. Then $Q_0$ is measurable in $X_{-\infty:-1}$, and $Z_1$ is measurable in $X_{1:\infty}$. Consequently $q=X_0$ almost surely and $d(P)=0$.

Proof. Theorem 3.3 makes the MAP errors for the fixed $Q_0$ from $X_{-r:-1}$ summable. Borel–Cantelli makes these estimates eventually correct almost surely; their limit is a past-measurable version of $Q_0$, hence also of $Z_0$.

For the future, the target is the initial residue $Z_1$. Every common word adds its number of ones modulo three to both initial residues. Trajectories from different residues never meet, so their full length-$r$ future affinity is exactly the pair sum (3.5), bounded by $\bar\rho^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}$. Group the masses $\pi_iP_i(w)$ by initial residue and apply (3.7). The MAP error for $Z_1$ from $X_{1:r}$ is at most $D\bar\rho^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}$. Borel–Cantelli now gives a future-measurable version of $Z_1$.

The actual rule (4.1) gives $Z_1=Z_0+X_0$ modulo three. The increments zero and one are distinct, so the two retained half-lines determine $X_0$. Hence the actual full erased-bit posterior equals $X_0$.

This future argument recovers only the initial residue. Both $F_z$ and $T_z$ can emit zero and reach $F_z$, with positive probabilities $1-p_z$ and one. Their initial F/T phases have overlapping future laws and are not recovered by this argument. $\square$

**Proposition 4.5 (Exact nonzero defects and the forcing qualification).** Under this same law,

$$
e_L(0)=e_R(0)=\frac9{62}>0.
\tag{4.5}
$$

Proof. Since $q=X_0$, these defects are the respective one-sided conditional Brier risks. Use the six quotient classes and their masses $\pi$ from Proposition 4.2; quotient path weights include both original hidden lifts. All sums below run over $z\in\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z$.

The whole past identifies $Q_0$. The actual outgoing rule gives $p_R(0)=p_z$ on $Q_0=F_z$ and $p_R(0)=0$ on $Q_0=T_z$. The joint masses for $X_0=0,1$ on $Q_0=F_z$ are respectively $\pi(F_z)(1-p_z),\pi(F_z)p_z$; on $Q_0=T_z$ they are $\pi(T_z),0$. These are also the whole-past conditional probabilities integrated over each class, because the future output law given that past depends only on $Q_0$. Therefore

$$
e_R(0)=\sum_z h\bigl(\pi(F_z)(1-p_z),\pi(F_z)p_z\bigr)
=2c\sum_z(1-p_z)=3c=\frac9{62}.
$$

For the left side, define two normalized measures on the entire future $X_{1:\infty}$. Under $\mathcal R_z$, the first bit $X_1$ is zero and the remaining process starts at time two in class $F_z$. Under $\mathcal S_z$, $X_1=1$ and the remaining process starts at time two in class $T_{z+1}$. Both have initial residue $Z_1=z$. Theorem 4.4 and the positive actual weights below make these six measures mutually singular: $X_1$ distinguishes the two types, and the future-measurable $Z_1$ distinguishes their residues. This does not recover the initial F/T phase.

For a measurable future event $D$, put $\mu_x(D)=J_\theta(X_0=x,\ X_{1:\infty}\in D)$. The complete joint future submeasures are

$$
\mu_0=\sum_z(a_z\mathcal R_z+d_z\mathcal S_z),\qquad
\mu_1=\sum_z b_z\mathcal R_z,
\tag{4.6}
$$

where the actual two-edge weights give

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_z
&=\pi(F_z)(1-p_z)^2+\pi(T_z)(1-p_z)
=\pi(F_z)(1-p_z),\\
b_z
&=\pi(F_{z-1})p_{z-1}=2c=\pi(T_z),\\
d_z
&=\pi(F_z)(1-p_z)p_z+\pi(T_z)p_z
=\pi(F_z)p_z=2c.
\end{aligned}
$$

In the first line the two center-zero paths are $F_z\xrightarrow{0}F_z\xrightarrow{0}F_z$ and $T_z\xrightarrow{0}F_z\xrightarrow{0}F_z$. In particular the latter has weight $\pi(T_z)(1-p_z)$: its first zero is certain, while its second zero has probability $1-p_z$. The center-one path is $F_{z-1}\xrightarrow{1}T_z\xrightarrow{0}F_z$. The two terms in $d_z$ similarly end at $T_{z+1}$ after a center zero and a future one. These are all possibilities under (4.1); a center one followed by a future one is impossible.

The simplifications use the stationary incoming-zero mass

$$
\pi(F_z)(1-p_z)+\pi(T_z)=\pi(F_z).
$$

Thus $a_z+b_z=\pi(F_z)$ and $\sum_z(a_z+b_z+d_z)=\sum_z(\pi(F_z)+\pi(T_z))=1$. The $X_1=0$ submeasure has total mass $22/31$, and the $X_1=1$ submeasure has total mass $9/31$.

Taking the density of $\mu_1$ with respect to $\mu_0+\mu_1$ determines the actual future-only posterior on every component: it is $b_z/(a_z+b_z)=p_z$ on $\mathcal R_z$ and zero on $\mathcal S_z$. Consequently

$$
\begin{aligned}
e_L(0)
&=\sum_z\bigl(a_zp_z^2+b_z(1-p_z)^2\bigr)
=\sum_z h(a_z,b_z)\\
&=\sum_z\pi(F_z)p_z(1-p_z)=\frac9{62}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The omitted forcing neighbors also have explicit positive contributions. Put $A_-=\{X_{-1}=1\}$ and $A_+=\{X_1=1\}$. Almost surely $A_-$ is the union of $Q_0=T_z$, so its future submeasure is

$$
J_\theta(A_-,\ X_{1:\infty}\in\cdot)
=\sum_z\pi(T_z)\bigl((1-p_z)\mathcal R_z+p_z\mathcal S_z\bigr).
$$

Here $q=X_0=0$, while $p_L(0)$ equals $p_z$ on the $\mathcal R_z$ part and zero on the $\mathcal S_z$ part. For the right defect on $A_+$, the $Q_0=T_z$ part has $p_R(0)=0$; the $Q_0=F_z$ part has weight $\pi(F_z)(1-p_z)p_z$ and $p_R(0)=p_z$. Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[\mathbf1_{A_-}(q-p_L(0))^2]
&=\sum_z\pi(T_z)(1-p_z)p_z^2\\
&=\mathbb E_P[\mathbf1_{A_+}(q-p_R(0))^2]

\end{aligned}
\tag{4.7}

## 5. Supported-word obstruction in every stationary presentation

**Theorem 5.1 (Law-level positive-rectangle obstruction).** Let $P$ be a stationary no11 law with every legal cylinder positive and with $\mathbb E_P[X_0\mid\mathcal E_0]=X_0$ almost surely. In every stationary finite marked presentation of $P$, no word $w$ with $P([w])>0$ has a nonzero product $T=M_w$ whose support is exactly a positive rectangle $I\times J$.

Because $P$ has full legal support, this excludes every legal word in every such presentation. Equivalently, after the proved positive-mass trimming, there is no nonzero positive-rectangle word product. If $P$ is ergodic, the exclusion also holds within each positive-weight closed irreducible component of every alternative stationary finite presentation.

Proof. Suppose $T$ has strictly positive entries on nonempty $I\times J$ and zero entries elsewhere, for a positive-probability word $w$. This word is legal. Both protected words $w0x0w$, $x=0,1$, are legal, including when $w$ begins or ends with one. Full support gives

$$
\lambda T B_xT\mathbf1>0,\qquad B_x=M_0M_xM_0.
$$

Thus $s_x=\sum_{j\in J,i\in I}(B_x)_{ji}>0$ for both $x$. The exterior guard $G=\{w0\,[\text{erased center}]\,0w\}$ has positive probability.

On any positive finite farther completion of $G$, the two center masses are $\alpha TB_xT\beta$, with nonnegative actual farther messages $\alpha,\beta$. Both $\alpha T$ and $T\beta$ are nonzero. Normalize them to a probability row $l$ on $J$ and a probability column $v$ on $I$. Put

$$
u=\min_{i\in I,j\in J}\frac{T_{ij}}{\sum_{k\in J}T_{ik}}>0,\qquad
v_*=\min_{i\in I,j\in J}\frac{T_{ij}}{\sum_{k\in I}T_{kj}}>0.
$$

Each $l_j$ is a convex combination of normalized positive rows of $T$, so $l_j\ge u$. Each $v_i$ is a convex combination of normalized positive columns, so $v_i\ge v_*$. These bounds depend only on $T$ and hold uniformly over all compatible farther completions. Therefore

$$
lB_xv\ge uv_*s_x=:m_x>0.
$$

The nonnegative matrix $B_0+B_1=M_0AM_0$ is row-substochastic, so $l(B_0+B_1)v\le1$. The common scaling factors cancel from the center posterior. Both that posterior and its complement are bounded below by $m_1,m_0$, respectively. Set $\delta=\min(1/4,m_0,m_1)>0$. Every positive finite erased-center atom refining $G$ has posterior in $[\delta,1-\delta]$; zero-total atoms require no normalization.

Increasing finite exterior fields generate $\mathcal E_0$. Conditional projections converge in $L^2(P)$, and $G$ belongs to all sufficiently large fields. The closed interval bound therefore survives for the actual full posterior on $G$. This contradicts $q=X_0\in\{0,1\}$ on a positive event. The argument applies to any presentation, using only the stated properties of the same output law.

Lemma 1.3 shows that any nonzero trimmed word has positive probability and is legal, proving the equivalent trimmed formulation. For the component assertion, a finite stationary hidden chain decomposes on its positive support into closed irreducible classes. Apply the preceding guard argument within each component. $\square$

## 8. Fixed-target finite-past support and the zero-error stopping boundary

**Definition 8.1 (Actual fixed target, information and returns).** Throughout this section fix exactly Definition 4.1's twelve-state parameter, its stationary joint law $J=J_\theta$ from (1.2), and its actual six-class quotient $Q_t=[H_t]$ from Proposition 4.2. The target is always $Q_0$. For integer radii $r\ge0$ put

$$
W_r=X_{-r:-1},\qquad
\mathcal F_r=\sigma(W_r),\qquad
\mathcal F_\infty=\sigma(X_j:j\le-1),\qquad
\nu_r(a)=J(Q_0=a\mid\mathcal F_r).
\tag{8.1}
$$

Here $W_0=\epsilon$, $\mathcal F_0$ is trivial, and the fields are completed under $J$. They increase with $r$ and generate $\mathcal F_\infty$ up to completion. Posterior vectors are probability-vector versions, chosen simultaneously for the countably many radii.

Optional randomization means adjoining a seed $U$ independent of the entire joint process $\sigma(H_t,X_t:t\in\mathbb Z)$, with product law $J^U=J\otimes\mathcal L(U)$. Put $\mathcal G_r=\mathcal F_r\vee\sigma(U)$, completed under $J^U$; without a seed take $U$ constant. A stopped classifier consists of a discrete stopping time $\tau\in\mathbb N_0\cup\{\infty\}$ for $(\mathcal G_r)$ and a returned class $A$, with $\{\tau=r,A=a\}\in\mathcal G_r$ for every finite $r$ and every class $a$. Its zero-error condition is

$$
J^U(\tau<\infty,\ A\ne Q_0)=0.
\tag{8.2}
$$

This permits abstention forever; an almost-surely finite classifier additionally requires $J^U(\tau<\infty)=1$. The fields in (8.1) specify mathematical information. Physical access to their coordinates requires an additional archive or access premise; these definitions supply neither an acquired archive, its resource cost, nor a deterministic $W_n$ transfer.

**Theorem 8.2 (Exact support of every finite-past posterior).** For every $r\ge1$ and every word $w$ with $J(W_r=w)>0$,

$$
\operatorname{supp}\nu_r(\cdot\mid W_r=w)
=\begin{cases}
\{F_0,F_1,F_2\},&w_r=0,\\
\{T_0,T_1,T_2\},&w_r=1.
\end{cases}
\tag{8.3}
$$

The symbols here are the quotient classes, with indices modulo three. At radius zero the support is all six classes. In particular no finite-radius posterior is Dirac on an event of positive probability.

Proof. Reuse the actual marked quotient $T_x$ of Proposition 3.2 and its strictly positive stationary masses $\pi$ from Proposition 4.2. For a positive word the posterior is exactly

$$
\nu_r(a\mid W_r=w)
=\frac{(\pi T_w)_a}{\pi T_w\mathbf1},\qquad
\pi T_w\mathbf1=J(W_r=w)>0.
$$

By Proposition 4.2 these positive words are precisely the legal no11 words. The last transition in (4.1) makes their final phase free for a last zero and tail for a last one, irrespective of the initial phase. Thus no other phase can enter the support.

Let $k(w)$ be the number of ones in $w$, reduced modulo three. From every initial free class $F_z$, every legal word has a positive path: a zero from a free class has probability $1-p_z>0$, a one has probability $p_z>0$, and the required zero after a one is certain. Each one increments the residue by one; each zero preserves it. For any desired final residue $b$, start in $F_{b-k(w)}$. Its positive stationary mass and positive path give strictly positive mass to the class of the prescribed final phase and residue $b$. This proves all three inclusions in (8.3). For $r=0$, $\nu_0=\pi$ has all six positive entries. Null words receive no quotient or division and affect none of these conclusions. $\square$

**Theorem 8.3 (No positive-probability finite zero-error return).** For the fixed law and target of Definition 8.1, every stopped classifier, including one using the independent seed, satisfies

$$
J^U(\tau<\infty)>0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
J^U(\tau<\infty,\ A\ne Q_0)>0.
\tag{8.4}
$$

More locally, if $E\in\mathcal G_r$ has positive probability for a finite $r$, any $\mathcal G_r$-measurable class choice on $E$ has positive error mass on $E$. Consequently an almost-surely finite zero-error stopped classifier does not exist.

Proof. The elementary finite-target criterion underlying this application is as follows. For a fixed finite-valued target $Y$, an increasing discrete filtration $(\mathcal A_r)_{r\ge0}$, and probability-vector posterior versions $p_r(a)=\Pr(Y=a\mid\mathcal A_r)$, set

$$
D_r=\{\max_a p_r(a)=1\}.
$$

An almost-surely finite zero-error stopped classifier exists if and only if

$$
\Pr\left(\bigcup_{r\ge0}D_r\right)=1.
$$

The same equivalence holds after adjoining a seed independent of $\sigma(Y,\bigcup_r\mathcal A_r)$. This criterion is a conditional-probability intermediate step, not an extra synchronization assumption.

To prove necessity, independence leaves the posterior unchanged after adjoining the seed. For each return event $E_{r,a}=\{\tau=r,A=a\}$, conditional expectation gives

$$
\Pr(E_{r,a},Y\ne a)
=\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{E_{r,a}}(1-p_r(a))].
$$

Zero error forces this nonnegative integrand to vanish almost surely. Thus $E_{r,a}\subseteq D_r$ up to a null set. The countable union over radii and the finite union over labels contain $\{\tau<\infty\}$, proving necessity when that event has probability one. For sufficiency, take the first radius with $D_r$, and return the unique label with posterior one. The first-hit rule is a stopping time since $D_r\in\mathcal A_r$; it is finite almost surely by the displayed union condition. The same conditional-expectation identity on each return event proves zero error. A seed is unnecessary for this construction.

In the present application, independence of $U$ from the whole joint law gives

$$
J^U(Q_0=a\mid\mathcal G_r)=\nu_r(a)
\quad\text{almost surely}.
$$

Indeed the conditional-expectation identity holds on intersections of an $\mathcal F_r$ event and a seed event by independence, and extends to $\mathcal G_r$. Theorem 8.2 makes $1-\nu_r(a)>0$ almost surely for every class $a$ at every finite radius. Hence for any $\mathcal G_r$-measurable class choice $C$ and positive $E\in\mathcal G_r$,

$$
J^U(E,C\ne Q_0)
=\mathbb E_{J^U}[\mathbf1_E(1-\nu_r(C))]>0.
$$

If the total return probability is positive, at least one finite-radius return event has positive probability, so this local statement proves (8.4). Equivalently, every $D_r$ in the fixed-target criterion is null here. Positivity of these integrals does not give a lower bound independent of the radius or return rule. $\square$

**Corollary 8.4 (Vanishing error without a finite certificate, and synchronization scope).** Let $m_r(W_r)$ be the fixed-order MAP choices of (2.2). For the same fixed law,

$$
0<\epsilon_Q(r)\le\tfrac52\bar\rho^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor},\qquad
\bar\rho=\sqrt{15}/4<1,\qquad
\sum_{r\ge0}\epsilon_Q(r)<\infty,
\tag{8.5}
$$

and

$$
J\bigl(\exists R<\infty\ \forall r\ge R:\ m_r(W_r)=Q_0\bigr)=1.
\tag{8.6}
$$

The posterior vectors converge almost surely to the Dirac vector at $Q_0$, although no finite-radius posterior is Dirac. Over all almost-surely finite stopped classifiers in Definition 8.1, the infimum of error probability is zero and is not attained. The actual six-state quotient is nonexact in the sense of [Travers–Crutchfield, Exact Synchronization, Definitions 5–8][TCe].

Proof. The strict positivity in (8.5) follows by integrating $1-\max_a\nu_r(a)>0$ from Theorem 8.2. Its upper bound and summability are the already proved Theorem 3.3 with the parameters of Proposition 4.3; (8.6) is the fixed-$Q_0$ eventual-correctness conclusion used in Theorem 4.4. That theorem also makes $Q_0$ measurable in $\mathcal F_\infty$, so bounded martingale convergence yields $\nu_r(a)\to\mathbf1_{\{Q_0=a\}}$ almost surely for each class $a$.

A deterministic return at radius $r$ with label $m_r(W_r)$ is an almost-surely finite stopped classifier. Its error tends to zero by (8.5), proving the zero infimum. Theorem 8.3 excludes its attainment by any almost-surely finite stopped classifier, randomized or otherwise. At a fixed radius, independent randomization cannot improve the MAP error, since the conditional error of any class choice is at least $1-\max_a\nu_r(a)$. Thus there is no positive error floor uniform over finite radii or almost-surely finite return rules. The samplewise finite stabilization radius in (8.6) supplies no observable zero-error stopping certificate.

For the synchronization statement, Propositions 3.2 and 4.2 give a strongly connected, stationary, row-unifilar quotient with probabilistically distinct states, satisfying [TCe, Definition 5][TCe]. The free classes $F_0,F_1$ have the same allowed finite-word language: all legal no11 words. On every common word their residues each increase by $k(w)$ modulo three, so they never reach the same class. This pair is neither topologically distinct nor path convergent. The exact characterization in [TCe, §IV.1 Theorem 3][TCe] therefore identifies this quotient as nonexact; (8.3) also directly shows that it has no synchronizing word. The distinction between exact and asymptotic synchronization is mature theory, as are the nonexact state-uncertainty estimates of [Travers–Crutchfield, Asymptotic Synchronization, §VI Theorem 1 and §VII.1 Proposition 3][TCa]. The exact support and stopped-classification consequences are elementary source-specific synthesis from (4.1).

Stationarity identifies the distribution at each separate radius of $(Q_0,X_{-r:-1})$ with that of $(Q_r,X_{0:r-1})$. The nested past fields estimate one fixed target $Q_0$; forward filtering instead estimates the moving target $Q_r$. Equality of these separate distributions does not identify the refinements pathwise or transport a stopping rule. It also does not recover an initial forward F/T phase, whose overlapping future laws are retained in Theorem 4.4. Increasing mathematical past windows require the access premise of Definition 8.1 for a physical procedure.

These conclusions concern exactly the fixed twelve-state law and its quotient, with no attainment premise. The supported-word guard is Theorem 5.1's law-level statement; the stopped-classification obstruction concerns this fixed actual quotient. The original finite-attainment and all-$N$ separation alternatives of Open Problem 6.1, and its general nonunifilar and uniform-rate residuals, remain unresolved by this fixed-target stopping result. $\square$
