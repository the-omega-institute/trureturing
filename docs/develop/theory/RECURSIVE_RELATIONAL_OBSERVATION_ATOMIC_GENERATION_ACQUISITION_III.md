## 2. Native material, the fixed law and acquired context

**Definition 2.1 (persistent material and legal edits).** Use the LAB
construction of [Atomic Generation and Acquisition, §§17 and 20](RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_ATOMIC_GENERATION_ACQUISITION.md),
abbreviated AA, and [its continuation, §84](RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_ATOMIC_GENERATION_ACQUISITION_II.md),
abbreviated AAII. A logical term is

$$
t::=\alpha\mid\beta\mid\langle t,t\rangle.
$$

Logical addresses are finite words in $L,R$, with root $\varepsilon$.
A logical alpha is one material vertex. A logical beta is its root with a
terminal left marker. A branch has its two ordered logical subtrees.
The marker is material, but is not a logical child available for addressed
growth. The reachable terms form $\mathcal D$: no logical alpha leaf has
an $L$-ending address. AA Theorem 17.2 proves this exact reachability
criterion; AA Definition 17.3 proves the support/boundary parsing rule.

At a logical alpha, $P_p$ changes alpha to beta and adds only $pL$.
At a logical beta, $S_p$ changes beta to $\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle$,
retains $p,pL$, and adds only $pLL,pR$. Thus old vertices and edges persist.
Write $V_t$ for the material address support and $A_t$ for its unit
undirected adjacency matrix. An enabled edit $g:t\to t'$ has the actual
zero extension $\iota_g:\mathbb Q^{V_t}\to\mathbb Q^{V_{t'}}$.
All fresh fields are zero. The auxiliary history tapes of AA §19 are not
installed.

Let $b(t)$ be the number of rooted childless material vertices, let
$N(t)=|V_t|$, and put $I(t)=N(t)-b(t)$. If a legal successful word has $P$
promotions and $S$ splits, its length $n$ and present sizes satisfy

$$
n=P+S,\qquad N=1+P+2S,\qquad b=1+S,\qquad I=n.
\tag{2.1}
$$

These are AAII (84.3). A rooted terminal is not an unrooted leaf: alpha
has its root as terminal, and beta has its unary marker as terminal.

**Definition 2.2 (the known rational law).** In both installations the
field law on each current tree is

$$
T_t=\frac{4\operatorname{Id}+A_t}{8},\qquad \lambda=\frac78.
\tag{2.2}
$$

There is no autonomous field evolution. A requested Tick applies $T_t$;
Read and Hold do not. AAII Proposition 86.1, using the degree-three bound,
supplies rational invertibility and

$$
\frac18\operatorname{Id}\le T_t\le\frac78\operatorname{Id},
\qquad \|T_ty\|_\infty\le\lambda\|y\|_\infty.
\tag{2.3}
$$

The second inequality also follows directly from the nonnegative row sums
of (2.2), and holds for every real vector. In particular
$\|T_t^{-1}y\|_\infty\ge\lambda^{-1}\|y\|_\infty$.
Positive diagonal and edge entries have the following supplied consequence:
one Tick after a zero extension of a strictly positive old field is
strictly positive everywhere. Each fresh vertex has an old positive
neighbor, including both fresh vertices of a split. We use exactly the
fresh-support mechanism of AAII §85.

**Assumption 2.3 (an actual acquired installation).** The observer has an
actually allocated known alpha, its genuine root/service/return bindings,
and exclusive current authority over its source and records. The initial
field is zero. It has a source-independent finite controller and the actual
input, output, work arenas and fresh-material provider needed for each
asserted finite execution. Fresh source vertices return or acquire the
actual references and rights used by the installed dispatcher. Address
syntax is interpreted by traversing those bindings; a numeral is never
cast into a source reference.

The controller earns the current term and terminal/ordinal correspondence
by AAII Proposition 84.4. Starting from alpha, it consumes each actual
complete Growth reply and performs precisely the persistent edit in
Definition 2.1. At $P_p$, its terminal roster replaces $p$ by $pL$; at
$S_p$, it replaces the old marker $pL$ by the ordered pair $(pLL,pR)$.
Other terminals retain their relative order. The resulting current code
does not record a serial ordering of independent old events. Terminal
ordinals always dispatch to these actual current terminals.

At a historical handoff the generation word, gap counters, earlier field
predictor, old cursors, order-dependent auxiliary records and any readable
input/output archive are absent from the recovery input. They are cleared
or isolated with their actual backing and access costs charged. The earned
current term/roster, known law, live bindings and rights remain. No source-
dependent program, old-order advice, allocation-name channel, capacity
channel or timing observation supplies the missing history. This is a
declared acquisition task, not an assertion that a machine which retained
all its old input has forgotten it automatically.

There is no concurrent writer, crash, interrupt, revocation or observable
resource-exhaustion branch. Every completed operation quantified below
has sufficient actual finite backing and current rights. This does not
assert one fixed-capacity machine for unbounded histories. A model making
capacity, timing, exhaustion, raw names or changing rights observable must
include them in its state and task; the content kernels below do not
silently extend to that model.

**Definition 2.4 (seed actually prepared).** At the acquired alpha execute
Reset, Add$(1,1)$, the specified verifying Read, and Hold, consuming each
complete reply and lifecycle. In A the verifying exact Read returns $1$.
In C it is Read$(1,1)$ with the digital interpretation in §8 and returns
$2$ or $3$. Both prepare the nominal seed $1$ under the known installed
law. This is paid preparation on a known alpha, not the recovery of an
unknown seed, an unknown law or an unknown previous source.

## 3. Installation A: raw clocks and separated histories

**Definition 3.1 (complete raw service).** Installation A exposes raw
growth: a successful $g:t\to t'$ sends $x$ to $\iota_gx$ and returns
Growth$(g,p)$, with no attached Tick. Its field menu is Reset, Add$(j,q)$,
Tick, Read$(j)$ and Hold, for integer $j$ and rational $q$.
Reset writes zero everywhere; a valid Add changes the actual current
terminal coordinate by $q$; Tick applies (2.2); Read returns the literal
current rational terminal field; Hold is the identity. Reset, Add, Tick
and Hold return their fixed acknowledgements. The remaining complete
branches and lifecycle are specified in §7 and apply on all rational
fields, including fields outside the historical image.

**Definition 3.2 (the entire separated history domain).** Write $K$ for an
actually completed requested Tick. Let $\mathcal H_A$ contain precisely

$$
h=K^{q_0}g_1K^{q_1}\cdots g_nK^{q_n},
\qquad n\ge1,
\tag{3.1}
$$

where $g_1\cdots g_n$ is any legal successful addressed native word from
alpha, $q_0,q_n\in\mathbb N$, and $q_i\ge1$ for $1\le i<n$.
It also contains $h=K^{q_0}$ for $n=0$, with $q_0\in\mathbb N$.
There is only one gap in that latter case. Put $Q(h)=\sum_{i=0}^nq_i$
for $n\ge1$, and $Q(h)=q_0$ for $n=0$. No bound on $n,Q$ or the individual
gaps is supplied to the decoder. They are finite, not necessarily known.

Starting at the actually prepared scalar $1$, execute precisely those raw
growths and Ticks in their indicated order. Write $F_A(h)=(t(h),x_h)$ for
the resulting pair. For example, the prefix just before $g_i$ is actually
on the predecessor tree; $g_i$ zero-extends that very field, and its following
$q_i$ Ticks use the new tree. This recurrence defines $x_h$ without any
physical inverse. Reset or Add does not occur in (3.1).

Reads, Holds, refused probes, frame reception and waiting are not letters
of Time. Inserting them with no Tick leaves $F_A(h)$ unchanged; their
literal outputs or elapsed durations are not claimed recoverable.
They still have their actual costs if performed.

**Observation 3.3 (positivity at every old side of a birth).** In a history
of Definition 3.2 the field immediately before every growth is strictly
positive on every current vertex. At a first growth it is the positive
scalar $2^{-q_0}$. Between two growths the first of the required positive
number of Ticks fills every fresh cell positively by Definition 2.2;
subsequent Ticks preserve positivity. Thus immediately after any raw
growth its zeros are exactly its fresh support. All coordinates throughout
are in $[0,1]$, and the field is nonzero. This is the supplied positivity
argument applied at the separated cuts, not a fixed one-clock assumption.

## 4. A: unknown-gap inverse and total rational image recognition

**Theorem 4.1 (full inverse without knowing the gaps).** The map
$F_A:\mathcal H_A\to\coprod_{t\in\mathcal D}\mathbb Q^{V_t}$ is injective.
The following local exact procedure on its image recovers the unique
entire $h$, including all its gaps.

At a non-alpha pair $(t,y)$, repeatedly replace $y$ by $T_t^{-1}y$ while
$y$ is strictly positive in every coordinate, counting these inversions.
At the first zero coordinate stop. If the counter is $q$, the zeros are
exactly one of these two possibilities:

| Fresh zeros and current literal constructor | Recovered last event and collapse |
| --- | --- |
| $\{pL\}$ and a logical beta at $p$ | $P_p$; replace that beta by alpha and delete $pL$ |
| $\{pLL,pR\}$ and literally $\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle$ at $p$ | $S_p$; replace that branch by beta and delete $pLL,pR$, retaining $pL$ |

Restrict the stopped field to the retained predecessor vertices and repeat.
At alpha the scalar is $2^{-q_0}$; multiply by two until reaching $1$,
counting $q_0$. Reverse the recovered growths and attach their recovered
following gaps. For $n=0$ only this scalar operation is needed.

**Proof.** Let the actual last growth be $g:t'\to t$ and the actual final
gap be $q_n$. Its predecessor field $z$ is strictly positive by Observation
3.3, and

$$
y=T_t^{q_n}\iota_gz.
\tag{4.1}
$$

For $q_n=0$ it already has zeros exactly at the fresh support. For
$0\le r<q_n$, $T_t^{-r}y=T_t^{q_n-r}\iota_gz$ is strictly positive;
at $r=q_n$ it is exactly $\iota_gz$. Thus the first zero occurs after
exactly $q_n$ inversions, regardless of the size of this unknown gap.
No negative-coordinate branch arises on this image.

The zero support identifies $g$ by the same literal birth test as AAII
Theorem 85.1. In the singleton case its sole element fixes $p$. In the
pair case the unique $R$-ending member is $pR$, so fixes $p$, and the
other member must be $pLL$. The required literal local constructors
exclude a collapse of an arbitrary branch or of a unary marker. The
retained material and edges are exactly $V_{t'}$; restriction gives $z$.
Each collapse decreases $N$. Applying this argument to the actual
predecessor recovers its last growth and gap, until the actual alpha is
reached. At alpha, $T_\alpha=1/2$, so the scalar test recovers exactly
the original $q_0$. It includes $q_0=0$ and arbitrary endpoint gaps.

This proves that decoding an executed history returns that history.
Conversely each recorded reverse equation is (4.1) for the indicated
literal edit, and the final scalar equation is the actual alpha Tick
equation. Replaying the recovered history from $1$ therefore reconstructs
the input pair. All counters and last events were forced. Consequently
two histories with the same pair have the same decoded gaps and letters,
proving injectivity and both inverse identities on the full stated image.
The procedure acts on local descriptions, not on the source. $\square$

**Definition 4.2 (total rational recognizer).** A candidate input is a
finite logical term and a finite list of rational coordinates indexed by
its material support. An invalid finite term/support/coordinate encoding
is rejected. Check $t\in\mathcal D$, the exact support, $0\le y_v\le1$
for every vertex, and $y\ne0$. Maintain a flag which is outermost at the
first non-alpha stage and inner after its first successful collapse.

At a non-alpha stage set a local gap counter to zero. As long as every
coordinate is positive, apply the rational inverse $T_t^{-1}$ once and
increment the counter. After each step reject a negative coordinate, a
coordinate greater than one, or the zero vector. At the first zero
coordinate, require exactly one of the two complete fresh-support tests
in Theorem 4.1, including its literal local constructor. Require that the
retained coordinates are all strictly positive and the collapsed term is
in $\mathcal D$. At an inner non-alpha stage also require the counter
to be at least one. Collapse, restrict, set the flag to inner, and continue.

At alpha require a scalar $a\in(0,1]$. Repeatedly double it while it is
less than one. Accept exactly if it reaches one; reject if it exceeds one.
The doubling count is the initial gap. Return the decoded canonical
history on acceptance. The inner-gap test is never applied to the alpha
scalar: the initial gap is allowed to be zero. The outermost final gap
is also allowed to be zero.

**Theorem 4.3 (decidability, exact image and unique certificate).** The
procedure in Definition 4.2 terminates on every finite rational input.
It accepts precisely $\operatorname{im}F_A$, and its returned history is
the unique preimage. Membership in this numerical image does not certify
that a candidate was physically acquired or that its holder has access.

**Proof.** Invalid encodings and finite guard tests terminate. Every
non-alpha $T_t$ is a finite nonsingular rational matrix by (2.3), so
exact elimination computes its inverse. Consider one positive inversion
loop after all preceding tests have passed. If it neither finds a zero
nor rejects, all its vectors have norm at most one, but after $r$ iterations
their norms are at least $\lambda^{-r}\|y\|_\infty$. Since the starting
norm is positive and $\lambda<1$, this eventually exceeds one. Hence such
an infinite loop is impossible. There are at most $N-1$ successful
collapses. The positive alpha scalar also cannot double indefinitely
without reaching or exceeding one. This proves total termination,
including off-image positive rational inputs.

Every actual history passes the cube and nonzero checks by Observation
3.3, and passes the reverse steps by Theorem 4.1. At each inner non-alpha
stage its gap is one of $q_1,\ldots,q_{n-1}$ and hence positive. Its alpha
scalar passes. This proves completeness.

For soundness, suppose the procedure accepts. It has produced an alpha
scalar $2^{-q_0}$ and a finite reverse chain of legal literal collapses.
Read the chain forward. Each inverse collapse is exactly an enabled $P$
or $S$ with the zero extension of Definition 2.1. The stored field
equations are exactly $y=T_t^q\iota_gz$. The stored counters are
nonnegative; all counters between successive growths are positive by
the inner test. Thus the chain defines a history in $\mathcal H_A$.
The alpha equation and then these equations show that its actual
recurrence returns the accepted input. Uniqueness follows from Theorem
4.1. None of these algebraic checks produces a reference, a right or an
actual observation; the distinction in the statement follows. $\square$

**Boundary 4.4 (why separation and the known origin matter).** Without
separation the first reverse zero support can contain several births.
More decisively, with no Ticks the two distinct legal words

$$
P_\varepsilon S_\varepsilon S_LP_R,
\qquad P_\varepsilon S_\varepsilon P_RS_L
\tag{4.2}
$$

end at the same term $\langle\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,\beta\rangle$
and the same raw zero-extended field: one at the original root and zero
elsewhere. Thus Theorem 4.1 does not extend to all zero internal gaps.
An unknown scalar seed also defeats the initial-gap inverse: seed $1$
with one alpha Tick and seed $1/2$ with none both give $1/2$.
Starting from zero, or applying Reset at the common final term, likewise
identifies the two orders in (4.2). These are actual collisions; they
do not rule out a different task with extra records or preparation.

## 5. A: paid boundary acquisition and the four exact routes

**Construction 5.1 (the existing forcing supplier on the earned tree).**
At a handoff after any $h\in\mathcal H_A$, let $t=t(h)$, $N=N(t)$,
$b=b(t)$, $I=I(t)=n$. The terminal address antichain $B_0$ and its actual
order were earned from the known alpha and actual Growth replies. Its
prefix closure is $V_t$ and arity parses $t$, including alpha and beta.
Let $J=[e_{\ell_1},\ldots,e_{\ell_b}]$ in that actual order, and set

$$
O_I=\begin{bmatrix}J^{\mathsf T}\\J^{\mathsf T}T_t\\
\vdots\\J^{\mathsf T}T_t^I\end{bmatrix}.
\tag{5.1}
$$

AAII Proposition 87.2 supplies a computed rational $D_I$ with
$D_IO_I=\operatorname{Id}$. Its hypotheses are exactly the earned rooted
tree, symmetric law (2.2) and terminal $J$ here. The supplied deepest-
unmarked-vertex forcing equation, in these parameters, is

$$
e_u=8T_te_v-4e_v-\sum_{\substack{z\sim v\\z\ne u}}e_z.
\tag{5.2}
$$

It constructs all coordinate expressions in at most $I$ additions of
vertices to the marked set, giving the asserted finite matrix. This is
a use of that already proved construction, not a new observability or
root-recognition theorem. At alpha $I=0,D_0=\operatorname{Id}$; beta
uses its one unary-marker step.

**Construction 5.2 (one actual assay).** Hold and Read all $b$ current
terminal ordinals in their earned order. For $r=1,\ldots,I$, actually
Tick once, consume its acknowledgement, Hold, and Read all $b$ terminals.
Consume all complete outputs, End/local acknowledgement/Return and
cleanup. No extra field action interleaves. The literal sampled block,
computed historical field, and physically returned field are respectively

$$
Y=O_Ix_h,\qquad x_h=D_IY,\qquad x_{\rm now}=T_t^Ix_h.
\tag{5.3}
$$

The equality for $Y$ follows epoch by epoch from the actually performed
Ticks and identity Reads/Holds. The left-inverse equation gives the
middle equality; the actual $I$ Ticks give the last. No source restoration
is performed. Retain the genuine current binding, code/roster and rights,
and compute the present autonomous prediction block

$$
m_{\rm now}=O_Ix_{\rm now}.
\tag{5.4}
$$

This block predicts the next $I+1$ nominal terminal epochs from the
present cut, conditional on those autonomous Ticks. Computing it does
not secretly execute them.

**Definition 5.3 (the common acquired image in A).** For the actual run
consisting of the paid seed, $h$, and Construction 5.2, expose:

| View | Independent exposed content at the designated cut |
| --- | --- |
| Space | current ordered material $V_t$ and its actual complete field $x_{\rm now}$ |
| Time | the actual finite successful-generation/Tick history $h$ before the designated assay |
| Boundary | earned current terminal antichain $B_0$ and the literal sampled block $Y$ in epoch/ordinal order |
| Memory | earned present term/roster and present autonomous prediction block $m_{\rm now}$ with its fixed interpreter |

The seed, law, assay convention and callable context are common parameters.
Time is an observation of what happened, not an additional input handed
to the Boundary decoder. Space has no birth-time labels. Boundary has
actual past values, not access to every possible response. Memory has
predictions for a particular autonomous window, not an old-word encoding
inserted by definition. The restricted recurrence makes these independent
contents interconvertible. Actual allocation-name spellings are not part
of these numerical/address observations; live references remain actual.
These are descriptions retained or reconstructed by the acquired observer,
not extra source ports. In particular no direct internal-field reader or
history oracle is added to obtain Space or Time.

**Theorem 5.4 (all routes, round trips and current-task kernel in A).** On
the entire image of Definition 5.3 each view recovers the other three and
the unique $h$. The following are finite explicit routes.

From Time, evaluate the actual recurrence in Definition 3.2 locally from
$1$, then (5.1), (5.3), (5.4) and the terminal prefix rule. From Boundary,
parse $t$ from $B_0$, construct $D_I$, compute $D_IY$, and use Theorem
4.1. From Space, parse $t$ and compute $T_t^{-I}x_{\rm now}$ before
Theorem 4.1. From Memory, compute $D_Im_{\rm now}=x_{\rm now}$ and use
the Space route. Each round trip is the identity on its stated image,
and a route through any intermediate view equals the direct route.

Within the fixed context of Assumption 2.3, unrestricted finite complete
current service in A has kernel exactly equality of $(t,x_{\rm now})$.
Consequently all four views on this image have that same kernel, and it
distinguishes all different $h\in\mathcal H_A$.

**Proof.** The terminal prefix rule and the supplied $D_IO_I=\operatorname{Id}$
give the stated Boundary and Memory calculations. Invertibility gives
$T_t^{-I}T_t^Ix_h=x_h$. Theorem 4.1 then returns exactly the original
$h$, and its replay identity reconstructs the same historical pair and
the same acquired/current blocks. This proves every direction, rather
than presupposing an inverse to define a view.

For explicit composition, write $E_i(h)$ for the independent content in
row $i$ and $R_i$ for its displayed decoder. We proved
$R_iE_i(h)=h$ for every legal finite $h$. The route $i\to j$ is the
already specified calculation $E_jR_i$. Thus on the joint image
$E_kR_jE_jR_i=E_kR_i$, and $E_iR_i$ is identity on row $i$'s image.
These equations recover the literal ordered $Y$, but do not manufacture
a second physical measurement or any extra intervening journal.

For the service kernel, §7 proves that equal current $(t,x)$ has equal
complete outputs and matched successors for every common finite backed
adaptive continuation. Conversely two distinct addressed terms have a
first differing logical constructor. A $P$ request there separates
alpha/beta/branch by Growth, wrong-type refusal or branch refusal.
If the terms agree and fields differ, (5.1) has zero kernel, so some
terminal Read after $r\le I$ actual Ticks separates the two fields.
These are legal complete finite continuations. Finally, for fixed $t$,
$T_t^I$ is injective and Theorem 4.1 distinguishes the historical pairs.
Transferring through the established routes proves the last assertion.
Outside this image different pasts can have identical current service,
as (4.2) and Reset demonstrate. $\square$

## 6. A: actual repeated rounds and lawful selected growth

**Theorem 6.1 (sliding actual acquisition).** Starting at any acquired cut
of Definition 5.3, actually Tick once, Hold, and Read all $b$ terminals.
Drop the oldest epoch of the retained $Y$ and append these actual new
responses. The new four views are exactly the acquired views for $hK$,
and the actual source is $T_t x_{\rm now}$. This operation can be repeated
any finite number of times with its actual backing and costs.

**Proof.** The retained old epochs are $J^{\mathsf T}T_t^rx_h$ for
$r=0,\ldots,I$. After the operation the retained epochs are those for
$r=1,\ldots,I+1$, exactly $O_IT_tx_h$. The current source is
$T_t^{I+1}x_h=T_t^I(T_tx_h)$. The new historical recurrence is $hK$,
which changes only the permitted final gap, or the sole alpha gap.
It remains in $\mathcal H_A$. Applying §5 to this block gives precisely
its routes and prediction. The actual last Read block was consumed, so
the new boundary is genuinely sampled. Induction proves finite repetition.
$\square$

**Theorem 6.2 (raw growth followed by a fresh actual assay).** At any such
cut, let $g:t\to t'$ be any actually selected enabled raw growth. Execute
it, consume Growth, earn the new roster, and run a fresh assay on $t'$ of
length $I'=I+1$. The new history represented by that acquired cut is

$$
H=hK^Ig,
\qquad x_H=\iota_gT_t^Ix_h,
\qquad x'_{\rm now}=T_{t'}^{I+1}\iota_gT_t^Ix_h.
\tag{6.1}
$$

It is a member of $\mathcal H_A$, and all routes in §5 apply to it.
Thus any finite combination of the sliding operations of Theorem 6.1
and these growth/reassay operations has a proved acquired-image meaning
and an actually advanced returned state.

**Proof.** The old assay has already executed $I$ real Ticks. The raw
growth acts on that advanced field, yielding the middle expression in
(6.1). If there was a previous growth, $n=I\ge1$, so the gap before
the appended growth is the old final gap plus $I$ and is positive.
If $n=0$, there is no internal gap to satisfy; a first growth after any
number of alpha Ticks is allowed. Thus $H\in\mathcal H_A$ in both
cases, even when the old final gap was zero. The added growth increases
$N-b$ by one by (2.1). Its fresh assay therefore has length $I+1$ and
the actual returned field in (6.1). Construction 5.2 now proves the new
literal block and all routes. Each finite repetition satisfies the same
argument on its current historical representative. $\square$

The distinction between $H=hK^Ig$ and $hg$ is essential. It accounts
for Ticks which really occurred during acquisition. This repeated-round
closure is a result for raw-clock A, not an assertion about clocked C.

**Construction 6.3 (selection from acquired content, followed by a real
call).** Recover $h$ by any route of §5. If it has a growth, take its
last growth address $p$; otherwise take $p=\varepsilon$. In the earned
present logical code start at $p$ and descend left while the constructor
is a branch. This finite descent ends at a logical leaf $q$. Submit $P_q$
if it is alpha, or $S_q$ if it is beta. The address $p$ still exists,
because the persistent edits never delete its logical position. Thus
the selected call is enabled. The finite selector uses the acquired
history and earned code, not a new Tag port or an external unknown
selection oracle.

The actual dispatcher traverses its genuine root links to $q$, checks
the guard/rights, obtains the fresh material, performs the raw edit and
returns its complete Growth. Its immediate field is
$\iota_gx_{\rm now}$. If Theorem 6.2's fresh assay is also executed,
the returned state is exactly (6.1), with its additional costs. The
decoded history and selected address may then be cleared or retained;
their real widths and retained capacities are charged either way.

## 7. Complete service, actual framing and the broader current domain

**Definition 7.1 (complete framing and enabledness).** Use AAII Definitions
84.2 and 90.2's finite field/growth codecs, with the changed C Read frame
specified in §8. Field and growth have disjoint fixed dispatch. For
growth the address payload is $1^{|p|}0p$, preceded by its native tag;
the request has $2|p|+4$ bits. A Growth payload has $2|p|+5$ bits.
Absent, branch and wrong-type refusals are distinct five-bit replies.
An otherwise valid complete Add or Read frame with an out-of-roster
integer ordinal returns RangeRefusal and leaves the state unchanged.
Invalid complete frames, unused complete
tags, noncanonical rational/integer payloads and invalid C precision
return BadFrame with no source change. Negative zero is noncanonical.
Canonical syntax, payload validity and C precision validity are checked
before ordinal range. Growth navigation first tests existence of the
logical address: failure gives absent refusal; at an existing branch it
gives branch refusal; at a logical leaf the wrong requested type gives
wrong-type refusal, and the correct type enables the edit. Traversal
through a unary marker is not logical existence.

A proper incomplete frame retains its actual acquired prefix, parser
tag/subphase and outstanding length information; it waits for more input
and has no semantic reply. Missing authority, unavailable required backing
or an attempted call in a disabled phase does not create a fabricated
refusal. The completion statements quantify the enabled sufficiently
backed calls. A busy serialized call admits no interleaving. Logical
navigation tests arity and cannot enter a unary marker as a growth target.

Every received bit occupies a pending latch, is written into actual
request backing before the latch is released, and is parsed from that
backing. Navigation retains root, actual cursor and remaining address;
arithmetic retains its actual operands. Reservation precedes an edit.
While busy the controller retains its input, target, working field,
reply and pending subphase. It constructs and seals a finite complete
reply, emits all its bits and End, consumes the supplied local
acknowledgement, and performs Return and cleanup to the same root/dispatch
binding. The matching local code, roster and predictor successor are
committed before Ready and any next complete call. A predicted value
never substitutes for this actual exchange.

In C a successful growth is the existing $+$ adapter: actually acquire
the outward request, send/consume the raw growth transaction, execute
and consume one Tick on the new tree, and only then emit the outward
Growth. It pays the inner and outward lifecycles. On a refusal or
BadFrame it executes no Tick and forwards that complete response.
The adapter's busy lock also blocks field calls. There is no hidden
field Tick on A's raw growth.

**Theorem 7.2 (complete current continuation, exact in A and set-valued
in C).** Suppose a settled current state has any $t\in\mathcal D$ and

$(\widehat t,\widehat x)=(t,x)$, and in C the digital contract of §8.

maintains the following actual successors:

Proper prefixes and disabled calls have exactly Definition 7.1's

**Proof.** AAII Theorem 89.2 already proves this invariant and its
acquired-frame implementation for the exact field menu and the raw or

law (2.2), and C uses its actual $+$ row with that same law. Geometry,

## 8. Installation C: the explicitly changed digital Read

**Definition 8.1 (post-growth clocks and the digital endpoint).** C uses
the existing $+$ installation of AAII §84 with law (2.2), the actually
prepared alpha of Definition 2.4, and the actual post-growth-clock
adapter in Definition 7.1. A successful exposed growth has successor
$T_{t'}\iota_gx$; a refused growth has no Tick. The only changed field
command is Read. For an integer ordinal $j$ and a nonnegative integer
precision $p$, a valid Read$(j,p)$ returns the complete signed integer

$$
k=\lfloor 2^p x_{\ell_j}\rfloor+\zeta,
\qquad \zeta\in\{0,1\},
\tag{8.1}
$$

and leaves the field unchanged. The floor is the ordinary signed floor,
including on negative rational fields reached by later controls.
There is no truncation of the integer payload. Both choices of $\zeta$
are legal after every prefix at which that Read is enabled. Choices can
be arbitrary, correlated and adaptive to the entire execution. There is
no persistent hidden sensor state constraining the next legal choice,
and no additional timing, bit-provider or digitizer side channel.
This is a set-valued service contract, without a probability law.

The digital endpoint is installed directly; the observer has no exact
Read bypass. A device implementing (8.1) by first calling an inner exact
Read must charge that inner transaction as well. The stated direct
implementation instead charges the actual source access, exact shift/
division/floor arithmetic, bit latch/provider and finite signed output
backing. One actual nuisance bit is consumed for each valid Read.
Providing every legal bit choice is a premise about that provider, not
an inferred property of numerical reports.

More explicitly, a finite ideal backend uses the source's actual rational
cell representation $A/B$, $B>0$, at the ordinal terminal selected by
the authentic current dispatcher. Under its installed internal field-access
rights and busy lock it copies those integers into actual scratch, forms
$2^pA$, and performs signed floor division by $B$. Integer division with
remainder in $\{0,\ldots,B-1\}$ gives that quotient also for negative
$A$. It acquires one bit through its genuine provider binding, adds it,
and builds the complete signed integer reply before End/Return. It
performs no field write or Tick. Every finite $p,A,B$ requires finite
but charged scratch, digit operations and output backing. This realizes
the changed ideal primitive from those installed accesses; it neither
exposes an exact Read to the observer nor derives a physical sensor from
an algebraic resemblance.

Use the field codec of Definition 13.2, with an additional canonical
nonnegative integer payload for $p$. A negative or noninteger precision,
or a noncanonical precision frame, takes BadFrame without a source
change. Valid precision with an invalid integer ordinal takes RangeRefusal.
Proper prefixes, guards, acknowledgements, End and Return are as in §7.

**Definition 8.2 (pure C histories and robust algorithms).** Let
$\mathcal W$ be all finite legal successful addressed native words from
alpha, including the empty word. For $w\in\mathcal W$ of length $n$,
execute its successful clocked growths after the paid seed, with no
extra field interventions during encoding. Its field obeys the existing
post-clock recurrence

$$
x_\varepsilon=1,\qquad x_{wg}=T_{t(wg)}\iota_gx_w.
\tag{8.2}
$$

No old serial word or predictor is supplied at handoff. The current
geometry/terminal correspondence is earned as in Assumption 2.3, and
$n=I(t(w))$ is therefore known without knowing the old serial order.
All coordinates of $x_w$ are positive, its denominators divide $8^n$,
and $0<x_{w,v}\le\lambda^n$. These are AAII §§85 and 90 on exactly
this recurrence and seed.

A robust recovery policy is a causal algorithm whose next acquired
input, local computation and stopping/output decision depend only on
its installed program, earned context, its own optional fixed random
tape and actual received complete record. It may request any legal
finite controls in the complete C menu, including arbitrary rational
Adds, Reset, further growth, refused probes, Holds and extra Ticks.
It must return the original successful word $w$ correctly and terminate
after finitely many operations for every $w$ in its asserted domain and
every legal sequence of nuisance choices. A precision cap $B$ means
every valid Read in every such execution uses $p\le B$. Local and
provider storage, input generation and all additional calls remain paid.
Pointwise finite termination is required; no common stopping-time bound
is assumed. For a randomized policy the pathwise version requires these
properties for every fixed random tape. No almost-sure assertion is
substituted for this version.
The lower bound below applies even when the seed's precision-one Read is
excluded from the recovery policy's cap. A cap for the whole installed
run must also cover that seed Read; the attained upper bound includes it.

## 9. C: a lower bound against all capped adaptive controls

**Lemma 9.1 (complete-record coupling under a precision cap).** Fix the
same earned term, bindings and context in two C sources with respective
fields $x,x'$ and $\|x-x'\|_\infty\le2^{-B}$, where $B\in\mathbb N$.
For any deterministic causal policy using only $p\le B$, the two sources
have compatible executions with the same entire observed record and
the same policy decisions for as long as the policy continues. Every
actual nuisance report chosen in each source is legal there. This is
an intersection of compatible transcript sets, not equality of their
whole sets of possible transcripts.

**Proof.** Maintain equal current terms, the same acquired record and
policy state, and field difference norm at most $2^{-B}$. Equal records
give the same next input. A common valid Add cancels from the difference;
Reset makes it zero; Tick contracts it by (2.3). A common successful
growth has zero extension followed by a contracting new-tree Tick, so
also preserves the bound. Equal geometry gives the same roster, guards
and growth/refusal decisions. Hold, refusals, RangeRefusal and BadFrame
preserve the bound and have the same fixed complete response.

For a valid Read$(j,p)$ put $a=2^px_{\ell_j}$ and
$a'=2^px'_{\ell_j}$. Their difference has absolute value at most one.
Thus the integers $m=\lfloor a\rfloor$ and $m'=\lfloor a'\rfloor$
differ by at most one. The sets $\{m,m+1\}$ and $\{m',m'+1\}$
intersect. Select an integer in that intersection. In each source it is
of form (8.1), with a possibly different legal bit. Both bits are allowed
after the actual prefix by Definition 8.1, so this choice does not require
a nonexistent sensor history or a probability assumption. Emit the same
complete integer code in both executions. The fields do not change.

The prefix parser and finite busy-control mechanics also agree: acquired
input bits are the same, a proper prefix has the same pending behavior,
and equal complete response content has equal framing, End, local
acknowledgement and Return. C's inner growth and Tick replies are fixed
and agree as well. No independent timing or capacity observation is
part of the record. Therefore the invariant extends through every case.
Induction supplies a common record for every finite stage. If the policy
continues forever, choose an intersecting integer at each successive Read
(for example the least one). Since every such next choice is legal,
this defines compatible infinite executions too. $\square$

**Theorem 9.2 (necessary linear peak precision).** For every integer
$n\ge4$ and every integer cap $B\ge0$ satisfying

$$
B\le n\log_2(8/7),
\quad\text{equivalently}\quad \lambda^n\le2^{-B},
\tag{9.1}
$$

no policy of Definition 8.2 with that cap is correct and pointwise
terminating for all length-$n$ histories and every legal nuisance
sequence. This remains true with unbounded finite extra work/calls,
arbitrary adaptive rational Add, Reset, extra growth or Ticks, and
pathwise randomized policies. Hence a uniformly robust finite peak
cap sufficient at length $n\ge4$ must satisfy
$B>n\log_2(8/7)$.

**Proof.** The distinct words in (4.2) both have length four and final
term $\langle\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,\beta\rangle$. Append the same
legal suffix of length $n-4$ to both; such a suffix of every finite length
exists, by repeatedly extending a current logical leaf with its enabled
$P$ or $S$. The resulting two distinct words $u,v$ have the same final
term, the same earned code/roster and the same acquired origin. They
have no retained old-order advice at the handoff.

Each word has exactly $n$ actual clock Ticks. By Definition 8.2 both
fields lie coordinatewise in $(0,\lambda^n]$, so

$$
\|x_u-x_v\|_\infty\le\lambda^n\le2^{-B}.
\tag{9.2}
$$

Apply Lemma 9.1 to any proposed policy. Its compatible complete records
are identical, including every adaptive probe and termination decision.
If it terminates, it gives the same answer on these two records and so
is wrong on at least one of the distinct original words. If it does not
terminate, its compatible legal nuisance sequence violates pointwise
termination. Either contradicts the required universal guarantee.

For a pathwise randomized policy fix any of its tapes and apply the
same argument to the resulting deterministic policy. Requiring correctness
and termination for every tape cannot evade it. The argument establishes
neither an almost-sure error bound nor equality of full transcript
languages; it uses one legal common record against a worst-case
guarantee. The pair construction requires $n\ge4$; it makes no assertion
of this lower bound for $n<4$. $\square$

The inequality counts peak requested precision, not the number of Reads,
an arbitrary-field rank task, or total resource cost. Additional controls
may increase both field magnitudes enormously while their difference stays
small; the common Add cancellation in the proof is why that does not
invalidate the bound. Known rational dithers are covered as Adds.

## 10. C: actual exact extraction at linear peak precision

**Construction 10.1 (an executed precision schedule).** At the actual
post-encoding state $(t(w),x_w)$, let $n=I(t)$ and use the earned terminals
$J$ and the existing $O_n,D_n$ of Construction 5.1. Hold and Read every
terminal at epoch zero; then, for $r=1,\ldots,n$, actually Tick, Hold
and Read every terminal. At epoch $r$ use precision

$$
p_r=3(n+r)+1.
\tag{10.1}
$$

Retain the actual integer $k_{r,j}$ and extract the nominal sample by

$$
a_{r,j}=\left\lfloor\frac{k_{r,j}}2\right\rfloor,
\qquad y_{r,j}=\frac{a_{r,j}}{8^{n+r}}.
\tag{10.2}
$$

All calls, provider bits, integer inputs/outputs and the two distinct
raw/nominal records have actual backing. There is no exact numerical
read beyond (8.1).

**Theorem 10.2 (sufficiency for every finite word and every nuisance
sequence).** For every $n\ge0$, every legal $w$ of length $n$, and every
legal nuisance sequence during preparation and Construction 10.1,
the extracted nominal block is exactly

$$
Y=(y_{r,j})_{r,j}=O_nx_w.
\tag{10.3}
$$

The observer computes $x_w=D_nY$ and recovers the unique original $w$
by the already proved AAII Theorem 85.1. The actual physically returned
source, its retained current predictor and the peak precision are

$$
x_{\rm now}=T_t^nx_w,\qquad m_{\rm now}=O_nx_{\rm now},
\qquad \max p=6n+1.
\tag{10.4}
$$

This includes the empty word and the paid verifying Read of precision
one. Together with Theorem 9.2, the necessary and sufficient-order
peak precision for the declared robust task is linear in $n$; no sharp
leading constant is asserted.

**Proof.** Before sampling, (8.2) has exactly $n$ Ticks and no other
field inputs except the unit seed. After another $r$ actual Ticks, every
coordinate is an integer divided by $8^{n+r}$, by AAII Proposition 90.3.
Write the current terminal value as $a/8^{n+r}$ with $a\in\mathbb N$;
this common-denominator integer need not be a reduced numerator. Then
(10.1) gives $2^{p_r}x_{\ell_j}=2a$. Every actual report is therefore
$k=2a+\zeta$, with $\zeta=0$ or $1$. Equation (10.2) extracts exactly
$a$ for either choice. This argument is pointwise at every Read and
does not use independence, a noise average or a repeated sample at the
same precision. Thus it holds for every correlated adaptive nuisance
sequence permitted by the contract.

The actual Ticks and identity Reads/Holds yield (10.3) epoch by epoch.
The known earned rooted tree and law meet exactly AAII Proposition
87.2's hypotheses, so $D_nY=x_w$. Its Theorem 85.1 applies directly to
recurrence (8.2), to all legal addressed words and the actually prepared
unit seed. It recovers $w$ with both inverse identities. We do not
reprove or rename this fixed-clock inverse as a new result. The $n$
sampling Ticks give the actual state in (10.4); local multiplication
gives the current prediction block. The largest scheduled precision
is $p_n=6n+1$. When $n=0$, the terminal is alpha, no assay Tick occurs,
and precision one again extracts its scalar $1$ from $2$ or $3$.
The seed verifying Read is a separate precision-one call and does not
increase the maximum. All loops have finitely many steps for each
finite $w$, with finite exact integer and rational arithmetic. This
proves sufficiency and the stated termination. $\square$

**Boundary 10.3 (the arithmetic property doing the work).** The exact
extraction uses the known denominator lattice and the added single bit
of scale in (10.1): noiseless scaled values are even integers, and the
two possible reports occupy exactly their adjacent integer pair.
An arbitrary real bounded-error sensor, an unknown rational denominator,
a different noise alphabet, a persistent hidden sensor or unknown Tick
gaps does not have this proved property. In particular this upper bound
does not give finite-noise recovery of A with unknown $Q$, and the
lower bound is not a lower bound for every bounded-error measurement
model. The exact rational source still has growing precision internally.

## 12. C: complete current language, real use and dynamics limits

**Theorem 12.1 (the exact current-service language kernel).** In the
fixed C context with every finite precision allowed, two settled rational
states have the same complete trace language for all common finite
sufficiently backed adaptive continuations if and only if their current
addressed terms and fields agree. On Definition 11.1's image the nominal
four views therefore have the same current-task kernel, which separates
every two distinct successful words.

**Proof.** If $(t,x)=(t',x')$, the deterministic branches and successors
in Theorem 7.2 agree, and every digital Read has exactly the same
two-element response set. There is no additional hidden sensor state.
For any allowed next integer in either execution the other admits it
after its matching prefix. Induction on input/output events gives the
same full finite trace language, including proper prefixes, malformed
frames, refusals, actual adapter replies and Return. The controller
predicts this set and consumes the actual member, not an invisible bit.

If terms differ, the first-constructor $P$ test in Theorem 5.4 gives
different deterministic growth/refusal responses. If terms agree and
$x-x'\ne0$, the forcing left inverse gives a terminal $j$ and $r\le I$
with $\delta=(T_t^r(x-x'))_{\ell_j}\ne0$. Actually perform those $r$
Ticks in both sources. Choose a finite nonnegative integer $p$ so large
that $2^p|\delta|>2$. The two floor integers then differ by at least
two, so their adjacent report pairs in (8.1) are disjoint. This fixed
finite continuation has disjoint possible final integer replies and
distinguishes the languages. It uses unrestricted finite precision,
and therefore does not contradict the capped common-record lower bound.

On the sampled pure image, $T_t^n$ is injective and AAII Theorem 85.1
is injective in $w$. Theorem 11.2 transfers that equality kernel to all
four nominal views. Literal nuisance journals can still differ at the
same kernel point as proved there. $\square$

Pair separation with unrestricted finite precision is not an algorithm
with a finite stopping certificate for an arbitrary unknown rational
field. The actual exact acquisition in §10 uses the smaller known-lattice
history domain; Theorem 12.1 does not enlarge that acquisition claim.

**Construction 12.2 (an acquired selector actually executed in C).**
Recover $w$ by §11. Take its last growth address, or the root for the
empty word, and descend left in the earned present logical code until
a leaf, just as in Construction 6.3. Its type chooses an enabled $P$
or $S$. Submit that real addressed clocked call using the current
binding/rights, consume the inner Growth, new-tree Tick acknowledgement
and full outward Growth/Return. Its actual successor is

$$
(t',T_{t'}\iota_gx_{\rm now})
=(t',T_{t'}\iota_gT_t^nx_w).
\tag{12.1}
$$

The complete current predictor in Theorem 7.2 follows this actual source
and predicts every later set-valued Read. The selector is a finite
calculation on acquired content and actual code; it is not an uninstalled
choice oracle or a historical Next port.

**Proposition 12.3 (which dynamics commute).** Before the assay, appending
an actual clocked event obeys (8.2); the fixed-clock inverse therefore
decodes that endpoint as $wg$. After the assay, the selected step is
(12.1), while the endpoint of a separately encoded $wg$ followed by
its own designated assay would be

$$
T_{t'}^{n+2}\iota_gx_w.
\tag{12.2}
$$

These formulas describe different executions. No identity between them
or closure of the pure sampled image under arbitrary post-assay controls
is asserted. A's Theorem 6.2 cannot be imported to remove these clocks.

**Proof.** The actual successful C step includes one new-tree Tick,
giving (8.2) and (12.1) at their respective starting fields. A newly
encoded $wg$ has field $T_{t'}\iota_gx_w$ and $I(t')=n+1$, so its
own assay adds $n+1$ Ticks and gives (12.2). The published fixed-clock
inverse applies at the former pure endpoints, and Theorem 7.2 applies
at the actual current endpoints. Nothing in these equations identifies
the two runs. For a concrete inequality take $w=\varepsilon$ and
$g=P_\varepsilon$: (12.1) is $T_\beta(1,0)^{\mathsf T}=(1/2,1/8)^{\mathsf T}$,
whereas (12.2) is $T_\beta^2(1,0)^{\mathsf T}=(17/64,1/8)^{\mathsf T}$.
Thus even this first post-assay growth need not be the separately
sampled $wg$ state. $\square$

Later Reset or arbitrary Add can likewise leave the historical image.
Current set-valued service remains valid there for all rational states;
the pure-word historical theorem is not extended by that fact. No
old journal, nuisance chronology, entry restoration or busy-cut inverse
is hidden inside the continued-service claim.

## 13. Same-run calls, widths, traffic and local work

**Proposition 13.1 (attained call vectors).** For A, execute exactly the
paid seed, $h\in\mathcal H_A$ with $n$ growths and $Q$ Ticks, and the
assay of length $I=n$. For C, execute exactly the paid seed, a word of
length $n$, and Construction 10.1. With final $b,N,I$ as in (2.1), the
same-run counts are:

| Coordinate through the completed assay | A | C |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reset | $1$ | $1$ |
| Add | $1$ | $1$ |
| Read | $1+b(I+1)$ exact calls | $1+b(n+1)$ digital calls |
| Tick, including actual inner clocks | $Q+I$ | $2n$ |
| Hold | $I+2$ | $n+2$ |
| successful growth transactions | $n$ raw | $n$ inner raw and $n$ outward |
| fixed field acknowledgement payloads | $Q+2I+4$ | $3n+4$ |
| actual nuisance bits for valid Reads | none | $1+b(n+1)$ |

Every complete transaction also has its actual End, local acknowledgement,
Return and cleanup. These are not included in the field-acknowledgement
row. The total number of A semantic calls, counting one raw growth as
one call, is $n+Q+2I+b(I+1)+5$.

For each actual refused probe, add one raw transaction in A and one
inner plus one outward transaction in C, with no Tick. Optional Reads,
Holds, waits, extra controls and record exports add their literal costs.
An A sliding operation adds one Tick, one Hold and $b$ Reads. An A
growth/reassay on a new tree with $I'=I+1$ adds one raw growth, $I'$
Ticks, $I'+1$ Holds and $b'(I'+1)$ Reads. The selected immediate C
growth adds one inner and one outward growth transaction and one
new-tree Tick/acknowledgement. These are distinct actual continuations;
one cannot use a cheaper coordinate from a different one.

**Proof.** The seed contributes one Reset, Add, verifying Read and Hold.
An assay of length $d$ contributes $d$ Ticks, $d+1$ Holds and $b(d+1)$
Reads. A has its actual $Q$ historical Ticks and $n$ raw growths; C has
one real inner Tick and two actual growth transactions per successful
letter. Summing gives every table row, including the empty history with
$N=b=1$ and $I=n=0$. The acknowledgements are exactly Reset, Add,
Tick and Hold replies. The stated deltas follow by the same count on
the actual successor trees and the operations in §§6 and 12. $\square$

**Definition 13.2 (a literal finite codec).** Use AAII Definition 90.2's
codec. Write $\ell(k)$ for the binary length of a nonnegative integer,
with $\ell(0)=0$. A canonical signed integer $z$ consists of its sign,
$1^{\ell(|z|)}0$, and its magnitude bits, of total length
$2\ell(|z|)+2$; negative zero is forbidden. A reduced rational $A/B$
with $B>0$ consists of the signed numerator and positive denominator
length prefix/bits, of total length

$$
\rho(A/B)=2\ell(|A|)+2\ell(B)+3.
\tag{13.1}
$$

Three-bit field tags give Reset/Tick/Hold request length three, exact
Read$(j)$ length $5+2\ell(|j|)$, and Add$(j,q)$ this latter length
plus $\rho(q)$. Exact scalar replies have length $3+\rho(q)$.
Fixed Ack, RangeRefusal and BadFrame tags have three bits each.
Digital Read adds the canonical signed code of its nonnegative $p$,
so its request length is $7+2\ell(|j|)+2\ell(p)$ and its signed integer
reply length is $5+2\ell(|k|)$. Complete invalid/noncanonical payloads
take the branches of §7; they are not alternative encodings of valid
values. This finite codec does not make a rational or a precision a
constant-width primitive.

For actual success addresses $p_1,\ldots,p_n$ and refused-probe addresses
$r_1,\ldots,r_f$, one raw or outward growth channel pays payload bits

$$
G_{\rm bits}=\sum_{i=1}^n(4|p_i|+9)
 +\sum_{j=1}^f(2|r_j|+9).
\tag{13.2}
$$

A pays this once; C's stated adapter pays it twice. Add every actual
field request and response at its above length, including seed and
inner Tick acknowledgements. Also add each actual End, local
acknowledgement, Return/navigation, dispatch, copy, clear, law/roster/
binding handoff and requested record/history export. Input generation
and provider traffic are actual resources, not absorbed by a semantic
call count. A different codec must pay its own literal words.

**Proposition 13.3 (source arithmetic at the actual sizes).** Let $N_i$
be the material size after the first $i$ growths, so $N_0=1$ and
$N_n=N$. A direct two-buffer Tick on a current $M$-vertex tree uses
$3M-2$ rational products and $2M-2$ rational sums. Therefore A's
attained source Tick work is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{Prod}_A&=\sum_{i=0}^nq_i(3N_i-2)+I(3N-2),\\
\mathsf{Sum}_A&=\sum_{i=0}^nq_i(2N_i-2)+I(2N-2),
\end{aligned}
\tag{13.3}
$$

with the sole gap $q_0$ when $n=0$. C's attained source Tick work is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{Prod}_C&=\sum_{i=1}^n(3N_i-2)+n(3N-2),\\
\mathsf{Sum}_C&=\sum_{i=1}^n(2N_i-2)+n(2N-2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13.4}
$$

Seed Add, the actual initial Reset write, fresh-cell zero initialization,
growth/roster/reference work and all predictor arithmetic are additional.

**Proof.** A tree has $M-1$ undirected edges. Its diagonal and directed
edge products number $M+2(M-1)=3M-2$; summing each row from its first
term uses $2(M-1)$ additions. A performs $q_i$ Ticks on tree $i$ and
then $I$ on the final tree. C performs its $i$th inner Tick on the new
tree $i$ and $n$ assay Ticks on the final tree. This proves the formulas
on those same runs, without replacing actual stage sizes by another
run's smaller sizes. $\square$

**Proposition 13.4 (actual source widths and C precision traffic).** After
$R$ Ticks on either restricted run, all source coordinates have a common
denominator $8^R$ and nonnegative integer numerators at most $7^R$.
Every positive coordinate is at least $8^{-R}$. Consequently source
scalar/reduced-rational widths are $O(R+1)$, not constant. In A through
the first assay $R\le Q+I$; in C $R\le2n$.

For C's assay, the sum of requested precisions, excluding its seed, is

$$
\sum_{r=0}^n\sum_{j=1}^bp_r
=b(n+1)\left(\frac92n+1\right).
\tag{13.5}
$$

The seed adds precision one. Each scheduled integer reply has at most
$2p_r+7$ payload bits in Definition 13.2's codec, including its three-bit
tag. Thus the attained assay's integer-reply traffic is
$O(b(n+1)^2)$, while its precision-code request contribution is
$O(b(n+1)\log(n+2))$; ordinal, address, fixed-tag and lifecycle traffic
remains additional as specified above.

**Proof.** Initially the numerator is one and $R=0$. Zero extension adds
zero numerators, and a Tick multiplies the numerator vector by
$4\operatorname{Id}+A_t$ and the common denominator by eight. Its
nonnegative row sums are at most seven, proving the numerator bound by
induction. A positive integer numerator is at least one. Reduction cannot
increase these bit widths. This is the same arithmetic supplier as AAII
Proposition 90.3 with the actual Tick counts of the present runs.

Sum (10.1) over $r=0,\ldots,n$ to obtain (13.5). On these runs every
field is in $[0,1]$, so a scheduled report lies between $0$ and
$2^{p_r}+1$. Since $p_r\ge1$, its binary magnitude length is at most
$p_r+1$. The signed code plus reply tag has length at most
$2(p_r+1)+2+3=2p_r+7$. Summing gives the reply bound. Also
$p_r\le6n+1$, giving the precision-code bound. No bound here applies
to the magnitude or denominator of arbitrary later rational Adds or

broader service. $\square$
