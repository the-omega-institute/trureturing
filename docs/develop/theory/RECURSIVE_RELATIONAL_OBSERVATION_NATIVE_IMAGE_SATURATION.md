## 1. Scope, complete original input and quantifiers

**Convention 1.1 (ordinary mathematical scope).** The arguments below are
ordinary ZFC mathematics. The new result is finite-exception completeness for
native image filters on finite functional recursive systems, its exact
epoch-potential correspondence, and its source-faithful acquired-input consumer.
Normal atomic codes, guarded continuation, the non-rational contact phenomenon,
signed local lifting and joint arithmetic support are owning-source suppliers.
The general mutual-recovery programme for spatial, temporal, boundary and memory
expressions keeps its unrestricted scope.

**Definition 1.2 (native sources and replies).** Sources are all nonempty finite
free ordered trees in the algebra

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal T&::=\alpha\mid\beta\mid\langle\mathcal T,\mathcal T\rangle,\\
\rho\alpha&=\beta,&
\rho\beta&=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,&
\rho\langle s,t\rangle&=\langle\rho s,\rho t\rangle,\\
T_j&=\rho^j\alpha\qquad(j\in\mathbb N).
\end{aligned}
\tag{NIS.1}
$$

The two leaf labels differ; pairing is ordered and injective. The interpreted
operation $\rho$ is not a free unary constructor. There is no bound on source
size, depth or atomic rank. Repeated occurrences of a variable use its same
whole value. Addresses are words in $L,R$, with root $\varepsilon$.
The reply $r_u(p)$ is $\alpha,\beta,\mathsf{branch}$ or $\mathsf{absent}$.
Every strict descendant of a leaf is absent. Inclusive agreement means

$$
u\equiv_h v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall |p|\le h\quad r_u(p)=r_v(p).
\tag{NIS.2}
$$

For a finite prefix-free cut $A$, the complete original row $R_A(u)$ retains
the ordered reached subset of $A$, the entire concrete exterior obtained by
replacing precisely those reached subtrees by numbered holes, and the full
equality partition of their whole values. Repeated equal subtrees retain
separate hole occurrences. Root, empty and partly absent cuts have these
literal meanings.

**Definition 1.3 (entire acquired input).** Fix the [ARH, §§1–2][ARH] input
$I=(P,R,H_0,H_{\rm rel},Q,N)$. The old whole $P$-row $R$ is attained.
A literal $(a,p,c)$ requires $r_{\rho^a u}(p)=c$. Each signed record
$(a,p,b,q,\bowtie)$, $\bowtie\in\{=,\ne\}$, requires

$$
p\in\operatorname{Pos}(\rho^a u),\qquad
q\in\operatorname{Pos}(\rho^b u),\qquad
(\rho^a u)|_p\ \bowtie\ (\rho^b u)|_q.
\tag{NIS.3}
$$

Absence invalidates either sign. Records retain repetitions and their actual
epochs; every record in one realization concerns the same original source.
Every old equality, old disequality, off-task constraint, reached guard and
concrete exterior remains in

$$
D=\{u\in\mathcal T:R_P(u)=R,\ H_0(u),\ H_{\rm rel}(u)\},\qquad
D_r=\{u\in D:R_Q(u)=r\}.
\tag{NIS.4}
$$

Supply any finite set $B$ of distinct attained complete $Q$-rows, $|B|\ge2$.
The target is the entire row, not selected comparison bits. Rows outside $B$
remain in $D$. The joint question is

$$
J_B(h)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists(u_r)_{r\in B}\in\prod_{r\in B}D_r\quad
\forall r,s\in B\quad u_r\equiv_h u_s.
\tag{NIS.5}
$$

These are different possible original realizations of one common producer
contract. They share one original prefix. Unrelated pairwise witnesses do not
suffice, and one actual source never carries contradictory target labels.

**Assumption 1.4 (paid acquisition and authentic service).** Retain the exact

All still accessible source-dependent descriptions, caches, addresses, counts,

No mathematical coordinate, inverse, contact or finite witness supplies a free

Acquisition, preparation, local computation, calls, literal word length,

**Definition 1.5 (completion).** Write $\widehat{\mathcal T}$ for all valid

## 2. Native image languages and bounded terminal exceptions

**Definition 2.1 (actual image).** For fixed $k\in\mathbb N$, put
$L_k=\rho^k[\widehat{\mathcal T}]$. In particular $L_0=\widehat{\mathcal T}$.
These are interpreted native images, including finite leaf images.

**Lemma 2.2 (unique image parse).** The language $L_k$ is exactly the greatest
fixed point of

$$
\mathcal P_k(A)=
\{T_k,T_{k+1}\}\ \cup\
\{\langle U,V\rangle:U,V\in A\}.
\tag{NIS.6}
$$

The two terminal alternatives and the recursive pair alternative are disjoint
on $L_k$. Every accepted parse has a unique actual preimage. Moreover $L_k$
is closed, $\Omega\in L_k$, and

$$
\rho^b Z\in L_k
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
\text{true},&b\ge k,\\
Z\in L_{k-b},&b<k.
\end{cases}
\tag{NIS.7}
$$

Proof. A source leaf gives respectively $T_k$ or $T_{k+1}$, and a source
pair gives two images. Conversely an accepted coinductive parse reconstructs
a full source tree: replace a terminal by $\alpha$ or $\beta$, and a pair
parse by paired source children. Infinite parse branches give infinite source
branches. Induction on each finite requested output prefix verifies that its
image is the parsed tree. This proves both directions, including the greatest
fixed point rather than only finite parses.

The atomic recurrence $T_{j+2}=\langle T_{j+1},T_j\rangle$ and completed
injectivity imply distinct terminals. A terminal cannot also be a pair of two
$L_k$ members: otherwise injectivity of $\rho^k$ would identify its source
leaf with a source pair. Completed injectivity then gives uniqueness throughout.
Closedness follows also from compactness and continuity of $\rho^k$.
If a parse fails, its failure is detected in a finite prefix, since the
complement of the closed image is open. Finally image nesting gives the
first case of (NIS.7). For $b<k$, an equality
$\rho^b Z=\rho^k V$ cancels to $Z=\rho^{k-b}V$; the converse is composition.
No inverse is applied outside its actual image. $\square$

**Lemma 2.3 (bounded atomic dependency).** Every rooted subtree of $T_j$ is
$T_l$ for some $0\le l\le j$. If a value of a finite native expression is
$T_j$ and an occurrence $\rho^b F$ appears below its explicit pair skeleton,
then

$$
F=T_{l-b}\quad\text{for some }b\le l\le j.
\tag{NIS.8}
$$

The same conclusion holds for a free-state dependency exposed by unfolding
a normalized pair-guarded recursive system whose value is $T_j$.

Proof. Induct using the atomic recurrence, with $T_0=\alpha,T_1=\beta$.
A displayed decorated occurrence is an actual whole rooted subtree of the
expression's value, hence is $T_l$. Its preimage is finite because infinite
branches persist. [ABC, Theorems 2.2–2.3][ABC] gives the unique atomic inverse
and $l\ge b$. In the guarded case, each unfolded dependency passes below
an actual pair and so enters a strict subtree of the finite $T_j$.
A reachable guarded cycle cannot have finite values: native leaf count
never decreases under a power, whereas a containing pair adds a nonempty
sibling. Thus a finite value has a finite acyclic unfolding to grounds and
free states. Distribution accumulates only nonnegative powers, so the finite
expression argument applies. This also shows that a finite terminal value
depending on a free state exposes such an occurrence. $\square$

## 3. The precise functional class and epoch correspondence

**Definition 3.1 (functional system with retained filters).** A finite system
has states $Y_1,\ldots,Y_m$, at most one defining equation per state,

$$
Y_i=E_i(\mathbf p,\mathbf Y),
\tag{NIS.9}
$$

and finitely many conjuncts $C_l(\mathbf p,\mathbf Y)\in L_{k_l}$.
Expressions use finite grounds, finite parameter atoms $T_{q(\mathbf p)}$,
$\Omega$, fixed nonnegative powers of states and ordered pairs.
Natural affine indices have explicit nonnegativity guards. The finite
parameter tuple $\mathbf p$ has a Presburger guard $\Gamma(\mathbf p)$.
An undefined state is unrestricted in the completion. All names and
multiplicities remain.

Use [FJC, Theorem 3.1][FJC] and [SFS, Theorem 4.1][SFS] to normalize root
links, retaining every original-state reconstruction and every filter.
Zero-weight root-link cycles become free aliases; positive root-link cycles
are $\Omega$. Remaining recursive definitions have explicit pair roots.
Let $\mathcal F$ be the resulting free-state list. Every assignment of
completed values to $\mathcal F$ has a unique guarded extension.
Finite parameter atoms remain finite symbolic terminals; no parameter
enumeration is part of normalization.

**Definition 3.2 (eligible decorated equations).** Before functionalization,
require that all remaining completed equations admit an orientation

$$
\rho^{a_i}X_i=E_i(\mathbf p,\mathbf X),\qquad a_i\ge0,
\tag{NIS.10}
$$

with at most one head definition for each state. Other states are free.
In distributed syntax each occurrence $\rho^bX_j$ in equation $i$ creates
an edge $i\to j$ of weight $a_i-b$. Retain parallel edges and repeated
occurrences. Every directed occurrence cycle must have total weight at most
zero. Optional additional filters $C(\mathbf p,\mathbf X)\in L_r$ are
retained conjuncts. This is a condition on the completed equation relation,
not a restriction on finite originals or on the pre-negative source chart.

**Theorem 3.3 (exact epoch-potential functionalization).** For (NIS.10),
the cycle condition holds if and only if there are integers $s_i$ satisfying

$$
s_i\ge0,\qquad s_i\ge a_i\ \text{for defined heads},\qquad
s_i-s_j\ge a_i-b\ \text{on every occurrence edge}.
\tag{NIS.11}
$$

One can compute a finite such tuple from the input graph. Put
$Y_i=\rho^{s_i}X_i$ and $t_i=s_i-a_i$ at defined heads.
Applying $\rho^{t_i}$ to equation $i$ produces (NIS.9), replacing
$\rho^bX_j$ by $\rho^{b+t_i-s_j}Y_j$, shifting finite grounds and parameter
atoms by $t_i$, and preserving $\Omega$. Retain every filter

$$
Y_i\in L_{s_i}\qquad(1\le i\le m).
\tag{NIS.12}
$$

For each fixed $\mathbf p$, this gives a bijection between the entire original
completed solution relation and the transformed functional relation with
all filters, preserving every original state by its unique native inverse.

Proof. Summing (NIS.11) around a cycle gives its nonpositive total.
Conversely put $d_j=a_j$ for defined heads and $d_j=0$ otherwise.
For each $i$, maximize
$\operatorname{weight}(\pi)+d_j$ over walks $\pi$ from $i$ to $j$, including
the zero walk. Removing a nonpositive cycle never lowers this value.
A maximum is therefore attained on a simple path, of which there are finitely
many. The resulting $s_i$ is nonnegative, meets its own lower bound and
satisfies every edge inequality by prepending that edge to a maximizing walk.
This is a terminating finite graph construction.

The exponents $b+t_i-s_j$ are nonnegative by the edge inequalities.
Forward mapping is literal native evaluation and satisfies (NIS.12).
Conversely those filters give unique $X_i$ with $\rho^{s_i}X_i=Y_i$.
Substitution identifies the transformed equation with the original equation
after applying $\rho^{t_i}$. Its injectivity recovers the original equation.
Thus no original valuation is lost or invented. States with no definition
also keep their filters and their unique inverse.

For an additional filter, choose fixed $t\ge0$ with $b+t-s_j\ge0$ for
every occurrence $\rho^bX_j$ in its expression. Distribution gives a finite
expression $C^\sharp(\mathbf p,\mathbf Y)=\rho^t C(\mathbf p,\mathbf X)$,
and injectivity proves

$$
C(\mathbf p,\mathbf X)\in L_r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
C^\sharp(\mathbf p,\mathbf Y)\in L_{r+t}.
\tag{NIS.13}
$$

Keep this transformed conjunct, in both directions. Any original
reconstruction is transported by the same procedure. All offsets are fixed
input-derived integers, independent of the later requested prefix depth.
$\square$

## 4. Finite-exception image saturation and effective rigidity

**Definition 4.1 (saturation modes).** For the normalized system of
Definition 3.1, after all filter transformations, set

$$
S=\max(\{0\}\cup\{k_l:l\}),\qquad
A_S=\{T_0,\ldots,T_{S+1}\}.
\tag{NIS.14}
$$

For each free state choose either one fixed value in $A_S$ or the live
condition $F\notin A_S$. These finitely many modes partition all free
assignments. In a mode, its canonical assignment keeps every fixed value
and gives $\Omega$ to each live state. Let $\mathbf K_\sigma(\mathbf p)$
be its unique guarded extension. The live condition is satisfied because
$\Omega$ is infinite.

**Theorem 4.2 (finite-exception completeness).** For every finite system of
Definition 3.1, every fixed legal $\mathbf p$, and every saturation mode
$\sigma$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\exists\mathbf Y\quad
 [\text{(NIS.9), mode }\sigma,\quad
       \bigwedge_l C_l(\mathbf p,\mathbf Y)\in L_{k_l}]\\
&\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \bigwedge_l C_l(\mathbf p,\mathbf K_\sigma(\mathbf p))\in L_{k_l}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{NIS.15}
$$

Thus the finite list of canonical tests is complete for existence, rather
than only sound for supplied certificates. It does not identify different live
solutions or replace the entire solution relation by canonical assignments.

Proof. The reverse implication uses the canonical assignment itself.
For the forward direction start from any admissible solution in the stated
mode and replace its live free values by $\Omega$, preserving finite fixed
free values and parameters. Root-link normalization already preserves both
solution directions and guarded extension is unique.

Follow an accepted image obligation of threshold $k\le S$.
A terminal match to $T_k$ or $T_{k+1}$ can contain no dependency on a live
free state: Lemma 2.3 would force that free state to be $T_j$ with
$j\le k+1\le S+1$, contrary to its live condition. A recursive guarded
dependency cannot hide inside a finite terminal either, by the same finite
unfolding argument. Every chosen terminal match therefore survives the
replacement unchanged.

For nonterminal obligations distribute decorators and use the exact rule
(NIS.7); thresholds only decrease. At an explicit pair the accepted parse
either takes the unchanged terminal match or requires its two children in
the same image language. At a finite fixed free state or ground the value
has not changed. At a live free state, discard its internal parse and use
$\Omega\in L_k$ for its replacement. An explicit $\Omega$ also passes.
These rules follow the finite expression syntax and the normalized guarded
definitions. They retain every accepted finite parse portion; when unfoldings
continue indefinitely they define an accepted coinductive parse. Equivalently,
collect the canonical obligations reached by these rules. Each has either
an accepted terminal witness, an accepted $\Omega$ value, an equivalent
decorated obligation, or two children in that same collected relation.
This is a post-fixed relation for the image rules of Lemma 2.2, so it is
contained in their greatest fixed point. All canonical filters pass.
$\square$

**Proposition 4.3 (effective parameter guard).** One can compute a Presburger
formula $\Gamma_{\rm img,\sigma}(\mathbf p)$ exactly equivalent to the right
side of (NIS.15). This holds for arbitrary guarded canonical native outputs,
including non-rational ones.

Proof. Canonical finite/infinite status is the finite graph test of
SFS Theorem 4.1, with the mode's fixed free atoms counted as finite terminals
and live states as $\Omega$. It is independent of legal parameter values.
A canonical finite expression is evaluated symbolically in normal atomic
codes. Its membership in $L_k$ is exactly that every normal atomic rank is
at least $k$, by ABC Theorem 2.3. Bottom-up smart pairing has finitely many
rank-equality or rank-inequality branches, so these are effective Presburger
conditions. They apply also to arbitrary fixed finite grounds.

For an infinite canonical expression a terminal match to $T_k$ or $T_{k+1}$
is false. At its explicit pair root, membership is exactly membership of
both children. A decorator uses (NIS.7); $\Omega$ and threshold zero pass.
There are only finitely many normalized syntax/state positions and thresholds
$0,\ldots,S$. Build this finite graph of AND obligations, retaining finite
terminal conditions symbolically. A root obligation fails precisely when
it can reach a failed finite terminal obligation. Indeed a false terminal
propagates back along a finite dependency path. If no reachable terminal
fails, the remaining obligations form a post-fixed relation, accepted by
Lemma 2.2. Cycles therefore use greatest-fixed-point acceptance and are not
unfolded in recognition. Conjoining all reachable finite conditions at
all filter roots gives $\Gamma_{\rm img,\sigma}$. $\square$

**Theorem 4.4 (rigidity in original coordinates and effective prefixes).**
In a passing mode from Theorem 3.3, each original-coordinate reconstruction
has decidable canonical finite/infinite status. A canonically finite original
coordinate is one symbolic finite value $f_i(\mathbf p)$ in every admissible
solution of that mode. Canonically infinite original coordinates have
computable prefixes at any supplied $\mathbf p,h$. They need not be infinite
in every other solution.

Proof. Before inversion, a canonically finite expression reaches no live
free state, $\Omega$ or guarded cycle. It is a finite expression in grounds,
parameters and fixed free atoms, so is the same in every solution of the mode,
as in FJC Theorem 3.1. For an original state $X_i$, its image is $Y_i$;
finiteness of either is equivalent to finiteness of the other. If the image
is finite and fixed, image injectivity makes its inverse finite and fixed.

For an original reconstruction $A(\mathbf p,\mathbf X)$, choose a fixed
common shift $t$ as in (NIS.13). Its transformed expression is precisely
$\rho^t A$ and lies in $L_t$ because each $X_i$ is an actual preimage.
The same finite classification and inversion apply. Finite symbolic inversion
uses normal codes with every rank at least $t$; subtraction is represented
by a natural index $q$ and the equation $q+t=n$, never truncated subtraction.
Thus each $f_i$ remains a finite native expression with a finite Presburger
partition of parameter space.

For an image in $L_s$, set
$H_s=\max(\operatorname{ht}(T_s),\operatorname{ht}(T_{s+1}))$.
An original reply at depth at most $h$ is determined by the image replies
through $h+H_s$. At each reached original node, its image subtree is either
one of the two finite isolated terminals, giving the corresponding leaf,
or the recursive pair case. A terminal comparison needs at most $H_s$
further levels. Original pair ancestors have the same addresses in the
image, so the deepest such request is at most $h+H_s$; lookahead does not
accumulate once per ancestor. The recursive parse also determines absence
below recovered leaves. Guarded iteration computes the necessary finite
image prefix and hence that original prefix. This is offline construction
on an actual image description, not a runtime inverse port. $\square$

**Example 4.5 (a necessary finite exception).** The system
$Z=\langle F,\alpha\rangle$ with $Z\in L_1$ has a solution, although giving
its free state $F$ the value $\Omega$ fails. The only possible parse is the
terminal $T_2=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle$: a pair of two $L_1$ members
cannot have right child $\alpha$. Thus $F=\beta$, $Z=T_2$.
This is one of the bounded exceptional modes. Omitting the filter would
admit invalid values; using only the unsaturated $\Omega$ test would reject
a valid value. This finite native diagnostic is not a counterexample to
the unrestricted acquired-input stopping problem.

## 5. Automatic whole-input synthesis and joint signed realization

$$
D_r=\bigcup_b

\tag{NIS.16}
$$

section, marker or marginal support never replaces this image. Source copies
are renamed apart, while repeated names inside each source retain sharing.

including unused ones. Finite ranks are finite parameters; divergent atomic
outputs become $\Omega$ only in completed comparisons. All actual finite rank
policies remain in the support. Compare each entire completed source output

This is one joint support, with every original sharing and correlation.

$\forall M\exists\mathbf p,\mathbf w$ nor separate cofinal marginals

**Theorem 5.4 (complete alternative on the recognized class).** For every
$(I,B)\in\mathcal C_{\rm img}$,

$$
\forall h\in\mathbb N\ J_B(h)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists\tau\ \exists\mathbf p\in G_\tau.
\tag{NIS.18}
$$

finite $H$ with $\neg J_B(H)$. Every witness satisfies the entire original

Proof. All formulas in (NIS.17) are Presburger by Proposition 4.3,
Theorem 4.4 and the owning symbolic negative projection. Presburger decision
therefore determines whether any $G_\tau$ is nonempty. Choose one fixed
$\mathbf p$ from a positive set before receiving any prefix depth.

Compute the canonical original coordinates and the common whole completed
source output $W$ through the exact forward and inverse reconstructions.
For a requested $h$, prescribe the depth-$h$ prefix of every canonical
infinite original residual; keep each finite original residual exactly fixed.
Choose one joint $\mathbf w$ in (NIS.17) with every divergent entry at least
$M_h=2h+2+\max(\{0\}\cup\{-c:\text{a divergent atom has offset }c\})$.
Then every divergent atomic occurrence has actual rank at least $2h+2$
and agrees through $h$ with $\Omega$.
At those same ranks, $\Psi_{r,\tau}$ supplies feasibility of the entire
negative list. [SLP, Lemma 2.1][SLP] produces one finite tuple with all
prescribed infinite-coordinate prefixes and all of that source's negatives
simultaneously. Its hypotheses apply to the original Cartesian chart
coordinates, not to constrained shifted states. Repeated names use one
filling. Different source copies can use different finite fillings, even when
their completed coordinates coincide.

Evaluate each original $\Theta_{r,b_r}$ with its actual ranks and filling.
Nonexpansion makes every resulting source agree through $h$ with the one
$W$. Both chart directions give its entire input, both reached guards for

It does not assert that the canonical solution equals the original limit.

## 9. Fixed-shift completed coding and the free-state boundary

The fixed-power normalization has a sound finite part, but its completion must
keep a distinction between a syntactic terminal-free graph state and a chosen
leafless value. The distinction is needed before any claim about all solutions,
all retained definitions, or effective joint support is made.

**Definition 9.1 (reduced completed code).** Let $\widehat{\mathcal T}$ be the
finite-prefix completion of the native finite ordered trees. Let
$\mathscr C_{\mathbb Z}$ consist of coinductive ordered binary trees with finite
leaves $[n]$, $n\in\mathbb Z$, and the unique leafless tree $\Omega$. The raw
constructor is $F(U,V)$, with $F(\Omega,\Omega)=\Omega$ extensionally. Thus
$\Omega$ is determined by all of its replies; it is not a leaf label and not an
infinity token.

For $d\in\mathbb Z$, write $\sigma^d$ for the shift of every finite label by
$d$, fixing $\Omega$. A code is reduced when it is $\Omega$, a leaf $[n]$, or
$F(U,V)$ with reduced children and neither

$$
(U,V)=([n+1],[n])\quad(n\in\mathbb Z),
\qquad (U,V)=(\Omega,\Omega).
\tag{NIS.30}
$$

The first forbidden pair is the native atomic collapse. The second is the
local representation of the leafless completion. A constructor node with no
finite leaf below it is therefore represented by $\Omega$ only after a value
has been chosen, not merely because the current syntax has no displayed
terminal.

**Theorem 9.2 (canonical coding).** Collapsing every maximal finite atomic
subtree and retaining every other ordered pair gives a bijection

$$
B:\widehat{\mathcal T}\longrightarrow \mathscr C_{\mathbb N}^{\rm red}
$$

such that

$$
\llbracket B(u)\rrbracket=u,
\qquad B(\rho^k u)=\sigma^kB(u)\quad(k\in\mathbb N).
\tag{NIS.31}
$$

In particular, the only code for the leafless completion is $\Omega$.

**Proof.** On finite trees, the recurrence
$T_{n+2}=\langle T_{n+1},T_n\rangle$ gives exactly the first collapse in
(NIS.30), and the finite normal-code induction of [ABC, Theorems 2.2--2.3][ABC]
gives both inverse identities. For a completed tree, apply that collapse at
every finite atomic subtree. A subtree with no finite leaf is the unique
leafless full tree and is represented by $\Omega$; every other root has the
same two possible collisions as in (NIS.30). Every finite requested prefix
meets finitely many such steps, so induction on prefix depth proves the inverse
identities and the shift identity. If a reduced leaf and a reduced pair had
the same value, atomic injectivity forces the forbidden pair $([n+1],[n])$.
If $\Omega$ and a pair had the same value, both children of the pair would be
leafless, forcing the second forbidden pair. Pair injectivity handles the
remaining cases. $\square$

This is a semantic bijection. It supplies neither an algorithm for finding
maximal atomic subtrees in an arbitrary infinite input nor continuity of its
integer-labelled code prefixes. Native fixed-power prefix continuity is the
separate property in Definition 1.5 and [SFS, Definition 1.4][SFS]. Evaluation
of an arbitrary infinite graph assignment requires its own representation and
effective interface.

**Definition 9.3 (arbitrary fixed-power presentation).** A presentation has
finitely many named values and finitely many retained positive conjuncts. Its
terms are generated by

$$
E::=g\mid T_{a(\mathbf p)}\mid\Omega\mid X_i\mid
   \rho^eX_i\mid\langle E,E\rangle,
\tag{NIS.32}
$$

where $g$ is a finite native ground, $e$ is a supplied nonnegative numeral,
and $a(\mathbf p)\ge0$ is an explicit affine guard. It may contain any finite
family $X_i=E_{i,\ell}$, repeated occurrences, positive equalities, and image
constraints $E\in L_k$. A signed presentation has in addition a finite list
$\Delta^-$ of native disequalities. Every positive equality, including an old
equality-partition bit that asserts equality, is promoted into the positive
presentation before saturation. No equality is silently treated as a
disequality or passed to a supplier whose hypotheses mention only
 disequalities.

**Definition 9.4 (finite graph saturation with free states).** Flatten the
finite displayed syntax, distributing fixed numeral powers through displayed
pairs, finite atoms, and $\Omega$. Name every displayed subterm and preserve
the lookup from every original name and occurrence to its slot. Let $S_0$ be
this finite slot set. For each $s\in S_0$, reserve two ordered child slots
$(s,L),(s,R)$ before choosing its root case. The initial slot universe is
$S_{\rm init}=S_0\cup(S_0\times\{L,R\})$, with fresh child-slot names.
The finite-atom, $\Omega$, and reduced-constructor root cases are exhaustive
by Theorem 9.2. A constructor case uses its one reserved pair of children;
all retained definitions of that name refer to the same pair. The resulting
facts have the forms

$$
U=[a],\qquad U=\Omega,\qquad U=\sigma^dV,\qquad
U=F(\sigma^{d_1}V,\sigma^{d_2}W).
\tag{NIS.33}
$$

Weighted congruence saturation keeps every parallel edge and propagates both
child relations whenever two pair roots have been equated. A nonzero shift
cycle is incompatible with a finite leaf and hence forces that component to
be leafless. A constructor cycle is retained as a guarded graph; it is never
converted into an occurs-check failure. A component with no reachable finite
terminal is recorded as a **free-state node**. The node may later be assigned
$\Omega$, but it may also be assigned any completed tree satisfying its guarded
cycle. Components with finite terminals retain all such terminals. Atomic
collapses and native nonnegativity, image lower bounds, rank guards, and
noncollapse guards are imposed on every branch.

For a declared finite slot set $S\supseteq S_{\rm init}$, a constructor
record has a parent in $S$, two children in $S$, and the two stored shifts in
(NIS.33). Let $\mathcal P_S$ be the declared finite set of such records.
The work universe must include every retained input relation, every root split
to be performed on a slot of $S$, and both propagation keys
$(c,c',L),(c,c',R)$ for every $c,c'\in\mathcal P_S$. A key records the
corresponding child equation when the parent values are equated, including
their shift difference. Every parallel relation and every cycle-consistency
condition remains a conjunct; a key is not permission to discard a second
definition. Any further constructor record must have its children reserved
in $S$ and all its propagation keys already reserved in the work universe.

The initial allocation does not prove closure under arbitrary further root
splitting. An introduced child can itself require a constructor case with
further child slots. A general saturation theorem must prove that all such
requests fit a computably supplied finite $S$ and finite obligation universe
$\mathcal F$, or retain the unprocessed request as a guarded equation. It
must also justify processing each key once, or reserve finitely many keys for
the required updates when weighted representatives change. These allocation,
closure and scheduling assertions are open for the arbitrary presentation.

Conditional on those finite closure and scheduling assertions, weighted
union-find may store component potentials and all parallel edge differences
without enumerating repeated path sums. The measure
$(|\mathcal F\setminus\mathcal A|,r)$, ordered lexicographically, decreases
when a reserved obligation enters the processed set $\mathcal A$, or one of
the $r$ reserved unresolved root splits is fixed. No new slot or unreserved
obligation may be added under this measure. It proves termination of that
closed syntactic worklist, including finite branching, but not termination
of an unrestricted slot-generating procedure. Even a proved finite graph
closure would supply no arithmetic elimination of its completed-tree values.

**Proposition 9.5 (sound guarded graph transformations).** For any branch
graphs obtained by the equivalence-preserving transformations in Definition
9.4, with all original slots and constraints retained, a native completed
solution induces the matching root branch and a full satisfying graph
valuation. Conversely, a full satisfying valuation, including every repeated
definition, image constraint and native guard, reconstructs a solution of
the retained positive presentation. Its restriction to the free-state nodes
need not determine the full valuation without a further extension theorem.

**Proof.** Naming a subterm retains its defining equality. Each root split
selects its actual reduced root case; ordered-pair injectivity propagates
both child relations, shift congruence preserves each weighted relation, and
(NIS.30) accounts for both collapses. All parallel relations are retained.
Composing these equivalences gives both directions, using the original-slot
lookup for reconstruction. Unprocessed equations remain conjuncts, so they
are also required of the valuation. $\square$

This is a statement about graph equations and their satisfying valuations.
It proves neither an exhaustive terminating saturation for arbitrary inputs
nor free-state elimination, a countable integer-parameter partition, or
Presburger decision of the remaining guarded equations.

**Proposition 9.6 (native obstruction to an all-solution finite partition).**
The presentation

$$
X=X
\tag{NIS.34}
$$

is an allowed retained definition. Every native completed tree is a solution.
There are continuum many pairwise distinct such solutions: for each
$s=(s_0,s_1,\ldots)\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}$ define coinductively

$$
U_s=\langle U_{\operatorname{shift}(s)},T_{s_0}\rangle.
\tag{NIS.35}
$$

At the first index $j$ where two sequences differ, the subtrees at address
$L^jR$ are $T_{s_j}$ and $T_{t_j}$, proving $U_s\ne U_t$. Each $U_s$ is
native, has finite leaves, and satisfies (NIS.34).

Consequently, no finite family whose branch data consist only of integer
parameters and canonical terminal/$\Omega$ assignments can be an exact
all-solution partition for Definition 9.3. Such data are countable, while
(NIS.34) has continuum many solutions. In particular, syntactic absence of a
finite terminal does not imply that the represented value is $\Omega$.

**Proof.** The coinductive equations in (NIS.35) define a full ordered tree;
its subtree at address $L^jR$ is $T_{s_j}$, so distinct sequences give distinct
finite prefixes. The equation $X=X$ imposes no further condition. The
cardinality comparison proves the final assertion. $\square$

The obstruction also applies to a free component attached to a nontrivial
presentation. A canonical choice $\Omega$ is legitimate for a theorem about
one selected witness, or after a separate cofinal-normalization theorem. It
cannot be used in a completeness direction that quantifies over arbitrary
completed values.

**Proposition 9.7 (guarded cycles are finite graphs, not finite terminal
lists).** The native equation

$$
X=\langle T_0,X\rangle
\tag{NIS.36}
$$

has the guarded solution $U=\langle T_0,U\rangle$. Its graph has one named
constructor node and one self-edge, but it has infinitely many constructor
occurrences in its expansion. A requested depth can be computed by finite
unfolding, while a procedure that enumerates only finite terminal paths does
not reconstruct the value. The same issue occurs for
$X=\langle\alpha,X\rangle$ with any finite native ground $\alpha$.

Thus a terminating saturation needs a separate finite-prefix evaluator for
free-state graphs and a proved interface from that evaluator to the native
image and rank guards. A finite graph, by itself, is not a finite arithmetic
elimination of guarded recursive trees. The whole-value equation
$X=\rho^dX$ with $d>0$ remains different: by (NIS.31), a finite leaf would
have to equal its nonzero translate, so its only completed solution is
$\Omega$.

## 10. Complete acquired charts and joint signed support

**Definition 10.1 (exact complete-row chart).** For each attained complete row
$r$ of an acquired input $I=(P,R,H_0,H_{\rm rel},Q,N)$, use a finite chart list

$$
D_r=\bigcup_{b\in\mathcal C_r}
\left\{\Theta_{r,b}(\mathbf n,\mathbf z):
 P_{r,b}(\mathbf n)\land E^+_{r,b}(\mathbf n,\mathbf z)
 \land\Delta^-_{r,b}(\mathbf n,\mathbf z)\right\},
\tag{NIS.37}
$$

where $\mathbf n\in\mathbb N^{k_{r,b}}$ and
$\mathbf z\in\mathcal T^{m_{r,b}}$ is the full Cartesian residual product
before the signed list. $E^+$ contains every retained positive equality,
partition equality, image constraint, endpoint condition, complete old and new
exterior, reached-hole condition, and off-task condition. $\Delta^-$ contains
only disequalities. Terms use finite grounds, affine atoms $T_{n_j+c}$,
ordered pairing, and fixed numeral powers $\rho^e z_i$. Both directions are
part of the chart: every chart point is the same original source in $D_r$,
and every source in $D_r$ has a chart point.

The exact-source results [NIC, Theorems 3.2--3.3 and 4.3][NIC] and [SFS,
Proposition 3.2][SFS] provide this two-way chart shape under their stated
complete-row hypotheses. The chart statement does not solve the positive
multiple-definition system created by $E^+$.

**Definition 10.2 (joint support with a common completed contact).** Let $B$
be any finite set of at least two distinct attained complete rows. Select one
chart $b_r$ and one guarded graph branch $\tau_r$ for every $r\in B$.
Let $S_{\tau_r}$ be its slot set and $F_{\tau_r}\subseteq S_{\tau_r}$ its
free-state slots. Write $B_{\rm code}$ for the bijection in Theorem 9.2.
A branch datum contains finite scalar data $\lambda_r$, including a partition
$K_r^{\rm fin}\sqcup K_r^{\rm div}=\{1,\ldots,k_{r,b_r}\}$ and fixed values
$c_r\in\mathbb N^{K_r^{\rm fin}}$,
free-state values $\xi_r:F_{\tau_r}\to\widehat{\mathcal T}$, a full reduced
code valuation $\gamma_r:S_{\tau_r}\to\mathscr C_{\mathbb N}^{\rm red}$,
and one completed tree $W$. The valuation extends the free-state assignment
by $\gamma_r(f)=B_{\rm code}(\xi_r(f))$ and satisfies every retained guarded
positive equation, repeated definition, image condition and native guard of its
completed branch. A full satisfying extension is part of the datum; neither
its uniqueness nor effective construction from $\lambda_r,\xi_r$ is assumed.

For each original chart residual index $1\le i\le m_{r,b_r}$, retain its
original slot $o_{r,i}\in S_{\tau_r}$. Reconstruction into the original
coordinates is the tuple

$$
\eta_r=\operatorname{Rec}_{r,\tau_r}(\lambda_r,\xi_r,\gamma_r)
 \in\widehat{\mathcal T}^{m_{r,b_r}},
\qquad
\eta_{r,i}=\llbracket\gamma_r(o_{r,i})\rrbracket.
\tag{NIS.38r}
$$

If an original slot is eliminated in favor of a weighted representative
$(s_{r,i},d_{r,i})$, its retained equation is
$\gamma_r(o_{r,i})=\sigma^{d_{r,i}}\gamma_r(s_{r,i})$, and this expression
is decoded in (NIS.38r). Negative shifts are used only with their native
nonnegativity and actual-image guards. Repeated occurrences use the retained
same-name lookup and all their defining equations. Thus reconstruction does
not identify the graph's free-state index set with the chart's residual
index set, or drop coordinates that were aliased during saturation.

Write $\widehat\Theta_{r,b_r}$ for the completed chart expression: fixed
scalar atoms keep their recorded finite ranks, divergent scalar atoms become
$\Omega$, and original residuals are replaced by $\eta_r$, with the same
pairing and fixed native powers. Require the completed positive contact
equation $\widehat\Theta_{r,b_r}(\lambda_r,\eta_r)=W$ for every row.
This is a semantic reconstruction, not an algorithm for evaluating arbitrary
infinite assignments. The finite witnesses below have
$\mathbf n_r\in\mathbb N^{k_{r,b_r}}$ and
$\mathbf z_r\in\mathcal T^{m_{r,b_r}}$. The datum has joint signed support when

$$
\begin{aligned}
\forall h,M\in\mathbb N\ \exists\,(\mathbf n_r,\mathbf z_r)_{r\in B}
\quad &\text{such that for every }r\in B,\\
&P_{r,b_r}(\mathbf n_r)\land E^+_{r,b_r}(\mathbf n_r,\mathbf z_r)
 \land\Delta^-_{r,b_r}(\mathbf n_r,\mathbf z_r),\\
&n_{r,j}=c_{r,j}\quad(j\in K_r^{\rm fin}),\qquad
 n_{r,j}\ge M\quad(j\in K_r^{\rm div}),\\
&z_{r,i}\equiv_h\eta_{r,i}\quad(1\le i\le m_{r,b_r}),\\
&\Theta_{r,b_r}(\mathbf n_r,\mathbf z_r)\equiv_h W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{NIS.38}
$$

All scalar data, graph valuations, reconstructed tuples and $W$ are fixed
before $\forall h,M$. The witnesses are one simultaneous tuple, and they use
the same producer contract and the same original/register/root premise.
Both signed endpoints must be reached; every repeated name, image constraint,
positive equality and complete chart condition remains on the finite
witnesses. Disequalities are evaluated only on those finite witnesses. No
completed disequality is imposed on $W$. An empty original residual product
has its singleton empty-tuple meaning even when the graph has other slots.

The common-source quantifier is essential: replacing each row independently
by a different canonical representative does not produce a common $W$ or a
single producer contract. Coordinatewise closure of the separate row supports
is therefore weaker than (NIS.38).

**Proposition 10.3 (the proved direction of joint support).** Assume the
charts in Definition 10.1 are exact. Joint signed support implies

$$
\forall h\in\mathbb N\;J_B(h),
\tag{NIS.39}
$$

where $J_B(h)$ has exactly the meaning in (NIS.5). The witnesses here also
share the displayed prefix of the datum's $W$.

**Proof.** Given $h$, use (NIS.38) with $M=0$ and set
$u_r=\Theta_{r,b_r}(\mathbf n_r,\mathbf z_r)$. Chart exactness gives
$u_r\in D_r$, including every endpoint and exterior field, reached hole,
partition bit and signed record. The last line of (NIS.38) gives
$u_r\equiv_h W$ for every $r$, hence $u_r\equiv_h u_s$ for every $r,s$.
This is one simultaneous tuple under the common producer contract. $\square$

The direct implication needs no graph evaluator or continuity of the coding
bijection. When finite native expressions are used in a completion bridge,
their fixed-power prefix continuity is supplied by Definition 1.5 and
[SFS, Definition 1.4][SFS], and their divergent atomic prefix bound by
[SFS, Lemma 2.1][SFS] and [PAC, Definition 3.1, (PAC.8)][PAC]. These are
distinct from effective evaluation of an arbitrary infinite code or graph.

**Proposition 10.4 (why the reverse and effective directions remain open).**
The converse from $\forall h\,J_B(h)$ to a datum satisfying (NIS.38) is not
established by Theorem 9.2 or by a rowwise saturation. A proof must supply all
of the following in one combined construction:

1. a finite positive presentation containing every row's $E^+$, the shared
   contact variable $W$, every cross-row contact equation, and the full
   original-coordinate reconstruction (NIS.38r);
2. a simultaneous completion theorem proving that $\forall h\,J_B(h)$
   supplies a datum with (NIS.38). It must preserve the full positive solution
   relation, repeated names, image/rank guards and signed finite neighborhoods,
   including a full graph valuation and one common $W$;
3. a computably supplied finite slot/obligation closure and scheduling theorem
   of the precise scope in Definition 9.4, together with a represented
   finite-prefix interface for guarded constructor cycles such as (NIS.36).
   Its correctness must be proved on the reconstructed native values;
4. a proof that the finite witnesses remain actual members of the complete
   charts on the same paid acquisition cut at the authentic epoch;
5. a total global decision on the finite input description, sound and complete
   for nonempty joint signed support after all scalar parameters, completed
   free-state values, full graph extensions and the common contact have been
   existentially quantified; and
6. a total, sound and complete fixed-depth decision with finite witnesses on a
   positive outcome and finite exclusion certificates on a negative outcome.

More precisely, the predicate required by item 5 is

$$
\mathsf{Supp}(I,B)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\bigvee_{\boldsymbol\tau}\exists
 \bigl((\lambda_r,\xi_r,\gamma_r)_{r\in B},W\bigr)\;
 \operatorname{JS}_{\boldsymbol\tau}
 \bigl((\lambda_r,\xi_r,\gamma_r)_{r\in B},W\bigr),
\tag{NIS.42}
$$

where $\operatorname{JS}$ includes all conditions of Definition 10.2 and
(NIS.38), hence its $\forall h,M\,\exists$ simultaneous finite-witness
quantifiers. A proposed global procedure $\mathsf{DecJoint}$ must terminate
on each permitted finite input, return a Boolean in $\{0,1\}$, and satisfy

$$
\mathsf{DecJoint}(I,B)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathsf{Supp}(I,B).
\tag{NIS.43}
$$

Its input contains no oracle for chosen infinite trees. Deciding each branch's
finite scalar guards, or deciding feasibility at one supplied depth, does not
decide (NIS.42). A finite branch count leaves its parameter and completed-tree
existentials and unbounded signed-support condition intact. A proof of
semantic compactness or existence supplies no total decision of them.

Item 6 requires a procedure $\mathsf{Depth}(I,B,h)$ that terminates for every
finite $h$. Its positive output must be a finite chart tuple satisfying all
conditions of (NIS.37) and (NIS.5); such an output exists exactly when
$J_B(h)$. Its negative output must contain a finite certificate $c$ for a
specified total finite verification relation $\mathsf{Excl}(I,B,h,c)$, with
$\neg J_B(h)\Longleftrightarrow\exists c\,\mathsf{Excl}(I,B,h,c)$, expressing
certificate soundness and completeness. The decision must return the appropriate
output in both cases. This supplies requested-depth extraction on a positive
global verdict and certified finite exclusion on a negative global verdict.
The global decision, exact fixed-depth decision and certificate interface are
future obligations; none is proved here for the unrestricted language.

In particular, replacing terminal-free components by $\Omega$ before proving
the completion bridge is unsound. The replacement can change a positive
equation or a shared contact, and [SLP, Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 3.1][SLP] applies
to finite disequality fillings under its fixed-coordinate hypotheses; it does not prove
this positive free-state normalization or a mixed-equality lifting theorem.

The supplier
`D5/S3/Arith/FibonacciAtomic/Observer/NativeUniformResidualRealization.lean`
at commit `c0d24fb522192e79c921df5bfed53306f7207903` supplies, through
`uniform_residual_reflection` and `simultaneous_completion`, one finite
residual tuple uniform over rank parameters for a finite list on the full
Cartesian finite residual domain. Their use after positive constraints requires
a proof that the chosen tuple preserves those constraints and that the
remaining domain has the supplier's hypotheses. Construction of a combined
positive system alone supplies neither premise. The results do not cover
arbitrary positive constrained systems, eliminate free-state values, prescribe
a prefix contact, or prove the shared-$W$ bridge.

**Example 10.5 (finite disequality is a witness condition).** Under the
finite-rank prefix relation of [SFS, Lemma 2.1][SFS] and
[PAC, Definition 3.1, (PAC.8)][PAC], set

$$
 u_h^{=} = \langle T_{2h+2},T_{2h+2}\rangle,
\qquad
 u_h^{\ne} = \langle T_{2h+3},T_{2h+2}\rangle.
\tag{NIS.40}
$$

The two trees have the same inclusive $h$-prefix, while their two child values
are respectively equal and unequal. Their leafless prefix limit is
$\langle\Omega,\Omega\rangle=\Omega$. Hence a row disequality can hold at every
finite witness while failing at the completed contact. This is why
$\Delta^-$ remains in (NIS.38) and is never evaluated at $W$.

**Conditional theorem 10.6 (semantic and effective interfaces for the
unrestricted alternative).** Assume the exact complete charts of Definition
10.1 and a finite branch family retaining all original-coordinate
reconstructions and guarded equations. Assume, as a separate future completion
interface, that $\forall h\,J_B(h)$ implies the existence of a branch datum
with (NIS.38), as required by Proposition 10.4, item 2. Then the semantic
equivalence

$$
\forall h\;J_B(h)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\bigvee_{\boldsymbol\tau}\exists\text{ branch datum }\mathfrak d\;
\bigl(\mathfrak d\text{ has joint signed support}\bigr)
\tag{NIS.41}
$$

holds. For an effective alternative, additionally assume the total global
decision (NIS.43), with both soundness and completeness, and the total exact
fixed-depth decision and finite witness/certificate interface in Proposition
10.4, item 6. All descriptions required by those procedures must be computably
supplied on the original finite input; a proposed saturation that supplies them
must discharge item 3's slot closure and scheduling obligations.

Under these additional assumptions, a positive global verdict supplies finite
actual simultaneous realizations at every requested depth. A negative global
verdict yields a terminating search for the first failed depth and its finite
exclusion certificate. The semantic completion interface, global support
decision and exact finite-depth interfaces are all open here for arbitrary
complete acquired signed-native inputs. This conditional consequence closes
none of those obligations and does not solve Open problem 7.3.

**Proof.** The right-to-left implication of (NIS.41) is Proposition 10.3.
The left-to-right implication is exactly the separately assumed simultaneous
completion interface, with reconstruction in the original coordinates.
If $\mathsf{DecJoint}(I,B)=1$, (NIS.43) and the right-to-left implication
give $J_B(h)$ for every $h$; running $\mathsf{Depth}(I,B,h)$ returns its
finite witnesses. If $\mathsf{DecJoint}(I,B)=0$, completeness in (NIS.43)
and the left-to-right implication give $\neg\forall h\,J_B(h)$, hence a
finite $h_0$ with $\neg J_B(h_0)$. Run the total exact depth procedure at
$h=0,1,2,\ldots$ in order. It reaches the first failed depth in finitely
many terminating calls and returns the stipulated sound exclusion certificate.
This uses the global decision to select a terminating branch of the
alternative; successive depth tests alone do not provide a positive global
verdict. No effective evaluation of an arbitrary semantic code, elimination
of guarded-tree assignments, or arithmetic reduction is inferred from the
finite graph. $\square$

## 11. Exact scope and remaining boundary

The ordinary theory established here is the semantic canonical fixed-shift
code (Theorem 9.2), sound guarded graph transformations with full satisfying
valuations (Proposition 9.5), the native all-solution obstruction (Proposition
9.6), the guarded-cycle boundary (Proposition 9.7), exact complete-row chart
obligations (Definition 10.1), and the direct joint-support implication
(Proposition 10.3). The worklist measure proves only the conditional finite
syntactic termination stated in Definition 9.4. Its unrestricted slot closure,
guarded-tree arithmetic elimination, original-coordinate simultaneous
completion, total global joint-support decision and exact finite-depth
witness/exclusion interfaces remain open. The unrestricted all-depth
equivalence and effective alternative in Open problem 7.3 require those
bridges at their stated scopes. No recognized subclass, bounded-depth
certificate or conditional criterion completes that target or replaces its
original quantifiers.

The fixed-power supplier [URR, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2][URR] and the newer
`uniform_residual_reflection`/`simultaneous_completion` result at [URR2] are
finite-list residual suppliers. They do not prove arbitrary positive
multiple-definition completeness. The variable-exponent syntax $\rho^pZ$ with
quantified $p$ is outside (NIS.32), and its source-faithful chart, rank
admissibility, and support interface remain separate obligations. The paid
same-register complete-access cut in NIS Assumption 1.4 is a premise of the
consumer and supplies no empirical resource theorem.

These are manuscript-level mathematical claims. They are not Lean truth
claims, and the open bridges above are not converted into completion claims.
