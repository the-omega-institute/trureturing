### 10.2 A connected-graph calculation inside the FIB reconstruction

**Proposition 10.2 (The five-mode graph ambiguity and its reference).**
The five-edge menu admits a normalized pure-state ambiguity when no
positive reference probability is supplied, but the known middle probability
removes it. To establish this in the source at hand, use the following
classical edge-propagation calculation as an intermediate. Let $G=(V,E)$
be any connected graph with at least two vertices, and let two normalized
full-support pure vectors $\psi,\phi$ have the same oriented coherences
on every edge; their diagonals need not agree. Put $q_i=\phi_i/\psi_i$. Then every edge satisfies

$$
\overline{q_i}q_j=1,
\qquad q_j=\frac1{\overline{q_i}}.
\tag{10.2}
$$

If $G$ contains an odd cycle, all $|q_i|=1$ and $q_i=e^{i\alpha}$ is a
single common ray phase. If $G$ is bipartite with parts $L,R$, then

$$
\phi_i=e^{i\alpha}\sqrt t\,\psi_i\ (i\in L),\qquad
\phi_j=e^{i\alpha}t^{-1/2}\psi_j\ (j\in R),
\tag{10.3}
$$

for some $t>0$. If $P_L=\sum_{i\in L}p_i$ and
$P_R=\sum_{j\in R}p_j$, normalization gives

$$
(t-1)(P_Lt-P_R)=0.
\tag{10.4}
$$

Thus a known positive probability at one vertex removes the nontrivial
bipartite branch. This is the connected-graph calculation used here; it is
not promoted to a separate general tomography theorem.

Proof. Equality on an edge is (10.2). Taking moduli gives
$|q_j|=|q_i|^{-1}$. Along an odd cycle the alternating modulus returns with
an odd number of inversions, so it equals its reciprocal and is one.
The phases then propagate as the same common phase. Along a bipartition the
modulus can alternate between $\sqrt t$ and $1/\sqrt t$, and connectedness
propagates the two values. Substitution into the norm gives
$tP_L+t^{-1}P_R=1$; the original norm gives $P_L+P_R=1$, and subtraction
factors as (10.4). A known positive vertex forces $t=1$, since its probability is multiplied
by either $t$ or $t^{-1}$. If $P_L=P_R$, the two roots in (10.4) already
coincide; the formula asserts at most two rays, not always two.

The graph-theoretic precedent is Singer–Cucuringu,
[*Uniqueness of Low-Rank Matrix Completion by Rigidity Theory*,
Proposition 9][GR10]: in the real one-dimensional Gram problem, the
minimal locally completable components are trees with one odd cycle.
Its real generic local-completion statement is not itself a theorem about
normalized complex pure rays. Equations (10.2)–(10.4) supply precisely the
complex phase propagation, normalization and positive-reference adaptation
needed for this five-mode application.

For the FIB graph, $L=\{0,13\}$ and $R=\{1,2,3\}$. Uniform fifths and

$$
p'=(3/10,2/15,2/15,2/15,3/10)
$$

with zero phases have all five native edge coherences equal to $1/5$,
yet are distinct normalized pure rays. They do not lie in the same
calibrated mean fiber: the ambiguity is a graph ambiguity, not the affine
mean direction $d$. The alternating log-amplitude kernel is
$(1,-1,-1,-1,1)$, whose middle entry differs from that of $d$.
Indeed, writing $x_i=\log|q_i|$, each observed edge gives $x_i+x_j=0$.
Connectivity leaves exactly this alternating direction for the displayed
bipartition. For the two examples, $P_L=2/5$, $P_R=3/5$ and $t=3/2$,
so every edge product is multiplied by $\sqrt t/\sqrt t=1$.
But $Z$ changes from $1/5$ to $2/15$ (and the other means also change);
this pair cannot contradict reconstruction at fixed calibrated means.
$\square$

### 10.3 Four edges, a known reference and the exact scope of minimality

**Mathematical input 10.3 (The supplied four-edge reference reconstruction).**

$$
\psi_0=\frac{\overline{C_{02}}}{\sqrt Z},\quad
\psi_1=\frac{C_{01}}{\overline{\psi_0}},\quad

$$

Proof. The first formula fixes the reference-to-$0$ edge and the remaining

### 10.4 The double wall and the base quotient

**Proposition 10.4 (The calibrated double-wall source and response).** Assume
$X=Y=r/2$, put $L=r/2$, and take $0<Z<1$ and $0<\kappa<L$. After removing
the known fixed preparation phases from each coherence, write

$$
\psi=(\sqrt\kappa e^{-iu},\sqrt{L-\kappa}e^{-iv},
\sqrt Z,\sqrt{L-\kappa}e^{-iv},\sqrt\kappa e^{-iu}).
$$

Set

$$
z=C_{01}=\sqrt{\kappa(L-\kappa)}e^{i(u-v)},\qquad
w=C_{02}=\sqrt{Z\kappa}\,e^{iu}.
\tag{10.7}
$$

The other native values are $C_{03}=z$ and
$C_{1,13}=C_{3,13}=\overline z$. Thus

$$
\kappa=|w|^2/Z,\qquad e^{iu}=w/|w|,\qquad
 e^{iv}=(w/|w|)\,\overline z/|z|,
\tag{10.8}
$$

and the intrinsic response image has three coordinates, with $(z,w)$ a
four-real-coordinate embedding. The future behavioral quotient for the base-only task will be shown
in Theorem 10.6 to be

$$
\mathcal B=(0,L)\times S^1,
\qquad (\kappa,e^{i(u-v)}),
\tag{10.9}
$$

with the product topology and its evident smooth structure. At the current instant $z$ alone also identifies $\kappa$ with $L-\kappa$;
it does not already recover (10.9). The common phase is invisible to every
base observation.

Proof. The equalities follow by multiplying the amplitudes in (10.7).
The inverse (10.8) is smooth on the positive domain. The map
$(\kappa,u,v)\mapsto(\kappa,e^{i(u-v)})$ is the quotient by the free
common-phase circle, so it is a smooth principal-circle quotient and is
homeomorphic, with its actual quotient topology, to (10.9). It is not a
quotient of the entire closed square including zero amplitudes; those are
walls requiring separate charts. More explicitly, for a raw coherence set
$\delta^0_{ij}=\theta_i^{\rm in}-\theta_j^{\rm in}$ and
$\widetilde C_{ij}=e^{-i\delta^0_{ij}}C^{\rm raw}_{ij}$.
Only the tilded coherences satisfy the grouped equalities (10.7).
The notation $z,w,C$ in the double-wall formulas suppresses this tilde.
The raw doubled output is
$2R_{\delta^0_{ij}}(\operatorname{Re}\widetilde C_{ij},
\operatorname{Im}\widetilde C_{ij})^{\mathsf T}$.
These fixed rotations preserve the original Euclidean metric and do not
remove its factor two. Transport §§8.2–8.4 and Theorem 8.6 supply this
phase domain, full reconstruction and capacity. In the present notation
the response image satisfies the exact constraint

$$
|z|^2=\frac{|w|^2}{Z}\left(L-\frac{|w|^2}{Z}\right),
\qquad 0<|w|^2<ZL.
$$

The smooth section of the common-phase quotient is
$(k,\zeta)\mapsto(k,e^{iu}=\zeta,e^{iv}=1)$.
In circle coordinates the change
$(e^{iu},e^{iv})\mapsto(e^{i(u-v)},e^{iv})$ is a torus diffeomorphism.
It conjugates the quotient map to the product projection, which is open.
This proves the asserted quotient topology and smooth product structure,
not merely a set-theoretic bijection. $\square$

### 10.5 Lawful commands and what a finite signature remembers

**Theorem 10.5 (Common commands act on the base quotient).** Let
$\gamma=(a,b)$ be an absolutely continuous or piecewise $C^1$ relative path
starting at zero, and use the notation of [Transport §9.1][T10]:

$$
A=a(T),\quad B=b(T),\quad K=\int a\,db,
\quad m=\min a,\quad M=\max a.
$$

The command is legal at height $\kappa$ exactly when

$$
\kappa+m>0,\qquad \kappa+M<L.
\tag{10.10}
$$

Its endpoint, with $r=2L$, is

$$
\kappa'=\kappa+A,\quad
u'=u+B/2-(\kappa B+K)/r,\quad
v'=v-(\kappa B+K)/r.
\tag{10.11}
$$

Consequently the base quotient update is

$$
(\kappa,e^{i(u-v)})\longmapsto
(\kappa+A,e^{i(u-v)+iB/2}),
\tag{10.12}
$$

on the partial domain (10.10). The command action and its legal domain are determined by the
summary $(A,B,K,m,M)$: it determines the full partial map on $\mathcal M$
and, by descent, the partial map on $\mathcal B$. Evaluating these maps
uses the corresponding current state. The summary
need not be unique, because different paths can have the same endpoint and
phase class.

A stationary command $a=b=0$ is legal on all $(0,L)$. An out-and-back path
with $b=0$ and $a$ going from $0$ to $\varepsilon$ and back, where
$0<\varepsilon<L$, has the same $(A,B,K)=(0,0,0)$ but is legal only on
$(0,L-\varepsilon)$. Thus endpoint data alone does not determine the
partial operation.

Proof. The legality criterion and endpoint formula are the direct
specialization of [Transport Theorems 9.2 and 9.3][T10]. Subtracting the
last two equations in (10.11) gives (10.12), while the real lift retains
$K$ and the phase law. The stationary and excursion paths have the stated
extrema, proving the domain distinction. For concatenation, the supplied
chronological law is

$$
(A,B,K)\star(A',B',K')=(A+A',B+B',K+K'+AB'),
$$

with
$m''=\min(m,A+m')$ and $M''=\max(M,A+M')$; the path followed by its
reverse is the identity only on its actual lawful domain, not a globally
lawful identity command. $\square$

It has a smooth global section, while the common phase remains invisible.

Proof. For a lawful command the single independent bottom coherence is

$$
z_\gamma=\sqrt{(\kappa+A)(L-\kappa-A)}
 e^{i(u-v)+iB/2}.
\tag{10.13}
$$

Its other bottom outputs are its copy and conjugates, followed by their

$(u,v)\mapsto(u+\lambda,v+\lambda)$ stays invisible after every command;
(10.11) adds the same height-dependent common term to both candidates.

Conversely the empty command fixes $e^{i(u-v)}$ and the positive magnitude
$\sqrt{\kappa(L-\kappa)}$, leaving only the possible reflection

Its two observations satisfy

|z_A|^2-|z_0|^2=A(L-2\kappa)-A^2,
\qquad
\kappa=\frac{L-A-(|z_A|^2-|z_0|^2)/A}{2}.
\tag{10.14}

The identity follows by expanding

Therefore identical behaviors force the same height. The case

### 10.6 Future behavior and the affine obstruction

**Definition 10.7 (Behavior for the declared partial action).**
Let $\bot$ be a refusal symbol and, for the chosen current observation $F$,
define

$$
B_s(\gamma)=
\begin{cases}
F(T_\gamma s),&\gamma\text{ lawful at }s,\\
\bot,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

Commands include the empty command and concatenate with the partial domains
of Transport Theorem 9.2. Put $s\sim t$ when these functions of commands
are equal. In this definition a sufficient summary $Q$ means that every
$B_s(\gamma)$, including refusal, is a function of $Q(s)$.
Consequently $Q(s)=Q(t)$ implies $B_s=B_t$, and the map

$$
Q(s)\longmapsto B_s
$$

is well-defined on the image of $Q$. In precise factorization notation,
$\beta=\overline\beta\circ Q$ for $\beta(s)=B_s$.
Thus every sufficient summary maps to behavior, not conversely in general.
The behavior itself is sufficient by evaluation. For a common lawful
$\alpha$, concatenation gives

$$
B_{T_\alpha s}(\beta')=B_s(\alpha*\beta').
$$

This includes the refusal case because the concatenated domain is exactly
the intersection of the first domain and the pulled-back second domain.
Hence equivalent current states have equivalent successors, so the quotient
supports the dynamics. These elementary behavioral-equivalence facts are
used inside the actual task: Theorem 10.6 computes the quotient for the
base menu as $J\times S^1$, whereas including the reference computes it as
$J\times\mathbb T^2$ by the existing Theorem 9.7.

This construction resembles the general idea that objects can be tested
by their relations. Leinster's [*Basic Category Theory*, chapter 4][Y11]
formulates the Yoneda lemma using all the appropriate hom-functor data.
Only the declared outputs and common partial commands are tested here;
there is no identification with that full categorical data or conclusion
about all possible experiments.

$$
w_B(k,u)=\sqrt{Zk}\,e^{iu}e^{iB(1/2-k/r)}.
\tag{10.16}

For distinct real $B_1,\ldots,B_N$, the constant and the real and

$$
\sum_j(a_j-i b_j)e^{iB_j/2}e^{-iB_j k/r}=0.

Differentiating at any interior $k$ in orders $0,\ldots,N-1$ yields a

This retains the exact comparison: intrinsic current dimension three,

## 11. Purity certification by reference triangles and residuals

### 11.1 Unknown density matrices and the native height error

**Definition 11.1 (The mixed comparison class).**
Let $\rho\succeq0$ be a $5\times5$ matrix of trace one in the mode order
$(0,1,2,3,13)$. Write $p_i=\rho_{ii}$ and retain the orientation

$$
C_{ij}=\langle j|\rho|i\rangle=\rho_{ji}.
$$

For $\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$ this is
$C_{ij}=\overline{\psi_i}\psi_j$. The reported pairs remain
$(2\operatorname{Re}C_{ij},2\operatorname{Im}C_{ij})$.
Fix the means $X=p_1+p_{13}$, $Y=p_3+p_{13}$ and $Z=p_2>0$.
With $r=1-Z$ and $\kappa=p_{13}$ the diagonals are necessarily

$$
p=(r-X-Y+\kappa,\ X-\kappa,\ Z,\ Y-\kappa,\ \kappa).
$$

The comparison class consists of all PSD trace-one matrices with these
means. It is larger than the calibrated pure source, and no nonlinear
transport law on this entire mixed class is assumed.

**Proposition 11.2 (Native height underestimation and its reference residual).**
For a matrix in Definition 11.1 put

$$
v=\rho|2\rangle/\sqrt Z,\qquad R=\rho-vv^\dagger,\qquad
\widehat\kappa=\frac{|C_{02}|^2}{Z}-(r-X-Y).
\tag{11.1}
$$

Then $R\succeq0$, $R|2\rangle=0$ and

$$
\operatorname{rank}\rho=1+\operatorname{rank}R,\qquad
\kappa-\widehat\kappa=R_{00}\ge0.
\tag{11.2}
$$

Thus the pure-source native inverse, when applied to mixed data, returns
a lower bound whose exact error is one residual diagonal. It need not
return a legal pure-source height. Vanishing of this one error does not
imply purity; purity is equivalent to the full $R=0$.

Proof. Use the classical Schur step of [Hidden Relations C-9.5][G11],
with its positive reference block specialized to the scalar $A=Z$.
Order the reference first and write

$$
\rho=\begin{pmatrix}Z&c^\dagger\\c&B\end{pmatrix},\qquad
v=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt Z\\c/\sqrt Z\end{pmatrix},\qquad
R=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&B-cc^\dagger/Z\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For a column $(a,x)$ the quadratic form is exactly

$$
Z\left|a+\frac{c^\dagger x}{Z}\right|^2
+x^\dagger(B-cc^\dagger/Z)x.
$$

Equivalently, for the invertible matrix
$L_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\-c/Z&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
one has $L_0\rho L_0^\dagger=\operatorname{diag}(Z,B-cc^\dagger/Z)$.
This proves positivity, the reference zero column and the rank formula.
A positive trace-one matrix is pure exactly when its rank is one,
so the rank formula proves the residual criterion.

In the specified orientation $C_{02}=\rho_{20}$ whereas
$v_0=\rho_{02}/\sqrt Z=\overline{C_{02}}/\sqrt Z$.
Hence $R_{00}=p_0-|C_{02}|^2/Z$, and substituting
$p_0=r-X-Y+\kappa$ gives (11.2). In particular
$|C_{02}|^2\le Zp_0$ is the native two-by-two positivity condition.
Only the fixed means, positivity and the known positive reference
entered this inference. $\square$

### 11.2 The additional edge and the exact reference triangle

**Proposition 11.3 (The three-vertex residual condition).**
Set $w=C_{02}$, $q=C_{12}$ and $z=C_{01}$. The edge $12$ is additional
to the native menu. Define

$$

\tag{11.3}
$$

The reference residual satisfies

\tag{11.4}
$$

Consequently

$$

\left|z-\frac{w\overline q}{Z}\right|^2
\le(\kappa-\ell)(h-\kappa).
\tag{11.5}
$$

Together, these conditions are equivalent to positivity of the principal

For a pure state $R=0$ and $z=w\overline q/Z$.

### 11.3 The Gram equality step inside native certification

**Proof ingredient 11.4 (Connected saturation with a fixed target).**
The following classical equality case is used inside Theorems 11.5 and
11.8. Suppose a PSD candidate has all diagonals $p_i>0$ and satisfies
$|C_{ij}|^2=p_ip_j$ on a connected graph. Factor $\rho=V^\dagger V$
and let $e_i$ be its columns. Then $\|e_i\|^2=p_i$ and the reported
$C_{ij}$ is the transposed Gram entry. Equality in Cauchy–Schwarz makes
adjacent columns collinear. Along the graph, all columns are collinear;
hence $\rho$ has rank one.

If the diagonals and complex edges agree with a fixed pure target $\psi$,
write a pure representative of the candidate as $\phi$. The ratios
$q_i=\phi_i/\psi_i$ have modulus one and every edge gives
$\overline q_iq_j=1$. Connectedness forces a single common phase.
Thus the candidate projector is the target projector.

[Hidden Relations C-29.3][G11] uses this same Gram equality mechanism
for unit diagonal environmental records in a calibrated Schur channel.
Here the normalized entries are $\rho_{ij}/\sqrt{p_ip_j}$, and the target
phases can be removed by its known diagonal unitary. This transfers the
equality mechanism, not that source's whole-channel identity conclusion.
Cycle phases alone would not force the required modulus saturation.
This paragraph is the intermediate Gram argument for the following
certificates, not a separate general tomography theorem.

### 11.4 Exact height interval and the native purity threshold

**Theorem 11.5 (The native menu on the double wall).**
Assume $X=Y=r/2$, put $L=r/2$, and fix $0<Z<1$ and $0<\kappa_0<L$.
Use the calibrated pure family

$$
\psi(\kappa,u,v)=
(\sqrt\kappa e^{-iu},\sqrt{L-\kappa}e^{-iv},\sqrt Z,
 \sqrt{L-\kappa}e^{-iv},\sqrt\kappa e^{-iu}),
$$

with known fixed preparation phases removed as in Proposition 10.4.
Let $\pi_0$ be the projector of $\psi(\kappa_0,0,0)$.
Among all PSD trace-one matrices with the same three means and all five
native complex coherences equal to those of $\pi_0$, the possible heights
are exactly

$$
\boxed{\ \kappa\in[\kappa_0,\max(\kappa_0,L-\kappa_0)]\ }.
\tag{11.6}
$$

For every $\kappa_0\ge L/2$, including equality, the only compatible
state is $\pi_0$. For $\kappa_0<L/2$, every height strictly greater than
$\kappa_0$ in that interval has a mixed realization. At $\kappa=\kappa_0$
the candidate is again uniquely $\pi_0$.

Proof. The native reference gives
$|C_{02}|^2=Z\kappa_0\le Z\kappa$, so $\kappa\ge\kappa_0$.
A bottom edge gives
$|C_{1,13}|^2=\kappa_0(L-\kappa_0)\le\kappa(L-\kappa)$.
Since the target value is positive, any candidate lies in $(0,L)$.
Subtracting the target product gives

$$
(\kappa-\kappa_0)(L-\kappa-\kappa_0)\ge0.
$$

Together with $\kappa\ge\kappa_0$, this yields exactly the interval
in (11.6). If $\kappa_0\ge L/2$, only $\kappa=\kappa_0$ is possible.
At that height all positive diagonals agree and all five native edges
saturate the target products. The Gram step of §11.3 forces $\pi_0$.
This also proves uniqueness at the lower endpoint for a lower-half target.

For $\kappa_0<L/2$ and $\kappa_0\le\kappa\le L-\kappa_0$, define

$$
\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_0(L-\kappa_0)}{\kappa(L-\kappa)}},\qquad
\beta=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_0}{\kappa}},\qquad
a=\arccos\alpha,\qquad b=\arccos\beta.
$$

These square roots lie in $(0,1]$. Form the density matrix

$$
\rho_\kappa=\frac14\sum_{\varepsilon,\eta=\pm1}
\psi(\kappa,\varepsilon b,\varepsilon b+\eta a)
\psi(\kappa,\varepsilon b,\varepsilon b+\eta a)^\dagger.
\tag{11.7}
$$

All four components belong to one declared current source: their means
and fixed preparation are common, and Transport Theorem 8.2 realizes
the entire double-wall phase torus. Their common diagonal is
$(\kappa,L-\kappa,Z,L-\kappa,\kappa)$. Since
$u-v=-\eta a$ and $u=\varepsilon b$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E C_{01}=\mathbb E C_{03}
 &=\sqrt{\kappa(L-\kappa)}\cos a
   =\sqrt{\kappa_0(L-\kappa_0)},\\
\mathbb E C_{1,13}=\mathbb E C_{3,13}
 &=\sqrt{\kappa(L-\kappa)}\cos a,\\
\mathbb E C_{02}
 &=\sqrt{Z\kappa}\cos b=\sqrt{Z\kappa_0}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence all five target coherences are attained, proving sufficiency.
At $\kappa=\kappa_0$, $a=b=0$ and this is the target.
For $\kappa>\kappa_0$, one has $0<b<\pi/2$.
Holding $\eta$ fixed and changing $\varepsilon$ gives vectors with the
same nonzero reference component but different nonreference phases.
They are not collinear, so their positive mixture has rank at least two.
At $\kappa=L-\kappa_0$, $a=0$ is allowed and $b>0$ still gives a mixed
state when $\kappa_0<L/2$. When $\kappa_0\ge L/2$ the singleton target
is the required realization; no reflected interval is presumed.
The mixture means forgetting the preparation label, without assuming an
instrument in the native command menu performs that operation. $\square$

### 11.5 An exact rank-two example and its future separation

**Proposition 11.6 (A native-indistinguishable state of purity $59/75$).**
Take $Z=1/5$, $L=2/5$, $\kappa_0=1/10$ and $\kappa=3/10$.
The common means are $(X,Y,Z)=(2/5,2/5,1/5)$.
Here $a=0$ and $\cos b=1/\sqrt3$, so (11.7) is

$$
\rho_\kappa=
\begin{pmatrix}
3/10&\sqrt3/10&\sqrt2/10&\sqrt3/10&3/10\\
\sqrt3/10&1/10&\sqrt6/30&1/10&\sqrt3/10\\
\sqrt2/10&\sqrt6/30&1/5&\sqrt6/30&\sqrt2/10\\
\sqrt3/10&1/10&\sqrt6/30&1/10&\sqrt3/10\\
3/10&\sqrt3/10&\sqrt2/10&\sqrt3/10&3/10
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{11.8}
$$

Its spectrum is
$0,0,0,(15-\sqrt{129})/30,(15+\sqrt{129})/30$ and its purity is $59/75$.
The target projector has rank one and purity one. Both have four bottom
native coherences $\sqrt3/10$ and reference coherence $\sqrt2/10$.

Proof. Let $e=|2\rangle$ and set

$$
g=\sqrt{5/4}\,
(\sqrt{3/10},\sqrt{1/10},0,\sqrt{1/10},\sqrt{3/10})^{\mathsf T}.
$$

Then $g,e$ are orthonormal. The two distinct pure components are
$\psi_\pm=\sqrt{4/5}e^{\mp ib}g+\sqrt{1/5}e$.
Their equally weighted mixture in this basis is

$$
\begin{pmatrix}4/5&2/(5\sqrt3)\\2/(5\sqrt3)&1/5\end{pmatrix},
$$

and it vanishes on the three-dimensional orthogonal complement.
Expanding this two-by-two matrix in the original mode basis gives
every entry of (11.8), including the unmeasured $12$ and $32$ entries
$\sqrt6/30$. Its trace is one and its determinant is
$4/25-4/75=8/75>0$, so its characteristic polynomial is
$\lambda^2-\lambda+8/75$ and it has rank two.
Thus the full characteristic polynomial is

$$
\det(\lambda I-\rho_\kappa)=
\lambda^3(\lambda^2-\lambda+8/75).
$$

The quadratic discriminant is $43/75=129/225$, giving the stated
eigenvalues. Its trace square is

$$
\operatorname{tr}\rho_\kappa^2
=1-2(8/75)=59/75
=\frac{2(225+129)}{900}.
$$

The target bottom amplitudes are the square roots of
$1/10,3/10,3/10,1/10$ in modes $0,1,3,13$ and its reference
probability is $1/5$. Their products give exactly the five values
displayed for the candidate. $\square$

**Proposition 11.7 (Componentwise comparison of declared ensembles).**
For any lower-half target and mixture (11.7), apply the same phase-only
command $a(t)\equiv0$, $b(T)=B$ to each constituent pure preparation,
and separately to the target. On a component of height $\kappa$ with
initial phases $u,v$, the transport supplier gives

$$
u'=u+B(1/2-\kappa/r),\qquad v'=v-\kappa B/r,\qquad
C_{02}'=\sqrt{Z\kappa}\,e^{iu}e^{iB(1/2-\kappa/r)}.
$$

This command is legal on the whole fiber by Transport Theorem 9.2.
Averaging the componentwise readouts yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline C_{02}(B)&=\sqrt{Z\kappa_0}\,e^{iB(1/2-\kappa/r)},\\
C_{02}^{\rm target}(B)&=\sqrt{Z\kappa_0}\,e^{iB(1/2-\kappa_0/r)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

In Proposition 11.6, $B=2\pi$ gives respectively $(1+i)/10$ and
$(-1+i)/10$. Thus equal current native menus can have different futures
for these specified preparation ensembles.

Proof. Set $A=K=0$ in (10.11), retaining each component's initial
$u=\varepsilon b$. Its average phase factor is
$\cos b=\sqrt{\kappa_0/\kappa}$, proving the first formula. The target
has initial phase zero and its own height $\kappa_0$, proving the second.
For the example $r=4/5$, the frequencies are $1/8$ and $3/8$, while
both initial reference values are $\sqrt2/10$. Multiplication by
$e^{i\pi/4}$ or $e^{i3\pi/4}$ gives the two exact values.

This comparison applies the command componentwise to the explicitly
declared ensembles. It neither defines a universal state-independent
CPTP map on arbitrary mixed matrices nor claims that different
decompositions of one mixed matrix acquire the same nonlinear future.
The retained preparation label is essential to this statement. $\square$

### 11.6 One additional edge certifies a full-support pure target

**Theorem 11.8 (Native menu plus the $12$ coherence).**
Fix any legal means with $Z>0$, not necessarily on the double wall.
Let $\pi_0=|\psi_0\rangle\langle\psi_0|$ be a full-support pure target
of height $\kappa_0$. If a PSD trace-one candidate has these means,
the same five native complex coherences as $\pi_0$, and the same
additional complex coherence $C_{12}$, then it equals $\pi_0$.
This is a six-complex-edge, twelve-real-output menu: only $12$ is new.

Proof. The $02$ and $12$ principal minors imply

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z(r-X-Y+\kappa_0)=|C_{02}|^2
 &\le Z(r-X-Y+\kappa),\\
Z(X-\kappa_0)=|C_{12}|^2
 &\le Z(X-\kappa).
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $Z>0$, these force both $\kappa\ge\kappa_0$ and
$\kappa\le\kappa_0$. All five probabilities now equal the positive
target probabilities. The original connected native graph has every
edge saturated, so the Gram step in §11.3 gives rank one and the target
phases give the same projector.

For a pure target the triangle also satisfies

$$
C_{01}=\frac{C_{02}\overline{C_{12}}}{Z},
$$

because $C_{02}\overline{C_{12}}=
\overline{\psi_0}\psi_1|\psi_2|^2$.
Knowing that identity for a pure target does not replace measuring
the extra edge on an arbitrary PSD candidate: (11.4) has an unknown
residual. Positivity, the means and the known positive reference
supply the opposite height bounds. Merely knowing an odd-cycle phase
would not supply them. This additional edge is not an original native
readout, and is distinct from the full reference star below. $\square$

### 11.7 Native instability and uniform two-reference recovery

**Proposition 11.9 (Failure of a global native inverse modulus).**
At fixed $0<Z<1$, compare the pure states
$\psi(\kappa,0,0)$ and $\psi(\kappa,0,\pi)$ for $0<\kappa<L$.
Their doubled-output Euclidean distance is

$$
8\sqrt{\kappa(L-\kappa)}\longrightarrow0\qquad(\kappa\downarrow0),
$$

but their trace distance is

$$
D=\sqrt{1-(4\kappa+2Z-1)^2}
\longrightarrow2\sqrt{Z(1-Z)}>0.
\tag{11.9}
$$

Consequently the native inverse is not uniformly continuous on the whole
open pure source. The same conclusion holds for any fixed finite stack
of commands that are each globally lawful on this source.

Proof. Write $s=\sqrt{\kappa(L-\kappa)}$.
Each of the four bottom coherences changes to its negative, and its
doubled real pair changes by norm $4s$. The four squared contributions
sum to $64s^2$; the $02$ coherence agrees. Fixed preparation rotations
are orthogonal and preserve this norm.
The pure vectors differ by a sign in modes $1,3$, hence have overlap
$2\kappa+Z-2(L-\kappa)=4\kappa+2Z-1$.

The classical pure-state trace formula, with
$D(\rho,\sigma)=\frac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1$, is
$D(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|,|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|)
=\sqrt{1-|\langle\psi,\phi\rangle|^2}$.
It is the equality case underlying the fidelity bounds of
[Fuchs–van de Graaf][FV11]. Directly, the difference of two pure
projectors is supported on their span, is traceless, and has trace square
$2(1-|\langle\psi,\phi\rangle|^2)$; its two possibly nonzero eigenvalues
are the positive and negative square roots in that formula.
Substitution proves (11.9).

For the command claim, Transport Theorem 9.2 says a relative command
has full lawful domain exactly when $a\equiv0$.
Indeed $m\le0\le M$, and including all of $(0,L)$ in (10.10) forces
$m=M=0$. Such commands may have arbitrary finite phase increment $B$,
but have $A=K=0$. At the same height the two candidates acquire the same
increment of $u$ and the same increment of $v$. The four bottom edges
stay opposite, with unchanged magnitude, and the reference edges agree.
Every future menu thus has the same norm difference $8s$.

For a fixed collection of $N$ commands, including fixed finite
concatenations, the Euclidean stack has difference $8\sqrt N\,s\to0$;
the maximum norm over commands has difference $8s\to0$. The ray distance
still has the positive limit in (11.9).
This is a fixed finite assertion, not a summation of an infinite
unweighted squared norm. An interior-height promise or adaptive sampling
and feedback defines a different task. $\square$

**Theorem 11.10 (Stable two-reference recovery of the calibrated pure family).**
On the known symmetric double-wall pure source with fixed $Z>0$, retain

$$
w=C_{02}=\sqrt{Z\kappa}\,e^{iu},\qquad
q=C_{12}=\sqrt{Z(L-\kappa)}\,e^{iv}.
$$

In the reference gauge one has the affine recovery

$$
\psi=\frac1{\sqrt Z}(\overline w,\overline q,Z,\overline q,\overline w).
$$

For $Y^*=(2\operatorname{Re}w,2\operatorname{Im}w,
2\operatorname{Re}q,2\operatorname{Im}q)$ and two states of this same
fixed-$Z$ family,

$$
\|\psi-\psi'\|^2
=\frac2Z(|w-w'|^2+|q-q'|^2)
=\frac{\|Y^*-Y^{*\prime}\|^2}{2Z},
\qquad
D(\psi,\psi')\le\frac{\|Y^*-Y^{*\prime}\|}{\sqrt{2Z}}.
\tag{11.10}
$$

The bound is uniform on the entire ray closure $0\le\kappa\le L$ for
fixed $Z$. Its constant diverges as $Z\downarrow0$; no uniformity in
that additional parameter is asserted.

Proof. Division by the fixed $\sqrt Z$ recovers the two distinct
nonreference amplitudes; the known symmetry duplicates them in the
indicated modes. The constraint $|w|^2+|q|^2=ZL$ makes the vector
normalized. Summing its component differences gives the norm equality.
For $a=\langle\psi,\psi'\rangle$,

$$
D^2=1-|a|^2\le 2-2\operatorname{Re}a=\|\psi-\psi'\|^2,
$$

because $|1-a|^2\ge0$. This gives the distance estimate.
The same argument applies after undoing the single known preparation
diagonal unitary, which preserves the norm and ray distance.

At $\kappa=0,L$ the reference stays nonzero and the formulas divide by
neither $\kappa$ nor $L-\kappa$. Zero-amplitude phases disappear at the
level of rays; the formulas extend continuously to precisely this ray
closure. Two reference coherences recover and stabilize a known pure
family. For certification against arbitrary mixed matrices, Theorem 11.8
uses the native menu plus $12$ and the calibrated means instead.
Current affine recovery also does not provide a finite shared affine
decoder for all future outputs: (10.16) still supplies arbitrarily many
independent future frequencies by Transport Theorem 9.8. $\square$

### 11.8 An exact residual bound from a full reference star

**Theorem 11.11 (Arbitrary-rank star certificate).**
Let $\rho$ be any PSD trace-one matrix with known $Z=\rho_{22}>0$.
Suppose all four exact coherences $C_{i2}$, $i\ne2$, are known and
consistent with this same matrix. Define

$$
\varepsilon_{\rm ref}
=1-Z-\frac1Z\sum_{i\ne2}|C_{i2}|^2=\operatorname{tr}R.
\tag{11.11}
$$

Then $0\le\varepsilon_{\rm ref}\le1-Z<1$ and

$$
\|v\|^2=1-\varepsilon_{\rm ref}\ge Z>0,\qquad
\widehat\psi=v/\|v\|.
$$

The state is pure exactly when $\varepsilon_{\rm ref}=0$.
For $\varepsilon_{\rm ref}>0$, $\sigma=R/\varepsilon_{\rm ref}$ is a
density matrix and

$$
\rho=(1-\varepsilon_{\rm ref})|\widehat\psi\rangle\langle\widehat\psi|
+\varepsilon_{\rm ref}\sigma,\qquad
D(\rho,|\widehat\psi\rangle\langle\widehat\psi|)
\le\varepsilon_{\rm ref}.
$$

Proof. In the orientation of Definition 11.1,
$v_i=\overline{C_{i2}}/\sqrt Z$ for $i\ne2$, and $v_2=\sqrt Z$.
Thus the star and $Z$ determine $v$, and

$$
\|v\|^2=Z+\frac1Z\sum_{i\ne2}|C_{i2}|^2=1-\varepsilon_{\rm ref}.
$$

Proposition 11.2 gives $R\succeq0$, and its trace gives (11.11).
The positive reference component gives the lower bound on $\|v\|^2$.
If $\operatorname{tr}R=0$, every nonnegative eigenvalue of $R$ vanishes,
so $R=0$ and the state is pure; the converse follows from the rank
formula. For positive trace, normalization of $R$ gives $\sigma$ and
the displayed convex decomposition. Finally

$$
D(\rho,|\widehat\psi\rangle\langle\widehat\psi|)
=\varepsilon_{\rm ref}D(\sigma,|\widehat\psi\rangle\langle\widehat\psi|)
\le\varepsilon_{\rm ref},
$$

using the trace-norm triangle inequality and the unit traces of the
two positive matrices.

The full star is $\{02,12,32,(13)2\}$. Its last three edges are additional
to the original native menu; reversed orientations can be conjugated.
Together with the native menu the union has eight complex edges.
The star certificate itself needs only those four coherences and $Z$,
not the other individual probabilities or means. It is a deterministic
bound for exact, mutually consistent data at arbitrary rank.
Substituting noisy entries without a separate positivity and statistical
model does not give a finite-shot confidence interval. $\square$

The relevant distinction between recovery within a rank-constrained family
and identification against all PSD states is the distinction called
rank completeness and strict completeness by Baldwin–Deutsch–Kalev,
[*Strictly-complete measurements for bounded-rank quantum-state
tomography*, §II, equations (1)–(2), and §IV.1][SC11]. Their rank bound
is specialized to one; their comparison sets are respectively rank-at-most-one
PSD matrices and all PSD matrices. Their general measurement framework is an intermediate
comparison here; it does not supply the fixed-mean height interval,
the particular additional $12$ edge or the componentwise future law.
The Schur and Gram inputs in §§11.1–11.3 are the mechanisms specialized
to these five modes. Likewise the current source and phase increments
are exactly Transport Theorems 8.2, 8.4, 9.2 and 9.3.

The behavioral terminology of §10.6 uses Leinster,
[*Basic Category Theory*, chapter 4][Y11], only for the scope of the
Yoneda comparison. Restricted coherences and partial commands do not
constitute the full hom-functor data of that lemma.
The trace-distance input is the pure specialization of
Fuchs–van de Graaf, [*Cryptographic Distinguishability Measures for
Quantum Mechanical States*, Theorem 1, equation (46)][FV11]. Their
Bhattacharyya fidelity $B$ is $|\langle\psi,\phi\rangle|$ for pure states,
and their trace distance is the same $\frac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1$ used here;
the direct two-projector calculation
above fixes its normalization for the present estimates.
