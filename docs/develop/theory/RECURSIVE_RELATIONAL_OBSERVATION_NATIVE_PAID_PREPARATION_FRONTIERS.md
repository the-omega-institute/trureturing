**Definition 1.2 (actual source, action and observation).** Let

$$
\mathcal T::=\alpha\mid\beta\mid\langle\mathcal T,\mathcal T\rangle,
\qquad h(\alpha)=h(\beta)=0,
\qquad h(\langle u,v\rangle)=1+\max\{h(u),h(v)\}.
\tag{PPF.1}
$$

Pairing is free and ordered. Every finite word over $L,R$, including
$\varepsilon$, is a legal original-root literal. Its actual reply
$r_t(w)$ is alpha or beta at that leaf, branch at a pair, and absent
after traversal passes a leaf. Thus the observation alphabet is exactly

$$
\mathcal A=\{\alpha,\beta,\mathsf{branch},\mathsf{absent}\}.
\tag{PPF.2}
$$

The homomorphic substitution and the full original families are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho(\alpha)&=\beta,&\rho(\beta)&=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,&
\rho(\langle u,v\rangle)&=\langle\rho(u),\rho(v)\rangle,\\
\mathcal F_D&=\{\langle\alpha,v\rangle:h(v)\le D\},&
W_D&=\{q:|q|\le D-1\},&\mathcal P_D&=\{Rq:q\in W_D\},
\end{aligned}
\tag{PPF.3}
$$

where $D\ge1$ is public. One actual initial $t\in\mathcal F_D$
remains stationary through preparation and late acquisition. The iterates
$\rho^kt$ define prediction targets, rather than changes to that
register. No subtree, equality, source-identity, age, reset, copying,

**Assumption 1.3 (complete acquired export and late entry).** Preparation
is one deterministic effective source-blind procedure, initialized from
$D$ and common source-independent installed data, without the later
request. It acquires source dependence only by charged literal Reads,
terminates on every $t\in\mathcal F_D$, and exports a finite code
$E(t)$. This code includes every accessible surviving source distinction:
selectors, control, operands, buffers, lengths, allocation, handles,
counters and accessible timing. All other accessible fields are functions
of the code and public inputs. Separately paid export, handoff and
access-isolation services make all other preparation dependence,
including transcripts, inaccessible and supply lawful reader entry.

Only after export, a public source-independent request
$p\in\mathcal P_D$ is supplied. A common deterministic effective
reader uses only $E(t),D,p$ and common tables to choose zero Reads or
one fresh Read at any finite original-root word on the same stationary
$t$. The choice precedes its reply and every supplied count. The reader
then produces a final predictor $Z$ satisfying

$$
\operatorname{Dec}(Z,D,p,k)=r_{\rho^kt}(p)
\quad(t\in\mathcal F_D,\ p\in\mathcal P_D,\ k\ge0),
\tag{PPF.4}
$$

for every subsequently supplied authentic $k$, without further Reads.
Correctness is simultaneous over all these sources and targets. Export,
isolation, continuity, authentication and entry are declared paid services,
without an asserted physical realization. Define exactly

$$
M_1(D)=\min |E[\mathcal F_D]|.
\tag{PPF.5}
$$

Only the complete attained alphabet is minimized in (PPF.5).

**Definition 1.4 (the three preparation charges).** For a correct
preparation/reader pair $A$, write its actual preparation submissions on
$t$ as $w_1,\ldots,w_{T_A(t)}$. Put

$$
L_A(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{T_A(t)}|w_j|,
\qquad H_A(t)=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{|w_j|:1\le j\le T_A(t)\}\bigr).
\tag{PPF.6}
$$

Every Read counts in $T_A$, including repeated, absent, constant and
empty-word calls. An empty word costs zero letters. With no Reads all
three charges are zero. Their worst-case values are maxima over
$\mathcal F_D$, which is finite. Preparation work and control,
installation, temporary storage, post-request workspace, predictor size,
address construction, authentic-count representation, access services and
physical time are distinct resources.

## 2. Credited temporal factors and canonical covers

**Convention 2.1 (the exact all-count quotient).** Use AAR Definition
12.2, Theorem 12.3 and Proposition 13.3. Put
$s(r)=\#L(r)+2\#R(r)$ and $e(\alpha)=0,e(\beta)=1$. If $q$
ends at an original branch, let $\Gamma_q(u)=(0,0)$. Otherwise its
path has a first original leaf $\ell$ at $a$, and $q=ar$. Set

$$
\Gamma_q(u)=
\begin{cases}
(2-e(\ell),1-e(\ell)),&r=\varepsilon,\\
(s(r)+2-e(\ell),\mathbf1_{r\text{ ends in }R}),&r\ne\varepsilon.
\end{cases}
\tag{PPF.7}
$$

The credited decoder on attained factors is

$$
\operatorname{Pred}((b,c),k)=
\begin{cases}
\mathsf{branch},&k\ge b,\\
\beta,&b\ge1,\ k=b-1,\\
\alpha,&c=1,\ k=b-2,\\
\mathsf{absent},&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{PPF.8}
$$

Here $c=1$ implies $b\ge2$. It equals $r_{\rho^ku}(q)$ for every
$k\ge0$. Conversely the trajectory recovers $b$ as its first branch
count and $c$ by whether an alpha stage occurs. Thus distinct factors
mean distinct full trajectories, including initially absent targets.
For the original wrapper,

$$
\Gamma_{Rq}(\langle\alpha,v\rangle)=\Gamma_q(v),\qquad
r_{\rho^k\langle\alpha,v\rangle}(Rq)=r_{\rho^kv}(q).
\tag{PPF.9}
$$

These are supplied temporal facts, not new factor or fixed-menu theorems.

**Convention 2.2 (actual finite canonical representatives).** Write

$$
S_1=\{\alpha,\beta,C_0\},\qquad
S_{m+1}=\{\alpha,\beta\}\sqcup

\tag{PPF.10}
$$

Hence $N_1=3$ and $N_{m+1}=2+N_m^2$. For $h(v)\le D$ and

**Convention 2.3 (the credited exact cover consumer).** A nonempty

\forall q\in W_D\ \exists a\in W_D\cup\{\bot\}\quad

r_x(a)=r_y(a)\Longrightarrow\Gamma_q(x)=\Gamma_q(y).

For $a=\bot$, $r_x(a)$ is one common formal symbol, modeling zero

## 3. Every correct preparer must enter each replaceable actual cone

**Definition 3.1 (actual occurrence and literal cone).** If $a$ is an
actually present relative address in $v$, its original-root literal
cone in $t=\langle\alpha,v\rangle$ is

$$
\mathcal C(a)=\{Raw:w\in\{L,R\}^{<\omega}\}.
\tag{PPF.12}
$$

The empty $a$ is allowed. A set of addresses is prefix-free when no
distinct member is a prefix of another. Occurrences, replacements and
cones below are proof objects; they are not inputs or new actions
available to a preparer.

**Theorem 3.2 (pointwise all-competitor cone acquisition).** Let $D\ge2$,
let $A$ be any correct pair under Assumption 1.3, and fix any actual
$t=\langle\alpha,v\rangle\in\mathcal F_D$. For every actually present
$a$ in $v$ with $|a|\le D-2$, the preparation execution on this same
$t$ submits at least one word in $\mathcal C(a)$. Consequently, for
every prefix-free set $B$ of such addresses,

$$
T_A(t)\ge|B|,\qquad
L_A(t)\ge\sum_{a\in B}(1+|a|),
\qquad
H_A(t)\ge\max_{a\in B}(1+|a|)\quad(B\ne\varnothing).
\tag{PPF.13}
$$

There is no upper bound on retained names, tables, work or late query
length in this statement. The later Read may be anywhere in the
original source.

Proof. Fix $a$ and suppose that the actual preparation execution never
submits a word in its cone. Replace only the subtree at that actual
occurrence by each of

$$
u_0=\alpha,\quad u_1=\beta,\quad
u_2=\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle,\quad
u_3=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,\quad
u_4=\langle\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle,\alpha\rangle.
\tag{PPF.14}
$$

Call the resulting sources $t_0,\ldots,t_4$. All five replacement
heights are at most two. Their insertion depth in $v$ is $|a|$, so
$|a|+2\le D$ keeps every new source in the same $\mathcal F_D$.
Every other subtree and every strict ancestor constructor is unchanged.
Each literal outside $\mathcal C(a)$ therefore has exactly the same
reply on the original source and all five replacements.

Induct on the deterministic preparation execution. Its common initial
state agrees on the six inputs. At each step, equality of the state
forces the same local action, query or halt; every query along the
original execution lies outside the cone and has equal replies. Thus
the whole preparation execution, termination and complete exported
value agree on $t_0,\ldots,t_4$. This uses actual charged observation
as the only source-dependent action.

Now use the single fixed public request $p=RaL$. Its length is
$|a|+2\le D$, so it is an original permitted request on every one
of those five sources. Homomorphic transport at the present occurrence
$Ra$ gives the five respective target factors

$$
\Gamma_L(u_0)=(3,0),\quad
\Gamma_L(u_1)=(2,0),\quad
\Gamma_L(u_2)=(2,1),\quad
\Gamma_L(u_3)=(1,0),\quad
\Gamma_L(u_4)=(0,0).
\tag{PPF.15}
$$

These values follow directly from the credited formula (PPF.7); the
last target ends at an original branch. They are five distinct
trajectories by Convention 2.1.

The five sources have identical $E,D,p$. A zero-Read reader produces
one predictor. Otherwise it submits the same single arbitrary finite
word on all five. Its reply has at most four values, so two sources
give the same reply and final predictor. The same predictor cannot
give their distinct full trajectories at every subsequently supplied
$k$. No count was available when the query was selected. Both branches
contradict simultaneous correctness, proving that the cone was entered.

The word $p$ is fixed throughout this five-source comparison.
Universal correctness for every public request licenses that comparison;
no execution obtains a request selected from its hidden source. Likewise
the replacements compare five possible stationary inputs, without
modifying a source during an execution.

For prefix-free $B$ the literal cones are disjoint. A submission enters
at most one of them, and a submission entering $\mathcal C(a)$ has
length at least $1+|a|$. Summing these charges and taking their maximum
proves (PPF.13). $\square$

**Corollary 3.3 (one actual worst-case witness).** For every $D\ge2$
and every correct original pair $A$,

$$
\max_t T_A(t)\ge2^{D-2},\qquad
\max_t L_A(t)\ge(D-1)2^{D-2},\qquad
\max_t H_A(t)\ge D-1.
\tag{PPF.16}
$$

The same actual source witnesses all three bounds.

Proof. Let $v$ be the perfect ordered tree of height $D-2$ whose
frontier leaves are all alpha, and let $t=\langle\alpha,v\rangle$.
Its $2^{D-2}$ relative frontier addresses form a prefix-free $B$,
all actually present and of length $D-2$. Apply Theorem 3.2 on this
source. The five-source comparison remains within $\mathcal F_D$
even though this selected witness itself has smaller height. No
product-fiber condition is imposed on $A$. $\square$

## 4. Paid boundary acquisition and exact unrestricted projections

**Lemma 4.1 (finite boundary acquisition at a possibly absent address).**
For every actual stationary tree $t$, every public original-root word
$a$ and every integer $h\ge0$, there is one deterministic source-blind
procedure acquiring the entire table

$$
B_h(t,a)=\bigl(r_t(aw)\bigr)_{|w|\le h}
\tag{PPF.17}
$$

using only actual literal Reads, with

$$
T\le2^h,\qquad H\le|a|+h,\qquad L\le(|a|+h)2^h.
\tag{PPF.18}
$$

Presence of $a$ is not a premise. The procedure neither navigates to a
subtree nor receives an acquired array as input. This is a particular
full-prefix acquisition method of AAR §16; the statement records the
explicit implementation and charges needed here.

Proof. Define $\operatorname{Acquire}(a,h)$ recursively. At $h=0$,
Read the full word $a$ and return its one reply. At $h>0$, first
run $\operatorname{Acquire}(aL,h-1)$.

If its root reply $r_t(aL)$ is absent, $a$ cannot be a branch:
a branch has two present children. Read $a$. Its reply is alpha,
beta or absent, and every strict descendant of $a$ is absent. This
determines (PPF.17), including its entire right part, without a
right-side acquisition.

If $r_t(aL)$ is nonabsent, $a$ is necessarily a present branch.
Run $\operatorname{Acquire}(aR,h-1)$ and combine both acquired
child tables with the inferred branch reply at $a$. This determines
exactly (PPF.17). The inference uses the actual reply already paid
for; it is not a navigation action.

Induction on $h$ proves termination and correctness in both cases,
including an absent $a$. The first case uses at most
$2^{h-1}+1\le2^h$ Reads, and the second at most
$2\cdot2^{h-1}=2^h$. Every recursive submitted word has length at
most $|a|+h$; the extra parent word has length $|a|$. This proves
the peak-length bound, and multiplying that bound by the number of
submissions proves the letter bound. $\square$

**Theorem 4.2 (exact three worst-case projections without a name cap).**
Let $\mathfrak A_D$ be all correct original pairs under Assumption 1.3,
with arbitrary finite complete attained alphabets. For every $D\ge2$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min_{A\in\mathfrak A_D}\max_{t\in\mathcal F_D}T_A(t)&=2^{D-2},\\
\min_{A\in\mathfrak A_D}\max_{t\in\mathcal F_D}L_A(t)&=(D-1)2^{D-2},\\
\min_{A\in\mathfrak A_D}\max_{t\in\mathcal F_D}H_A(t)&=D-1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{PPF.19}
$$

One correct pair attains all three minima simultaneously and has exactly
$N_{D-1}$ complete attained names. At $D=1$ the three minima are zero
and one name suffices.

Proof of acquisition and names. For $D\ge2$, before any request run
$\operatorname{Acquire}(R,D-2)$ on the actual
$t=\langle\alpha,v\rangle$. Its table gives exactly the canonical
representative $e=\pi_{D-1}(v)\in S_{D-1}$, by Convention 2.2.
Export only its fixed-width public dictionary index. No actual subtree
is supplied to the algorithm: every observation used the full
original-root word produced in Lemma 4.1. Every element of $S_{D-1}$
occurs as an actual $v$ and is fixed by $\pi_{D-1}$, so all and only
$N_{D-1}$ names are attained. The other acquired distinctions are
isolated under Assumption 1.3.

Proof of the late reader. After any $p=Rq$ arrives, $|q|\le D-1$.
If $|q|\le D-2$, its full factor is determined by the acquired
boundary, so compute $\Gamma_q(e)=\Gamma_q(v)$ and use zero Reads.
For $|q|=D-1$, inspect its strict prefixes, all in that boundary.
If one is a leaf, its first such label and remaining suffix determine
$\Gamma_q(v)$ by (PPF.7), again with zero Reads. If no strict prefix
is a leaf, all those prefixes are present branches: starting from the
present root $v$, induct along the path, since a child of a branch
is present. Thus $q$ is actually present. Submit the one original
word $p=Rq$. Its reply is alpha, beta or branch and gives respectively

$$
(2,1),\qquad(1,0),\qquad(0,0).
\tag{PPF.20}
$$

These are exactly the factors at a present endpoint. This construction
serves every permitted request on every variable shape, including
requests initially absent because of an earlier leaf. Its query choice
depends only on the exported name, $D,p$ and public data, before the
reply and every count. Equation (PPF.8) supplies every subsequently
authenticated $k$ without another Read.

Proof of the extrema. Lemma 4.1 with $|R|=1,h=D-2$ gives the three
simultaneous upper bounds in (PPF.19). Corollary 3.3 gives their lower
bounds for every pair. Consequently these upper bounds are attained
worst-case values, as well as bounds on every actual execution.

At $D=1$ the only request is $R$. Preparation exports a constant,
uses zero Reads, and the late reader Reads $R$ once. The three
nonabsent replies give (PPF.20) and then every count. All preparation
charges are nonnegative, proving the zero minima. $\square$

The larger retained code in this theorem is paid for by its actual
boundary acquisition. The result concerns the late-target interface,
where one further Read remains. AAR's already supplied full-menu
zero-later-Read endpoint has a different acquisition obligation.

## 5. Improved acquisition while preserving exact original name optimality

**Theorem 5.1 (the exact $M_1(D)$ alphabet with a smaller sufficient scan).**
There is one deterministic effective construction from public $D$ of a
correct original preparation and reader with complete attained alphabet
exactly $M_1(D)$. For every $D\ge2$ and every $t\in\mathcal F_D$,
its preparation satisfies

$$
T_{\rm prep}(t)\le2^{D-1},\qquad
L_{\rm prep}(t)\le D2^{D-1},\qquad
H_{\rm prep}(t)\le D.
\tag{PPF.21}
$$

Every source-bearing late query has length at most $D$. At $D=1$
the constant-name zero-preparation construction in Theorem 4.2 already
attains $M_1(1)=1$.

Proof. Use the credited FMR/CBN exact minimum admissible cover of
$S_D$, with public ordering, per-target witnesses and reply-to-factor
tables. Generate it by finite enumeration from $D$. This common
installation depends on no unknown actual source and precedes use of
its selected name. Its size and generation cost are separate resources.

Replace the credited full scan of every word $Rq$, $q\in W_D$,
by $\operatorname{Acquire}(R,D-1)$. Lemma 4.1 acquires the same
entire actual reply table on $W_D$, hence the same
$\pi_D(v)\in S_D$, with the three charges (PPF.21). Compute its
first-containing-cover-member index and export only that complete name.

After the request, use the installed witness for that member and target:
a constant uses zero Reads, and a boundary witness $a\in W_D$ uses
the one actual word $Ra$. Canonicalization preserves that reply and
the requested full factor on the entire assigned original fiber.
Equation (PPF.8) gives every authentic subsequent count. Thus only
the acquisition algorithm has been replaced; no member, donor,
decoder, original target or correlated fiber has been independently
projected or restricted.

A minimum cover has no redundant member. Each member has an element
covered by none of the others, since otherwise it could be deleted.
Every member therefore receives some point under first-membership
naming, in any public ordering. Each canonical point is attained by
an actual original source. Thus the complete exported alphabet has
exactly the cover minimum $M_1(D)$, not merely an upper bound on it.
The construction is effective uniformly in $D$, since all the finite
enumerations, acquisitions and table computations terminate.
$\square$

**Corollary 5.2 (name-constrained preparation intervals).** Minimize each
worst preparation charge separately over correct pairs with exactly
$M_1(D)$ attained names, and denote the resulting numbers by
$T_M(D),L_M(D),H_M(D)$. For every $D\ge2$,

$$
\begin{aligned}

(D-1)2^{D-2}&\le L_M(D)\le D2^{D-1},\\
D-1&\le H_M(D)\le D.
\end{aligned}
\tag{PPF.22}
$$

The same name-optimal construction attains all the stated upper bounds
as guarantees. No exact value for these constrained minima is asserted.

Proof. Apply Corollary 3.3 to this subclass for the lower bounds and
Theorem 5.1 for the simultaneous upper bounds. The subclass is nonempty
by that theorem, and each minimized charge is an integer, so its
minimum exists. $\square$

**Proposition 5.3 (the two explicit codes have different objectives).**
At $D=3$ the preparation-optimal construction in Theorem 4.2 has

This comparison proves that the displayed preparation-optimal code and

$(D-2)2^{D-1}+1>0$; the peak bound remains $D$. This changes no value
of $M_1(D)$ and no memory-rate endpoint.

## 6. Complete exported state and the recoverable relation

**Convention 6.1 (counted acquisition and isolated dependence).** Both
constructions begin from public $D$ and common tables and actually
acquire every source-dependent table entry they use by the charged

materials. Export is only the fixed-width name declared in the theorem.

used at each fixed $D$; finite reply histories and bounded acquisition

The supplied paid handoff/isolation service removes all other accessible
source dependence, including preparation histories and timing. If any
additional acquired distinction survives, it belongs in the complete
attained alphabet and the stated name count no longer describes that
implementation.

The scan adapts its actual queries to paid replies. Its queries are
whole original-root strings, never movement to a cursor or a copied
subtree. Repeated and absent submissions would still be charged. The
proof's alternate inputs, canonical representatives and cover members
are mathematical comparison objects; they create no readable source
register or free acquired shape. The same stationary actual $t$
underlies preparation and its one possible late Read.

**Convention 6.2 (separate resource coordinates).** For Theorem 4.2 the

names. Common codebooks and their generation, installation, membership

The final factor alone satisfies $0\le b\le2D$, $c\in\{0,1\}$ and

FMR/CBN. The authentic count input and its representation are separate.

**Proposition 6.3 (observer-relative recovery).** Under the declared paid
services, both constructions implement exactly

$$
(E(t),D,p,\text{zero or one actual reply})
\longrightarrow\Gamma_p(t)
\longleftrightarrow\bigl(r_{\rho^kt}(p)\bigr)_{k\ge0}.
\tag{PPF.23}
$$

The inverse on the right is first branch count and alpha-stage presence.

Proof. Theorem 4.2 and the credited cover witnesses used in Theorem 5.1

The recovered relation is the specified predictive boundary of one

## 7. Exact source-domain boundary for finite predecessor synthesis

**Proposition 7.1 (failure of an identity-port substitution).** For every
$D\ge1$,

$$
\mathcal F_D\cap\rho(\mathcal T)=\varnothing.
\tag{PPF.24}
$$

Consequently one cannot instantiate a predecessor-recovery service whose
actual runtime source is $\rho(u)$ by identifying that very runtime
source with any unchanged original $t\in\mathcal F_D$.

Proof. For every $u$, $\rho(u)\ne\alpha$: an alpha maps to beta,
a beta maps to a pair, and a pair maps to a pair. If $u=\alpha$,
its image is a leaf and cannot have the required original shape. If
$u=\beta$, its image is $\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle$, with left
child beta. If $u=\langle x,y\rangle$, its image has left child
$\rho(x)\ne\alpha$. None equals $\langle\alpha,v\rangle$.
$\square$

## 9. Credited frontier and the unresolved all-size bridge

**Convention 9.1 (supplied results that retain their own consumers).**
[NPC, Theorems 5.3 and 6.2][NPC] gives semantic product-pressure

For the unrestricted joint integral covers $K_d(n)$ defined below,
reuse FMR's rate definitions and existence theorem:

$$
\sigma_d=\inf_{n\ge1}\frac{\log_2K_d(n)}n,\qquad
\lambda=\lim_{D\to\infty}2^{-D}\log_2M_1(D)
       =\sup_{d\ge1}2^{-d}\sigma_d.
\tag{PPF.25}
$$

[NCE, §§13–19][NCE] supplies the positive asymmetric depth-three law

\quad(D\ge3),\qquad M_1(6)\ge1874.
\tag{PPF.26}
$$

**Open problem 9.2 (the original effective all-size obligation).** At
fixed $d$, put $S=S_d$. For every length $n\ge1$, a complete native
template $\Theta$ chooses for every $(i,q)\in[n]\times W_d$ a
constant factor or an equation
$\Gamma_q(x_i)=\delta(r_{x_j}(a))$, with a total four-reply decoder,
$j\in[n]$ and $a\in W_d$. Let $C_\Theta\subseteq S^n$ be its
simultaneous solution set. All shared references, arbitrary correlated
solutions, off-target donors, guards, cycles and compatibility zeros
are retained. Each full block weight is used once. For a probability
$\mu$ define

$$
\beta_d(\mu)=\sup_{n\ge1}\max_\Theta
\left(\sum_{x\in C_\Theta}\prod_{i=1}^n\mu(x_i)\right)^{1/n},
\qquad b_d=\min_\mu\beta_d(\mu)=2^{-\sigma_d}.
\tag{PPF.28}
$$

The equality is credited NPC semantic completeness. Its effective
consumer still requires a total computable predicate $V(d,\mu,C,\pi)$
on rational probability vectors, rational $C>0$ and finite certificates,
whose acceptance implies

$$
\forall n\ge1\ \forall\Theta,\qquad
\sum_{x\in C_\Theta}\prod_i\mu(x_i)\le C^n,
\tag{PPF.29}
$$

and whose accepted endpoints are cofinal: for each $d\ge1$ and
rational $\epsilon>0$, some accepted input satisfies
$-\log_2C>\sigma_d-\epsilon$. Alternatively one needs a uniform
effective vanishing error in
$\sigma_d\ge n^{-1}\log_2K_d(n)-\eta_d(n)$, where $K_d(n)$ is
the unrestricted native integral-cover minimum. These are the original
[NPC Definition 8.1][NPC], [GDB Definition 11.1][GDB] and
[COK Open problems 9.3 and 25.2][COK] obligations.

Theorems 3.2–5.1 supply no such universal cap, cofinality proof, length
modulus or equality between the conditional-owner envelope optimum and
$b_d$.
They determine preparation-resource projections and reduce sufficient
acquisition at exact $M_1(D)$; finite cover generation remains separate
from effective convergence of the original rate. The constrained
preparation minima in (PPF.22), a joint Pareto frontier of names and
acquisition, general exact $M_1(D)$, sharp $\lambda$, terminating
two-sided rate approximation and full mutual recovery remain
unresolved. The precise source-domain and budget obstructions in §7
do not strengthen these open boundaries into global impossibility.
