# Quantum origin: whole two-pure balls and global certificates VI

## 89. The original carrier and the embedded pair spheres

**Definition 89.1 (Whole-carrier L3 hypotheses).** Let $V\ne0$ be a
finite-dimensional real vector space, $C\subset V$ a closed, pointed,
generating convex cone, and $u\in V^*$ strictly positive on
$C\setminus\{0\}$. Throughout this volume retain exactly

$$
K=C\cap\{u=1\},\qquad P=\operatorname{ext}K,\qquad
E=[0,u]_{C^*},\qquad |P|\ge2.
\tag{89.1}
$$

Let $H$ be an actual compact matrix subgroup of
$\operatorname{Aut}(C,u)=\{h\in GL(V):hC=C,\ uh=u\}$, transitive on
all of $P$. For distinct $p,q\in P$ write $F(p,q)$ for their entire
minimal face in $K$. The condition $\mathrm{L3}$ is

$$
\forall p\ne q\in P,\qquad F(p,q)\simeq_{\rm aff}
\{z\in\mathbb R^3:|z|\le1\},
\tag{89.2}
$$

where the affine isomorphism is onto the entire closed ball. A section,
a proper subset of a face, or one selected pair does not satisfy (89.2).
These are the whole-base parameters of [V, Definition 88.1][V88];
(89.2) specializes its entire-pair-ball condition to dimension three.
The group $H$ is distinct from the common-support condition denoted
$\mathrm H$ in V88. No common support, $\mathrm{Pair}_{\perp}$,
least confirmation, spectrality, self-duality, strong, bit or frame
symmetry, cone homogeneity, Hilbert representation, spectator system
or dynamical law is part of Definition 89.1.

**Definition 89.2 (Original prediction specialization).** For the actual
probabilities of one original source $\Omega$, keep the original legal
words $\mathsf W_{11}=\{0,\ldots,10\}^{<\infty}$, their empty word
$\epsilon$, and their complete original terminal-future domain. As in
[VII, Definition 13.1][VII], set

$$
x_a=(1,(p(af))_{f\in\mathsf W_{11}}),\quad
V=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{x_a\},\quad
u(z)=z(\star),\quad
K=\overline{\operatorname{conv}\{x_a\}},\quad C=\operatorname{cone}K.
\tag{89.3}
$$

The finite-rank condition and the actual all-prefix/all-future return
$p(aj^{10}f)=p(af)$ are the hypotheses of VII13.2. That proposition
supplies the compact group of normalized continuations, the whole
regular cone, and $P=Hx_\epsilon=\overline{\{x_a\}}$ for this very
carrier. It does not supply (89.2). More general complete original
future-outcome readouts use the same construction with all their
original coordinates and the normalization column retained; no
restriction of a previously fixed prediction domain is understood.
The compact-continuation and centered-span interfaces are also owned
by [OriginalProbabilityCompactAction][Compact]. Mathematical closures,
convex combinations and the entire dual interval confer no additional
preparation, word, measurement or inverse program.

**Lemma 89.3 (Center, smooth pure locus and roundness in every invariant
metric).** Under Definition 89.1 and L3, $K$ is a compact full base,
$K=\operatorname{conv}P$, and $C=\operatorname{cone}P$. The Haar average
$\mu=\int_H hp\,dh$ is independent of $p\in P$, lies in
$\operatorname{int}C\cap K$, and equals the volume centroid $b$ of $K$.
Put $W=\ker u$. For every $H$-invariant positive definite form $Q$ on
$W$, $P-b$ is a compact smooth connected embedded manifold without
boundary, of dimension $m\ge2$, on a sphere of radius $R>0$.
Every pair face has the actual embedded form

$$
F=c_F+\{w\in L_F:Q(w,w)\le\rho_F^2\},\quad
\dim L_F=3,\quad c_F-b\perp_Q L_F,\quad
Q(c_F-b,c_F-b)+\rho_F^2=R^2.
\tag{89.4}
$$

Its entire boundary sphere consists of global pure points. Moreover
$H^0$ is transitive on $P$. This asserts neither connectedness of $H$
nor membership of every original generator in the identity component
of a cone-automorphism group.

Proof. On the compact intersection of $C$ with any norm-unit sphere,
$u$ has a positive minimum. Hence $K$ is bounded and closed. Generation
gives $\operatorname{aff}K=\{u=1\}$ and $\operatorname{span}K=V$.
Finite-dimensional compact convex geometry gives $K=\operatorname{conv}P$.
The orbit $Hp=P$ is compact. Haar invariance and transitivity make its
average independent of the pure starting point; by finite convex
decomposition the average of every $x\in K$ is the same $\mu$.
If $\mu$ were on the boundary of $C$, some nonzero $f\in C^*$ would
vanish there. The nonnegative continuous function $h\mapsto f(hp)$
would have zero integral, and thus vanish on the whole orbit. Since
$P$ spans $V$, this contradicts $f\ne0$. Thus $\mu$ is interior.
Every affine automorphism of the full-dimensional body $K$ fixes its
volume centroid: change of variables cancels its constant Jacobian
in the normalized first moment. In particular $b$ is fixed by $H$.
Averaging $b\in K$ gives $b=\mu$.

Averaging any inner product on $W$ supplies invariant positive definite
forms. A compact matrix group is a closed Lie group. The orbit map
$H/H_p\to V$ is an injective immersion; its compact domain makes it
an embedding. Thus $P$ is smooth and has no boundary, whether or not
$H$ is connected. An extreme point of a face of $K$ is extreme in
$K$. Consequently the full sphere of every affine ball in (89.2)
lies in $P$, and conversely pure points of that face lie on its sphere.
Any two distinct pure points can be joined on this $S^2$, so $P$ is
path connected and $m\ge2$. The $H^0$-orbits in $H/H_p$ are open:
$H^0$ is open of finite index in the compact Lie group $H$. They are
also closed, and connectedness leaves just one orbit.

Invariance makes $Q(p-b,p-b)=R^2$ constant on $P$. It is positive
because $P$ has two points. Write an affine ball chart as
$c_F+A n$, $|n|\le1$, with $A:\mathbb R^3\to L_F$ injective.
All its sphere points have squared norm $R^2$ about $b$. Comparing
$n$ and $-n$ gives $Q(c_F-b,An)=0$. The remaining identity says that
$Q(An,An)$ is constant on the unit sphere, so $A^*QA=\rho_F^2 I$
with $\rho_F>0$. This proves the entire-ball identity (89.4).
It requires no exposedness of the face, no smoothly varying family of
faces and no coverage of tangent spaces by these spheres. ∎

## 90. Equal radii supply the common global supports

**Theorem 90.1 (One equalizing form and its full support effects).**
Under Definition 89.1 and L3, suppose one invariant positive definite
$Q$ on $W$ makes every $\rho_F$ in (89.4) equal to $\rho>0$.
Set $\alpha=R^2-2\rho^2$. For every $p\in P$ define on the original
whole $V$

$$
e_p(x)=\frac{Q(p-b,x-u(x)b)-\alpha u(x)}{2\rho^2}.
\tag{90.1}
$$

Then $e_p\in[0,u]$, $e_p(p)=1$, and $e_p$ vanishes on every
antipode of $p$ in every incident pair face. In particular it supplies
the common-support condition of V88.10. Its conclusion is the full
least-confirmation condition $S$ on the same cone:

$$
e_p\le f\quad(f\in[0,u],\ f(p)=1),\qquad
e_p(q)=e_q(p),\qquad
\mathcal T_p\!\left(\sum_i\lambda_iq_i\right)
=\sum_i\lambda_i\mathcal T_{q_i}(p).
\tag{90.2}
$$

Here $\mathcal T_p(x)=\min\{f(x):f\in[0,u],f(p)=1\}$, and the
last identity includes every finite real probability mixture, repeated
labels and zero weights.

Proof. For distinct pure $p,q$ in their actual pair face write
$p=c+v$, $q=c+w$, $|v|_Q=|w|_Q=\rho$. Equation (89.4) gives

$$
Q(p-b,q-b)=R^2-\rho^2+Q(v,w)
\in[R^2-2\rho^2,R^2].
\tag{90.3}
$$

The same upper endpoint holds for $q=p$. Thus (90.1) is between zero
and one on all pure points, is one at $p$, and is zero when $w=-v$.
Linearity, $K=\operatorname{conv}P$ and $C=\operatorname{cone}P$
now give $0\le e_p\le u$ on the entire cone. This establishes a
global effect before invoking any local comparison formula.

Apply [V88.10][V88] with $g_p=e_p$. Its whole-pair-ball premise is L3;
its positive common-support premise is exactly (90.1)–(90.3).
It yields (90.2), including global cone-order leastness, rather than
only leastness on one ball. Explicitly, on any incident unit ball
the restriction is $(1+n_p\cdot n)/2$; any proper confirming effect
has restriction $1-\beta+\beta n_p\cdot n$ with
$0\le\beta\le1/2$. Their difference is nonnegative on that whole
ball, hence on every pure point and then on $C$. Symmetry of $Q$
gives reciprocity in (90.1); leastness and linearity give the last
identity of (90.2). These are applications of the common-support
bridge, without a new classification premise. ∎

**Theorem 90.2 (Static global certificates on the original domain).**
Under Definition 89.1 and L3, the following are equivalent:

1. There is a real-linear bijection
   $\Phi:V\to\operatorname{Herm}_N(\mathbb C)$, $N\ge2$, with
   $\Phi(C)=\operatorname{PSD}_N$ and $\operatorname{Tr}\Phi=u$.
2. The full normalized group $\operatorname{Aut}(C,u)$ is transitive
   on the set of all actual pair faces.
3. There exists one $H$-invariant positive definite $Q$ equalizing
   the radii of all those faces.
4. For some one $p_0\in P$, there exists one such $Q$ for which all
   faces $F(p_0,q)$, $q\ne p_0$, have the same radius.

For any such $\Phi$, the whole original base and all its pure points
are exactly the density matrices and rank-one density matrices. The
whole original mathematical effect interval corresponds bijectively to
all $0\le B\le I_N$ through

$$
e(x)=\operatorname{Tr}(B\Phi(x)).
\tag{90.4}
$$

Proof. Under item 3, Theorem 90.1 supplies full $S$. Reuse the
[owned complete $S$-to-Jordan bridge][SBridge], as in V88.4: positive-dual
generation, signed spectrality, the self-dual pairing, projectivity and
purity-preserving face filters provide the full premises of its cited
Jordan reconstruction. These conclusions are used after $S$, not
inserted as hypotheses. In a nontrivial direct sum of simple Jordan
cones, pure points from different summands generate an interval face,
contrary to L3. In a simple matrix or Albert cone the normalized
rank-two faces have real affine dimensions respectively $2,3,5,9$
for real, complex, quaternionic and octonionic types. Simple rank-two
spin factors have ball bases; L3 selects the three-ball, including
$\operatorname{PSD}_2(\mathbb C)$. Rank one is excluded by $|P|\ge2$.
Thus precisely the complex cones survive, at every rank $N\ge2$.
These type and face facts are the standard inputs of
[Barnum–Hilgert §4][BH], not a new classification.

For completeness, any resulting cone isomorphism $\Psi$ can be made
to preserve the original unit. Write
$u(x)=\operatorname{Tr}(A\Psi(x))$. Strict positivity on every
nonzero positive matrix forces $A>0$. Then

$$
\Phi(x)=A^{1/2}\Psi(x)A^{1/2},\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}\Phi(x)=u(x).
\tag{90.5}
$$

The square root, not its inverse, is required in this direction.
Trace duality represents every original real-linear functional
uniquely by a Hermitian $B$. Evaluation on every rank-one positive
matrix proves $e\ge0\iff B\ge0$; applying this to $u-e$ gives
$u-e\ge0\iff I-B\ge0$. This proves (90.4) for the whole interval
and whole cone, not merely a table of pure comparisons.

Under item 1, normalized complex cone automorphisms are unitary
conjugations or unitary conjugations followed by transpose
([BH §4.2, Table 1 and footnote 17][BH]). Hence the pullback of
$\operatorname{Tr}(XY)$ on traceless matrices is invariant under the
given $H$, including its disconnected components. Every pair face is
the density body on a two-dimensional complex support, of radius
$1/\sqrt2$. This proves item 3. Unitary conjugation takes any such
support to any other and proves item 2.

Conversely the full normalized group is compact. Indeed its operators
and inverses send the bounded spanning base $K$ onto itself; boundedness
on a finite basis from $K$ bounds both operators and inverses. A limit
has an inverse limit and still preserves $K$, so the group is closed
and compact. Average an inner product on $W$ over this full group.
Lemma 89.3 makes every pair face round in it, and item 2 makes their
radii equal. This proves item 3 from item 2.

Item 3 implies item 4. For the reverse, choose $h\in H$ sending
$p_0$ to an arbitrary $p$. Every $F(p,q)$ is
$hF(p_0,h^{-1}q)$, with the same $Q$-radius by invariance. Thus the
one fixed star in item 4 equalizes all faces. ∎

## 91. Diameter faces, positive secants and the curvature certificate

**Definition 91.1 (Actual Euclidean quantities).** Under Definition
89.1 and L3, fix any invariant $Q$ and use the induced metric on the
actual embedding $P-b\subset(W,Q)$. Let

$$
D_Q=\max_{p,q\in P}|p-q|_Q>0,\qquad
k_Q=\max_{p\in P,\ v\in T_pP,\ |v|_Q=1}
|\mathrm{II}^Q_p(v,v)|_Q.
\tag{91.1}
$$

Compactness of $P$ and of its unit tangent bundle gives both maxima.
The Euclidean focal radius is the radius of nonsingularity of the
normal exponential, as in [Mendes §2.2][Mendes]; it is not defined by
global normal injectivity or by uniqueness of nearest points.

**Proposition 91.2 (Diameter pair spheres).** For every invariant $Q$,
the maximum pair-face radius is attained and is $D_Q/2$. Every sphere
of that radius is totally geodesic inside $P$. On its unit tangent
directions $|\mathrm{II}^Q_p(v,v)|_Q=2/D_Q$. Every pure point lies on
at least one such sphere, and $k_Q\ge2/D_Q$.

Proof. The antipodes of each face sphere are global pure points, so
$2\rho_F\le D_Q$. Choose $p,q$ attaining $D_Q$. Their face contains
this chord, so $2\rho_{F(p,q)}\ge D_Q$. Equality follows, without
assuming compactness or continuity of a space parametrizing the faces.

On any radius-$D_Q/2$ face sphere, let $p$ and $q$ be antipodes and
$c=(p+q)/2$. The smooth function $x\mapsto|x-q|_Q^2$ on $P$ has a
maximum at $p$. Hence $p-q$ is normal to the full $T_pP$, not just
to the face sphere. For tangents $v,w$ to that sphere its ambient
second fundamental form is

$$
\mathrm{II}^{\rm sphere}_p(v,w)
=-Q(v,w)\frac{p-c}{\rho_F^2}.
\tag{91.2}
$$

This is normal to $P$. In the second-fundamental-form inclusion formula
for sphere $\subset P\subset W$, the tangential-to-$P$ component
therefore vanishes and the normal component is
$\mathrm{II}^Q_P(v,w)$. This proves both total geodesicity and the
stated curvature. Transitivity of $H$ moves one point of one diameter
sphere to any prescribed pure point and preserves its radius.
These give equality directions and a lower bound only. They do not
assert that all pair spheres are totally geodesic, that their tangent
directions cover $T_pP$, or that other directions have an upper bound. ∎

**Proposition 91.3 (Entire positive binary fibers and their maximum
rank).** Write $m=\dim P$ and $d=\dim\operatorname{aff}K$.
The smooth map

$$
\mathcal B:((P\times P)\setminus\Delta)\times(0,1)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{aff}K,\qquad
\mathcal B(p,q,t)=tp+(1-t)q
\tag{91.3}
$$

has every nonempty fiber an embedded $S^2$. Its maximum differential
rank is exactly $2m-1$. If $K$ is not itself an affine three-ball,
its entire image is in $\partial K$ and $d\ge2m$. In the whole
three-ball case $m=2$ and $d=3$.

Proof. For $x=tp+(1-t)q$, the nontrivial open chord is in the
relative interior of the strictly convex face $F(p,q)$. Its minimal
face is precisely $F=F(p,q)$: every face containing $x$ contains
both endpoints, and $F$ contains $x$. In any other positive binary
decomposition $x=sa+(1-s)b$, the face property puts $a,b$ in $F$;
global purity places them on its whole sphere.

Write $F=c+\{w\in L:|w|_Q\le\rho\}$ and $x=c+z$ with
$|z|_Q<\rho$. For an arbitrary first endpoint $a=c+w$,
$|w|_Q=\rho$, put

$$
s(w)=\frac{\rho^2-|z|_Q^2}
{2(\rho^2-Q(z,w))},\qquad
b(w)=\frac{x-s(w)a}{1-s(w)}.
\tag{91.4}
$$

The denominator is positive. Its difference from the numerator is
$|w-z|_Q^2>0$, so $0<s(w)<1$. Expanding the sphere equation for
$b(w)-c$ gives exactly (91.4). Thus this is the unique second sphere
intersection along the line, distinct from $a$, and all decompositions
are obtained. There are no singular denominators anywhere on the
fixed sphere. The parametrization $w\mapsto(a,b(w),s(w))$ is smooth
and injectively immersed because its first component is $a$. Projection
to the first endpoint is its inverse. The fixed affine sphere is a
smooth embedded submanifold of the smooth embedded $P$; smooth
factorization through these embeddings is legitimate. Compactness of
the sphere makes this an embedding, proving the entire-fiber assertion.
No smooth dependence of $F$ on a varying pair or on $x$ is needed.

The domain has dimension $2m+1$. Each embedded fiber gives two
independent kernel directions, so $\operatorname{rank}d\mathcal B
\le2m-1$. Let $r$ be its maximum rank and take a point attaining it.
A nonzero $r$-minor persists near that point; maximality makes the
rank constantly $r$ there. The constant-rank theorem gives a local
fiber of dimension $2m+1-r$. This is an open part of the explicitly
embedded two-sphere, hence $r=2m-1$. This argument establishes maximum
rank, not constant rank at every point or a global fiber bundle.

If any pair face equals $K$, L3 makes $K$ an affine three-ball.
Otherwise every pair face is proper and lies in the relative boundary
of $K$. Then a rank-$d$ point would give an open image neighborhood,
impossible in that boundary. Thus $2m-1\le d-1$. The sphere of the
whole three-ball gives the stated remaining case. In particular the
real positive fibers alone supply no complex projective variety,
complex closure, signed or complex entry locus, modular face lattice
or classification by a complex secant defect. ∎

**Theorem 91.4 (Existential curvature certificate on the whole original
cone).** Under Definition 89.1 and L3, the conditions of Theorem 90.2
are equivalent to

$$
\exists\ Q>0\text{ invariant under }H:\quad
|\mathrm{II}^Q_p(v,v)|_Q\le2/D_Q
\quad\text{for every }p\in P,\ v\in T_pP,\ |v|_Q=1.
\tag{91.5}
$$

Proof. First $k_Q>0$, already by Proposition 91.2. In Euclidean
space the differential of the normal exponential is singular along
a unit normal $n$ at positive distance $1/\lambda$ when the shape
operator in direction $n$ has a positive eigenvalue $\lambda$.
Maximizing over both signs of $n$ and over unit tangents identifies
the greatest such eigenvalue with
$\max_{p,v}|\mathrm{II}^Q_p(v,v)|_Q=k_Q$. Thus the focal radius is
$1/k_Q$, with no assertion about a global cut locus.

Lemma 89.3 checks precisely the compact, smooth, connected,
boundaryless embedded-manifold hypotheses of
[Mendes, Theorem A][Mendes] in the Euclidean ambient space $(W,Q)$.
It gives $D_Qk_Q\ge2$. Assumption (91.5) forces equality. The
equality classification concerns the actual immersion up to congruence
and the scaling and totally umbilical inclusions specified in its §2.3.
The alternative which covers a real projective space from a sphere
is noninjective and cannot be this embedding. The remaining image is
a round sphere or a standard Veronese projective image over
$\mathbb R,\mathbb C,\mathbb H$ or $\mathbb O$.

Take the convex hull of that actual image. A sphere gives its whole
ball. The standard projective images are precisely rank-one Hermitian
idempotents; the Jordan spectral theorem gives their entire trace-one
positive bodies as convex hulls. This uses spectrality only after the
embedding classification has supplied a Jordan representation.
The octonionic possibilities are projective dimension one or two.
There is no unused affine direction because $P$ affinely spans
$\{u=1\}$. Thus this congruence induces an affine bijection of the
whole $K$ onto one of these bodies.

For a ball any distinct pure pair generates the whole ball, so L3
selects the three-ball. For the projective images take two orthogonal
primitive idempotents in one frame. Their whole rank-two faces have
dimensions $2,3,5,9$ as in [BH §4][BH]. L3 selects exactly the
complex bodies, retaining $N=2$ and excluding the singleton. These
are cited intermediate classification facts; the implication here
composes them with the original whole-face and convex-hull conditions.

If $\phi:K\to\{\rho\ge0:\operatorname{Tr}\rho=1\}$ is the
resulting affine bijection and $A$ its linear part on $W$, homogenize
without changing the original slice:

$$
x=t b+w,\quad t=u(x),\quad w\in W,\qquad
\Phi(x)=t\phi(b)+Aw.
\tag{91.6}
$$

This is a real-linear bijection, its trace is $u(x)$, and
$C=\operatorname{cone}K$ gives $\Phi(C)=\operatorname{PSD}_N$.
Equation (90.4) then transports all original effects and evaluations.
This proves item 1 of Theorem 90.2 from (91.5).

Conversely use item 1 to pull back Hilbert–Schmidt $Q$ to $W$.
The normalized automorphism classification in the proof of 90.2
makes it invariant under all of $H$. For rank-one projectors $D_Q=\sqrt2$.
Every unit tangent, after unitary conjugation, is the initial tangent of

$$
P(t)=|\cos(t/\sqrt2)e_1+\sin(t/\sqrt2)e_2\rangle
\langle\cos(t/\sqrt2)e_1+\sin(t/\sqrt2)e_2|.
\tag{91.7}
$$

It has constant unit speed. At zero its acceleration is $P_2-P_1$,
normal to the rank-one manifold and of norm $\sqrt2$. The unitary
isotropy is transitive on unit tangents, so
$k_Q=\sqrt2=2/D_Q$. The pair radii are $1/\sqrt2$, agreeing with
Theorem 90.2. This proves the existential equivalence. None of these
arguments obtains (91.5) from the equality directions of 91.2 alone. ∎

## 92. A faithful control for a supplied invariant metric

**Proposition 92.1 (Unequal radii on the same complex cone).** Let
$V=\operatorname{Herm}_4(\mathbb C)$, $C=\operatorname{PSD}_4$,
$u=\operatorname{Tr}$, with the whole density base, all rank-one pure
points and the full effect interval. Define the antiunitary $\Theta$ by

$$
\Theta e_1=e_2,\quad\Theta e_2=-e_1,\quad
\Theta e_3=e_4,\quad\Theta e_4=-e_3,
\qquad \tau(X)=\Theta X\Theta^{-1}.
\tag{92.1}
$$

Let $G=\{g\in U(4):g\Theta=\Theta g\}=\operatorname{Sp}(2)$ and
take its actual conjugation image
$H=\{\operatorname{Ad}_g:g\in G\}\subset\operatorname{Aut}(C,u)$.
Then $H\simeq\operatorname{PSp}(2)$ satisfies Definition 89.1 and L3.
For each $0<\varepsilon<1$ the form on $W=\{X:\operatorname{Tr}X=0\}$

$$
Q_\varepsilon(X,Y)=\operatorname{Tr}(XY)
 +\varepsilon\operatorname{Tr}(X\tau(Y))
\tag{92.2}
$$

is invariant and positive definite. Its pair-face radii are not all
equal, and the curvature upper bound in (91.5) fails for this supplied
$Q_\varepsilon$, although the same carrier and group admit an equalizer $Q_0$.

Proof. For every unit vector $v$, $v\perp\Theta v$. Quaternionic
orthonormal completion of the pair $(v,\Theta v)$ supplies a unitary
commuting with $\Theta$ and sending $e_1$ to $v$. Thus $H$ is
transitive on all rank-one projectors. A unitary acting trivially on
all Hermitian matrices is scalar. A scalar unitary commuting with
the antiunitary $\Theta$ is $\pm I$. Hence the conjugation kernel is
exactly $\{\pm I\}$; the actual subgroup here is its image, not a
faithfully embedded copy of $\operatorname{Sp}(2)$. Every distinct
pure pair has its whole minimal face on its two-dimensional complex
support, exactly a three-ball.

The real-linear $\tau$ is trace preserving and Hilbert–Schmidt
orthogonal. Since $\Theta^2=-I$, $\tau^2=I$, so $\tau$ is also
self-adjoint for that real inner product. It commutes with $H$.
Therefore (92.2) is symmetric, invariant, and

$$
Q_\varepsilon(X,X)\ge(1-\varepsilon)\operatorname{Tr}(X^2)>0
\quad(X\ne0).
\tag{92.3}
$$

For pure $P$, $\operatorname{Tr}(P\tau(P))=0$ by
$v\perp\Theta v$. At $b=I/4$ this gives
$R^2=(3-\varepsilon)/4$. Write $P_j=|e_j\rangle\langle e_j|$.
In the faces on supports $\{1,2\}$ and $\{1,3\}$, the respective
center-to-pure vectors are
$Z_{12}=(P_1-P_2)/2$ and $Z_{13}=(P_1-P_3)/2$.
Now $\tau(Z_{12})=-Z_{12}$, whereas $\tau(Z_{13})$ has disjoint
support $\{2,4\}$. Lemma 89.3 makes the entire faces round, so

$$
\rho_{12}^2=(1-\varepsilon)/2,\qquad \rho_{13}^2=1/2.
\tag{92.4}
$$

For arbitrary pure $P,Q$, put $t=\operatorname{Tr}(PQ)\ge0$ and
$s=\operatorname{Tr}(P\tau(Q))\ge0$. Orthogonality and
self-adjointness of $\tau$ give

$$
|P-Q|_{Q_\varepsilon}^2=2(1-t)-2\varepsilon s\le2.
\tag{92.5}
$$

$P_1,P_3$ attain two, so $D_{Q_\varepsilon}=\sqrt2$.
At $P_1$ the full pure-manifold tangent space consists of Hermitian
off-diagonal terms between $e_1$ and its orthogonal complement.
The vector $Z_{12}$ is Hilbert–Schmidt normal to all of them and,
since $\tau(Z_{12})=-Z_{12}$, is also $Q_\varepsilon$-normal to
them. For a $Q_\varepsilon$-unit tangent $v$ to the $12$ sphere
at $P_1$, the inclusion formula therefore yields

$$
\mathrm{II}^{Q_\varepsilon}_{P_1}(v,v)
=-Z_{12}/\rho_{12}^2,\qquad
k_{Q_\varepsilon}\ge\sqrt{2/(1-\varepsilon)}
>\sqrt2=2/D_{Q_\varepsilon}.
\tag{92.6}
$$

No uncomputed value of the global curvature maximum is needed.
For the same $V,C,u,K,P,E,H$, $Q_0(X,Y)=\operatorname{Tr}(XY)$
has all pair radii $1/\sqrt2$ and $k_{Q_0}D_{Q_0}=2$ by (91.7).
Thus this example refutes the every-invariant-metric assertion, not
the existence of an equalizer or the existential curvature certificate.
It is a model of the stated convex and geometric hypotheses; no
realization by the original source or its legal words is asserted. ∎

## 93. Whole-cone flows and a single pure-directed tangent

**Definition 93.1 (Global order derivations).** For a regular cone
$C$ on a finite-dimensional real $V$, an order derivation is a
real-linear $D:V\to V$ such that

$$
\exp(tD)C=C\qquad(t\in\mathbb R).
\tag{93.1}
$$

Equivalently its exponentials are positive for all real $t$, since
