# Recursive relational observation: self-generated predictive memory

## 1. The source and the continuing decoder contract

This volume studies when a retained predictive boundary can generate its own complete future law through the same update that consumes actual observations. It refines the unequal-holding interval in [Finite-start Predictive Memory, §§1–5 and Definition 9.3](RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_FINITE_START_PREDICTIVE_MEMORY.md), with ordinary mathematical proofs. An externally assigned coherent decoder and a law generated by the retained boundary have different contracts. The results concern the declared stochastic model and abstract configuration cardinality; they carry no proof-assistant or physical-realization claim.

**Assumption 1.1 (the unchanged passive source).** The hidden modes are $0,1,2,3,4$, corresponding respectively to the five configurations $000,100,101,001,010$ of [Auric, §§122–123](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/blob/ad512afaf86ab2258ef58a78089f247e190866f4/docs/develop/theory/AURIC_FIB_HISTORY_RECORDS_TIME_ARROW.md). Assume

$$
p,q,r>0,\qquad p+q+r<1,\qquad
s=p+q,\quad a=1-s,\quad b=1-r,\quad c=1-s-r,
$$

$$
P=\begin{pmatrix}
c&p&0&q&r\\
q&a&p&0&0\\
0&q&a&p&0\\
p&0&q&a&0\\
r&0&0&0&b
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\pi=\frac15(1,1,1,1,1).
$$

Rows are departure states, $X_0\sim\pi$, and the only acquired symbols are

$$
\mathcal A=\{0,1,3,B\},\qquad
\chi(0)=0,\quad\chi(1)=1,\quad\chi(3)=3,
\quad\chi(2)=\chi(4)=B,\qquad Y_t=\chi(X_t).
$$

The Markov law is an additional model assumption. The observer receives successive actual symbols, with no hidden-mode read, source reset, copy, control, supplied prehistory, discarded archive, or external recording-age or current-symbol register. Its only changing input is its retained configuration together with the next read. Equalities $p=q$ and $r=s$ remain allowed.

**Definition 1.2 (queryable boundaries and profile distance).** Let $\mathcal H_n$ be the positive-probability visible words of length $n$, and put $\mathcal H=\{\varnothing\}\cup\bigcup_{n\ge1}\mathcal H_n$. For $h\in\mathcal H_n$, $n\ge1$, the profile $F_h=(F_h^H)_{H\ge1}$ is the conditional law of $(Y_n,\ldots,Y_{n+H-1})$ given $h$. At the empty history, $F_{\ast}$ begins with $Y_0$. A profile consists of normalized finite-word laws consistent under deletion of final symbols. Define

$$
d(F,G)=\sup_{H\ge1}\operatorname{TV}(F^H,G^H),\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(L,Q)=\frac12\sum_{w\in\mathcal A^H}|L(w)-Q(w)|.
$$

For a current hidden distribution $\mu$, with $D_y=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf1_{\chi(i)=y})$,

$$
F_\mu^H(y_1\cdots y_H)
=\mu P D_{y_1}\cdots P D_{y_H}\mathbf1,\qquad F_i=F_{e_i},
$$

whereas

$$
F_{\ast}^H(y_1\cdots y_H)
=\pi D_{y_1}P D_{y_2}\cdots P D_{y_H}\mathbf1.
$$

Column sums of $P$ are one, so $\pi P=\pi$ and $F_{\ast}=F_\pi$. This stationary identity supplies no observed prehistory and does not change the empty-query convention.

**Definition 1.3 (the unrestricted self-generated observer).** A competitor consists of a finite set $Z$, initialization $z_{\ast}$, a total map $U:Z\times\mathcal A\to Z$, and a normalized nonnegative emission row $e_z$ on $\mathcal A$ at every $z$. Actual retention satisfies

$$
z(\varnothing)=z_{\ast},\qquad z(hy)=U(z(h),y).
$$

Its decoder is required to be the law generated by these very emissions and this very update:

$$
D_z^H(y_1\cdots y_H)=\prod_{j=1}^H e_{z_{j-1}}(y_j),\qquad
z_0=z,\quad z_j=U(z_{j-1},y_j).
$$

The guarantee is

$$
\sup_{h\in\mathcal H}\sup_{H\ge1}
\operatorname{TV}(F_h^H,D_{z(h)}^H)\le\epsilon.
$$

It covers the empty history, every positive finite actual history however rare, and every finite future horizon. The minimum initialized cardinality is $N_{\mathrm{gen}}(\epsilon)$. Initialization and all configurations used by actual histories or generated words count, including generated-only configurations. Updates remain defined on actual source-legal symbols assigned forecast probability zero. No emission shape, transient-chain form, unique recurrent presentation, exact synchronized decoder, or one-state tail is assumed of competitors.

In the weaker common-decoder contract, one externally assigns any coherent profile $D(z)$ to each configuration, without requiring its positive conditional residual to equal $D(U(z,y))$. Finite-start Predictive Memory proves its initialized minimum $6+N_2$ in the small-error domain below. Its midpoint decoder fails that residual identity when cast as self-generated; that particular failure alone says nothing about every possible competitor.

**Definition 1.4 (separation domain and unequal-holding coordinates).** Use the published constant

$$
\kappa=\min\left\{c,p,q,a,\frac{cs}{s+r},\frac{ap}{s},\frac{aq}{s},\frac1{25}\right\}>0.
$$

Unless a theorem says otherwise, assume $0<2\epsilon<\kappa$. For $a\ne b$ let

$$
u=\min(a,b),\quad v=\max(a,b),\quad\theta=u/v,\quad
\lambda=\log(v/u)>0,\quad
\delta_k=\frac{\theta^k}{1+\theta^k}.
$$

The dominant mode $d$ has hold $v$, and the other mode $f$ has hold $u$: $(d,f)=(2,4)$ if $a>b$, and $(d,f)=(4,2)$ if $b>a$. The symbol $d$ as a mode index is distinguished by its arguments from the profile metric $d(F,G)$. Put $G_x=(1-x)F_d+xF_f$, and define

$$
N_j=\min\{k\ge0:\delta_k\le j\epsilon\}\quad(j=1,2),\qquad
J=\min\{k\ge0:\delta_k<2\epsilon\}.
$$

The $N_j$ comparisons are non-strict, and $J$ is strict. Thus $J=N_2$ off a tie and $J=N_2+1$ when $\delta_{N_2}=2\epsilon$. All are finite positive integers and $N_2\le J\le N_1$. No unequal-holding cutoff is used at $a=b$.

## 2. Reused source geometry and finite exit partitions

**Proposition 2.1 (actual reachable laws and quantitative separation).** The complete actual family is the initialized profile, the five pure profiles $F_i$ after synchronization, and the startup profiles on $B^{k+1}$, with probability $(a^k+b^k)/5>0$. For unequal holdings these are $G_{\delta_k}$; at equality they are all $(F_2+F_4)/2$. Its visible pure rows, in order $(0,1,3,B)$, are

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_0&=(c,p,q,r),&v_1&=(q,a,0,p),\\
v_2&=(0,q,p,a),&v_3&=(p,0,a,q),&v_4&=(r,0,0,b).
\end{aligned}
$$

From a known mode, a positive singleton $j$ leads to mode $j$, and a positive $B$ leads by

$$
0\mapsto4,\qquad1,3\mapsto2,\qquad2\mapsto2,\qquad4\mapsto4.
$$

For unequal holdings, $x,y\in[0,1]$ and $H\ge1$,

$$
\operatorname{TV}(G_x^H,G_y^H)=|x-y|(1-u^H),\qquad
d(G_x,G_y)=|x-y|.
$$

Distinct pure profiles are separated by at least $\kappa$. Every singleton profile $F_j$, $j\in\{0,1,3\}$, is at least $\kappa$ from every mixture of $F_2,F_4$. Also

$$
d(F_{\ast},F_i)\ge\frac15,\qquad d(F_{\ast},G_x)\ge\frac1{25}.
$$

These are the results of Finite-start Predictive Memory, Theorems 2.1, 3.1 and 3.3, at their full source and acquisition hypotheses.

**Proof (2.1).** A singleton identifies its current hidden mode. The displayed unique positive successors preserve a pure posterior thereafter. The histories $0,1,3,1B,0B$, with respective probabilities $1/5,1/5,1/5,p/5,r/5$, realize all pure cases. Before any singleton, the only compatible paths on $B^{k+1}$ stay throughout at mode $2$ or throughout at mode $4$, with masses $a^k/5,b^k/5$. Their posterior gives the displayed mixture and the minority coordinate $\delta_k$. Thus the list exhausts all actual histories.

From $2$ and $4$, first non-$B$ exits are respectively in $\{1,3\}$ and $\{0\}$. The only common horizon-$H$ word is $B^H$, with probabilities $a^H,b^H$. The overlap identity $\operatorname{TV}(L,Q)=1-\sum_w\min(L(w),Q(w))$ gives $1-u^H$ for these pure profiles. Homogeneity of their mixture difference gives the formula.

For completeness, write $M_x=xF_2+(1-x)F_4$. Its next-symbol row is $((1-x)r,xq,xp,xa+(1-x)b)$. For $T=\operatorname{TV}(F_0^1,M_x^1)$, the events $\{1,3\}$ and $\{0\}$ give $T\ge s(1-x)$ and $T\ge|c-r(1-x)|$, so $T\ge cs/(s+r)$. Against $F_1$, the events $\{3\}$ and $\{1\}$ give $T\ge xp$ and $T\ge|a-xq|$, hence $T\ge ap/s$; against $F_3$ they give $aq/s$. Pure pairs other than $2,4$ have a singleton-coordinate difference at least $\min(c,p,q,a)$; the remaining pair has distance one.

The empty row is $(1/5,1/5,1/5,2/5)$. Its event gaps against $M_x$ imply

$$
\operatorname{TV}(F_{\ast}^1,M_x^1)
\ge\frac{(2/5)r+(1/5)s-sr}{s+r}
\ge\left(\frac{s}{s+r}-\frac35\right)^2+\frac1{25}.
$$

The last inequality uses $s+r<1$. For a pure start, the first future $B$ enters only one of the two modes $2,4$. One of the two events consisting of first symbol $B$ and subsequent first exit in $\{0\}$ or $\{1,3\}$ is therefore impossible. Under $F_{\ast}$ each of these event probabilities tends to $1/5$ as the finite horizon increases, proving the pure empty separation. These proofs preserve both holding orders, $p=q$, and $a=b$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.2 (finite partitions of first exits).** For any coherent profile $Q$, let $q(n,j)$ be the probability of prefix $B^n j$, $n\ge0$, $j\in\{0,1,3\}$, and put $q_\infty=\lim_{H\to\infty}Q^H(B^H)$. These numbers form a normalized countable distribution. The TV of the first-exit distribution of any two profiles is at most their profile distance. If both profiles exit almost surely and, conditional on each exit prefix $B^n j$, have the same complete continuation, their first-exit TV equals their profile distance.

**Proof (2.2).** Coherence gives

$$
\sum_{n=0}^{H-1}\sum_j q(n,j)+Q^H(B^H)=1.
$$

The survivor probability decreases, so its limit exists and the identity yields normalization. Coarsen an $H$-word into exits before $H$ and one survivor atom. Summing probabilities over fibers contracts the half-$\ell^1$ norm by the triangle inequality. As $H$ increases, the exit sums converge absolutely, and the survivor difference tends to the difference of the infinity masses. Thus countable first-exit TV is bounded by the profile distance.

Under common conditional continuations, partition full words into $B^n j$ branches and $B^H$. On each exit branch the difference is its prefix-coefficient difference times one normalized continuation law. Its $\ell^1$ contribution is exactly the absolute coefficient difference. Hence

$$
\operatorname{TV}(L^H,Q^H)
=\frac12\left[\sum_{n<H,j}|l(n,j)-q(n,j)|
+|L^H(B^H)-Q^H(B^H)|\right].
$$

Marginalization shows finite-horizon TV is nondecreasing in $H$. If both survivor masses tend to zero, its limit is exactly the full exit TV. The infinity atom is a limit used for analysis, never an acquired infinite history. $\square$

## 3. Exactness forced on recurrent synchronized components

**Theorem 3.1 (unrestricted recurrent pair-core rigidity).** For any observer of Definition 1.3 satisfying its uniform guarantee with $\epsilon<1$, every closed irreducible class of the actual synchronized source–observer pair chain has source-exact emissions and source-exact complete profiles. Such a class projects onto all five source modes. Consequently at least five distinct actually reachable configurations decode exactly $F_0,\ldots,F_4$.

**Proof (3.1).** Let $S$ be the pairs $(i,z(h))$ reached by positive actual histories containing a singleton, with current posterior $e_i$. The index $i$ is a proof coordinate, not an observer input. The set is finite, nonempty, and closed under every positive source transition. Unifilarity gives a unique successor $\tau(i,y)$ for each positive visible symbol $y$ from mode $i$. The pair transition has probability $v_i(y)$ and successor $(\tau(i,y),U(z,y))$. This defines a finite stochastic matrix $K$ on $S$. A finite directed stochastic graph has a closed irreducible class: choose a terminal strongly connected component in its finite component graph.

Fix any such class $C$. On it define the nonnegative affinity matrix

$$
A(t,t')=\sum_{\substack{y:v_i(y)>0\\
t'=(\tau(i,y),U(z,y))}}\sqrt{v_i(y)e_z(y)},\qquad t=(i,z).
$$

Every source-positive successor stays in $C$. Its row sum satisfies the exact identity

$$
1-(A\mathbf1)(i,z)
=\frac12\sum_{y\in\mathcal A}
\bigl(\sqrt{v_i(y)}-\sqrt{e_z(y)}\bigr)^2\ge0.
$$

Thus $A$ is substochastic, with row sum one exactly when $e_z=v_i$ on the full alphabet, including zero coordinates. On a row with equality, its weights agree with those of $K$.

Suppose some row is deficient. From every $t\in C$ choose a positive $K$-path to a deficient row, stopping at the first deficient row. All preceding rows have equality, so the path has positive $A$-weight. Its product times the terminal row deficit is a positive amount of lost mass after the path and one additional step. More explicitly, for a path of length $\ell$ ending at $t'$, the identity

$$
\mathbf1-A^{\ell+1}\mathbf1
=(\mathbf1-A^\ell\mathbf1)+A^\ell(\mathbf1-A\mathbf1)
$$

bounds the loss from below by that path product times the deficit at $t'$. Since $A$ is substochastic, $A^n\mathbf1$ decreases componentwise. Finitely many paths therefore give an integer $L\ge1$ and $\eta>0$ such that

$$
A^L\mathbf1\le(1-\eta)\mathbf1,\qquad
A^H\mathbf1\le(1-\eta)^{\lfloor H/L\rfloor}\mathbf1.
$$

For a known source mode and retained configuration, every source-positive visible word determines one source path and one observer path. Multiplying geometric means of their probabilities and summing words yields the exact finite-word identity

$$
\sum_{w\in\mathcal A^H}\sqrt{F_i^H(w)D_z^H(w)}
=(A^H\mathbf1)(i,z),\qquad(i,z)\in C.
$$

Generated words outside the source support contribute zero; no generated-only state is removed and no likelihood division is used. Since $\min(x,y)\le\sqrt{xy}$,

$$
\operatorname{TV}(F_i^H,D_z^H)
\ge1-(A^H\mathbf1)(i,z)\longrightarrow1.
$$

Every pair in $C$ is reached by a positive finite synchronized history, so this contradicts its guaranteed distance at most $\epsilon<1$. All rows on $C$ must therefore be exact. Its generated positive symbols are now exactly its source-positive symbols. Closure and induction on word length imply $D_z^H=F_i^H$ for every $H$ on $C$.

Finally the source graph is strongly connected: its positive square edges connect $0,1,2,3$, and its positive leaf edges connect $0,4$. A positive source path from a pair in $C$ to any desired mode lifts inside $C$. Its projection is therefore all five modes. Choose one pair for each mode. The corresponding configurations are distinct because their complete profiles are distinct by Proposition 2.1. $\square$

The affinity recursion is a classical Markov-path tool; a readable primary version is Daskalakis–Dikkala–Gravin, Lemma 3.1, attributed there to Kazakos. Here unifilarity establishes its use for visible words, and substochastic leakage accounts for generated support outside the source. The theorem forces exactness on synchronized recurrent classes; it does not assert that every configuration has exact emissions or that these classes have a unique five-state presentation.

## 4. Strict recurrence and a stronger full-class lower bound

**Lemma 4.1 (periodicity excludes the diameter tie).** Suppose a self-generated profile $Q$ starts on a periodic orbit of its $B$ update, and, for some $D\in(0,1)$,

$$
d(F_d,Q)\le D/2,\qquad d(G_D,Q)\le D/2.
$$

If $0<u<v<1$, such a profile cannot exist.

**Proof (4.1).** Coarsen first exits to $(n,d)$ or $(n,f)$ according to their disjoint singleton categories, and include the infinity atom. Put $h_t(n)=(1-t)t^n$ for $0\le t<1$, with $0^0=1$. The endpoint exit distributions are

$$
F_d:(h_v(n),0),\qquad
G_D:((1-D)h_v(n),Dh_u(n)),
$$

and both have zero infinity mass. Lemma 2.2 bounds each projected distance by $D/2$. The endpoint distance is $D$, so both distances equal $D/2$ and equality holds in their half-$\ell^1$ triangle inequality. Its coordinatewise slacks are nonnegative with sum zero. Each coordinate of $Q$ therefore lies between the corresponding endpoint coordinates:

$$
(1-D)h_v(n)\le q(n,d)\le h_v(n),\qquad
0\le q(n,f)\le Dh_u(n),\qquad q_\infty=0.
$$

Normalization and the first distance give $\sum_n q(n,f)=D/2>0$.

Let the $B$ orbit have period $L$, and let $\gamma$ be the product of its $L$ emission probabilities for $B$. For every phase $\ell\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}$ there are constants $c_\ell^d,c_\ell^f\ge0$ with

$$
q(mL+\ell,d)=\gamma^m c_\ell^d,\qquad
q(mL+\ell,f)=\gamma^m c_\ell^f.
$$

The strictly positive dominant lower bound forces $c_\ell^d>0$ and $\gamma>0$. Its upper and lower geometric bounds as $m\to\infty$ force $\gamma=v^L$; a smaller or larger exponential ratio violates one of them. Positive total minority mass gives some phase with $c_\ell^f>0$. Its upper bound would then require

$$
v^{mL}c_\ell^f\le D(1-u)u^{mL+\ell}\quad\text{for every }m,
$$

which is impossible because $v>u$. The continuation after an exit was never restricted in this argument. $\square$

**Theorem 4.2 (strict startup recurrence cutoff).** Write $z_k=z(B^{k+1})$. If $z_i=z_j$ for $i<j$ in an admissible unequal-holding observer, then $\delta_i<2\epsilon$. In particular $z_0,\ldots,z_{J-1}$ are distinct and never recur later on this orbit.

**Proof (4.2).** Put $L=j-i$. Determinism gives $z_{i+mL}=z_i$ for every $m\ge0$. Each associated finite history is actual and positive. Since $d(G_{\delta_{i+mL}},F_d)=\delta_{i+mL}\to0$, the triangle inequality and uniform guarantee yield $d(F_d,D_{z_i})\le\epsilon$. Also $d(G_{\delta_i},D_{z_i})\le\epsilon$, so $\delta_i\le2\epsilon$. At equality the retained $B$ orbit is periodic and Lemma 4.1 applies with $D=2\epsilon<1$. It excludes equality. $\square$

**Theorem 4.3 (the sharpened initialized lower bound).** In the unequal small-error domain, every competitor satisfies

$$
\boxed{N_{\mathrm{gen}}(\epsilon)\ge6+\min(N_1,J+1).}
$$

Thus $N_1>N_2$ forces at least $7+N_2$ configurations. If $\delta_{N_2}=2\epsilon$ and $N_1\ge N_2+2$, at least $8+N_2$ are required.

**Proof (4.3).** Select five exact configurations from Theorem 3.1. Initialization is distinct from all of them because $d(F_{\ast},F_i)\ge1/5>\epsilon$. For $k<N_1$, a startup configuration cannot be one of them: its true profile has distance $\delta_k>\epsilon$ from $F_d$, distance $1-\delta_k\ge1/2>\epsilon$ from $F_f$, and distance at least $\kappa>\epsilon$ from every singleton pure profile. Initialization is distinct from every startup configuration because $d(F_{\ast},G_{\delta_k})\ge1/25>2\epsilon$.

Put $M=\min(N_1,J+1)$. The first $J$ startup configurations never recur by Theorem 4.2. If $M=J+1$, the additional $z_J$ cannot equal an earlier one. Thus $z_0,\ldots,z_{M-1}$ are mutually distinct, disjoint from initialization and from the five exact configurations. This counts $6+M$, including any generated-only configurations without assuming they are absent. The two stated consequences follow from the strict versus non-strict cutoff relation. $\square$

**Corollary 4.4 (cost of entering an exact synchronized recurrent component).** If the startup $B$ orbit ever enters the configuration projection of a synchronized closed recurrent class, its observer has at least $6+N_1$ configurations.

**Proof (4.4).** All profiles on that projection are exact pure profiles. Each pure mode has positive $B$ probability, so class closure makes the configuration projection closed under $U(\cdot,B)$. If $m$ is the first startup index entering it, its distance constraint forces $\delta_m\le\epsilon$, hence $m\ge N_1$. No earlier startup state can recur: a repetition makes its deterministic $B$ orbit periodic forever, preventing first entry into the closed projection at a later index. The preceding $m$ states avoid this projection and initialization. The projection contains at least five distinct configurations, and initialization avoids it. Therefore $|Z|\ge m+6\ge N_1+6$. A smaller construction must keep its startup orbit outside such components; this does not prescribe the shape of that orbit. $\square$

## 5. Geometric tails: exact family radius and general feasibility

**Definition 5.1 (marked geometric tail laws).** The conditional exit rows of the two $B$ modes, on $\{0,1,3\}$, are

$$
E_2=(0,q/s,p/s),\qquad E_4=(1,0,0).
$$

Their disjoint supports define the dominant and minority exit categories $E_d,E_f$. For $t\in[0,1)$ and $\beta\in[0,1]$, let $Q_{t,\beta}$ be generated from a tail configuration $T$ with

$$
U(T,B)=T,\quad U(T,j)=R_j,\qquad
e_T(B)=t,\quad
e_T(j)=(1-t)\bigl((1-\beta)E_d(j)+\beta E_f(j)\bigr),
$$

where $R_i$ are the exact pure source generator with rows $v_i$, singleton updates to $R_j$, and the pure $B$ updates in Proposition 2.1. The row is normalized. Its first-exit category masses are $(1-\beta)h_t(n)$ and $\beta h_t(n)$, followed after exit symbol $j$ by $F_j$. This definition specifies a family of lawful generators, without restricting general competitors to that family.

**Theorem 5.2 (exact radius with dominant holding fixed).** Define the reciprocal constants

$$
\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}
=\max_{m\ge1}\frac{1-u^m}{2-v^m},\qquad
C_{\mathrm{tail}}=\frac1{\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}}
=\min_{m\ge1}\frac{2-v^m}{1-u^m}.
$$

Both extrema are attained at finite indices, and

$$
\frac12<\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}<1,\qquad1<C_{\mathrm{tail}}<2.
$$

For every $D\in(0,1/2]$, the exact minimax radius within the stated fixed-holding family is

$$
\min_{0\le\beta\le1}\ \sup_{0\le x\le D}d(G_x,Q_{v,\beta})
=\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}D=\frac{D}{C_{\mathrm{tail}}},
$$

attained at $\beta=\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}D$. This is a family optimum, not an optimum over all finite observers or all one-state holding probabilities.

**Proof (5.2).** Lemma 2.2 applies because the two compared laws have the same continuation $F_j$ after each exit. At horizon $H$ their TV is

$$
\frac12\left[
x(v^H-u^H)+|x-\beta|(1-v^H)
+\sum_{n=0}^{H-1}|x h_u(n)-\beta h_v(n)|
\right].
$$

The first term is the survivor difference, the second sums dominant exits, and the last sums minority exits. Taking the increasing-horizon limit gives

$$
d(G_x,Q_{v,\beta})
=\frac12\left[|x-\beta|+\sum_{n\ge0}|x h_u(n)-\beta h_v(n)|\right].
$$

This is convex in $x$, so its maximum on $[0,D]$ is the larger endpoint distance. At $x=0$ the distance is $\beta$. For $0\le\beta\le D$, the ratio $D h_u(n)/(\beta h_v(n))$, when $\beta>0$, decreases strictly from a value greater than one to zero. The negative part of $D h_u-\beta h_v$ is a terminal tail. Its mass is

$$
\sup_{m\ge0}(\beta v^m-Du^m),
$$

including limiting value zero. The same formula holds when $\beta=0$. Since the total signed mass is $D-\beta$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
d(G_D,Q_{v,\beta})
&=D-\beta+\sup_{m\ge0}(\beta v^m-Du^m)\\
&=\sup_{m\ge0}\bigl[D(1-u^m)-\beta(1-v^m)\bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$

The ratios defining $\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}$ tend to $1/2$ and exceed $1/2$ whenever $\theta^m<1/2$. One such value separates the eventual ratios from a candidate maximum, so a finite index attains the maximum. Every ratio is strictly less than one since $1+u^m-v^m>0$; a finite maximum is therefore less than one. This proves the ranges and reciprocal identities.

At $\beta=\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}D$, the defining inequality

$$
D(1-u^m)\le\beta(2-v^m)
$$

holds for all $m\ge1$, with equality at a maximizing index. The $m=0$ expression is zero. The last supremum is therefore exactly $\beta$, as is the other endpoint distance. Convexity proves the claimed radius on the entire interval.

Conversely, $\beta>D$ already has endpoint error $\beta>D>\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}D$. For $\beta\le D$, if the maximum endpoint error is $R$, then $\beta\le R$ and

$$
D(1-u^m)\le R+\beta(1-v^m)\le R(2-v^m).
$$

Taking the supremum of reciprocal ratios gives $R\ge\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}D$. This proves optimality within the precise family. $\square$

**Proposition 5.3 (finite exact extremum and geometric overlap).** Let

$$
f_m=2(1-u)\theta^m-(1-v)-(v-u)u^m,\qquad
M=\min\{m\ge0:f_m\le0\}.
$$

Then $M\ge1$ is finite and $\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}=(1-u^M)/(2-v^M)$. If $f_M=0$, the indices $M$ and $M+1$ tie. Also, for

$$
n_0=\min\{n\ge0:h_u(n)\le h_v(n)\},\qquad
\rho=\operatorname{TV}(h_u,h_v),\qquad C_{\mathrm{ov}}=2-\rho,
$$

one has

$$
\rho=v^{n_0}-u^{n_0}=\max_{m\ge0}(v^m-u^m),\qquad
1<C_{\mathrm{ov}}<C_{\mathrm{tail}}<2.
$$

The geometric overlap is $1-\rho=\sum_n\min(h_u(n),h_v(n))$. These constants have distinct roles: $\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}$ is the exact radius factor, $C_{\mathrm{tail}}$ its reciprocal cutoff multiplier, and $C_{\mathrm{ov}}$ a simpler sufficient cutoff multiplier.

**Proof (5.3).** Cross-multiplication shows that the sign of the difference between successive ratios $(1-u^m)/(2-v^m)$ is the sign of $f_m$. Moreover $f_0=1-u>0$, $f_m\to-(1-v)<0$, and

$$
f_{m+1}-f_m=(1-u)(v-u)\left(u^m-\frac{2\theta^m}{v}\right)<0.
$$

Thus the ratios increase until $M$, and decrease thereafter, with the stated adjacent tie when the difference vanishes. All definitions use exact comparisons.

The likelihood ratio $h_u(n)/h_v(n)=((1-u)/(1-v))\theta^n$ decreases from greater than one to zero. Summing the positive part through the crossing gives $\rho=v^{n_0}-u^{n_0}$. The cumulative difference before any index $m$ is $v^m-u^m$, and its maximum is the same value. At an exact crossing $h_u(n_0)=h_v(n_0)$, the adjacent cumulative indices $n_0,n_0+1$ give the same value. Distinct strictly positive normalized geometric laws have $0<\rho<1$.

For each $m\ge1$,

$$
(2-v^m)-(2-\rho)(1-u^m)
=\rho+(2-\rho)u^m-v^m
\ge(1-\rho)u^m>0.
$$

Take the attained finite minimum defining $C_{\mathrm{tail}}$ to conclude $C_{\mathrm{tail}}>2-\rho$. Finally the overlap identity gives $1-\rho$; equivalently its geometric sums give $1-v^{n_0}+u^{n_0}$. $\square$

**Definition 5.4 (arbitrary-holding geometric feasibility).** For nonnegative $A,B$ and $x,y\in[0,1)$ define

$$
\mathcal O(A,x;B,y)=\sum_{n\ge0}\min(Ah_x(n),Bh_y(n)).
$$

For $D\in[0,1/2]$, $t\in[0,1)$, $\beta\in[0,1]$, put

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_0(t,\beta)&=1-\mathcal O(1,v;1-\beta,t),\\
\Delta_D(t,\beta)&=1-\mathcal O(1-D,v;1-\beta,t)
-\mathcal O(D,u;\beta,t).
\end{aligned}
$$

Say $D$ is geometrically feasible when there exist such $t,\beta$ with both $\Delta_0(t,\beta)\le\epsilon$ and $\Delta_D(t,\beta)\le\epsilon$. Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_{\mathrm{geo}}&=\min\{k\ge0:\delta_k\text{ is geometrically feasible}\},\\
K_{\mathrm{tail}}&=\min\{k\ge0:\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}\delta_k\le\epsilon\}
=\min\{k\ge0:\delta_k\le C_{\mathrm{tail}}\epsilon\},\\
K_{\mathrm{ov}}&=\min\{k\ge0:\delta_k\le C_{\mathrm{ov}}\epsilon\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The endpoint feasibility inequalities and the last two cutoffs are non-strict, including their exact ties. The existential definition of $K_{\mathrm{geo}}$ is a mathematical criterion, not an arbitrary-real comparison algorithm.

**Theorem 5.5 (exact geometric criterion and cutoff order).** The law of Definition 5.1 satisfies

$$
d(F_d,Q_{t,\beta})=\Delta_0(t,\beta),\qquad
d(G_D,Q_{t,\beta})=\Delta_D(t,\beta).
$$

Feasibility at $D$ guarantees $d(G_x,Q_{t,\beta})\le\epsilon$ for every $x\in[0,D]$, and implies $D<2\epsilon$. All cutoffs are finite, with

$$
J\le K_{\mathrm{geo}}\le K_{\mathrm{tail}}\le K_{\mathrm{ov}}\le N_1.
$$

**Proof (5.5).** First-exit categories have masses $h_v,0$ under $F_d$, $(1-D)h_v,Dh_u$ under $G_D$, and $(1-\beta)h_t,\beta h_t$ under $Q_{t,\beta}$. Within each category exit-symbol proportions agree, and after each individual exit symbol the continuation is $F_j$. Lemma 2.2 and the normalized overlap identity give both formulas. Convexity of each finite-word TV bound, using $G_x=(1-x/D)F_d+(x/D)G_D$ when $D>0$, proves the interval guarantee. The case $D=0$ is immediate.

Triangle inequality gives $D\le2\epsilon$ for feasible $D$. Equality is impossible by Lemma 4.1, since $Q_{t,\beta}$ has a $B$ self-loop and both endpoint bounds would be $D/2$. Theorem 5.2 supplies feasibility at $\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}D\le\epsilon$, and the pure choice $t=v,\beta=0$ supplies it at $D\le\epsilon$. Thus $K_{\mathrm{geo}}$ is finite, and the first two cutoff comparisons follow. The remaining comparisons follow from $1<C_{\mathrm{ov}}<C_{\mathrm{tail}}<2$ and strict decrease of $\delta_k$.

The simpler overlap sufficient condition can also be seen directly. With $t=v,\beta=\epsilon$ one endpoint error is $\epsilon$ and

$$
\Delta_D(v,\epsilon)=\max(D,\epsilon)-\mathcal O(D,u;\epsilon,v).
$$

For $D\le\epsilon$ it is at most $\epsilon$. For $D\ge\epsilon$, termwise $\min(Dh_u,\epsilon h_v)\ge\epsilon\min(h_u,h_v)$, so the error is at most $D-\epsilon(1-\rho)\le\epsilon$ whenever $D\le(2-\rho)\epsilon$. This proof uses actual complete-word laws, not only next-symbol marginals. $\square$

## 6. Counted constructions and the first candidate cardinality

**Theorem 6.1 (a total initialized generator for every feasible cutoff).** If $D=\delta_K$ is geometrically feasible, there is an observer of Definition 1.3 with exactly $7+K$ configurations satisfying the original uniform guarantee. Consequently

$$
\boxed{
6+\min(N_1,J+1)\le N_{\mathrm{gen}}(\epsilon)
\le\min(6+N_1,\,7+K_{\mathrm{geo}})
\le\min(6+N_1,\,7+K_{\mathrm{tail}}).
}
$$

The fixed-holding construction attains the last upper bound with minority exit weight $\beta=\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}\delta_{K_{\mathrm{tail}}}$.

**Proof (6.1).** Choose feasible $t,\beta$. Use the labels

$$
I,\quad R_0,\ldots,R_4,\quad A_0,\ldots,A_{K-1},\quad T.
$$

Every singleton $j\in\{0,1,3\}$ sends every label to $R_j$. On $B$ define

$$
\begin{aligned}
I&\mapsto A_0, &A_k&\mapsto A_{k+1}\quad(k<K-1),
&A_{K-1}&\mapsto T,&T&\mapsto T,\\
R_0&\mapsto R_4,&R_1,R_3&\mapsto R_2,
&R_2&\mapsto R_2,&R_4&\mapsto R_4.
\end{aligned}
$$

Emit $(1/5,1/5,1/5,2/5)$ at $I$, the true source row $v_i$ at $R_i$, the row $(1-\delta_k)v_d+\delta_kv_f$ at $A_k$, and the normalized row of Definition 5.1 at $T$. These are all the emission and update data. Since feasibility forces $K\ge J\ge1$, the displayed indices exist. The update is total on all four symbols, even where a row has a zero forecast. Iterating it and these rows defines every decoded word law on the listed set.

The actual-history invariant is: empty occupies $I$; $B^{k+1}$ occupies $A_k$ for $k<K$ and $T$ for $k\ge K$; any history containing a singleton occupies its exactly inferred $R_i$. Proposition 2.1 and the update prove this by induction. Pure labels generate exact source laws. At a saturated startup label, $\delta_k\le\delta_K$ and Theorem 5.5 gives error at most $\epsilon$.

At $A_k$, $k<K$, the generated and true laws agree until a singleton or $m=K-k$ more $B$ symbols. A singleton gives the same exact continuation. The common prefix $B^m$ has probability

$$
S_k=\frac{u^K+v^K}{u^k+v^k}\le1.
$$

After that prefix the true residual is $G_{\delta_K}$ and the generated residual is $Q_{t,\beta}$. For $H\le m$ the error is zero; for $H>m$ the finite-word error is exactly $S_k$ times their horizon-$(H-m)$ error, because every other prefix branch agrees. Its supremum is at most $S_k\epsilon\le\epsilon$. At initialization, the singleton first-read branches are exact. Its first $B$, of probability $2/5$, enters $A_0$, so the empty profile error is at most $(2/5)\epsilon$. This verifies every actual history and every horizon.

Every listed label is actually used: $I$ at empty, $A_k$ at $B^{k+1}$, $T$ at $B^{K+1}$, and the pure labels at $0,1,3,1B,0B$. Generated paths stay in that same finite set. Thus the count $1+5+K+1=7+K$ includes all initialization, actual and generated resources. Singleton overwrites change the retained observer label after a passive read; they do not reset the source. A source-legal minority exit assigned zero probability by another row still has a defined update.

For the reused upper $6+N_1$, omit $T$, take $K=N_1$ and send the last transient on $B$ to $R_d$. At $A_k$ the sole discrepant prefix has probability $S_k$ and residual distance $\delta_K$, so its profile error is

$$
S_k\delta_K=\frac{u^K}{u^k+v^k}\le\delta_K\le\epsilon.
$$

Later actual startup histories have error $\delta_j\le\epsilon$. Pure histories are exact, and the empty error is $u^K/5\le\epsilon$. This is precisely the published exact-emission truncation, with its total actual-input update and full-horizon scope. The lower bound is Theorem 4.3. $\square$

**Corollary 6.2 (matching exact strata).** If $N_1\le J+1$, then $N_{\mathrm{gen}}=6+N_1$. If $K_{\mathrm{tail}}=J<N_1$, then $N_{\mathrm{gen}}=7+J$. In particular every gap-one case $N_1=N_2+1$ is exact, and so is every tied gap-two case $\delta_{N_2}=2\epsilon$, $N_1=N_2+2$, with value $6+N_1$.

**Proof (6.2).** In the first case the lower in Theorem 6.1 equals its reused upper. In the second case the lower is $7+J$, the tail upper is $7+J$, and the reused upper is no smaller. Apply $J=N_2$ or $N_2+1$ in the stated special cases. Non-strict ties at $\delta_{N_1}=\epsilon$ and $\delta_{K_{\mathrm{tail}}}=C_{\mathrm{tail}}\epsilon$ are included. $\square$

**Theorem 6.3 (count-forced criterion for the first candidate).** Suppose $N_1\ge N_2+2$. Then, over the full competitor class,

$$
N_{\mathrm{gen}}(\epsilon)=7+N_2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists t\in[0,1),\ \beta\in[0,1]:
\Delta_0(t,\beta)\le\epsilon,\quad
\Delta_{\delta_{N_2}}(t,\beta)\le\epsilon.
$$

Equivalently, this is $K_{\mathrm{geo}}=N_2$. At $\delta_{N_2}=2\epsilon$ the criterion is infeasible. It classifies this total cardinality only.

**Proof (6.3).** Feasibility gives a $7+N_2$ observer by Theorem 6.1. Theorem 4.3 gives at least $7+N_2$, so equality follows.

For necessity, suppose an admissible observer has exactly $7+N_2$ configurations. Theorem 3.1 supplies five distinct exact core configurations $R_i$. Initialization is separate. The $N_2$ early startup states $z_0,\ldots,z_{N_2-1}$ never recur, and are disjoint from initialization and these exact states by Theorem 4.3. Exactly one further label $T$ remains. Since both $\delta_{N_2}$ and $\delta_{N_2+1}$ exceed $\epsilon$, their states cannot be exact core configurations. They cannot be initialization by separation or an early state by nonrecurrence. Both are therefore $T$, forcing $U(T,B)=T$ and all later startup states to be $T$. The first occurrence gives $d(G_{\delta_{N_2}},D_T)\le\epsilon$, and the later-history limit gives $d(F_d,D_T)\le\epsilon$.

Choose a synchronized closed pair class from Theorem 3.1. Its labels cannot be $I$, any early startup state, or $T$: the uniform bounds at their startup or empty witnesses and the separations exclude exact pure profiles there. Its projection onto configurations is consequently contained in the five selected $R_i$. Exactness and distinct pure laws force precisely one pair per source mode. Closure then forces all its source-positive updates to be the pure source table. The core rows are exact; its remaining zero-emission transitions do not affect generated laws.

For any singleton $j$, $B^{N_2+1}j$ is source-positive, because its posterior has positive weights on both $B$ modes and every singleton has positive exit mass from one of them. Its successor must approximate $F_j$. It cannot be $I$ or any startup label: $F_{\ast}$ versus $F_j$ and every mixture versus $F_j$ have distances greater than $2\epsilon$. It cannot be a wrong exact core label. Hence $U(T,j)=R_j$ for all three singleton inputs, including inputs assigned forecast zero.

Write $t=e_T(B)$. If $t=1$, the generated future remains $B$ forever and has distance one from $F_d$, since $v^H\to0$. Therefore $t<1$. Define $\beta$ to be the conditional probability of a minority-category singleton under $e_T$, divided by $1-t$. Its actual exit-symbol proportions were not assumed equal to the source proportions. Coarsening to exit time and category gives exactly masses $(1-\beta)h_t(n),\beta h_t(n)$. Lemma 2.2 contracts the two endpoint distances, yielding the two displayed inequalities. Conversely the source exit proportions in Definition 5.1 attain those coarsened distances, which is why this necessary criterion is also sufficient.

No tail topology or emission form was imposed before the count forced it. The exact diameter tie is excluded by Lemma 4.1 or Theorem 4.2. Finally $K_{\mathrm{geo}}\ge J\ge N_2$ makes feasibility at $N_2$ equivalent to $K_{\mathrm{geo}}=N_2$. $\square$

**Corollary 6.4 (a two-value gap-two classification).** If $N_1=N_2+2$, then the minimum is $7+N_2$ when the criterion in Theorem 6.3 is feasible, and $8+N_2$ otherwise. More generally, under $N_1\ge N_2+2$, its infeasibility gives the extra lower bound $N_{\mathrm{gen}}\ge8+N_2$.

**Proof (6.4).** Theorem 4.3 gives at least $7+N_2$, and Theorem 6.3 decides whether that integer is attainable. If it is not, integrality gives the next lower integer. In the gap-two case the reused upper is exactly $6+N_1=8+N_2$. This is an exact existential classification, not an analytical elimination of the criterion for every real parameter. $\square$

## 7. Equality and the bounded additive gap near it

**Theorem 7.1 (the separate exact-equality stratum).** At $r=s$, equivalently $a=b$, the queryable initialized minimum is seven both at zero error and throughout $0<2\epsilon<\kappa$. At unequal holdings, zero error admits no finite observer.

**Proof (7.1).** At equality every unresolved startup posterior is the half-mixture. Use $I$, five pure labels $R_i$, and one label $A$. Give the pure labels their source rows and pure $B$ updates; give $A$ the row $(v_2+v_4)/2$ and a $B$ self-loop; give $I$ the uniform first-read row and $U(I,B)=A$. Every singleton from every label goes to $R_j$. Equal holdings preserve the half-mixture posterior after $B$, so all these generated laws are exact, including initialization. The seven actually reachable profiles $F_{\ast},F_0,\ldots,F_4,(F_2+F_4)/2$ are pairwise more than $2\epsilon$ apart: the three profiles on the $B$ fiber have distances $1/2,1/2,1$, and Proposition 2.1 separates the other pairs. Any common decoder, hence any self-generated decoder, needs seven labels. Exact separation also proves the zero-error lower bound.

For unequal holdings, the positive histories $B^{k+1}$ have infinitely many distinct coordinates $\delta_k$. Their profiles have positive pairwise distances by Proposition 2.1. An exact decoder cannot put any two at one label, so no finite observer exists. The equality result is not a limit of the unequal cutoffs. It keeps the empty query; the separate post-read-only six-label convention of the source volume is a different problem. $\square$

**Theorem 7.2 (bounded additive excess and the leading divergence).** Fix $p,q>0$ with $s=p+q<1/2$, and fix $0<2\epsilon<\kappa(p,q,s)$. As $r\to s$ through unequal allowed values, with the original hypotheses valid in a sufficiently small neighborhood,

$$
N_{\mathrm{gen}}(\epsilon)=6+N_2+O_{p,q,\epsilon}(1),
$$

$$
N_{\mathrm{gen}}(\epsilon)\sim
\frac{1-s}{|r-s|}\log\frac{1-2\epsilon}{2\epsilon}.
$$

For $\rho\le1/2$ an explicit bound is

$$
0\le N_{\mathrm{gen}}-(6+N_2)
\le2+C_\epsilon\frac{v}{1-v},\qquad
C_\epsilon=\frac{\epsilon}{1-2\epsilon}+\frac23.
$$

This holds on both sides of equality and includes $p=q$. At equality itself the minimum is seven, as in Theorem 7.1.

**Proof (7.2).** Let $\Delta=v-u=|r-s|$. For $n\ge1$,

$$
v^n-u^n
=\Delta\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}v^{n-1-j}u^j
\le\Delta n v^{n-1}
\le\frac{\Delta}{1-v}.
$$

The last inequality follows because each term in $\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}v^j$ is at least $v^{n-1}$. Proposition 5.3 gives $\rho\le\Delta/(1-v)\to0$. Also

$$
\lambda=\int_u^v\frac{dx}{x}\ge\frac{\Delta}{v}.
$$

For $L(x)=\log((1-x)/x)$, the exact non-strict cutoff formula is $\min\{k:\delta_k\le x\}=\lceil L(x)/\lambda\rceil$ when $0<x<1/2$. With $\rho\le1/2$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
L((2-\rho)\epsilon)-L(2\epsilon)
&=\log\left(1+\frac{\rho\epsilon}{1-2\epsilon}\right)
-\log(1-\rho/2)\\
&\le C_\epsilon\rho.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $\log(1+x)\le x$ and $-\log(1-y)\le y/(1-y)$, with $y=\rho/2\le1/4$. Taking ceilings, which also preserves integer threshold ties, gives

$$
0\le K_{\mathrm{ov}}-N_2
\le1+C_\epsilon\frac{\rho}{\lambda}
\le1+C_\epsilon\frac{v}{1-v}.
$$

The upper $N_{\mathrm{gen}}\le7+K_{\mathrm{ov}}$ follows from Theorems 5.5 and 6.1. Theorem 4.3 gives $N_{\mathrm{gen}}\ge6+N_2$. Subtracting yields the stated additive bound. Since $v\to1-s<1$, it is uniformly bounded in a sufficiently small punctured neighborhood. Continuity and positivity of $\kappa$ at $r=s$, together with the strict fixed error margin, keep the original small-error assumption valid there.

Finally $\lambda\sim\Delta/(1-s)$, so

$$
N_2=\left\lceil\frac{L(2\epsilon)}{\lambda}\right\rceil
\sim\frac{1-s}{\Delta}L(2\epsilon)\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

The bounded additive excess transfers this same leading coefficient to $N_{\mathrm{gen}}$. In contrast,

$$
N_1-N_2=\frac{L(\epsilon)-L(2\epsilon)}{\lambda}+O(1)\longrightarrow\infty,
$$

while $K_{\mathrm{ov}}-N_2$ stays bounded. Hence the new upper is strictly smaller than $6+N_1$ for all sufficiently close unequal holdings. This discontinuity at the equality stratum concerns worst-case treatment of arbitrarily long positive startup histories, not a physical clock or singular source dynamics. $\square$

## 8. Exact rational matching and threshold witnesses

**Proposition 8.1 (an interior optimum at an exact tail cutoff).** Take either

$$
(p,q,r)=(1/8,1/8,1/2)
\quad\text{or}\quad
(p,q,r)=(1/4,1/4,1/4).
$$

These give opposite holding orders, $p=q$, $\kappa=1/25$, and $(u,v,\theta)=(1/2,3/4,2/3)$. Put

$$
D=\delta_{10}=\frac{1024}{60073},\qquad
\epsilon=\frac{240}{431}D.
$$

Then $N_2=J=K_{\mathrm{tail}}=10$, $N_1=12$, and the unrestricted minimum is $17$. The common-decoder minimum is $16$ and the source-exact truncation has $18$ labels. The tail cutoff accepts the exact tie $D=C_{\mathrm{tail}}\epsilon$.

**Proof (8.1).** Both parameter triples obey the strict source inequalities; direct substitution into Definition 1.4 gives $\kappa=1/25$. In Proposition 5.3,

$$
f_3=\frac{13}{864}>0,\qquad f_4=-\frac{353}{5184}<0.
$$

Strict decrease of $f_m$ makes $M=4$, so

$$
\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}
=\frac{1-2^{-4}}{2-(3/4)^4}=\frac{240}{431},\qquad
C_{\mathrm{tail}}=\frac{431}{240}.
$$

Thus $K_{\mathrm{tail}}=10$ by strict decrease of $\delta_k$ and the exact tie. Since $D<1/50$, $2\epsilon<2D<1/25$, so the required error domain holds. The neighboring ratios are

$$
\frac{\delta_9}{D}=\frac3{2+D},\qquad
\frac{\delta_{11}}{D}=\frac2{3-D},\qquad
\frac{\delta_{12}}{D}=\frac4{9-5D}.
$$

The first is greater than $150/101>480/431=2\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}$. Also $1<2\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}$, so $\delta_9>2\epsilon>D$ and $N_2=J=10$. The second exceeds $2/3>240/431$, whereas the third is less than $40/89<240/431$. Hence $\delta_{11}>\epsilon>\delta_{12}$ and $N_1=12$. Theorem 4.3 gives at least $17$ labels, and the explicit construction in Theorem 6.1 gives $17$. All-horizon and all-history validity comes from that proof, rather than from a finite parameter or horizon sample. $\square$

**Proposition 8.2 (the strict common-cutoff tie costs another label).** For either source in Proposition 8.1, instead take

$$
\epsilon=D/2=\frac{512}{60073}.
$$

Then $N_2=10$, $J=11$, $N_1=12$, and $N_{\mathrm{gen}}=18$. The common-decoder minimum is $16$, and the first candidate size $17$ is impossible.

**Proof (8.2).** Here $2\epsilon=D<1/25$, and strict decrease gives $N_2=10$, $J=11$ at the exact tie. The ratios above show $\delta_{11}/D>2/3>1/2$ and $\delta_{12}/D<40/89<1/2$, so $N_1=12$. Theorem 4.3 requires $6+\min(12,12)=18$, and the source-exact truncation attains $18$. Lemma 4.1 explains why a periodic retained decoder cannot use the diameter tie. The result concerns positive finite histories, not an actual infinite $B$ run. $\square$

**Proposition 8.3 (an exact source-truncation tie).** For either

$$
(p,q,r)=(1/10,1/10,3/5)
\quad\text{or}\quad
(p,q,r)=(3/10,3/10,1/5),
$$

take $\epsilon=1/129$. Then $\theta=1/2$, $\kappa=1/25$, $N_2=J=6$, $N_1=7$, and $N_{\mathrm{gen}}=13$. The upper accepts $\delta_7=\epsilon$ exactly.

**Proof (8.3).** Both allowed triples have opposite holding orders $\{u,v\}=\{2/5,4/5\}$ and the stated positive minimum $\kappa$. The error margin is $2/129<1/25$. Now $\delta_k=1/(2^k+1)$, and

$$
\delta_5=1/33>2/129>1/65=\delta_6>1/129=\delta_7.
$$

Therefore $N_2=J=6$ and $N_1=7$. Corollary 6.2 yields $13$, with no strictness required at the source-truncation threshold. This is another full-contract exact witness, with $p=q$ in both holding orders. $\square$

These three propositions test distinct mathematical boundaries: an interior optimum with an exact family-cutoff tie, a forbidden recurrence tie, and an accepted exact-emission tie. Their source parameters and tolerances are rational, and their proofs are analytic. None infers an unrestricted theorem from a numerical feasible point or finitely many future horizons.

## 9. Optional exact representation and the retained resource

**Assumption 9.1 (optional effective data).** Exact rational $p,q,r,\epsilon$ are supplied in binary, together with rational and integer arithmetic and a request for a symbolic law descriptor, one finite-word probability, or a complete finite-horizon law. Installed data, planning and evaluation workspace, initialization, actual acquisition, output and physical storage are separate resource coordinates. This effective specialization does not add a recording-age input to $U$.

**Proposition 9.2 (finite rational descriptions with separately charged costs).** Under Assumption 9.1, equality of holdings, their order, $N_j$, $J$, $M$, $K_{\mathrm{tail}}$ and $K_{\mathrm{ov}}$ are decidable by terminating exact comparisons, including ties. The fixed-holding, overlap, equality and source-exact constructions have rational emission rows. A general-holding construction has rational rows if its chosen $t,\beta$ are additionally rational; rational source data alone do not establish that such a feasible rational choice exists at every boundary. If each input rational has numerator and denominator of at most $B$ bits, a sufficient explicit table for the fixed-holding construction at cutoff $K$ with extremum index $M$ has description size

$$
O\bigl((K+1)^2B+(M+1)B+(K+1)\log(K+2)\bigr).
$$

No minimality of this description is claimed. No decision procedure for $K_{\mathrm{geo}}$ is implied, even by this rational specialization.

**Proof (9.2).** Rational comparisons decide $r=s$ and the holding order. Since $\theta^k\to0$, successive exact comparisons terminate for all the non-strict and strict scalar cutoffs; exact equality is not replaced by rounded logarithms. Proposition 5.3 makes $f_m$ strictly decreasing to a negative limit, so its first nonpositive index is found finitely and yields rational $\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}$. The geometric crossing is similarly a finite comparison of $h_u(n)$ and $h_v(n)$, giving rational $\rho$. Sums, products and divisions by the positive normalizers then give every coefficient in the fixed-holding and overlap constructions.

Derived source parameters have $O(B)$-bit rational descriptions. An index-$k$ power and its transient row have $O((k+1)B)$ bits. Summing these descriptions over $k<K$ gives $O((K+1)^2B)$. The single tail row carries $\alpha_{\mathrm{tail}}$ and $\delta_K$, with $O((M+K+1)B)$ bits, and a constant number of successor addresses per label adds $O((K+1)\log(K+2))$. The overlap alternative has the same type of estimate with its geometric crossing index in place of $M$. These are sufficient static descriptions; finding the indices requires its own time and working registers. The existential two-variable criterion is not reduced here to a finite exact decision method. $\square$

**Definition 9.3 (configuration naming is one resource coordinate).** Under unrestricted fixed-width binary naming, $N$ distinct labels require and admit $\lceil\log_2N\rceil$ naming bits: $b$ bits have $2^b$ values, and any $N\le2^b$ values can be chosen as names. This says nothing about the storage of installed rows and model constants, computation of probabilities, read acquisition, clocks, output, or thermodynamic cost. In these constructions every retained transient index and branch distinction is already included in the counted configuration; there is no free side register.

A horizon-$H$ law has $4^H$ word addresses when materialized. Symbolic descriptions avoid that expanded table but provide no constant-workspace or constant-time evaluation theorem. For arbitrary real or generic computable-real parameter names, the information-theoretic existence theorems do not supply exact equality or cutoff-tie decisions. Finitely many approximations to an equality name can also be extended to a sufficiently close unequal allowed parameter. A uniform exact equality decider based only on such finite approximation access would therefore give the same answer to distinct cases. A stronger comparison representation or certified slack is additional data.

**Definition 10.2 (the unresolved minimization obligation).** For unequal allowed sources in $0<2\epsilon<\kappa$, define

Theorems 4.3 and 6.1 give $L\le N_{\mathrm{gen}}\le V$. Theorem 6.3 decides whether the first candidate $7+N_2$ is attainable when $N_1\ge N_2+2$, and Corollary 6.4 raises the lower integer if it is not. The still-open obligation is to determine the minimum in every remaining unmatched regime, under the entire competitor class of Definition 1.3.

**Proposition 10.3 (prediction and retained archives remain different tasks).** These memory bounds do not recover a general old archive or recording age. For every $L\ge1$, a decoder determined only by the last $L$ symbols has worst profile error at least $1/2$ on the unchanged source.

**Proof (10.3).** Positive histories $0$ and $00$ have different ages but the same current pure mode $0$ and future law. Positive equal-length histories $00$ and $10$, of probabilities $c/5,q/5$, have different archives and the same future law. Thus exact future equivalence cannot invert either general archive or general age. Moreover $1B^L$ and $0B^L$ have positive probabilities $pa^{L-1}/5$ and $rb^{L-1}/5$, identical length-$L$ suffixes, and future profiles $F_2,F_4$ at distance one. A common suffix decoder must be at least $1/2$ from one of them by triangle inequality. These statements survive $p=q$ and $r=s$. $\square$
