# Acquired-kernel blind directions after paired calibration on one original stopped source

## 1. The exact risk-identification question

**Convention 1.1 (coarse semantic data).** The object is a finite observer on the original stopped source of Convention 2.1, with the same acquired-letter updates used in its synthetic generator. The interface supplies its emissions, suspended-alpha kernel, actual stationary rows, two configuration-indexed event arrays and all configured generated transcripts through a specified number of future Reads. It omits the acquired p-beta kernel. Supplying an installed kernel table is a different interface: no claim below treats a readable table as unknown.

The family of Definition 3.1 has strictly positive full-rank acquired phase kernels and distinct interior emissions at both phases. For every $q\ge3$, it has $N=2q+2$ private labels per phase. Theorems 4.2 and 5.1 show equality of this entire interface through $N-1$ p Reads and $N$ suspended Reads, followed by a nonzero difference on a completed word. Theorem 6.1 shows that the difference survives marginalization with the same actual row. Theorem 6.3 establishes different original worst-positive-history p configuration risks for the eight-label pair under one fixed two-endpoint prior. These statements concern each constituent table; the single-program construction of Proposition 6.2 has two closed sign components and is positive within each component.

**Convention 1.2 (extent of the obstruction).** The fixed-kernel inverse of [PAIR, Theorem 4.1] is a supplied result. The question here is whether its stated coarse interface, augmented even by the emissions and the indicated entire configured prefixes, determines a complete law or the exact original p configuration-risk coordinate when that acquired p-beta kernel is omitted. Theorem 6.3 answers negatively on a fixed finite table shape and the unchanged source. This is an exact risk-identification obstruction; it does not exclude conservative risk bounds or positive-tolerance approximations. Exact common conf/conf attainment, an unattained zero infimum, a positive unrestricted gap and fixed-resource optima remain separate mathematical questions.

## 3. A positive rational family with one hidden acquired direction

**Definition 3.1 (construction).** Fix $q\ge3$ and set

$$
N=2q+2,\qquad s=N-1=2q+1,\qquad
\eta=\frac1{20N},\qquad
v_i=u_i=\frac{17}{50}+i\eta.
\tag{3.1}
$$

Thus $1/3<u_i=v_i<39/100<2/5$, and both emission vectors have distinct entries. Let $J$ have every entry $1/N$, and put

$$
C=\frac12(I+J),\quad A=C,
\quad \pi=\tau=\frac1N\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{3.2}
$$

Define the columns

$$
z=e_0-e_1,\qquad
b_i=(-1)^{s-i}\binom{s}{i},\qquad
M=\max_i\binom{s}{i},\qquad
\theta_0=\frac1{4NM}.
\tag{3.3}
$$

For every real $|\theta|\le\theta_0$, set

$$
B_\theta=C+\theta z b^{\mathsf T},
\qquad L_\theta=SB_\theta VA,
\qquad g_\theta=SB_\theta(1-v).
\tag{3.4}
$$

Here $S=\operatorname{diag}(1-v)$ is fixed across the entire family. Parameters $q,N$ describe the installed table, not the actual number of source Reads or an observer clock.

**Lemma 3.2 (positive flow and full rank).** Every $A,B_\theta$ in (3.4) is doubly stochastic. Their entries obey

$$
A_{ij}\ge\frac1{2N},\qquad
(B_\theta)_{ij}\ge\frac1{4N}.
\tag{3.5}
$$

Both matrices have full rank. In particular the acquired circulation $B_\theta A$ is irreducible and aperiodic, and the rows (3.2) satisfy (2.3). If $\theta$ is rational, all table entries are rational.

**Proof.** The alternating binomial identity gives $b^{\mathsf T}\mathbf1=0$, while $\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}z=0$. Thus the perturbation has zero row and column sums. Its entry magnitude is at most $\theta_0M=1/(4N)$, while the smallest entry of $C$ is $1/(2N)$. This proves stochasticity and (3.5); row sums one and positivity also bound every entry above by one.

The matrix $C$ acts as the identity on constants and as multiplication by $1/2$ on zero-mean columns, so is invertible. To prove $B_\theta$ invertible, suppose $B_\theta x=0$. Its column sums give $\pi x=0$. Therefore

$$
x=-2\theta z(b^{\mathsf T}x).
$$

Since $s$ is odd, $b_0=-1$ and $b_1=s$, so $b^{\mathsf T}z=-N$. Applying $b^{\mathsf T}$ gives

$$
(1-2N\theta)b^{\mathsf T}x=0.
$$

But $|2N\theta|\le1/(2M)<1$, so $x=0$. Strictly positive finite stochastic matrices and their product are irreducible and have positive diagonal entries, hence are aperiodic. Rationality follows directly from the formulas. $\square$

**Corollary 3.3 (the sign pair is not a relabeling).** The two signs $\theta=\pm\theta_0$ are not emission-preserving hidden-state relabelings of each other.

**Proof.** The same emission table has distinct entries, so any emission-preserving relabeling fixes every index in each phase. But $B_{\theta_0}\ne B_{-\theta_0}$. $\square$

## 4. Exact cancellation on the live synthetic return path

The following finite-difference fact is classical. Its role is to construct this family's blind direction, not to claim a new general moment or factorization theorem.

**Mathematical citation 4.1 (classical forward differences, used in Theorem 4.2).** For a polynomial $p$ of degree below $s$,

$$
\sum_{i=0}^s b_i p(v_i)=0.
\tag{4.1}
$$

If $p$ has degree exactly $s$ and leading coefficient $c_p$, that sum is $c_p s!\eta^s$.

The intermediate identity follows directly: the sum is the $s$-fold forward difference at $17/50$ with step $\eta$. One forward difference lowers a nonconstant polynomial's degree by one and multiplies its leading coefficient by its degree times $\eta$. Repeating annihilates every degree below $s$ and gives $s!\eta^s$ on a monic degree-$s$ polynomial. Linearity proves the identity used in Theorem 4.2; this is a classical ingredient, not a new general moment theorem.

Write $g_0,L_0$ for the zero-parameter table. For a polynomial $p$, denote its sampled column by $p[v]=(p(v_i))_i$. Both $A=C$ and the unperturbed $B_0=C$ map a sampled polynomial to the sampled polynomial

$$
p(t)\longmapsto\frac12p(t)+\frac1{2N}\sum_i p(v_i).
\tag{4.2}
$$

In this expression the finite sum is a constant in $t$. Consequently $L_0$ maps a nonconstant degree-$a$ polynomial to a degree-$(a+2)$ polynomial with leading coefficient multiplied by $-1/4$: apply $A$, multiply by $t$, apply $B_0$, and multiply by $1-t$.

Moreover,

$$
g_0(t)=(1-t)\left[\frac12(1-t)+\frac12\overline U\right],
\qquad
\overline U=\frac1N\sum_i(1-v_i).
\tag{4.3}
$$

It has degree two and leading coefficient $1/2$. Thus

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{sampled column}&\text{polynomial degree}&\text{leading coefficient}\\
\hline
L_0^j g_0&2j+2&\frac12(-\frac14)^j\\
L_0^j u&2j+1&(-\frac14)^j\\
L_0^j U&2j+1&-(-\frac14)^j.
\end{array}
\tag{4.4}
$$

The identity for $U$ includes $j=0$; $U(t)=1-t$ is nonconstant. The degrees needed before the first separation are at most $s$. A polynomial representative above that degree is still usable, but no uniqueness of such a representative on $N$ nodes is asserted.

**Theorem 4.2 (exact return-word alias and first separation).** Define

$$
\gamma_q=(-1)^{q-1}\frac{(2q+1)!}{4^q}\eta^{2q+1}\ne0.
\tag{4.5}
$$

For every $|\theta|\le\theta_0$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_\theta&=g_0,\\
L_\theta^j g_0&=L_0^j g_0 &&(0\le j\le q-1),\\
L_\theta^j u&=L_0^j u &&(0\le j\le q),\\
L_\theta^j U&=L_0^j U &&(0\le j\le q),\\
L_\theta^q g_0&=L_0^q g_0+\theta\gamma_q Sz.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.6}
$$

All identities concern the actual same-update synthetic generator (2.4), with no replacement of an acquired update by a synthetic conditioning row.

**Proof.** Because $1-v$ is sampled from a degree-one polynomial, (4.1) gives $b^{\mathsf T}(1-v)=0$. Hence $g_\theta=g_0$. The only changed return operator is

$$
L_\theta-L_0=\theta Sz b^{\mathsf T}VA.
\tag{4.7}
$$

For a sampled polynomial column $x=p[v]$ of degree at most $2q-1$, the polynomial representing $VAx$ has degree at most $2q<s$. Therefore $b^{\mathsf T}VAx=0$.

For $x=L_0^j g_0$, (4.4) shows this cancellation whenever $j\le q-2$. Starting from the common $g_0$, induction gives equality of its powers through $j=q-1$. For $x=L_0^j u$ or $L_0^j U$, cancellation holds whenever $j\le q-1$, giving their power equalities through $q$.

At the next g power, $L_0^{q-1}g_0$ has degree $2q$ and leading coefficient $\frac12(-\frac14)^{q-1}$. Applying $A$ halves that leading coefficient and multiplying by $v$ raises the degree to $s=2q+1$. Its new leading coefficient is $(-1)^{q-1}/4^q$. Citation 4.1 therefore gives

$$
b^{\mathsf T}VA L_0^{q-1}g_0=\gamma_q.
$$

Since the preceding g power is already exactly independent of $\theta$, applying (4.7) gives the final identity in (4.6), including its exact linear dependence on $\theta$. The changed term remains on the live proof path to the completed word $w_{q,1}$; it is not an unrelated dependency or a canceled auxiliary component. $\square$

## 5. Paired arrays and the entire finite transcript prefix

**Theorem 5.1 (paired invisibility with positive acquired kernels).** The family in Definition 3.1 has the same emissions, $A,\pi,\tau$, and the same configuration-indexed arrays $f,h$ for every $\theta$. Both arrays are nonconstant. From each corresponding p label, its truncated original transcript law through $2q+1=N-1$ Reads is independent of $\theta$. From each suspended label, the same holds through $2q+2=N$ Reads. Nevertheless

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_i^\theta(w_{q,1})-Q_i^{\theta'}(w_{q,1})
&=(\theta-\theta')\gamma_q U_i z_i,\\
W_i^\theta(\alpha w_{q,1})-W_i^{\theta'}(\alpha w_{q,1})
&=(\theta-\theta')\gamma_q v_i(ASz)_i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
$$

In particular the first line is nonzero at $i=0,1$ for distinct $\theta,\theta'$. Its first separating completed word has exactly $N$ future Reads.

**Proof.** Since $q\ge3$, the common g powers in (4.6) include $j=0,1,2$, proving equality of $f$ in (2.6). Equality of $g,A,v$ proves equality of $h$. For nonconstancy, $f$ is sampled from a degree-six polynomial: the $L_0^2g_0$ summand has leading coefficient $1/32$, and the other summands have lower degree. If all $N\ge8$ entries were equal, this nonconstant degree-six polynomial minus that common value would have at least eight distinct roots, which is impossible. Similarly $h$ has degree three with leading coefficient $1/4$, so cannot be constant on $N\ge8$ distinct nodes.

From p, the completed raw words through $2q+1$ Reads are exactly $w_{j,0}$ for $j\le q$ and $w_{j,1}$ for $j\le q-1$. Their probabilities are the common columns in (4.6). The sole unfinished legal prefix at that horizon is $(\beta\alpha)^q\beta$, whose probability is

$$
(L_\theta^q U)_i.
\tag{5.2}
$$

It too is common by (4.6). These completed words and this unfinished prefix partition the truncated observation space. Hence the entire truncated law agrees, including every coarser observation at an earlier Read cut.

From suspension there is the immediate completed beta, with common probability $1-v_i$. Otherwise the first future alpha has probability $v_i$ and uses the same $A$ to enter the p family. Thus one extra future Read transports the p prefix equality to suspension.

The final identity of (4.6) and (2.4) prove the first line of (5.1). The suspended generation equation proves the second. Since $Sz=(U_0,-U_1,0,\ldots,0)$ and $U_0-U_1=\eta$,

$$
(ASz)_0=\frac12U_0+\frac{\eta}{2N}>0.
$$

So the suspended laws also differ. Rendering the same raw words on the same current $C_0$ preserves these masses and the prefix equality, inserts the same operation blocks, and retains the mandatory Stop. No private mode or transition draw is inserted into that original transcript. $\square$

**Corollary 5.2 (no kernel-free exact inverse or zero-discrepancy modulus).** Even after supplying the full emissions, $A,\pi,\tau$, and both configuration-indexed paired arrays, there is no function that reconstructs all complete configured laws throughout this family without additional information distinguishing $B_\theta$. There is also no TV bound depending only on the discrepancy of those supplied data and vanishing at discrepancy zero. This failure holds on the fixed shape $N=8$.

For every fixed finite Read horizon $H$, there is a finite rational positive family of this kind having identical entire configured transcript laws through $H$ and different complete laws. The necessary $N$ here grows with $H$; this is not a fixed-state impossibility of eventual finite identification.

**Proof.** For $N=8$ choose $q=3$ and the rational signs $\theta=\pm\theta_0$. All proposed inverse inputs agree exactly. Their p laws at labels zero and one differ by (5.1), so an inverse output cannot equal both, and a purported vanishing modulus would bound a positive TV distance by zero. For any $H$, choose $q\ge3$ with $H\le2q+1$ and apply Theorem 5.1. $\square$

**Mathematical citation 5.3 (fixed-kernel and tolerance boundaries).** The result does not contradict [PAIR, Theorem 4.1]: its fixed acquired-kernel premise distinguishes these tables. Nor does it rule out a positive-tolerance approximation from long prefixes. In fact the shared unfinished event in (5.2) has probability at most $(2/3)(4/15)^q$. The complete p-law TV between any two members is at most this mass, because every already completed atom agrees. At suspension the corresponding bound is at most $(4/15)^{q+1}$. These are reused regular survival estimates, not positive unrestricted risk gaps.

## 6. Marginalized and configuration-level differences on the same actual history

**Theorem 6.1 (the blind direction survives actual-row marginalization).** Write

$$
\overline Q^\theta=\frac1N\sum_i Q_i^\theta.
$$

For any distinct $\theta,\theta'$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac1N\sum_i\operatorname{TV}(Q_i^\theta,Q_i^{\theta'})
&\ge\frac{|\theta-\theta'||\gamma_q|}{N}(U_0+U_1),\\
\operatorname{TV}(\overline Q^\theta,\overline Q^{\theta'})
&\ge\frac{|\theta-\theta'||\gamma_q|\eta}{N}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.1}
$$

After installation by Convention 2.2, these are respectively an average of individual decoder differences and a difference of averaged decoders at the same positive actual p history. They apply on every original record fibre with its current renderer, under the same arbitrary allowed $\mu$.

**Proof.** TV dominates the discrepancy of a singleton event. Apply the first line of (5.1) at each configuration and average its absolute value, obtaining the first inequality. Average the signed mass difference before taking its absolute value to obtain

$$
\overline Q^\theta(w_{q,1})-\overline Q^{\theta'}(w_{q,1})
=(\theta-\theta')\gamma_q\frac{U_0-U_1}{N}
=(\theta-\theta')\gamma_q\frac\eta N.
$$

This proves the second inequality. The two bounds are distinct: in general TV and the actual configuration average cannot be interchanged.

The source's actual letters are drawn using $r_K$, not the synthetic label-dependent emissions. The source-independent latch row is uniform. Conditioning on any actual acquired word therefore updates the private row by $B_\theta$ or $A$ without synthetic likelihood weighting. Doubly stochasticity preserves the uniform row after every noncompleting letter. Thus the actual row at every positive active fourth history is exactly the same $\pi$ or $\tau$ for every family member. The original $C_0$ and its target law depend on that same source history and are unaffected by the private table. The full-record renderer preserves both comparisons in (6.1). $\square$

**Convention 6.1.1 (target comparisons on the correct carrier).** At a positive actual p history $h$, define $\widetilde Q_{h,i}^\theta=(I_{C_0(h)})_*Q_i^\theta$ and $\widetilde{\overline Q}_h^\theta=(I_{C_0(h)})_*\overline Q^\theta$. The target $T_h^\mu$ includes the full held records and every future operation. The two quantities

$$
\frac1N\sum_i\operatorname{TV}(\widetilde Q_{h,i}^\theta,T_h^\mu),
\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(\widetilde{\overline Q}_h^\theta,T_h^\mu)
\tag{6.2}
$$

must still be computed in their own order. The target uses the same posterior generated by the same actual $h$, including all paid rejected pairs and partial parses. The general family construction does not supply their equality, their endpoint maxima, a risk excess tending to zero or a positive excess infimum. Theorem 6.3 supplies the exact worst-positive-history p configuration-risk comparison for its specified rational pair and two-endpoint prior. Actual-history averages and worst-positive-history suprema also remain different. Marginalized conditioning defects are not bounded here, and no zero-defect or hard-defect-budget membership is asserted.

**Proposition 6.2 (one common installed program).** There is one finite original observer whose corresponding configurations have all the equal coarse data of Theorem 5.1 and whose complete laws have the different values in (5.1). Both signs are reachable with positive probability after every positive active fourth history.

**Proof.** At the original write/latch update, sample a source-independent fair sign and a uniform label. Use the finite private configuration $(\sigma,i)$, with $\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}$. Preserve the sign on returns, use $B_{\sigma\theta_0}$ after p-beta and $A$ after suspended-alpha, and clear the private configuration on completion. The installed update kernel is one fixed block-diagonal kernel on this finite carrier; its synthetic generator uses that very kernel. The sign, table selector, labels, random states and sampler workspace all belong to COMPLETE.

Each sign component has the uniform actual row by Theorem 6.1, and its sign has probability $1/2$ after every source history. The paired arrays and emissions agree at corresponding $(+,i),(-,i)$ by Theorem 5.1, but the complete laws differ. All comparisons now occur inside one common program, source, prior, actual history and original record chart. The sign is an actual internal configuration coordinate, not a hidden free port. A reader given the full installed kernel can distinguish the two components; the coarse semantic interface in Corollary 5.2 omits that information. $\square$

The single program has one total worst-history risk; Theorem 6.3 compares its two constituent installed tables, not two different values of that total risk. Its private sign is not a recovered source depth. The coarse interface is insufficient even though the runtime can read its counted configuration and a full-table analyst can distinguish the components. The doubled program has two closed sign components; strict positivity and irreducibility hold within each component, not across the components.

**Theorem 6.3 (a common paired signature does not determine the original p configuration risk).** Specialize to $q=3$, $N=8$ and the rational pair $\theta=\pm\theta_0=\pm1/1120$. Install either table on the original source with one prior supported exactly on $\{1,2\}$, assigning positive mass to both. Let $R_{{\rm conf},p}^{+},R_{{\rm conf},p}^{-}$ be their worst-positive-history p configuration risks on the full original fourth-segment domain. Then

$$
R_{{\rm conf},p}^{+}-R_{{\rm conf},p}^{-}
=\frac{9}{219902325555200000000}>0.
\tag{6.3}
$$

Their complete emissions, known suspended-alpha kernel, stationary rows, paired arrays and entire configured p transcript laws through seven Reads coincide. The source law, installed prior, actual source-history law, record renderer and permitted source actions are also the same. Thus those data do not determine this original risk coordinate even on a fixed finite table shape and one common prior. A risk interval guaranteed to contain the exact coordinate for both compatible tables must contain both values in (6.3), and therefore has width at least their stated difference. This does not exclude a larger conservative bound, supply an optimum or assert a common risk-excess gap.

**Proof.** Write $G_j=L_0^jg_0$ and let $\theta$ denote either rational sign. All rows of $L_\theta$ have the same sums, because $b^{\mathsf T}v=0$ and $A\mathbf1=\mathbf1$. Explicitly their sum at index $i$ is

$$
(1-v_i)\left(\frac12v_i+\frac12\overline v\right),
\qquad \overline v=\frac{579}{1600}.
$$

## 7. Finite effective representation and the resource boundary

**Proposition 7.1 (finite rational realization and a sufficient description bound).** For each fixed $q\ge3$, the rational signs admit finite exact stochastic service programs with the same state-carrier and table-denominator sizes. One explicit stored-table representation uses $O(N^2(N+\log N))$ bits for update probabilities, in addition to original control, emissions, selectors and sampler workspace. No prescribed total budget or finite worst-case execution time is asserted.

**Proof.** At the rational signs, all $B$ rows have the common denominator $4NM$ and integer numerators

$$
2M(N\mathbf1_{i=j}+1)\pm z_i b_j.
\tag{7.1}
$$

Their numerators are positive and sum to that denominator. The rows of $A$ use denominator $2N$ and numerators $N\mathbf1_{i=j}+1$. The emissions use denominator $100N$ and numerators $34N+5i$. Thus no exact-real constant or noncomputable sampling procedure is needed for the rational pair.

For denominator $D$, take $t=\lceil\log_2D\rceil$ independent fair bits, accepting their integer when below $D$ and otherwise repeating. Each attempt succeeds with probability $D/2^t>1/2$, so the procedure terminates almost surely and generates a uniform integer below $D$. Selecting the assigned interval gives the finite categorical sampler. For each fixed $q$ its partial integer, comparisons, selected outcome, program counter and workspace have a finite carrier. Rejected private bit attempts do not become source Reads. Their fresh bits are independent of $K$. All sampler microstates and persistent randomness must be counted. Internal states carry the conditional continuation of that same finite service program; they are not assigned unrelated decoders. They create no additional source-query cuts or Read permissions. At a returned original operation cut the effective joint synthetic transition is exactly $q_i(o)P_o(i,i')$, using independent fresh emission and update bits. This is the represented-service convention of [PAID, Lemma 2.1.1]. These facts are the represented rational sampling convention already used by [PAIR, Sections 7–8] and [PAID, Lemma 2.1.1].

Because $M\le2^{N-1}$, the denominators in (7.1) require $O(N+\log N)$ bits. One explicit stored-table representation uses $O(N^2(N+\log N))$ bits for its update probabilities, in addition to original control, model selectors, emissions, indices and sampler workspace. This is a sufficient representation, not an optimum. The two signs share the same denominator sizes, state carrier and program; a fixed mode selects their counted matrices.

Unbounded source rejections and returns, and unbounded private rejection-sampler attempts, preclude a finite worst-case total runtime. Program description, current configuration, random bits, arithmetic, output generation, source Reads, physical preparation, retention, energy and runtime remain separate accounts. The delay $N$ is a number of future Reads in this law comparison, not a total acquisition, execution or address cost. $\square$
