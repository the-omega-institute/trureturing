## 4. Full finite first-pair successor and amortization

**Lemma 4.1 (Finite successor, legality and complete tape cost).** Start at the low origin of the finite $Z(A)$ tape. Scan with a previous-bit finite control until the first adjacent $00$ at positions $j,j+1$. Mark position $j$, clear exactly all positions below it, write one at $j$, preserve every higher digit, adjust the two sentinels/endmarker locally if reached, erase working marks and return to the low origin. This gives exactly $Z(A+1)$ in at most $a(j+1)$ paid bit actions for one fixed constant $a$.

Proof. The two zeros ensure a finite hit. Before the first $00$ there is no $00$, and legality forbids $11$. For $j>0$, the digit at $j$ is zero, the previous one is one, and all lower $j$ digits therefore alternate ending in one. Let $L_j$ be their weighted value. Bases and the two-step extension are

$$
L_0=0=G_0-1,\qquad L_1=G_0=G_1-1,
\qquad L_{j+2}=L_j+G_{j+1}=G_{j+2}-1.
\tag{13}
$$

Thus $L_j=G_j-1$ in both parities. Replacing it by zeros and setting $j$ changes value by $G_j-L_j=1$, with all higher contributions unchanged. Both neighbours of the new one are zero, including $j=0$ with no lower neighbour; higher legality is unaltered. Finite uniqueness, also established by the highest-digit argument in Section 5, identifies this legal word with $Z(A+1)$.

The scanning head detects the pair on visiting its second cell. It peeks one further cell, at $j+2$, to distinguish the two terminal sentinels followed by the endmarker from an interior pair, saves this one-bit end condition, and moves back to mark $j$. It traverses the prefix back to the marked origin while clearing, revisits the marked $j$ by a forward pass, writes one, removes its mark and returns. A constant number of traversals of $[0,j+1]$, with only a fixed number of additional cells at its reached end, pays all reads, writes, cleared zero cells, the endmarker peek and returns, giving $a(j+1)$ after increasing $a$ for fixed binary marker codes. No numerical $j$ register is maintained or incremented.

If the old top is $h$ and the pair is interior, the high digits and endmarker are left untouched. If no earlier pair occurs, $j=h+1$: old sentinels at $h+1,h+2$ and endmarker at $h+3$ become a new top at $h+1$, zeros at $h+2,h+3$ and endmarker at $h+4$. These are a constant number of LOCAL actions where the scan has reached; there is no separate end pass. The zero word has $j=0$ and the same local extension rule creates its first one and two sentinels. This covers arbitrary higher digits, both prefix parities, $j=0$, top growth and sentinel motion. $\square$

**Lemma 4.2 (Exact weighted potential and one terminal reset).** Through the first new block's Return the cache successor instructions, including all head returns, cost $O(T+d_0^2+1)$. The potential used in the proof is never computed by the program.

Proof. On a finite canonical word put

$$
\Omega(z)=\sum_{i\ge0}(i+1)z_i,\qquad\Psi=\lambda\Omega.
$$

For $j=2m$ the cleared ones are at $1,3,\ldots,2m-1$, of weighted sum $m(m+1)$; the new one has weight $2m+1$. For $j=2m+1$ they are at $0,2,\ldots,2m$, of sum $(m+1)^2$; the new weight is $2m+2$. Higher contributions cancel, so EXACTLY

$$
\Delta\Omega=\begin{cases}1+m-m^2,&j=2m,\\1-m^2,&j=2m+1.\end{cases}
\tag{14}
$$

At $j=0,1,2$ the change is $+1$; at $j=3$ it is zero. For even $j\ge4$, $m\ge2$ and

$$
5(m^2-m-1)-(2m+1)=(m-2)(5m+3)\ge0.
$$

For odd $j\ge5$, $m\ge2$ and

$$
5(m^2-1)-(2m+2)=(m+1)(5m-7)\ge0.
$$

Therefore every $j\ge4$ has $\Delta\Omega\le-(j+1)/5$. Choose fixed $\lambda\ge5a$ for the COMPLETE tape-action constant of Lemma 4.1. Then $\mathrm{cost}+\Delta\Psi\le0$ on those steps. For $j\le3$, it is at most $4a+\lambda$, an absolute constant, including all positive small exceptions.

With $L$ used positions, $0\le\Psi\le\lambda L(L+1)/2$. Initially $A=s_0<G_{d_0+1}$; before the new block it never exceeds $G_{d_0+1}-2$. At the terminal child, pre-Emit $A$ is $G_{d_0+1}-2$ for zero, or $G_{d_0+2}-2$ for $10$; post-Advance it is respectively one larger. Width is $O(d_0+1)$, because the increasing weights have $G_{j+2}\ge2G_j$, and these bounds put the highest occupied index within a fixed additive constant of $d_0$. Initial potential is $O(d_0^2+1)$, final potential is nonnegative.

Telescope cost plus potential over the actual increments. There is just ONE new terminal conversion replacing the old cache; its possible positive jump of $\Psi$ is at most $O(d_0^2+1)$, charged once, separately from its actual quadratic conversion work. Thus summing the inequalities gives $O(T+d_0^2+1)$ successor work. Equivalently telescope over the $T-1$ ordinary increments and pay the one terminal increment separately. No unbounded stream of unpaid cache resets is allowed by this first-block claim. $\square$

## 5. Exact binary-to-Z conversion with paid normalization

**Lemma 5.1 (Finite maximum, uniqueness and greedy conversion).** Let $w$ bound the binary operand width and the length of any preexisting used cache/scratch to be replaced; an empty destination is allowed. A binary integer $C\ge0$ of at most $w$ bits has an exact finite conversion to the stated canonical $Z(C)$, including sentinels/endmarker, in $O((w+1)^2)$ bit work and $O(w+1)$ reusable scratch. This includes actual generation, addition, comparison, copy/reversal, scratch clearing and every head return; no exponential table is used.

Proof. Let $M_j$ be the largest no11 value on positions $[0,j)$. Its bases $M_0=0=G_0-1$, $M_1=1=G_1-1$ and, for $j\ge1$,

$$
M_{j+1}=\max(M_j,G_j+M_{j-1})=G_{j+1}-1
\tag{15}
$$

follow by splitting on the top bit: a top one forces the next lower bit zero. Induction proves the formula. At the highest difference of two finite legal words, its $G_j$ outweighs every possible lower opposing contribution $G_j-1$. High-to-low lexicographic and numerical orders coincide; equality of values therefore implies equality of words. This supplies the finite uniqueness used above.

For $C=0$ install two zeros and the marker and clear the previously used cache/scratch. Otherwise generate consecutive $G$ weights from $1,2$ through the first one greater than $C$, retaining only two weights and an index. Retain the last fitting weight and its successor, and residual $C$. For each descending position, compare residual with the weight, write digit one and subtract precisely when it fits, otherwise write zero. To move down, replace a consecutive pair by its lower pair using subtraction. Stop at position zero; do not form a negative-index weight. All choices refer to finite binary integers.

Before considering $G_j$ the residual is less than $G_{j+1}$. If it is selected at $j\ge1$, the new residual is less than $G_{j+1}-G_j=G_{j-1}$, so the adjacent lower bit is zero. If it is not selected, the residual is less than $G_j$, preserving the next-stage invariant. At the final unit the remaining residual is either zero or one and is exhausted. Thus the generated word is legal and its selected weights sum exactly to $C$. By (15) it is $Z(C)$; zero is the separate empty selection. Leading generated zeros can be omitted or removed during the same copying pass.

Since $G_{j+2}\ge2G_j$, only $O(w+1)$ generation and descent stages occur, with $O(w+1)$-bit operands and index fields. Ordinary addition/subtraction/comparison/copy and paid register navigation take $O(w+1)$ per stage on dedicated tapes; updating the small index is covered by that allowance. Digits generated high-first are written to scratch and reversed/copied into the low-first cache by full linear passes. Install precisely two high zero sentinels and the endmarker at the known reached end, clear all used scratch and restore heads. The cache itself has $O(w+1)$ cells; clearing its previous finite used segment has the same bound by the stated destination premise. In the applications both old and new cache lengths are $O(d_0+1)$, including terminal normalization; empty initialization covers zero separately. Generating/copying/clearing weights, residual and output does not allocate a table of all weights. With a fixed number of registers these actions total $O((w+1)^2)$, with $O(w+1)$ simultaneous scratch.

Forming $N_p+n$ or $N_{p'}+n_{\rm old}$ is an additional ordinary binary addition/copy within that bound. Therefore the routine handles the optional rebuild and BOTH terminal blocks, even terminal zero; no illegal digitwise sum is passed off as a canonical word or emitted. The result is private to the producer. $\square$

**Lemma 6.2 (Exact private countdown with constant local ZERO test).** The derived $R=k_p-n$ and ZERO flag can be maintained with $O(T+d_0+1)$ work through the first block, including canonical shrinking, initial/terminal loads, clearing and head returns. The origin mark and adjacent delimiter detect that case locally. In all other cases the result remains positive, and ZERO stays false. Thus a separate zero scan is unnecessary, including $R=1$.

Before the first new block, $R_{\rm initial}=T-1$. Each read-free Advance is followed by exactly one such decrement; after $T-1$ it is zero. Here there is one initial interval of length $T-1$, at most one initial load and one terminal load, all widths $O(d_0+1)$; hence the claimed bound. Future calls remain correctly represented but do not inherit a first-block reset count for free. The flag is an exact invariant, not a guessed death signal. $\square$
