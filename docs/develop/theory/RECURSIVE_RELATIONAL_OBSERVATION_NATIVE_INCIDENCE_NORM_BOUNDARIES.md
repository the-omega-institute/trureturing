# Recursive relational observation: native incidence norm boundaries

This volume compares one application of the heterogeneous graphical
Brascamp–Lieb inequality to an exact equality-incidence lift with direct
Finner bounds. It gives three different limitations: a sharp loss under
whole-scope compensation on constant cycles, an exact unary-gauge optimum
on positive parity potentials, and a common-law entropy obstruction on
the full constructor representation. The compensation classes are specified
separately. The native embeddings, motif pressure and paid canonical
acquisition used below are credited suppliers, not new embeddings or
memory-rate bounds. All proofs here are ordinary mathematics; no Lean
certification or worldwide originality is asserted.

## 1. Native interface and the pressure question

**Convention 1.1 (the unchanged observer).** Use [NPC, §§1–2][NPC]. Trees
are freely generated by the ordered pair constructor and leaves
$\alpha,\beta$, with leaf height zero and pair height one plus the maximum
child height. The substitution is

$$
\rho\alpha=\beta,\qquad \rho\beta=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,
\qquad \rho\langle u,v\rangle=\langle\rho u,\rho v\rangle.
$$

At public $D\ge1$ the entire source family and late menu remain

$$
\mathcal F_D=\{\langle\alpha,v\rangle:h(v)\le D\},\qquad
W_D=\{q\in\{L,R\}^*:|q|\le D-1\},\qquad
\mathcal P_D=\{Rq:q\in W_D\}.
\tag{INB.1}
$$

Every finite original-root word is a legal Read, returning a leaf label,
branch, or absent beyond a leaf. One actual initial source stays stationary
through preparation and late acquisition. Preparation is deterministic,
effective, source-blind, independent of the later target, and terminating
on every source. All source dependence is acquired by charged Reads.
Its complete attained export alphabet counts every accessible surviving
source distinction, including control, selectors, buffers, lengths,
allocation and accessible timing. A paid handoff/isolation service removes
all other accessible dependence. Only then is the public source-independent
$p\in\mathcal P_D$ supplied. The reader chooses zero Reads or one fresh
original-root Read of that same source, before its reply and before any
authentic count. Its final predictor answers $r_{\rho^kt}(p)$ for every
subsequently supplied authentic $k\ge0$ without further Reads.

$M_1(D)$ minimizes the complete attained alphabet. Installation, work,
temporary storage, post-request workspace, final predictor, literal lengths
and letters, authentic-count representation, handoff, isolation, entry,
authentication and physical time remain separate resources. No subtree,
identity, equality, age, reset, copying, navigation or advice port is added.
Isolation, lawful entry, continuity and authentication are service premises.

**Convention 1.2 (credited finite native data).** Keep

$$
C_0=\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle,\quad
S_1=\{\alpha,\beta,C_0\},\quad
S_{d+1}=\{\alpha,\beta\}\sqcup(S_d\times S_d).
\tag{INB.2}
$$

In the last term the ordered pair is the tree constructor. The credited
canonicalization $\pi_d$ preserves every reply on $W_d$ and every temporal
factor $\Gamma_q$, $q\in W_d$, and fixes $S_d$ [CBN, Convention 1.5][CBN].
Write $r_q(x)$ for a reply. The credited all-count factor and decoder are
[NPC, Convention 2.1][NPC]: a branch endpoint has factor $(0,0)$;
otherwise let $q=ar$ first encounter the original leaf $\ell$ at $a$,
put $e(\alpha)=0,e(\beta)=1$ and $s(r)=\#L(r)+2\#R(r)$, and set

$$
\Gamma_q(x)=
\begin{cases}
(2-e(\ell),1-e(\ell)),&r=\varepsilon,\\
(s(r)+2-e(\ell),\mathbf1_{r\text{ ends in }R}),&r\ne\varepsilon.
\end{cases}
\tag{INB.3}
$$

For an attained $(b,c)$ its trajectory is branch for $k\ge b$, beta for
$b\ge1,k=b-1$, alpha for $c=1,k=b-2$, and absent otherwise. The
trajectory recovers the factor. This includes initially absent targets.

A complete native template on $n$ blocks chooses for every $(i,q)$ either
a constant equation or

$$
\Gamma_q(x_i)=\delta(r_a(x_j)),\qquad a\in W_d,
\quad \delta:\mathcal A\longrightarrow\Gamma_q(S_d),
\quad \mathcal A=\{\alpha,\beta,\mathsf{branch},\mathsf{absent}\}.
\tag{INB.4}
$$

All equations concern one common tuple $x\in S_d^n$. Donors can be self,
off-target or cyclic. Write $C_\Theta$ for the simultaneous solutions and

$$
Z_\Theta(\mu)=\sum_{x\in C_\Theta}\prod_i\mu(x_i),\qquad
\beta_d(\mu)=\sup_{n\ge1,\Theta}Z_\Theta(\mu)^{1/n}.
\tag{INB.5}
$$

NPC supplies the semantic identity
$2^{-\sigma_d}=\min_\mu\beta_d(\mu)$. Its Definition 8.1 separately
requires a total sound all-size cap verifier and accepted endpoints
cofinal in $\sigma_d$. The results below concern precisely defined
per-template estimates. A pointwise failure at a chosen $\mu$ does not
establish a failure after optimization over $\mu$.

## 2. Exact lift, repeated coordinates and unary gauges

**Definition 2.1 (finite joint factors and degenerate cases).** Let $J$
be a finite set of variables with finite domains $T_c$. Each factor $f$
has a declared scope $I_f\subseteq J$ and a finite nonnegative table
$\Phi_f$ on $T_{I_f}=\prod_{c\in I_f}T_c$. Define

$$
Z(\Phi)=\sum_{s\in\prod_{c\in J}T_c}\prod_f\Phi_f(s_{I_f}).
\tag{INB.6}
$$

An empty domain makes the sum zero. With no variables there is one empty
assignment. A constant factor on an empty scope multiplies the sum by
its scalar; a zero scalar or an identically zero factor gives zero mass.
An isolated variable contributes $|T_c|$ and is removed with this exact
multiplier. The subsequent formulas apply to the remaining nonempty
scopes and domains, with every variable incident to a factor. If none
remain the constant product already evaluates the sum.

A table originally written with several occurrences of one coordinate
is first restricted to their diagonal, using that one variable. Thus
$I_f$ is a set of distinct variables. A constraint on two references
that coincide becomes its unary diagonal restriction. Its degree counts
one incidence. Distinct factor occurrences remain distinct factor vertices
even if their tables agree. Singleton domains and arguments on which a
table happens to be constant can remain in the declared scopes; they
are not silently deleted when comparing estimates. Zeros are not filled
or divided out. These conventions retain feasible hard supports and all
shared equalities.

RRN Theorem 5.2 supplies exactly (INB.6) for native owned-seed potentials:
each factor is zero or the weight of its uniquely reconstructed actual
block, each seed is an actual owner reply, and each original whole-block
weight occurs once. CBN Theorem 6.2 supplies another such sum, with one
whole-array owner factor per block and unary/binary indicator factors.
Both are equality-aware correspondences in both directions. Arbitrary
finite tables in this section have a native interpretation only when such
a correspondence or an explicit native realization is given.

**Theorem 2.2 (evaluated equality-incidence bound).** For data remaining
after Definition 2.1, put $k_f=|I_f|\ge1$ and
$r_c=|\{f:c\in I_f\}|\ge1$. Choose strictly positive port functions
$h_{fc}:T_c\to(0,\infty)$ and seed functions $g_c:T_c\to(0,\infty)$.
Require

$$
\prod_c g_c(s_c)\prod_f\prod_{c\in I_f}h_{fc}(s_c)=1
\quad\text{whenever }\prod_f\Phi_f(s_{I_f})>0.
\tag{INB.7}
$$

Set

$$
\nu_f(z)=\Phi_f(z)\prod_{c\in I_f}h_{fc}(z_c),\qquad
m_{fc}(a)=\sum_{z:z_c=a}\nu_f(z).
$$

Then the exact incidence lift and its SSSZ upper are

$$
Z(\Phi)\le U(h,g):=
\prod_f\prod_{c\in I_f}
\left[\sum_{a\in T_c}m_{fc}(a)^{r_c}g_c(a)^{k_f}
\right]^{1/(k_fr_c)}.
\tag{INB.8}
$$

Proof. Make one factor vertex with state $z\in T_{I_f}$ and atomic
measure $\nu_f(z)$, and one variable vertex with state $a\in T_c$ and
atomic measure $g_c(a)$. Join $f$ to $c\in I_f$ by the binary equality
function $\mathbf1_{z_c=a}$. Every common assignment $s$ has exactly
one equality-consistent lift, namely $z_f=s_{I_f}$ at every factor.
Conversely every consistent lift descends to that same $s$. Its weight
is the original weight times (INB.7), hence is the original weight;
at an original zero it is still zero because the gauges are positive.
The lifted partition sum is therefore exactly $Z(\Phi)$, not just an
upper or a projection. No original weight is attached to additional copies.

The graph is finite, simple and bipartite, hence triangle-free, with no
isolated vertices. Apply Sah–Sawhney–Stoner–Zhao [SSSZ, Theorem 1.12][SSSZ]
to these nonnegative binary functions and finite vertex measures. The
theorem allows measures without normalization. At edge $(f,c)$ its
swapped-degree norm is $K_{r_c,k_f}$: it uses $r_c$ factor-state copies
and $k_f$ variable-state copies. The complete bipartite equality tests
force their $c$-coordinates to one value $a$. Summing the factor copies
gives $m_{fc}(a)^{r_c}$ and the variable copies give $g_c(a)^{k_f}$.
The $1/(k_fr_c)$ root is exactly the factor in (INB.8). Multiplying
these edge norms proves the inequality. Zero marginal sums cause no
difficulty. The separate constant and isolated-variable multipliers in
Definition 2.1 apply on both sides. $\square$

**Definition 2.3 (three classes kept distinct).** The unmodified estimate
has $h=g=1$ and is denoted $B_{\rm inc}(\Phi)$. Whole-scope
compensation, used in §4, instead replaces the factor tables by arbitrary
nonnegative $\Psi_f$ on their unchanged scopes satisfying

$$
\prod_f\Psi_f(s_{I_f})=\prod_f\Phi_f(s_{I_f})
\quad\text{for every common assignment }s,
\tag{INB.9}
$$

and then evaluates $B_{\rm inc}(\Psi)$ with seed counting measures.
Unary port compensation, used in §5, keeps each original $\Phi_f$ and
uses precisely (INB.7)–(INB.8), including the seed measures $g_c$.
The constructor class in §§6–7 uses the particular reciprocal choice
$g_c=\prod_{f:c\in I_f}h_{fc}^{-1}$ on its specified constructor lift.
No identification of these three optimization problems is assumed.

For each class one may take finite minima or geometric averages of its
sound upper estimates and enlarge the result. This does not add scope
splitting, regrouping, elimination, conditional branching, new variables,
joint-port gauges or any other inference rule. In §7 the trivial
probability upper $1$ is also allowed. The estimates are analytical;
their auxiliary tuples or gauges are not source observations.

## 3. Regular incidence: direct Finner dominates

**Theorem 3.1 (regular-incidence comparison).** Suppose every surviving
variable belongs to exactly $r$ factor scopes, for an integer $r\ge1$.
With finite counting norms on each full factor domain,

$$
Z(\Phi)\le B_F(\Phi):=\prod_f\|\Phi_f\|_r
\le B_{\rm inc}(\Phi)
=\prod_f\prod_{c\in I_f}\|m_{fc}\|_r^{1/k_f},
\tag{INB.10}
$$

where here $m_{fc}(a)=\sum_{z:z_c=a}\Phi_f(z)$. This includes
arbitrary full joint tables, zeros and cycles. Over the same set of
whole-scope compensations (INB.9),

$$
\inf_{\Psi}B_F(\Psi)\le\inf_{\Psi}B_{\rm inc}(\Psi).
\tag{INB.11}
$$

Proof. Choose any strictly positive probability $q_c$ on each $T_c$.
On its scope put

$$
f_f(z)=\Phi_f(z)\prod_{c\in I_f}q_c(z_c)^{-1/r}.
$$

Every variable appears $r$ times, so under the product probability
$\prod_cq_c$, the integral of $\prod_ff_f$ is exactly $Z(\Phi)$.
Also $\int f_f^r\,d q_{I_f}=\sum_z\Phi_f(z)^r$. Finner's
generalized Hölder inequality, in the inspected attributed restatement
[Lubetzky–Zhao, Theorem 3.1][LZ], applies: its scopes are nonempty,
its exponents are $r\ge1$, and at each coordinate their reciprocal
sum is $r/r=1$. It yields the first inequality in (INB.10). This is
a product integration carrier, not an assertion that the constrained
actual seeds are independent.

For each port, nonnegativity and integer $r\ge1$ give

$$
\sum_a\left(\sum_{z:z_c=a}\Phi_f(z)\right)^r
\ge\sum_z\Phi_f(z)^r.
$$

Indeed expansion of an integer power includes the individual $r$th
powers and has only nonnegative additional terms. Taking $r$th roots
and the geometric mean over the $k_f$ ports proves the second
inequality. The final equality follows from Theorem 2.2 with counting
seed measures. Apply this comparison to each $\Psi$ satisfying
(INB.9), whose partition sum is still $Z$, and take infima. $\square$

Regular incidence is essential to the stated common exponent and counting
normalization. No claim that bounded native incidence implies regularity
is made. The inequality is a comparison of these two estimates on the
same tables; §5's varying seed measures are not included in (INB.11).

## 4. Sharp constant-cycle loss with full-scope compensation

**Definition 4.1 (constant cycle and its actual realization).** Fix
$n\ge3$, binary variables $s_1,\ldots,s_n$ cyclically indexed, and
one table $\Phi_i(s_i,s_{i+1})=a>0$ per edge. Thus
$Z=(2a)^n$. For the native instance take $a=1/11$ and the strictly
positive law $\mu=\operatorname{Unif}(S_2)$, since $|S_2|=2+3^2=11$.
The credited [GCB, Theorem 1.2][GCB] realization has

$$
x_i=\langle\operatorname{bit}(s_i),\operatorname{bit}(s_{i+1})\rangle,
\qquad \operatorname{bit}(0)=\alpha,\quad
\operatorname{bit}(1)=\beta.
\tag{INB.12}
$$

Its complete target equations are root factor $(0,0)$, $L$ factor
decoded from own $L$, and $R$ factor decoded from block $i+1$'s $L$.
The total terminal decoder sends alpha to $(2,1)$, beta to $(1,0)$,
and branch or absent to $(2,1)$. The root forces a pair, the own $L$
equation forces a labelled left leaf, and the $R$ equation makes its
right leaf equal the next left leaf. Conversely every binary $s$
produces exactly this actual tuple. This is the supplied embedding
specialized to constant weights; it has $2^n$ distinct solutions.

**Theorem 4.2 (exact optimum in the full-scope class).** For Definition
4.1, over every nonnegative whole-scope compensation (INB.9),

$$
\inf_{\Psi}B_F(\Psi)=(2a)^n,
\qquad
\inf_{\Psi}B_{\rm inc}(\Psi)=(2\sqrt2\,a)^n.
\tag{INB.13}
$$

Thus the second estimate has exactly $n/2$ bits of optimized log-mass
excess on these actual native instances, even though the full cyclic
graph and both coordinates of every original factor remain.

Proof. Every local pair extends to a common binary assignment. Since
the product in (INB.9) is the positive constant $a^n$, every entry of
every $\Psi_i$ is positive. Let $F_i=\log\Psi_i$. Apply the mixed
two-coordinate finite difference in $s_i,s_{i+1}$ to
$\sum_iF_i(s_i,s_{i+1})=n\log a$. For $n\ge3$ only $F_i$ contains
both variables. Hence its mixed difference is zero and

$$
\Psi_i(0,0)\Psi_i(1,1)=\Psi_i(0,1)\Psi_i(1,0).
$$

This positive rank-one table has a factorization
$\Psi_i(s,t)=A_i(s)B_i(t)$: for example take
$A_i(s)=\Psi_i(s,0)$ and $B_i(t)=\Psi_i(0,t)/\Psi_i(0,0)$.
Varying just $s_i$ in the global identity now shows
$A_i(s_i)B_{i-1}(s_i)=\kappa_i$, independent of $s_i$. Thus

$$
\Psi_i(s,t)=c_i\frac{u_i(s)}{u_{i+1}(t)},\qquad
u_i>0,\quad c_i>0,\quad\prod_i c_i=a^n.
\tag{INB.14}
$$

For example $u_i=A_i,c_i=\kappa_{i+1}$ gives this formula.
Each variable has degree two and each scope has size two. Substitute
(INB.14) into (INB.10). The first marginal of table $i$ is
$c_i u_i\sum u_{i+1}^{-1}$ and the second is
$c_i u_{i+1}^{-1}\sum u_i$. Regrouping the cyclic product gives

$$
B_{\rm inc}(\Psi)=a^n\prod_i
\left[\|u_i\|_2\|u_i^{-1}\|_2
              \left(\sum u_i\right)\left(\sum u_i^{-1}\right)
\right]^{1/2}.
\tag{INB.15}
$$

Cauchy–Schwarz on a positive binary vector gives
$\|u\|_2\|u^{-1}\|_2\ge2$ and
$(\sum u)(\sum u^{-1})\ge4$. Therefore (INB.15) is at least
$a^n(\sqrt8)^n$, attained by constant $u_i$ and in particular by
the original constant factors. This proves the second infimum.

Theorem 3.1 gives $B_F(\Psi)\ge Z$ for every compensation. Each
original two-by-two constant table has counting $2$-norm $2a$, so
the unmodified factors attain $B_F=Z$. This proves the first
infimum and the stated excess. $\square$

**Corollary 4.3 (scope of the cycle obstruction).** The class in
Definition 2.3 with fixed seed counting measures and unchanged cycle
scopes cannot have uniformly $o(n)$ per-template logarithmic excess.
Increasing gauge precision, taking finite minima, or taking geometric
averages inside that class cannot remove (INB.13).

Proof. Every permitted estimate is at least the second infimum in
(INB.13); minima and geometric averages preserve that lower bound.
Its log-ratio to the true mass is $n/2$. $\square$

This is not a global-cap separation at the chosen law. The one-block
set of all four labelled-leaf pairs is admissible by its own leaf
queries and has mass $4/11>2\sqrt2/11$. Thus the displayed cycles
do not maximize pressure at that law. Their paid original-source
consumer is given in §8.

## 5. Positive parity potentials: the distinct unary-gauge optimum

**Definition 5.1 (positive parity sector).** Fix $d\ge2$,
$k=2^{d-1}$, and a connected simple $k$-regular graph $G$ on $n$
vertices. Every edge $e$ carries one shared binary variable $s_e$;
the factor at vertex $i$ has its full $k$-edge star as scope. Fix
rational $0<a<1$ and $0<\epsilon<1$. At the $2^k$ actual perfect
height-$(d-1)$ trees $u_b$, in the declared local leaf order, set

$$
\mu(u_b)=a2^{-k}\bigl(1+\epsilon(-1)^{\sum_jb_j}\bigr),
\qquad
\mu(x)=\frac{1-a}{|S_d|-2^k}\quad(x\notin\{u_b\}).
\tag{INB.16}
$$

There are states outside this perfect set, including the two root
leaves. Both parity classes contain $2^{k-1}$ strings, so their
total mass is $a$, and (INB.16) is a strictly positive probability
on the entire $S_d$. The credited perfect-leaf construction
[GCB, Theorem 1.2][GCB] gives a complete native template whose
solutions are precisely the edge-bit assignments, with local factor

$$
\Phi_i(s_{\operatorname{st}(i)})
=a2^{-k}(1+\epsilon\chi_i(s)),\qquad
\chi_i(s)=(-1)^{\sum_{e\ni i}s_e}.
\tag{INB.17}
$$

Explicitly all strict internal targets are branch constants. For each
edge choose one actual terminal occurrence as its owner; both endpoint
terminal targets query that owner leaf with the total decoder of
Definition 4.1. The owner's self-equation excludes branch and absent;
all internal targets force the perfect shape. Every bit assignment
then has exactly one actual lift, with every $\mu(x_i)$ once. This
is a specialization of the credited embedding, not a free seed port.

**Corollary 5.2 (credited parity identity on this sector).** Its exact
mass is

$$
Z_G=a^n2^{-nk/2}(1+\epsilon^n).
\tag{INB.18}
$$

Proof. This is [GDB, Theorem 13.1][GDB] for $k=4$, with the same
standard parity-character argument for general $k$. Expand
$\prod_i(1+\epsilon\chi_i)$ by vertex subsets $A$. Summing a term
over the independent counting coordinates $s_e$ gives zero if an edge
has exactly one endpoint in $A$, since changing that edge bit changes
its sign. Connectedness leaves exactly $A=\varnothing,V(G)$. Each
surviving character is identically one and contributes $2^{|E|}$.
Since $|E|=nk/2$, multiplication by $(a2^{-k})^n$ proves (INB.18).
The product counting carrier in this finite summation is not an
independence assumption on the normalized actual solution law. $\square$

**Theorem 5.3 (sharp positive unary-port compensation).** On the
unchanged full star scopes in Definition 5.1, over all positive $h,g$
satisfying (INB.7),

$$
\inf_{h,g}U(h,g)=a^n2^{-n/2}.
\tag{INB.19}
$$

Consequently the best bound in this class has exact log-mass excess

$$
\frac{n(k-1)}2-\log_2(1+\epsilon^n).
\tag{INB.20}
$$

Proof. The factors in (INB.17) are positive on the entire edge-bit
cube. Thus (INB.7) is a pointwise identity on that entire cube. Put
$\xi_{ic}(b)=\log h_{ic}(b)$ and $\eta_c(b)=\log g_c(b)$.
Feasibility is an affine constraint in these log variables. The
logarithm of each edge integral in (INB.8) is convex in them:
expand the integer power $r_c$ to write the integral as a finite
sum of exponentials of affine expressions with nonnegative constant
coefficients. The logarithm of such a sum is convex, since its Hessian
is the covariance of those affine gradients under their normalized
positive coefficients. The positive root scales the logarithm by
$1/(k_ir_c)$. Summing shows $\log U$ is convex.

Let $K$ be the binary cycle space of $G$, consisting of edge flips
of even degree at every vertex. Such a flip preserves every parity
potential $\Phi_i$. Apply it simultaneously to a shared seed value
and all of its factor-coordinate occurrences. Reindexing the sums
shows that it preserves $U$ and the affine compensation identity.

Every edge lies on a cycle. Indeed a bridge would leave, on one
side of its cut, total degree equal to twice the number of internal
edges plus one, whereas the sum of the even degrees $k$ is even.
This is impossible. A cycle through an edge gives an element of $K$
that flips it. Hence the projection of the finite group $K$ onto
each individual edge bit is onto. Averaging log gauges over $K$
therefore makes each $\xi_{ic}$ and $\eta_c$ constant in its binary
argument. The average is feasible and by convexity cannot increase
$\log U$. No independence or full-star surjectivity of these flips
is used; the one-coordinate projections suffice.

Write the resulting constant multipliers as
$\nu_i=A_i\Phi_i$ and $g_c=G_c$. Formula (INB.8) scales by
$\prod_i A_i\prod_cG_c$: a factor has $k_i$ ports each contributing
$A_i^{1/k_i}$, and a seed has $r_c$ ports each contributing
$G_c^{1/r_c}$. Identity (INB.7) makes this total multiplier one.
Thus every original gauge has $U(h,g)\ge U(1,1)$.

Here $k\ge2$, and summing (INB.17) over the other $k-1$ bits
cancels parity. Each one-bit marginal sum is $a/2$ and $r_c=2$.
Every edge factor in (INB.8) is
$[2(a/2)^2]^{1/(2k)}$. There are $nk$ incidences, so
$U(1,1)=a^n2^{-n/2}$. Unit gauges attain the infimum. Dividing
by (INB.18) proves (INB.20). $\square$

**Corollary 5.4 (unbounded actual family and its boundary).** For
each fixed $d\ge2$, choose dyadic $n>k$ and the simple circulant
with offsets $\pm1,\ldots,\pm k/2$. It is $k$-regular, connected
because it contains the step-one cycle, and satisfies Theorem 5.3.
Every local coordinate is essential: flipping it changes parity and
changes the positive table because $\epsilon>0$. The limiting
logarithmic loss per block is $(k-1)/2>0$.

Proof. The offsets are nonzero and distinct modulo $n$, giving degree
$k$ without loops or repeated edges. The limit follows from
$0<\log_2(1+\epsilon^n)<1$. The essential-coordinate assertion
follows directly from (INB.17). $\square$

This theorem optimizes unary port and seed gauges, not the arbitrary
full-scope tables of §4. It retains the entire cyclic graph, so it
is not a forest or proper-star restriction as in GDB §§20–25. It
also does not supply pressure-maximizing templates: at this very law
the one-block perfect-leaf set is admissible and has mass $a$, so
$\beta_d(\mu)\ge a>a/\sqrt2$. Full-potential Finner at
$\epsilon=0$ is exact on this graph, while its direct incidence
estimate still has (INB.20). No failure of Finner, joint-scope
compensation or optimizer-cofinality follows.

## 6. One actual joint law controls every constructor port gauge

**Definition 6.1 (the fixed constructor estimate).** Fix $d\ge2$,
$k=|W_d|=2^d-1$. Take all reply variables $v=(i,q)$ with domain
$\mathcal A$. Owner $i$ has the full disjoint scope
$\{i\}\times W_d$ and potential

$$
\phi_i(y)=
\begin{cases}w_i(c_d^{-1}(y)),&y\in H_d,\\0,&y\notin H_d,
\end{cases}
\qquad W_i=\sum_{x\in S_d}w_i(x)>0,
\quad\mu_i=w_i/W_i.
\tag{INB.21}
$$

Here $c_d(x)$ is its actual reply array and $H_d=c_d(S_d)$,
with the bijection of CBN Theorem 2.2. Keep every target-constraint
occurrence in CBN Definition 4.1: a donor equation contributes for
each prefix $v\preceq q$ the indicator that its reply belongs to
the projection of the factor level selected by the donor reply.
A constant target gives the corresponding unary tests. Coincident
references are substituted before defining a scope. These indicators
$\phi_h$ have nonempty scope size $k_h\in\{1,2\}$. Owner zeros
already impose actual-tree legality; adding redundant constructor
indicators is allowed but their incidences must then be counted.

The weighted sum is exactly $Z_\Theta(w_1,\ldots,w_n)$ by CBN
Theorems 4.2 and 6.2. Do not combine target tests or delete their
declared arguments in this estimate. For every incidence $(f,v)$
choose a positive $h_{fv}$ and put

$$
b_f(y)=\phi_f(y)\prod_{v\in I_f}h_{fv}(y_v),\quad
a_v(z)=\prod_{f:v\in I_f}h_{fv}(z)^{-1},\quad
M_{fv}(z)=\sum_{y:y_v=z}b_f(y).
$$

If $m_v$ is the number of incident indicator occurrences then
$r_v=m_v+1$. Theorem 2.2 gives the sound estimate

$$
B=\prod_{f,v\in I_f}
\left[\sum_zM_{fv}(z)^{r_v}a_v(z)^{k_f}\right]^{1/(r_vk_f)}.
\tag{INB.22}
$$

This is the constructor class of Definition 2.3, with each entire
original weight retained once. Every gauge can depend on the full
template, weights and size, with arbitrary positive real entries.

**Theorem 6.2 (same-law entropy lower bound on all gauges).** Let $Q$
be any probability on the positive-weight actual solutions of this
template. Let $q_i$ be its original block marginals and $\pi_v$ its
reply marginals. Put

$$
S_v=\sum_{h:v\in I_h}\frac1{k_h},\qquad
\delta_v=\frac{S_v-m_v/k}{m_v+1}\ge0.
$$

With base-two entropy and relative entropy,

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle
\log_2\frac B{\prod_iW_i}
\ge-\sum_iD_2(q_i\|\mu_i)
       +\sum_v\delta_vH_Q(X_v).}
\tag{INB.23}
$$

In particular if every $q_i=\mu_i$, every such $B$ is at least
$\prod_iW_i$. All marginals in (INB.23) are from this one $Q$;
separately chosen locally consistent laws do not meet its premise.

Proof. For a finite nonnegative vector $t$ and a probability $p$
supported where $t>0$, the finite Gibbs inequality is

$$
\log_2\sum_x t_x\ge\sum_xp_x\log_2t_x+H(p).
\tag{INB.24}
$$

Subtracting the right side gives
$D_2(p\|t/\sum t)\ge0$. This follows, in natural logs, from
$-\log u\ge1-u$ applied to the ratios of the reference probability
to $p$ on its positive support; summing gives at least zero because
the reference mass on that support is at most one. Division by
$\ln2$ gives the displayed base-two version.

Let $q_f$ denote the marginal of the same $Q$ on scope $I_f$;
for an owner this is the legal-array image of $q_i$. Conditional
Gibbs on each positive-probability fiber $y_v=z$ gives

$$
\mathbb E_{\pi_v}\log_2M_{fv}(X_v)
\ge\mathbb E_{q_f}\log_2b_f(Y)
       +H(q_f)-H(\pi_v).
\tag{INB.25}
$$

Apply (INB.24) also to the outer sum in (INB.22), with law $\pi_v$:

$$
\log_2\sum_zM_{fv}(z)^{r_v}a_v(z)^{k_f}
\ge r_v\mathbb E_{\pi_v}\log_2M_{fv}
       +k_f\mathbb E_{\pi_v}\log_2a_v+H(\pi_v).
$$

The support conditions hold because $Q$ uses positive owner weights
and satisfying indicators, and all gauges are positive. Combine with
(INB.25), divide by $r_vk_f$, and sum over incidences. The coefficient
of each factor energy and joint entropy is one, since it has $k_f$
ports. Each log gauge appears once in that factor energy and once
with the opposite sign in the incident seed term. All gauge
expectations cancel. For $t_v=1/k+S_v$ the result is

$$
\log_2B\ge
\sum_f\{\mathbb E_Q\log_2\phi_f+H(q_f)\}
-\sum_v\frac{m_v}{m_v+1}t_vH(\pi_v).
\tag{INB.26}
$$

Each owner brace equals $\log_2W_i-D_2(q_i\|\mu_i)$, using the
bijective legal-array encoding. Each indicator energy is zero on
$Q$. For an indicator with $k_h$ coordinates, its joint entropy
is at least each marginal entropy, since conditional entropy of a
finite law is nonnegative. Averaging these $k_h$ inequalities gives
$H(q_h)\ge k_h^{-1}\sum_{v\in I_h}H(\pi_v)$. Thus its contribution
over all indicators is at least $\sum_vS_vH(\pi_v)$. The remaining
coefficient is

$$
S_v-\frac{m_v}{m_v+1}(1/k+S_v)
=\frac{S_v-m_v/k}{m_v+1}=\delta_v.
$$

Finally $k\ge3$ and $k_h\le2$ imply $S_v\ge m_v/2\ge m_v/k$.
This proves (INB.23), including $m_v=0$. No Gibbs-law premise on
$Q$, no local-to-global completion, and no equality between separately
optimized entropies was used. $\square$
