## 1. The original objects, history domain, and task

**Definition 1.1 (fixed original runtime).** Let

$$
\mathcal T::=\alpha\mid\beta\mid\langle s,t\rangle,
\qquad
\rho\alpha=\beta,\quad
\rho\beta=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,\quad
\rho\langle s,t\rangle=\langle\rho s,\rho t\rangle.
$$

Pairing is ordered. Fix $H\ge1$, $N=2H-1$, the preparation occurrence
$\xi$, and exactly the canonical continuing dispatcher of
[Acquired Input Frontier and Replay](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/blob/346647b9c5e41ec76f0ea19d5796323dc813f750/docs/develop/theory/RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_NATIVE_ACQUIRED_INPUT_FRONTIER_AND_REPLAY.md)
(AIF), Definitions 2.1–2.5. Its literal native rule table, port assignments,
symbol encoding, reference conventions, and finite control are fixed public
parameters. They do not depend on the unknown source. The source is installed
by the paid private input of
[Transport Memory Completion](RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_TRANSPORT_MEMORY_COMPLETION.md)
(PR57), §57, Definition D5, as its unique padded $N$-token packet:

$$
\mathsf{PAD}=00,\qquad\alpha=01,\qquad\beta=10,
\qquad\mathsf{PAIR}=11.
$$

Validation scans the entire allocated packet. The valid installations are
exactly $u\in\mathcal T_H=\{t:1\le\lambda(t)\le H\}$. Initial input,
allocation, zeroing, control installation, validation, and descriptors are
part of the original execution, not source-dependent initial advice.

The supplied no-update producer is executed, its lossless output is
materialized, and its paid delivery to the declared controller owner is
completed. Let $a(u)$ be that acquisition fence. The producer's return is
a subroutine return to the continuing dispatcher. In particular, its native
Stop is not global halt. The ordinal of $a(u)$ can depend on $u$.

**Definition 1.2 (canonical frames and all lawful cuts).** Use exactly AIF's
language $\mathsf C$. A frame has a three-bit tag, the unary length
$U(d)=1^d0$, and $d$ payload bits. The tags are ReadE `000`, Rho `001`,
TagRead `010`, Left `011`, and Right `100`. ReadE and Rho have empty
payloads. A TagRead payload is its address, with 0 meaning L and 1 meaning
R. A context payload has the prefix code branch 0, alpha 11, beta 10.
All finite contexts are admitted as material, including those whose entire
modification will reject. Context names and descriptors are constructed
canonically from their actual occurrences.

Put

$$
\mathsf P=\{p:\exists w\in\mathsf C,\ p\preceq w\},
\qquad
\operatorname{Adm}(h)=\{b\in\{0,1\}:p(h)b\in\mathsf P\},
$$

where $p(h)$ is the consumed prefix of the frame at the next prescribed
source-frame IN. Let $\mathcal R_H$ be AIF's full actual prefix class after
$a(u)$, over all valid $u$ and all finite lawful request sequences. It
includes every extendible incomplete tag, unary length and payload; the
IN-to-WR latch interval; parsing and generation; private gates; invalid
source-write packets; rejects; counter preparation; commitment; partial
rows; seals; both copy stages; and acknowledgement. No fixed input schedule,
bounded context size, or completion-only restriction is imposed.

An enabled raw IN consumes either physical bit, advances its native control,
and constructs its receipt. If $p(h)b\notin\mathsf P$, that occurrence
executes but leaves $\mathcal R_H$. It is not an illegal-unexecuted `none`.
Subsequent malformed-frame behavior is outside this defined domain.

**Definition 1.3 (original task, before any view).** Let $Q$ be precisely
AIF Definition 2.6. Its primitive experiments are Advance of the next
prescribed non-IN primitive and Supply($b$) at a prescribed IN. A Supply
there is defined on this lawful task domain exactly for
$b\in\operatorname{Adm}(h)$. A trial of a physically disabled or
unauthorized operation returns `none` without execution. An enabled
noncanonical Supply instead makes the experiment undefined. These cases
are different coordinates of the partial response map.

The task also includes its specified finite lawful semantic continuation
and authentic-row audit experiments, with finite adaptive branching only on
acquired public data. Responses retain actual labels, released outputs,
legality, and the complete original joint successor after each executed
action. Empty responses retain running/terminal type and terminal output
when applicable. The complete successor is a mathematical coordinate of
this task; it is not a new publicly transmitted value or read permission.
Write $h\sim_Q k$ for equality of these partial response maps, including
their domains.

The original source ports remain TagRead, ReadE, guarded Rho, Left, and
Right. TagRead and ReadE preserve the source. Rho and the two grafts test
their entire actual candidates against $\lambda\le H$. Rejection preserves
the source tree while executing work, commitment, recording, and delivery.
TagRead absent, an executed reject, a paid `no-row`, local evaluator work,
an unavailable destination, and termination have their original distinct
types. A Fetch index requires the original sealed-prefix descriptor,
material references, ownership, and paid lookup. A submitted source address
does not supply that index or descriptor.

**Definition 1.4 (physical record and semantic annotations).** Write $W(h)$
for the actual finite tape words, all eight cell tracks, the finite encoded
PC, latches and tokens, and the fixed port origins. All constructed cells,
including erased cells, old versions, native/dynamic code, descriptors,
archives, mailboxes, and partial destinations, occur in these words.
Let $b\ge1$ be the fixed total number of finite register bits, including
all symbol-latch subfields and finite control/permission bits. Unbounded
integers, stacks, and names are tape material, not extra registers.

Let $X(h)$ be the complete joint endpoint of AIF Definition 2.5. Decoded
request/event/counter interpretations and occurrence provenance in $X$ need
not be additional physical tapes: they are interpretations of retained
material and the actual execution. During a busy overwrite $W$ contains the
actual possibly invalid source bytes and actual phase. It contains no
fictional valid current source tree. The full primitive prefix $h$ contains
the actual operands and outcomes, including private ones mathematically.

## 2. Retained evidence and the exact original kernel

**Proposition 2.1 (extraction from retained material).** On the actual image
of $\mathcal R_H$, there is a finite mathematical extraction

$$
\mathcal E(W(h))=K(h)
 =\bigl(\operatorname{code}(u),I(h),n(h)\bigr),
\tag{SSA.1}
$$

where $I$ is the actual consumed post-acquisition input and $n$ is the
absolute original primitive ordinal. Together with AIF Theorem 4.4, this
makes both $W(h)$ and $X(h)$ determine $h$. The extraction does not constitute
a callable handle or an authorized all-owner scan.

Proof. The acquisition is a no-update run on the actual installed $u$.
Its truthful addressed preorder TagRead replies are in authentic completed
producer rows before $a(u)$; those rows and their origin chain are immutable
under Literal Journal Adapter (LJA), Definitions 5.1–5.2 and Theorem 5.3.
They survive later overwrites of the current-source block. Reading the
acquisition segment in its actual order and applying the supplied preorder
decoder reconstructs $u$. The materialized lossless delivered seed is
another witness while retained; persistence of a temporary output is not
needed in place of the immutable acquisition rows. The initial acquisition
segment is identified by its original phase/template and fence chain, not
by a chosen later source value. Its read projection is the production trace
of `NativeFullSourceProducer.native_source_producer`. No subtree used as a
parameter in that theorem's private proof invariant is runtime input.

AIF Definition 2.3 retains earlier consumed frame blocks and their actual
chronological descriptors. Their written concatenation is $L(h)$. The
current input latch contributes a singleton only in its occupied IN-to-WR
phase; otherwise it contributes the empty word. Thus a stale physical
latch outside that phase is ignored. AIF Theorem 4.2 gives
$I(h)=L(h)\mathbin{+\!+}Q_{\mathrm{in}}(h)$ with
$|Q_{\mathrm{in}}(h)|\le1$. References must be followed to retained
payloads; their printed names alone do not perform this extraction.

Finally count the original meter's receipt cells. PR57.D1 constructs exactly
one receipt per primitive from its irreducible entrance, so this count is
$n(h)$, including boot and acquisition. This is a finite inspection, not a
free stored integer. These three operations give SSA.1. AIF Theorem 4.4
then recovers the full actual prefix and endpoint with the fixed canonical
installation and actual occurrence names. A complete $X$ contains $W$, so
the same argument applies to $X$. No physical source query, private-latch
read by a new actor, or receipt-index acquisition has been introduced. ∎

**Theorem 2.2 (one-step rigidity of this continuing task).** For all
$h,k\in\mathcal R_H$,

$$
h\sim_Q k
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
K(h)=K(k)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
W(h)=W(k)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h=k.
\tag{SSA.2}
$$

In fact the partial task responses to at most one primitive action already
have this kernel. This assertion uses the complete-successor coordinate
and the continuing, nonabsorbing dispatcher.

Proof. Every cut has a lawful next primitive. A non-IN cut has its
prescribed Advance. At IN, the current frame is a proper prefix in
$\mathsf P$, so a witnessing completion supplies at least one next
admissible bit. At the final idle cut of a finite witnessing request list,
append the canonical ReadE frame `0000`; AIF Lemma 2.7 gives the dispatcher's
next reservation/ingress work. Idle is therefore not global halt.

Suppose the one-step partial response maps agree. If Advance succeeds at
$h$, it must succeed at $k$ with the same complete successor. Proposition
2.1 extracts from that successor
$\bigl(\operatorname{code}(u),I(h),n(h)+1\bigr)$ on the first side and
the corresponding tuple on the second. A non-IN has consumed no additional
external source-frame bit. Cancelling $+1$ gives equal predecessor keys.

Otherwise $h$ is at IN. Choose $c\in\operatorname{Adm}(h)$. Supply($c$)
has a defined successful response. At $k$ it cannot be disabled (`none`),
and cannot be an enabled noncanonical Supply (undefined). It must be the
same successful Supply with an equal complete successor. Extract its key:

$$
\bigl(\operatorname{code}(u_h),I(h)c,n(h)+1\bigr)
 =\bigl(\operatorname{code}(u_k),I(k)c,n(k)+1\bigr).
$$

Right cancellation of the common bit and cancellation of $+1$ again give
equal predecessor keys. This argument does not confuse undefinedness with
disabledness, and does not require an inadmissible bit to be prevented
physically. AIF Theorem 4.4 gives $h=k$ from either equal-key case.

Equal full histories give equal states, keys, and response-map domains.
Determinism with the same subsequent supplied bits gives the same responses
to every allowed finite experiment, including the semantic and audit
experiments. Thus equality of histories implies full $Q$ equivalence, and
full $Q$ equivalence implies the one-step equivalence just considered.
Proposition 2.1 and replay give both remaining equivalences in SSA.2. ∎

**Proposition 2.3 (the role of the hypotheses).** Theorem 2.2 does not give a
diagonal kernel for the public-output-only task, for a dispatcher with
unrecorded external choices, or for an arbitrary absorbing terminal
extension.

Proof. Its predecessor argument reads retained coordinates from a complete
successor, which is absent from the public-output-only response. If two
unrecorded choices can reach the same retained record at the same ordinal,
SSA.1 does not distinguish their histories and the cited replay premise no
longer applies. For an absorbing terminal extension with a single fixed
terminal output and all nonempty actions disabled, any two different
terminal endpoints have the same task response map. Thus the progress step
used in Theorem 2.2 is essential. These observations delimit the proof;
they do not assert that the public-output-only kernel of the present
runtime has any particular unidentified pair. ∎

## 3. The additional finite port primitive

**Assumption 3.1 (priced atomic structural patches).** The alternative
storage machine has vertices with the finite port alphabet

$$
\{\mathsf P,\mathsf L,\mathsf R,
  \mathsf O_0,\mathsf O_1,\mathsf H_0,\mathsf H_1\}.
$$

An occupied port is joined to exactly one occupied partner port; the two
endpoint slots agree. An unoccupied port is local empty incidence, not an
address integer. The ports $\mathsf L,\mathsf R$ connect to a child's
$\mathsf P$. The remaining ports give origin/head access links. A known
endpoint permits a local partner probe. This includes traversal of
$\mathsf P$; there is no global incoming-edge search.

The irreducible mechanism admits a bounded atomic patch: follow a specified
finite list of port walks from its distinguished root; inspect the stated
finite components; compute one finite truth-table result; assign a bounded
list of endpoint slots; create a bounded finite template; and commit the
native control, core effect, and receipt effect together. All these
suboperations are charged. Their internal partial edge assignments are
not separately observable cuts. The bounds and finite truth tables may
depend on the fixed installed controller, but not on $H$, source values,
context length, tape length, input history, or elapsed ordinal.

Only the patch for the original prescribed row, with its original authority
and guards, is enabled. The core's internal structural access is confined
to the original addressed cells, adjacent moved-to cells, finite registers,
and meter footprint, together with their finite routing scaffold. It does
not release an additional private value. A private switch has exactly the
privacy of the bit it replaces. The distinguished root is a primitive
access location, not an unbounded selected matching site.

A patch costs one commitment unit, one unit per port probe, one per endpoint
slot assignment, and one per new vertex. Assignments of an unchanged value
and initialization of empty slots are charged. This is an additional ideal
primitive and price convention. It is not an assertion that ordinary PR57
bit operations already perform atomic graph patches, or that its probes
have a particular physical duration.

**Definition 3.2 (fixed apparatus and naming).** A full ordered binary
scaffold $F_m$ with $m\ge1$ sockets is chosen recursively by splitting
the ordered socket list into $\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ and
$\lceil m/2\rceil$ nonempty parts when $m>1$; $F_1$ is one socket.
The outer scaffold is $F_{65}$: ports 0–63 in order, then the register
component. The register scaffold is $F_b$. These are finite fixed apparatus.
Their orientation, root, seven endpoint-slot types, finite rule mechanism,
and original symbol/register encodings are explicit model structure.

The rule apparatus is the finite table constructed in Definition 5.2. Its
literal description size $d_{\mathrm{patch}}$ and finite primitive
mechanism are additional fixed infrastructure. No mutable unrepresented
driver tape is present between patch events; the bounded temporary work
is internal to the declared atomic primitive. The fixed rule table is not
source-specific storage and is included in the apparatus resource bill.
Choose its description encoding as follows. Number the seven ports in
their displayed order and number bounded local roles by first occurrence
in the row's walks, old roles before new template roles. Flatten each
table entry into its original row number, finite tested pattern, guarded
walk lists, finite output-label code, endpoint-assignment lists, and new
template incidence lists, in that order. Encode a natural by $U(n)$ and
a finite list by $U(\text{length})$ followed by its entries, recursively.
Endpoint entries are the two local role/port indices or the empty-slot
tag. Thus $d_{\mathrm{patch}}$ is the bit length of this completely
specified finite table encoding. These indices are fixed finite rule
roles, not runtime numeric addresses. Installing this additional immutable
description, when it is material rather than primitive backing, requires
ingress and writing of all $d_{\mathrm{patch}}$ bits, together with its
actual allocation, movement, receipt, descriptor and token work and backing;
such a preparatory implementation must retain its own cuts. The theorem
uses the explicitly endowed finite rule mechanism as its primitive basis.

Original cell names are their actual port/creation occurrences. Auxiliary
vertices are the fixed ordered template occurrences of that same creation.
The terminal that is expanded by a cell append retains its occurrence.
A bit's fork and three terminals retain their occurrences on reassignment.
There is no independent fresh numeric name input. Equivariant descriptions
may rename auxiliary vertices consistently while preserving ports, root,
cell provenance, and template roles. Such renaming is not permission to
alter an original descriptor or preparation/version reference. Equalities
of tree supports below use rooted ordered positions; exact original names
are recovered by the original canonical replay, not guessed from an
arbitrary graph isomorphism.

## 4. Storage by incidence and its two natural readouts

**Definition 4.1 (one bit and one cell).** Let $\bullet$ be a terminal, and
write ordered pairing for incidence only. A bit is stored as

$$
g_0=\langle\langle\bullet,\bullet\rangle,\bullet\rangle,
\qquad
g_1=\langle\bullet,\langle\bullet,\bullet\rangle\rangle.
\tag{SSA.3}
$$

Its five actual occurrences are root $r$, fork $f$, singleton terminal
$t$, and the ordered fork terminals $v_0,v_1$. The fork's children do not
move. For bit 0 the root targets are $(f,t)$, and for bit 1 they are
$(t,f)$. Assignment sets both root target slots and both target parent
slots: $r.\mathsf L,r.\mathsf R,f.\mathsf P,t.\mathsf P$. All four
assignments occur and are charged even for an unchanged bit.

An eight-track original cell $c=(c_0,\ldots,c_7)$ is the fixed balanced
$F_8$ with $g_{c_i}$ substituted at socket $i$, in the original track
order. Denote it $G(c)$. Thus marker symbols, data symbols, all three head
tracks, seal track, and reserved track are actual incidence distinctions.
There is no second byte payload alongside $G(c)$.

**Definition 4.2 (tapes and access incidence).** For a finite tape word
$w=c_1\cdots c_k$, including its actual boundary cells, set

$$
\mathcal L(w)=
\langle G(c_1),\langle G(c_2),\ldots,
    \langle G(c_k),\bullet\rangle\ldots\rangle\rangle,
\qquad\mathcal L(\varepsilon)=\bullet.
\tag{SSA.4}
$$

The tape origin is this component's root, a fixed outer socket occurrence.
Before its boot construction a tape can have the empty word; partial boot
words are represented literally. Post-acquisition every tape has its
original constructed interval and marked heads. The finite-register
component is $F_b[g_{r_1},\ldots,g_{r_b}]$. Substitute the 64 tape
components and this register component into $F_{65}$, obtaining the full
ordered storage tree $U(W)$.

For each ordinary tape add one origin-to-head link, and for the meter add
its two distinct origin-to-head links. They join the appropriate
$\mathsf O_j$ at the tape origin to $\mathsf H_j$ at the root of its
marked cell $G(c)$. Before a particular head exists its link is absent.
These 65 links at a post-acquisition cut are genuine additional maintained
incidences. They are uniquely determined by the original mark tracks.
They supply local access, not descriptor-based random access to arbitrary
cells. Let $\Gamma(W)$ be the tree with these links. No descriptor-to-block
shortcut is added.

**Definition 4.3 (support and terminal boundary).** For a finite full
ordered tree $U$, let $\operatorname{Pos}(U)$ be its root-relative
$\{0,1\}$ incidence paths and $\operatorname{Leaves}(U)$ the terminal
paths. For the structural runtime corresponding to $h$, define

$$
S(h)=\operatorname{Pos}(U(W(h))),\qquad
B(h)=\operatorname{Leaves}(U(W(h))).
\tag{SSA.5}
$$

These are two independently specified relations of the actual storage-tree
layer: its entire support and its terminal antichain. Neither is an event
word, a tuple placed in a decorative tree, nor the old source-tree frontier.
The access links are another actual relation of the carrier; they are
derivable from either view and the fixed layout. Root-relative paths in
SSA.5 are mathematical coordinates, not automatically maintained printed
address strings.

**Theorem 4.4 (cellwise inverse and boundary inverse).** On the actual
storage image, each of $S$ and $B$ alone determines $W$, $\Gamma(W)$,
the full original $h$, and $X(h)$. The inverse includes source-invalid,
live-latch, descriptor, authority, seal, and partial-copy states.

Proof. In a bit component, the unique root child which has children is the
fork. Its side distinguishes 0 and 1. The fixed three-level $F_8$
therefore recovers each of the eight cell tracks without an external label.
The right spine of a tape ends at its actual terminal; its successive left
components recover exactly its finite cell word. A zero-length tape is
recognized at that component's terminal. The 65 fixed outer socket paths
and $F_b$ recover every tape and every finite register bit. All marks and
tokens are recovered, so the access links are then uniquely recoverable.
This proves $S\mapsto W\mapsto\Gamma(W)$ without first replaying a
transcript to discover the byte values.

For a finite full ordered tree every position extends a terminal position:
follow children until termination, which holds by finiteness. Conversely
every prefix of a terminal path is a tree position. Consequently

$$
\operatorname{Pos}(U)
 =\{v:\exists w\in\operatorname{Leaves}(U),\ v\preceq w\}.
\tag{SSA.6}
$$

Taking maximal elements of a full finite support gives exactly its leaves.
Thus $B$ reconstructs $S$ by prefix closure, and $S$ reconstructs $B$ by
maximality. The terminal antichain is that of the actual tree incidence,
not a general interface asserted to determine an arbitrary graph interior.
The head links are determined by the already recovered mark bits.

No packet-validity premise was used: $G$ stores every byte. In particular,
an IN changes the input-latch component immediately; the source's individual
WRs change its actual raw cell components; and complete-but-unsealed copy
bytes retain their unavailable seal/token state. Proposition 2.1 and AIF
replay now recover the original history, complete semantic interpretations,
and actual original occurrence identities. Fixed local template roles
recover the auxiliary identities as well. This last provenance step is
where canonical naming matters; arbitrary externally selected names would
require additional acquired material. ∎

**Proposition 4.5 (exact carrier counts).** If $K_{\mathrm{cell}}$ is the
number of all constructed original tape cells at a post-acquisition cut,
then the storage tree has

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=127+6b+48K_{\mathrm{cell}},\\
L&=64+3b+24K_{\mathrm{cell}},\\
E_{\mathrm{tree}}&=V-1,\qquad
E_{\mathrm{access}}=65.
\end{aligned}
\tag{SSA.7}
$$

The number of occupied endpoint slots is $2V+128$. A cell append adds
48 vertices, 24 terminals, and 48 tree edges. Changing a bit creates no
vertex. The finite port capacity is seven slots per vertex. The fixed
rule apparatus is additional infrastructure, not included in $V$.

Proof. A bit has five vertices and three terminals. $F_8$ contributes
seven internal vertices, hence a cell has $7+8\cdot5=47$ vertices and
24 terminals. A $k$-cell right spine contributes $k$ new internal vertices
and one final terminal, so SSA.4 has $48k+1$ vertices and $24k+1$
terminals. The register component has $b-1+5b=6b-1$ vertices and $3b$
terminals. The outer scaffold contributes 64 internal vertices. Summing
over 64 tapes gives SSA.7. A tree has one fewer edge than vertices; adding
65 two-ended access links gives $2(V-1)+130=2V+128$ occupied slots.
All changes in the stated append differences follow by subtraction.
The empty boot tapes satisfy the same tree formulas with $k=0$; during
boot the link count is the actual number of installed heads, at most 65. ∎

## 5. Finite constructive primitive correspondence

**Definition 5.1 (local access and elementary patch templates).** The core
uses the fixed outer path to a tape origin followed by its head link to
reach the current cell root. From a current cell root, the next cell root
is reached by $\mathsf P\mathsf R\mathsf L$, and the previous cell root
by $\mathsf P\mathsf P\mathsf L$ when the original move is enabled.
The trailing terminal after a final cell is reached by
$\mathsf P\mathsf R$. These use actual parent endpoint slots; they are
not searches for an incoming edge.

There are four elementary templates, all within the atomic patch:

1. Inspect a finite component by depth-first traversal of its
   $\mathsf L,\mathsf R$ edges, probing both child slots at each vertex
   and its parent slot on each return. Decode switch side as in Theorem 4.4.
2. Assign each bit in the original row's designated symbol, mark, latch,
   control, or token field by the four endpoint assignments of Definition
   4.1. A whole byte assignment uses eight such assignments when that is
   the original field. An originally assigned bit is assigned even if its
   value is unchanged. Other fields, particularly immutable read operands,
   receive no slot assignment merely because they were inspected.
3. Append a cell by expanding the existing trailing terminal $z$ into
   $\langle G(c),z'\rangle$. Create the 47 cell vertices and new terminal
   $z'$, initialize all seven slots of each, and assign $z.\mathsf L$
   and $z.\mathsf R$. New child parent slots are part of initialization.
4. Retarget a head link by clearing its old target's head slot, assigning
   the origin slot, and assigning the new target's head slot. An initial
   activation has only the latter two assignments. The respective head
   mark bits are assigned in their cell templates. The distinct meter
   head slots prevent an alias when both meter heads occupy the same cell.

The assignments are evaluated on the pre-patch values, with the original
core/receipt combination determining each final byte and its designated
target tracks. If two named cell roles denote the same occurrence,
combine their original target fields on that one physical cell. Assign
only those target bits to their combined final values. This is a bounded equality check on reached
endpoints, not matching over the tape. The three retarget assignments are
a charged ordered list inside one atomic patch; an unchanged target still
has its final head slot restored. There is no cut after its clearing.

**Definition 5.2 (the finite row compiler).** Take the fixed native rows of
PR57.D1 and the AIF dispatcher, including their finite bootstrap variants.
For each row, enumerate its finitely many control/token/latch bit patterns,
the eight-track bytes in its bounded cell roles, the finite endpoint alias
patterns, and its allowed input bit when it is IN. Use the following table
to assign the core effect. Afterwards combine its receipt and control
effects as described below. A row guard and permission failure admits no
executing patch.

| Native core row | Structural operands and exact core effect |
| --- | --- |
| RD($i,j$) | Inspect the actual current cell at the named head. Decode the original symbol into the specified finite latch. No core head or tape-symbol change. The original testing row, if any, tests that symbol with its original finite immediate. |
| WR($i,s$) | At the original writable ordinary cell assign precisely the original symbol or latched bit. Other symbol/mark/seal tracks retain their original values. Source writes act on these actual cells, including invalid source packets. |
| ML($i$), MR($i$) | Follow the displayed adjacent-cell route, apply the original boundary guard, clear the old head mark and set the new mark, and retarget that head link. There is no offset arithmetic. |
| EX($i$) | At the original right marker change its symbol to blank, append one new R cell, and leave the ordinary head on the old cell, now blank. Its core head link therefore stays there. This is not the meter append-head convention. |
| BF($f$) | Inspect the specified finite operand-latch bits, use exactly the eight-entry truth table $f$, and assign the result-latch bit. There are exactly 256 three-input tables. |
| IN($i$) | Assign the actually supplied bit to the designated input latch and take the prescribed control successor. Do not write or move a tape. Both physical raw bits have their patches, including a bit outside $\operatorname{Adm}$. |
| OUT($j$) | Release exactly the specified output-latch bit at its original public output permission. This does not acquire private data into a different output latch. |
| TOK($a,b$) | Transfer exactly the original finite availability/owner token; no arbitrary block data or address is copied. |
| CTL | Take exactly the finite original control/phase/fence/seal edge. A designated cell seal mark, when present, is in its actual bounded addressed footprint. Commitment interprets already prepared material; it does not atomically construct an unbounded request, counter, or row. |
| Boot boundary constructor and bit installer | At the fixed port's actual empty/partial word append one original boundary cell, or perform the specified single-bit installation assignment, with the original finite boot successor. There is no preconstructed data arena. |

Every executing row also changes the old meter append R into the original
ordinary or fence receipt, appends a new meter R, moves its append mark,
and retargets its append-head link to that new R. A meter read head remains
at its original cell unless the original core moves it. In particular,
when that read head is on the old append R, it stays on the cell now holding
the receipt. Overlap between a core meter operation and the receipt side
effect is resolved by the original finite joint rule on that shared cell,
not by two inconsistent copies of its byte. The native control successor
and all finite register assignments occur in the same patch.

For specificity the finite enumeration orders rows by their installed
number, bytes lexicographically in original track order, register bits
lexicographically, and the bounded named-role equality partitions
lexicographically. Reject inconsistent endpoint/mark patterns. For each
remaining pattern apply the displayed finite symbol/Boolean/mark effect,
then the receipt rule and original combined-overlap convention. Expand the
four templates of Definition 5.1 in that order, assigning old target bits
once in first-role/track occurrence order, appending the core cell before the receipt
cell if both exist. This constructs a finite literal patch table. It is a
per-row finite construction, rather than an appeal to an unspecified
universal graph machine.

**Proposition 5.3 (uniform local footprint).** The compiler of Definition
5.2 is finite and source uniform. Every original executing primitive uses
at most three old cell components, at most two appended cell components,
the $b$ finite-register switches, and bounded fixed routing paths. It
creates at most 96 vertices. Its old and new footprint is contained in a
fixed-radius port neighborhood of the distinguished root; for example
$R=20+\lceil\log_2(b+1)\rceil$ suffices for the specified scaffolds.

Proof. A core tape row addresses one cell and, for a move, one neighbor.
The receipt adds its old append R. Other core rows touch finite registers
and at most their designated seal cell. Thus at most three old cell
components are needed, with overlap reducing this number. EX or a boot
constructor creates at most one core cell and the receipt creates one
meter cell, giving two cell appends and $2\cdot48=96$ new vertices.
Other writes rewire existing switch occurrences.

The outer scaffold has height at most seven. Its origin/head route has
at most eight edges. A neighbor adds three; a cell component has height
five. The finite-register component has height at most
$\lceil\log_2 b\rceil+2$. Append terminals are two edges from their
old final cell root, and the new cell is attached there. These estimates
are bounded by the displayed $R$, also including returns and head slots.
All relevant sites are reached by specified walks from the distinguished
root. Only finitely many bytes, finite registers, and aliases occur in
these fixed roles, so the enumeration terminates and yields a finite table.
Neither its size nor $R$ depends on a tape length or private input.

The meter access link is essential to this locality assertion. On a bare
growing tape with access only from its left origin, the append receipt site
can be arbitrarily far away. Calling such a bare-chain rule local would
not prove this proposition. The explicit head links are paid additional
relations, and port locality here is not Euclidean locality. ∎

## 6. Canonical installation and all-cut conformance

**Definition 6.1 (structural entrance).** Replace the original irreducible
primitive port by its fixed structural version: $F_{65}$, the finite
register component in the same fixed initial state, the two meter boundary
cells and their original head marks, and the meter's two head links.
The other tapes are empty components. This is a declared finite primitive
foundation, with construction/endowment charged in Definition 8.2.
Its native event ordinal is zero. It contains no unknown source material.

Run the original finite bootstrap, installing every ordinary boundary,
native control-description bit, and finite control/token bit via its
compiled single-row patch. Supply the same canonical private source bits
by their IN/WR/MR rows. Allocate and zero source/workspace cells and
construct Base offsets/descriptors by the original literal routines.
Execute the original fixed validation and no-update acquisition, every
row, seal, mailbox copy, receiver copy, and token operation included.
Thereafter use the unchanged dispatcher and source programs, with each
native row interpreted by its compiled patch. Denote the resulting
structural history over an original prefix $h$ by $\widehat h$.
The structural machine holds the switch components and their incidences,
not an additional original byte machine or a stored $K$. The original
record $W(h)$ is the comparison object in the correspondence proof.

**Theorem 6.2 (same-source complete primitive correspondence).** Under
Assumption 3.1, canonical structural installation is finite and uniform.
For every $h\in\mathcal R_H$ its actual endpoint is

$$
\widehat X(\widehat h)=\Gamma(W(h)).
\tag{SSA.8}
$$

For each lawful primitive action $a$ with executed successor $ha$,

$$
\operatorname{step}_{G}(\Gamma(W(h)),a)
 =\Gamma(W(ha)).
\tag{SSA.9}
$$

Decoding that graph and applying Proposition 2.1 returns the complete
original successor $X(ha)$, including original identities and all semantic
annotations. Every lawful original primitive cut has exactly its
corresponding structural cut. There is no extra maintenance phase under
this atomic primitive convention.

Proof. Initially the finite structural port encodes exactly the original
irreducible meter/register state and empty unconstructed ordinary ports.
The boot constructor case appends exactly one boundary cell per original
constructor and activates its head when appropriate. The single-bit
installer case changes exactly its prescribed bit. Both append the
original receipt. This proves the bootstrap correspondence without a
post-acquisition conversion or an uncharged source renderer.

For the induction step suppose the current carrier is $\Gamma(W)$.
The fixed finite-register and cell decoders return the actual operand
bits, not substituted representatives. RD therefore gives the same latch
and original testing successor. WR assigns the same symbol/bit and retains
all other tracks. The neighbor routes give precisely the adjacent original
cells, so ML/MR have the same guard, mark changes and target. EX changes
the old R to blank and adds the single new R while keeping the core head
where PR57.D1 requires it. BF uses the identical finite truth table. IN
sets the identical actual supplied bit without an early tape write. OUT
releases the same permitted bit. TOK and CTL alter just their original
finite state or addressed seal mark. The complete per-opcode effects are
therefore the original ones. No row performs a full TagRead, ReadE, graft,
counter arithmetic, seek, archive scan, or copy in place of its paid
multirow computation.

The receipt template assigns exactly the original old append cell and new
R, with both meter heads represented. A possible overlap is the same
finite union of original operands, so the enumerated joint effect agrees
on that cell too. Assigning the corresponding structural switches and
links consequently gives exactly $\Gamma$ of the next physical record.
This proves SSA.9 at each boot, acquisition, and later step, hence SSA.8
by induction. Original cell occurrences have the same creation events;
auxiliary names have their fixed template roles. All descriptor/source
versions and preparation identity remain those of this one execution.

Proposition 2.1 decodes the actual endpoint and AIF replay recovers its
whole original prefix and complete $X$. Thus the complete successor
correspondence includes semantic interpretations even when they are not
separate physical stores. The additional slot rewiring and temporary
bounded truth-table work are parts of one atomic occurrence, with no
intermediate cuts by Assumption 3.1. The many intermediate cuts of the
original programs remain, since every one of their native rows remains
an individual patch. This proves all-cut coverage at exactly the declared
granularity. ∎

**Corollary 6.3 (lawful closure and original services).** The structural
image of $\mathcal R_H$ is closed under every prescribed non-IN successor
and exactly the admissible IN successors. Every illegal-unexecuted original
action has the same nonexecuting result, and every enabled noncanonical
raw IN has its actual executing structural successor outside that image.
Source-preserving queries and whole guarded modifications, including their
complete semantic successors, retain their original meanings.

Proof. AIF Lemma 2.7 specifies precisely the original lawful successors.
Apply SSA.9 to each. The compiled row's guards and token checks are the
original ones, so a disabled action has no executing patch. Both enabled
physical IN bits still have their finite patch, but a bit leaving
$\mathsf P$ has no same-image successor by AIF's necessity direction.
It is not reassigned `none`. Composing SSA.9 along an original service
fragment gives its actual source, response and successor. At busy source
WR cuts this composition uses raw bytes; it never invokes a nonexistent
tree decoder. At release the original whole-service theorem supplies the
same accepted candidate or unchanged rejected tree, and the same literal
or numerical response. The same composition transports every specified
paid audit fragment with its actual owned descriptor and data. No new
unframed choice or Fetch-index acquisition is supplied by the composition. ∎

**Proposition 6.4 (invalid packets and availability stay distinct).** The
representation includes genuine invalid source packets and does not infer
source or receiver availability from a byte string's being complete or
grammatically valid.

Proof. At $H=1$, the accepted Rho on alpha changes its source token from
01 to 10 by the original individual bit WRs. After the first changed bit
the actual two-bit token is 00 or 11, neither a valid one-leaf packet.
Its cell components are nevertheless defined by Definition 4.1. With beta
already installed at $H=1$, Rho rejects; its in-place MUX writes retain
valid beta bytes throughout, while its busy availability token still
forbids a second semantic call. Thus validity does not imply availability.
After the final payload WR of a nonempty receiver copy, payload bytes may
already equal the sender, while the destination remains unavailable until
its final seal/handoff. The representation stores these distinct finite
tokens and seal marks, so it distinguishes the cuts even if the payload
bytes agree. ∎

## 7. Four mathematical expressions and their separate kernels

**Definition 7.1 (ordered time and retained memory).** Let $T(h)$ be the
full chronological word of actual structural patch occurrences from the
primitive entrance, with original row/operand/outcome occurrences and their
finite structural effects. It includes preparation and private effects
mathematically. Erasing the structural refinement leaves the original
primitive prefix $h$. It is not the publicly released response word or an
additional installed event archive.

Let $M_{\mathrm{ret}}(h)$ be the actual retained material consisting of
the immutable acquisition rows and their bound origin chain; chronological
consumed frame blocks, descriptors and unfinished-frame written prefix;
the phase-typed current input-latch material; and the actual meter receipt
chain. Retained backing and actual binding relations are part of this
object, rather than only printed reference strings. A stale input latch
contributes no acquired bit outside its occupied phase. These materials
can lie under different owners. This definition asserts retention, not
simultaneous receiver possession or callable access.

Both objects have independent meanings: $T$ is order on actual occurrences;
$M_{\mathrm{ret}}$ is a selected family of retained original material.
The tuple $K$ is an extraction from the latter, not a fourth physical
store and not a callable object.

**Theorem 7.2 (four individual exact original task kernels).** On the whole
$\mathcal R_H$, separately for each
$V\in\{S,B,T,M_{\mathrm{ret}}\}$,

$$
V(h)=V(k)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h\sim_Q k.
\tag{SSA.10}
$$

Proof. Equal $S$ gives equal physical records by the direct cellwise inverse
of Theorem 4.4. Proposition 2.1 gives equal keys and replay gives identical
histories, hence $Q$ equivalence. Conversely $Q$ equivalence gives $h=k$
by Theorem 2.2, hence equal $S$. This proves both inclusions for $S$.

Equal $B$ gives equal $S$ by SSA.6, then the same implication to $Q$.
Conversely $Q$ equivalence gives identical histories and therefore equal
actual terminal antichains. This proves both inclusions for $B$
independently of taking any intersection with the other kernels.

Equal $T$ gives the same original full prefix by erasure, so it gives equal
task maps. Conversely $Q$ equivalence gives identical original prefixes,
whose canonical compiled patch sequences and actual names coincide. Thus
their $T$ values agree. This proves both inclusions for time.

Equal $M_{\mathrm{ret}}$ gives the same surviving acquisition code, the
same chronological $L$ and occupied pending bit, and the same number of
receipts, hence the same $K$ by Proposition 2.1. AIF replay gives identical
histories and $Q$ equivalence. Conversely $Q$ equivalence gives the same
history, hence exactly the same retained material and binding relations.
This proves both inclusions for retained memory. The last proof makes no
claim that extraction is a permitted read by any selected receiver. ∎

**Theorem 7.3 (constructive actual-image atlas).** On the four actual view
images there are finite mathematical recovery maps in all twelve directed
cases. They are pairwise inverse, satisfy the triangle identities, and
transport every lawful primitive with its complete original successor.

Proof. The following directed cycle supplies explicit algorithms:

$$
S\ \xrightarrow{\text{maximal paths}}\ B
\ \xrightarrow{\text{prefix closure, cell decoding}}\ M_{\mathrm{ret}}
\ \xrightarrow{\mathcal E,\ \text{AIF replay, row compiler}}\ T
\ \xrightarrow{\text{finite patch execution}}\ S.
\tag{SSA.11}
$$

The second arrow first recovers $W$, then selects exactly the retained
materials in Definition 7.1 using their actual decoded phase/descriptors.
The third arrow computes $K$ by the finite extraction and uses the existing
actual-image replay, then the explicit compiler. The fourth starts at the
fixed structural entrance and executes the given finite event word. Each
algorithm is finite on its actual image: its traversals concern finite
trees, tapes or words; replay stops at its supplied finite ordinal. No
future input or alternate source witness is selected.

Compose zero to three consecutive arrows to obtain each directed recovery.
For example $B\to S$ can directly use prefix closure; $S\to T$ can use
cell decoding, extraction and replay; $T\to M_{\mathrm{ret}}$ executes
the patches then selects retained material; $M_{\mathrm{ret}}\to B$
replays and takes the resulting terminal antichain. Each such composite
sends the view of $h$ to the corresponding other view of exactly that same
$h$. Theorem 7.2 guarantees that a different representative with the same
starting view is not chosen. Consequently reverse composites return the
starting view, and any two paths between the same images agree. This
proves the inverse and triangle identities, not merely their existence.

At an admissible IN, extraction changes $(u,I,n)$ to $(u,Ic,n+1)$;
at a non-IN it changes it to $(u,I,n+1)$. AIF Corollary 4.5 and SSA.9
show that decoding, taking the original successor, and re-encoding commute
with these maps. The same is true of any specified finite semantic/audit
fragment by composition. All successor payloads and failure types are
the original ones. The resulting maps are mathematical transformations
of complete descriptions. They are not assertions of equal fees, equal
elapsed physical duration, or an installed view-acquisition service. ∎

## 8. Exact resource recurrences on one execution

**Definition 8.1 (evaluable stencil bill).** Fix the literal compiled rule
table. The following prescription determines its charge without a
source-dependent unspecified service constant. A component inspection of
$v$ vertices probes its two child slots at every vertex and its parent slot
on each nonroot return; its probe count is $3v-1$. Thus a cell inspection
costs 140 probes and the full finite-register inspection costs $18b-4$.

For an executing row let $\mathcal C$ be its ordered list of old cell
roles: its core current cell, its moved-to neighbor when present, and its
old meter append cell. A CTL seal uses its designated current cell in this
list; rows without a tape core have just the meter cell. Repeated roles
are retained as repeated probes. Their routes from the distinguished root
are those of Definition 5.1. Let $\mathcal A$ list its cell-append sites
(core first, meter second). Each site has the root-to-trailing-terminal
route, with two child-slot probes to check its terminal. During the first
boot append to an empty tape that route is the fixed tape-origin path.
Let $\mathcal J$ list changed/activated head links in fixed port/lane
order. For each such link probe the origin slot, old target slot if present,
and new target slot, each by its root route followed by that slot probe.
Repeated walks and probes are charged again. Appended new endpoints are
known template occurrences and their checks are initialization assignments,
not old-graph probes.

Let $d_r$ be the fixed root-to-register-component path length. Execute, in
order, the register route/inspection, each route and inspection in
$\mathcal C$, each append-site route and its two terminal probes, and
each old-graph endpoint probe in $\mathcal J$. Every route costs its word
length. This is a complete finite prescription for the number $p_j$ of
port probes. In particular, if $\mathcal J_{\mathrm{old}}$ is the list
of the latter root-to-slot words, then

$$
p_j=d_r+18b-4
 +\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}(|\pi_c|+140)
 +\sum_{z\in\mathcal A}(|\pi_z|+2)
 +\sum_{w\in\mathcal J_{\mathrm{old}}}|w|.
\tag{SSA.12}
$$

Let $\mathcal F_j$ be the finite-register bit positions assigned by the
original row, including its PC successor, and let $\mathcal D_j$ be the
old cell/track positions assigned by its core/receipt rule. These are
the original designated write fields, including value-preserving writes,
not the positions whose values happen to change. Combine duplicate target
positions as in Definition 5.1. Put $t_j=|\mathcal F_j|+|\mathcal D_j|$,
$e_j=|\mathcal A|$, and let $r_j$ count retargeted links and $a_j$ initial
activations. Then set

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_j&=48e_j,\\
w_j&=4t_j+2e_j+3r_j+2a_j+7v_j,\\
g_j&=1+p_j+w_j+v_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{SSA.13}
$$

Each original target bit incurs its four slot assignments even when
unchanged; a read-only operand incurs probes and no gratuitous writes.
Each append assigns the two
slots of its old terminal and initializes all seven slots of each new
vertex. The head terms are the exact three/two assignments of Definition
5.1. Empty-slot initialization and repeated assignments remain charged.
The old/new pattern and endpoint aliases determine the displayed integers
by finite walks, not an unbounded matching operation. Every finite
combinational selection or pattern-table entry belongs to the fixed
primitive apparatus; no separate unbounded arithmetic service is assumed.

**Definition 8.2 (initial foundation bill and cumulative resources).** At
native ordinal zero there are the two original meter cells, all other
tape words empty, the fixed registers, and two meter head links. Its tree
has

$$
V_0=223+6b
$$

vertices and $L(0)=112+3b$ terminals. Count its construction/endowment as $V_0$ vertex units,
$7V_0$ endpoint-slot initialization units, and one foundation commitment,
so $g_0=1+8V_0$. The fixed literal rule apparatus contributes
$d_{\mathrm{patch}}$ description bits and its declared finite mechanism.
This fixed foundation replaces an irreducible port convention; it is not
a source-dependent execution before ordinal zero. A model constructing
this foundation from smaller observable steps needs its own initial
construction domain and cuts.

For one run of $n$ original primitives, including boot, define

$$
\begin{aligned}
G(n)&=g_0+\sum_{j=1}^n g_j,&
K_{\mathrm{cell}}(n)&=2+\sum_{j=1}^n e_j,\\
V(n)&=V_0+48\sum_{j=1}^n e_j,&
L(n)&=L(0)+24\sum_{j=1}^n e_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{SSA.14}
$$

The original reference coordinates are $n$ logical primitive events and
$2n$ PR57 fee units. If an accounting convention charges both the original
service fees and structural work, its total is $2n+G(n)$ plus fixed rule
apparatus; if it substitutes the structural primitive price, its charge is
$G(n)$. These are explicitly different price conventions. Neither is an
automatic numeral stored by the meter. The actual meter is still its
original one-receipt-per-event chain, structurally represented and included
in $K_{\mathrm{cell}}$.

**Theorem 8.3 (resource and public-schedule transport).** SSA.12–14 give an
evaluable finite bill on every original run. Under the atomic convention,
the primitive ordinal and original source/input/copy occurrences correspond
one for one, while the additional structural prices and storage remain
as stated. For equal original public boot, acquired public history and
submitted request, the added publicly charged schedule before response
commitment is source uniform. This includes private assignments that are
no-ops.

Proof. The inspection count $3v-1$ follows by two child probes per vertex
and one return per tree edge. All switch patterns have the same number
of vertices, so this count is independent of the bits. Every probe in
the compiler is one of the listed component or route probes. SSA.13
counts exactly the original-field assignment lists, 48 new vertices per cell
append, and one commitment. Construction from the primitive foundation
and summation prove SSA.14. Every original primitive contributes its actual
receipt append, so no source input, internal gate, source WR, row operation,
copy, seal, or readout has disappeared from this sum.

PR57's source programs and LJA's literal circuit have value-independent
precommit instructions, addresses, allocations, and cursor occurrences
for equal public request data. The new role routes have fixed outer and
head lengths; moved-to routes have the fixed displayed length. Endpoint
aliases, terminal tests, and head movement are determined by those public
cursor occurrences, not private 0/1 values. The finite-register and cell
components have constant size. Thus every summand of SSA.12 and every
integer in SSA.13 is the same for the two compared precommit schedules.
Assignments are not omitted according to their private value. Internal
port probes/assignments are private suboperations of the atomic patch;
their private switch topology is not an extra emitted label. The public
patch label retains the original row/phase, logical cursor occurrence,
release, tick, and its publicly determined charge. At a permitted response
release, subsequent public branches can depend on that now acquired
response, just as in the original machine. Induction through releases
therefore gives a prefix-causal source-uniform public correspondence.

The graph event's ordinal equality is a consequence of its declared atomic
granularity, not a physical speed comparison. A physical device exposing
internal topology probes as public data, or charging differently for
private no-op writes, would violate the stated observation/price
hypotheses. No such device is covered by the theorem. ∎

**Proposition 8.4 (the original material bill is retained).** Structural
replacement does not remove the source/input/copy and archive costs of
AIF or make pointer width into memory capacity.

Proof. Source preparation still contains $2N$ actual private INs, their
WR/MR occurrences, allocated zeroed source slots, and the full validation
computation. Initial acquisition still executes $2m-1$ TagReads and
$2(m-1)$ producer branch-local markers for an $m$-leaf source, together
with their full tape implementations, rows, framing, seals and delivery.
The acquired prefix code has $3m-1$ payload bits before its metadata;
the padded private block remains $2N$ bits. Canonical input parsing retains
AIF's header, unary-length and payload work, including every unwritten
pending input interval. None is inferred from a source epoch or event count.

All Base/Allocate/Seek routines, unary counter versions, dynamic gate
records, stack frames, scratch erasures, and retired cells remain the
same original sequences and are included in SSA.14. AIF's explicit
two-stage length-controlled copy costs $12\ell+6$ primitives plus actual
allocation, initialization, descriptors and positioning. The older
$8\ell+4$ expression counts only its payload/seal/handoff bodies and
does not replace that total. Sender, mailbox and receiver coexist.
They each contribute their actual cells to SSA.7. Copy payload bits and
source-query counts are unchanged coordinates of the same run.

The support counts are numbers of graph vertices, tree/access incidences,
and finite slot capacities. They do not assign a binary width to an
arbitrary physical endpoint. If references are serialized as strings,
their actual widths, framing, construction, scans and copies must also
be counted. Fixed-radius access across a head link assumes that declared
link primitive; it supplies no constant-length physical wire. Thus neither
SSA.7 nor a short descriptor proves storage optimality, physical embedding,
or a numerical saving over the original machine. ∎

## 9. Owners, observation, and the remaining callable interface

**Definition 9.1 (owner correspondence).** Replace the storage of each
original owner by its own switch components on the same assigned ports.
The producer retains the private raw source and scratch authority; ingress
retains its input-latch/frame authority; journal and channel owners retain
their archive, mailbox, seal and token authority; the meter keeps its
original append/readout permissions. A cell descriptor is still its paid
original encoding, followed by paid positioning; no edge to every named
block is installed.

The trusted primitive core maintains the bounded footprint of its admitted
row under these permissions. An owner-local RD is the corresponding paid
structural inspection and latch update, returning exactly its original
finite result. A WR requires exactly its original authority. Hidden
structural probes needed to preserve a writable cell's remaining fields
are internal to that primitive, not a capability passed to the actor.
The full support and frontier remain mathematical relations across all
owners' actual storage; there is no default traversal permission over them.

**Theorem 9.2 (local authorized use and conditional conversion).** Every
already authorized original primitive operation and finite service has
the structural operation of Theorem 6.2 under the same owner/token guards.
If a receiver additionally possesses a complete authentic finite
description of one of the four views for a correctly bound named cut,
the mathematical conversions of Theorem 7.3 determine the other three.
This conditional determination does not establish an oldcut acquisition
or callable-memory service.

Proof. The first assertion follows from the per-row permission checks
and direct storage reads/writes in Definition 5.2. For example, the literal
source service still uses its authorized private gates and raw source
RD/WR operations; it does not replace TagRead by an actor's exploration
of the whole support. Each private source byte is the switch component
on which those original operations actually act. The semantic result and
all partial availability states therefore agree by SSA.9.

For the conditional second assertion, a complete authentic description is
in the actual image of the relevant view. Apply the finite maps of
Theorem 7.3; their inverse proof gives the unique same-cut descriptions.
If the receiver executes a conversion, its program, scratch, scans,
serialization, output framing, seals and copies are additional paid work.
No exact numerical cost for that unprovided acquisition/conversion program
is asserted by the mathematical map. Existing archive handles can perform
their original paid lookup because their backing and permissions persist;
they do not thereby acquire the pending current input latch or another
owner's entire support. ∎

**Definition 9.3 (open named-cut obligation).** A callable version at an
arbitrary lawful busy cut would require an actual authorized protocol to
bind that cut, retain its occupied input bit, acquire a complete appropriate
input/acquisition prefix, bind the real receipt position and preparation,
and seal/transmit a package or preserve a correctly bound callable backing.
Every added protocol phase and its physical successors and resources must
then be represented. A mathematical $K$, an original receipt chain
inspected across owners, or a numeral purporting to name a cut is not
that protocol. No arbitrary receipt-index possession, busy interrupt,
private-latch read, simultaneous snapshot, or spontaneous oldcut access
is a premise here. No separate named-cut construction is assumed.

**Proposition 9.4 (representation does not complete a partial copy).** The
observation consisting of a receiver's currently materialized copy prefix
and the common copy layout/schedule need not determine $S$ or $B$, even
though each is a mathematically exact $Q$ view. This assertion concerns
that specified observation, not all earlier data an owner might possess.

Proof. Take a receiver during a copy with two possible sealed senders
having a common already-read prefix and different not-yet-read suffixes.
Before the next payload RD, its acquired payload prefix is the same;
the sender remains immutable under another owner and the destination is
not yet readable as a complete block. AIF Theorem 6.4 gives exactly such
actual partial-copy cuts and the conditional suffix ambiguity. The
structural carrier stores the distinct sender bytes, so its supports and
terminal antichains differ by Theorem 4.4. The specified materialized
prefix observation has not acquired those distinctions. Earlier retained
input or another complete copy could supply them; no equality of such
additional records is asserted. An authorized later read can acquire
them, and a genuine handle includes that backing; a common pointer string
alone cannot do so. This is a distinction between the given partial
acquisition and the complete representation, not a failure of either
structural inverse. ∎

## 10. Atomicity and observable-phase obstructions

**Proposition 10.1 (a split switch is outside the claimed image).** The
bit assignment of Definition 4.1 does not preserve its structural image
when its two root-edge updates are separately observable.

Proof. Initially bit 0 has ordered root targets $(f,t)$. To obtain bit 1,
changing only the left target first yields $(t,t)$ and detaches $f$ from
the root. Changing only the right target first yields $(f,f)$ and detaches
$t$. Either has repeated target occurrences and is not the stipulated
full ordered occurrence tree. Parent endpoint slots also fail their
inverse agreement until coherently updated. Hence the switch's final
correctness proves no all-cut theorem for either split order. A different
carrier could explicitly store maintenance phases and preserve their
own inverses; that would be another domain and proof. ∎

**Proposition 10.2 (paid inserted phases require their own boundary values).**
Suppose a serial realization inserts $m\ge1$ paid non-IN maintenance
steps before the next original primitive at a continuing cut. Its $m+1$
successive running cuts all project to the same original cut. With its
own complete-physical-successor task and one receipt per step, these
$m+1$ cuts are pairwise task-distinct. A view factoring only through that
common original cut cannot be exact for this extended task.

Proof. Two maintenance cuts with different elapsed ordinals have different
successful next-Advance successors because their successor receipt counts
differ. At the final phase the next original non-IN, if present, has yet
another successor receipt count; if it is IN, Advance is disabled there
and separates it from the earlier phases. Thus all listed cuts differ
in their one-step maps. A pullback of the common original view is constant
on them, proving the claim. Under PR57 pricing the added steps cost $m$
ticks, $2m$ fee units and $m$ original eight-bit receipt cells, besides
their core work/storage. These are conditional phase distinctions, not a
lower bound charged again if a realization already stores its meter/phase.
They do not arise in Theorem 6.2, which declares a one-patch-per-original-
primitive alternative basis. Nor do they preclude a separately proved
retimed simulation of the original semantic task. ∎

**Proposition 10.3 (private no-op omission can disclose a source bit).**
Value-dependent omission of structural assignments cannot replace the
fixed charging rule in Theorem 8.3 without a separate noninterference
proof.

Proof. In LJA Definition 3.2 at $H=1$, the first enumerated two-bit packet
is $z=00$. Its first comparison has target bit $e_{z,0}=1$ and computes
$\neg x_0$. On the installed alpha packet 01 this is 1; on beta 10 it is
0. Its fresh destination was zero-initialized. If assignment is omitted
exactly when unchanged, the beta case omits that private gate write and
the alpha case does not. Their public work/charge traces then differ
before literal reply commitment despite common public boot and request.
The fixed assignment list avoids this disclosure. The conclusion concerns
that proposed omission rule, not every possible optimized representation. ∎

## 13. Restricted set-to-value refinement by three-slot relocations

**Definition 13.1 (positioned component, operation and task).** Fix an
already constructed bit component of Definition 4.1, its actual root $r$
and retained occurrences $r,f,t,v_0,v_1$. Write $0=\mathsf L$ and
$1=\mathsf R$. The actual stored relations are reciprocal child and
$\mathsf P$ incidences. The external attachment of $r$ is fixed; the other
four port types on these bit occurrences are empty. Throughout,

$$
f.0=v_0,\qquad f.1=v_1.
\tag{SSA.15}
$$

At a canonical entrance of value $x$, $r.x=f$ and $r.(1-x)=t$; all
other child slots are empty. Intermediate vertices may be unary. The
carrier is this actual occurrence tree, with no second bit payload,
version selector, saved byte or detached retained component.

A strict relocation has an occupied source slot $p.a=v$, a distinct
vacant destination slot $q.c$, and $q$ outside the current subtree of
$v$. Its single atomic data effect is

$$
p.a:=\varnothing,\qquad q.c:=v,\qquad v.\mathsf P:=(q,c).
\tag{SSA.16}
$$

These three distinct endpoint slots change coherently, without internal
observable cuts. No control transition, operand acquisition, access-link
retargeting, allocation or receipt is implicit. For the paths below, guards
are established from bounded five-occurrence patterns; no unbounded
ancestry test is supplied.

Fix the supplied row-scoped authorization interface as an external
boundary parameter. The local structural task takes a specified relocation
command on reached roles, retains SSA.15, tests its geometric guards and
that interface's permission, and on success has its actual complete local
incidence successor as mathematical observation. A disabled command has no
executing successor. Continuations are finite command sequences. This
observation grants no client read or graph serialization.
The assignment below is a three-command path in that relation. It is not
an autonomous assignment controller or the original task $Q$.

The root must actually be reached under the original designated field's
write authority. Maintenance inspection stays within that row's permitted
footprint. The target $b$ is an original rule immediate or an actual
authorized acquired operand, available throughout the path. A latched
operand is distinct read-only material. Merely supplying a mathematical
parameter, private value or address does not acquire it. For a prescribed stage, a fixed finite
private selector may inspect this component and the available operand to
choose one relocation; supplying and retaining the stage is external to
this data contract. It may not bundle a whole native-row patch or an
unbounded transaction. Its apparatus, reads, positioning and persistent
control remain explicit additional resources. A permit surviving the
inserted phases is a premise of an application, not a protocol constructed
by this definition.

**Theorem 13.2 (uniform assignment and its scoped lower bound).** For each
$x,b\in\{0,1\}$, under Definition 13.1 there is a path of exactly three
nontrivial strict relocations from $g_x$ to $g_b$ on the same occurrences,
fixing SSA.15 and every exterior incidence. Its worst-case relocation
count is least in this fixed-occurrence relocation basis; a common length
for all four assignments cannot be less than three.

Proof. Put $a=x$, $c=1-x$. First move $f$ from $r.a$ to $t.0$.
Then execute:

$$
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &\text{second relocation}&\text{third relocation}\\ \hline
 b=x
 &f:\ t.0\longrightarrow t.1
 &f:\ t.1\longrightarrow r.a\\
 b\ne x
 &t:\ r.c\longrightarrow r.a
 &f:\ t.0\longrightarrow r.c .
\end{array}
\tag{SSA.17}
$$

Initially $t$ is a terminal sibling of $f$, so $t.0$ is vacant and
outside $f$'s subtree. After the first move the whole nonroot component
is attached through $r.c=t$, with $t.0=f$ and $r.a$ vacant.
If $b=x$, $t.1$ is vacant and its parent $t$ is outside $f$'s subtree.
Moving $f$ there and then to still-vacant $r.a$ is legal and restores
exactly the original named incidences. Thus unchanged assignments perform
three actual relocations.

If $b\ne x$, move the whole $t$ subtree into vacant $r.a$; $r$ is
outside that subtree. This vacates $r.c$. Moving $f$ there from $t.0$
is legal and leaves $r.a=t$, $r.c=f$, exactly $g_b$. The fork children
and all occurrence identities are retained.

Each command reattaches one subtree outside itself, preserving acyclicity,
unique parents, root reachability and reciprocal incidence. Every exposed
cut is therefore an actual spanning tree. No source-packet validity,
acceptance, seal or completed-request hypothesis is used.

The fixed-occurrence three-slot argument is as follows. Changing a
vertex's parent-slot partner changes its own $\mathsf P$, its old
parent child slot and its new parent child slot. No nonzero change of
such reciprocal tree incidence has fewer than three changed slots.
Exactly three forces the new child slot previously to be vacant:
displacing another child also changes that child's $\mathsf P$.
This is the restricted relocation fact, not a universal atomicity bound.

For a changed assignment, both $f$ and $t$ change parent-slot partners.
A relocation changes that partner only for its moved root, even when
carrying descendants. With at most two relocations each would have to
move once directly to its final root slot, but either first destination
is occupied by the other. Thus a changed assignment needs at least three.
SSA.17 attains the bound and has the same length for unchanged writes.
An unchanged write alone admits a zero-move identity; the bound concerns
worst-case and common length. Allocation, renaming, forest carriers and
other primitive effects are outside this lower bound. ∎

**Proposition 13.3 (actual support and terminal-boundary inverses).**
For each component cut of Theorem 13.2 let $S_{\rm loc}$ be its actual
root-relative child-path support, including the empty path, and
$B_{\rm loc}$ its paths ending at vertices with neither child occupied.
Each view separately reconstructs its current incidence with the fixed

claim holds when this component is grafted at its original socket in a

$$
S_{\rm loc}=\operatorname{Pref}(B_{\rm loc}),\qquad
B_{\rm loc}=\operatorname{Max}_{\preceq}(S_{\rm loc}).
\tag{SSA.18}
$$

From support, place a vertex at each path, join $w.a$ to $w$ through
child port $a$ and the child's $\mathsf P$, and leave absent child
extensions vacant. The empty path is $r$. In this family $f$ is the
unique nonroot vertex with two children, whose order identifies $v_0,v_1$;
the remaining nonroot occurrence is $t$. Thus the fixed roles are recovered.
Arbitrary external creation names are not inferred from paths.

For this decoder $D_S$, $D_S(S_{\rm loc}(C))=C$ under the fixed role
and attachment convention, and $S_{\rm loc}(D_S(A))=A$ for every support
