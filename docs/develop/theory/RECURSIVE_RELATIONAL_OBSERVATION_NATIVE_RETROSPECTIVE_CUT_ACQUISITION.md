## 1. The observer's task and its actual entrance

**Definition 1.1 (one source and its original machine).** Let

$$
\mathcal T::=\alpha\mid\beta\mid\langle s,t\rangle,\qquad
\rho\alpha=\beta,\quad \rho\beta=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,\quad
\rho\langle s,t\rangle=\langle\rho s,\rho t\rangle.
$$

Fix $H\ge1$, $N=2H-1$, one preparation identity $\xi$, and one actual
$u\in\mathcal T_H=\{t:\lambda(t)\le H\}$. The original machine $M_0$
is the canonical serial machine of AIF Definitions 2.1–2.6. Its entrance
is the paid canonical $2N$-bit installation, followed by the supplied
no-update acquisition of that same source and the seed's actual delivery.
Write $a=a(u)$ for the acquisition fence's absolute primitive ordinal.
The fixed native graph, literal boot encoding, descriptor conventions,
port assignments and preparation data are denoted collectively by $v_0$.
They are part of a replay specification, not runtime advice about $u$.

Let $\mathcal R_H$ be the full lawful post-acquisition prefix class of
that machine. Every finite address and every finite complete context tree
is allowed. Whole-candidate capacity rejection does not remove an input
from this class. At a source-frame IN, the lawful supplied bits are
exactly those preserving extendibility to a canonical well-typed frame.
Both raw physical bits still execute when physically supplied. A bit
destroying that extendibility leaves $\mathcal R_H$; it is not a disabled
IN and does not have the illegal-unexecuted result.

**Definition 1.2 (task, before representations).** Fix an actual prefix
$h\in\mathcal R_H$, with absolute ordinal $n=|h|$. The paid historical
consumer task is to acquire an authorized finite object naming this very
prefix, and later execute an admitted finite computation returning

$$
\operatorname{HistoricalImage}(v_0,\xi,n,h,X(h)).
\tag{RCA.1}
$$

Here $X(h)$ is the complete original joint endpoint: raw source and
scratch bits, installed and dynamic code, every constructed and retired
cell, PC, heads, latches, counters and their versions, request work,
pending events, partial producer records, sender/mailbox/receiver bytes,
origins, descriptors, seals, permissions and meter. There need not be a
decoded current tree during source overwrite. A represented old permission
is output data. It grants no present source, archive, latch or destination
authority. The actual call has a different, later complete successor,
including all its work, receipts and permission changes.

**Definition 1.3 (owners and admissible operations).** The producer owns
the private source. The seed recipient owns its acquired seed. An ingress
or archive principal owns the retained complete request material and its
actual read/copy tokens. A service principal $O$ may use both only when
it actually possesses them, either initially or through paid authorized
copies. A downstream client $C$ needs its own actual delivery and invocation
rights. Equal bit strings held by different principals do not identify
their authorities.

The original source ports are literal TagRead($p$), numerical ReadE, and
guarded Rho, Left($t$), Right($t$). During unfinished work only the
prescribed next native primitive is enabled. At a prescribed IN the
external actor supplies a bit; at other phases Advance executes the
prescribed row. Existing complete-prefix Fetch and closed-fence scans are
usable only at their admitted entry states with their actual descriptors,
constructed arguments and tokens. There is no original RecoverAt opcode,
arbitrary busy interrupt, private-latch read or instantaneous snapshot.

**Convention 1.4 (failures and target designation).** Literal `absent`,
executed source `reject`, executed archive `no-row`, and illegal-unexecuted
busy/missing-token/unsealed-destination operations are distinct. The
consumer additionally has distinct executed `CutIndexReject`,
`NoPackage`, `NoCover`, `End` and malformed-service-frame outcomes. These do not become
source replies. Input not supplied at an IN is not an emitted refusal.

Four designation relations must remain separate:

1. A mathematical choice of $h$ or $n$ fixes a specification, not acquired
   input, a serialized numeral or permission.
2. An acquired cut reference has actual finite backing, a version/source
   binding and an invocation authority. A theorem assuming one does not
   acquire it.
3. A prospective request to mark the current busy boundary needs an
   enabled target-dependent acquisition occurrence there.
4. Retrospective retrieval can acquire references after a finite covering
   flight. Selecting one of them still requires an actual acquired choice.

The requested target in (RCA.1) is never replaced by the later covering,
copying, publication or invocation boundary.

## 3. Two operational obstructions and one selector obstruction

**Proposition 3.1 (the missing busy-admission edge).** The original menu
does not admit immediate recovery or fence registration at every lawful
primitive cut.

Proof. Use a nonempty AIF payload reader immediately after a payload IN.
Its actual input bit is in the designated latch, its payload cell has not
yet been written, and its PC is the reader's write row. The unique enabled
non-IN successor is the prescribed WR. RecoverAt, Fetch and a new CTL
fence are different rows. LJA Definitions 4.1 and 4.4 give them no
transition there. Inserting one changes the enabled-transition relation;
describing its absence as an executed reject also changes that relation.
The same prescribed-successor argument applies during source overwrite
and receiver copying. The assertion is an enabling obstruction, not an
information-theoretic impossibility of later reconstruction. ∎

**Proposition 3.2 (finite completion is not unconditional progress).**
There is no uniform finite primitive deadline to idle admission, even for
one fixed $H$. There is also no guarantee of reaching idle without the
remaining required external supply.

Proof. AIF's payload loop has the five rows test, IN, WR, payload move,
length move, with one final zero test. Immediately after input number
$r$, $1\le r\le d$, exactly

$$
3+5(d-r)+1=5(d-r)+4
\tag{RCA.2}
$$

further primitives are needed just to leave that loop. Thereafter parsing,
source work, records, copies and acknowledgement remain. Arbitrarily long
finite TagRead addresses are lawful on the same finite-capacity source;
descent beyond a leaf gives literal `absent`. Taking $r=1$ and unbounded
$d$ defeats any proposed common finite deadline.

For failure of unconditional progress take $d=2,r=1$. The prescribed WR,
two moves and next length test reach the second payload IN. Without its
next supplied bit there is no Advance-only path to idle. Alternatively,
an actor can perpetually extend an unfinished unary length with ones;
every finite prefix remains extendible, but no complete frame is reached.
Neither example uses a malformed lawful prefix, a hostile writer or a
crash. ∎

**Theorem 3.3 (independent target-selection information).** Fix one actual
later prefix and $m$ distinct eligible earlier ordinals in it. Conditional
on identical backing, a deterministic exact consumer selecting every one
of them needs at least $m$ distinguishable acquired selector states. A
fixed-width binary selection channel therefore needs at least
$\lceil\log_2m\rceil$ bits.

Proof. Prefixes with different ordinals have different lengths and old
receipt counts. If two target choices give the same complete acquired
selector, authority and backing, deterministic execution gives the same
answer for both, which cannot be both required prefixes. Hence the
selector relation is injective on the $m$ choices. There are only $2^b$
binary strings of width $b$. Timing, navigation operations, object identity
or permissions must be included in the selector state if they communicate
the choice. A finite enumeration and its index attain the cardinality
bound as an abstract selection alphabet, without asserting any physical
storage or acquisition optimum. ∎

In particular, a later directory containing both the after-IN and
after-WR prefixes does not infer which one a silent external observer
intended. This is a choice obstruction in one history, distinct from AIF's
unfinished-request counting fibers.

## 4. A closed flight determines a finite old-cut interval

**Definition 4.1 (authentic closed-flight backing).** A closed flight ends
at an original ready cut $s$: acquisition has completed, a finite number
$j\ge0$ of canonical source frames have completed all their prescribed
services and acknowledgements, and the next frame has not begun.

Its backing $B_s$ consists of the actual acquired seed, $v_0,H,\xi$,
the acquisition origin and a sealed chronological prefix of complete
frame material through those $j$ requests. It includes all material
behind context references. The length and end of the backing are physical
sealed bounds; $j$ is obtained by scanning that backing, not supplied as
a free counter. A lossless actual complete-row prefix with its complete
material may replace retained ingress. Arbitrary submitted bytes cannot
create this backing type.

Let $J_s$ be the chronological concatenation of those frames. Ready means
the fixed post-acknowledgement control boundary, not merely an empty
semantic pending-event field during unrelated unfinished work.

**Lemma 4.2 (all original finite cuts have a finite cover).** For every
$h\in\mathcal R_H$, a finite original lawful extension reaches a closed
flight $s\succeq h$. For every such completion,

$$
I(h)\preceq I(s)=J_s.
\tag{RCA.3}
$$

The completion is conditional on actually supplying a finite canonical
remainder when the current frame is unfinished.

Proof. At ready take $s=h$. Otherwise the consumed current frame prefix
has some finite canonical completion by the definition of $\mathcal R_H$.
Choose and actually supply that remainder at its prescribed INs. Do not
resupply a bit already held in the latch. LJA Theorem 4.5 and the tape and
copy progress lemmas finish the finite parsing, generation, source work,
counter work, row and acknowledged delivery. The same argument begins
directly with the remaining prescribed work if ingress was already
complete. Rejection retains the complete request and does not obstruct
termination. At the resulting ready cut there is neither a current input
frame nor an unsealed committed source event, so AIF's second residual
identity gives $I(s)=F(A_P(s))=J_s$. Its first identity has empty $Q$.
Input acquisition never retracts, giving the prefix relation. Authenticity
and lawful access to the completed backing use the actual retention or
LJA copy chain, not decoding of an arbitrary proposed transcript. ∎

**Definition 4.3 (boundary-stopped replay).** From $B_s$, reconstruct the
canonical boot and acquisition using its actual seed and fixed $v_0$.
Then feed the scanned complete frames in order to the literal original
transition simulator. For each frame continue through its mandatory
acknowledgement to the fixed ready PC. Stop after the last complete frame
at that PC, before the next ingress. Keep a unary tally of simulated
original primitives, and retain the tally at the acquisition fence.

This stopping rule uses sealed frame exhaustion and the represented PC.
It does not use the live physical meter, an end-of-input guess during a
frame, or a supplied mathematical primitive ordinal. Every simulated
primitive includes its original receipt and complete state effect.

**Theorem 4.4 (closed-flight stopping and target-reference acquisition at
the finite-string level).** Boundary-stopped replay terminates, determines
$a$ and $b=|s|$, and constructs their finite unary representations. Its
represented chronology is exactly $s$. Consequently the interval

$$
\{\operatorname{CutRef}(v_0,\xi,B_s,k):a\le k\le b\}
\tag{RCA.4}
$$

can be acquired by finite enumeration without an externally supplied
$n$ or $b$. Every issued reference denotes one actual original prefix of
this same flight. The theorem is about finite-string computation and its
owner-authorized execution requirement; it does not assert that the
original dispatcher already admits this computation.

Proof. Canonical boot and no-update acquisition terminate for the supplied
finite source. For each complete frame, the prescribed original service
terminates and reaches exactly its post-acknowledgement ready boundary.
Induct on the number of scanned complete frames. The actual frame word
fixes every external input in each such fragment; all other original
control, allocation and work are deterministic. AIF's literal actual-image
replay identifies this represented fragment with the actual one. Thus the
final stopping boundary is $s$, including all its original record/copy
work, rather than its earlier response commitment or row seal.

Appending a tally mark after each simulated original primitive gives
exactly $b$ marks; the retained acquisition tally gives $a$. Terminators
give $U(b)$ and $U(a)$. These are computed strings, not physical receipt
readouts. All loops are finite because there are finitely many complete
frames and each has finite service. Finally copy $U(a)$, issue its binding,
and repeatedly increment a fresh unary version while it is at most the
constructed $U(b)$. There are $b-a+1$ iterations. Each integer is a
unique prefix position in $s$, proving the authenticity of its denotation.
Which issued reference is later selected is an additional acquired choice,
as Theorem 3.3 requires. ∎

**Lemma 4.5 (strict prefix selection).** For every $a\le k\le b$, a
literal replay stopped at $k$, with $J_s$ available as an input reservoir,
consumes exactly $I(s[0:k])$ and returns the full original
$s[0:k],X(s[0:k])$. Its unused reservoir suffix has no effect.

Proof. Apply the prefix of AIF's actual-image replay through original
ordinal $k$. At any simulated IN before that stop, the next consumed bit
is the next actual bit in $I(s[0:k])$, which is a prefix of $J_s$ by
(RCA.3). The other rows are determined by the equal prior complete state.
Thus no later input bit is used. This is a prefix application of the
supplied replay theorem; it is not a new generic replay theorem. ∎

**Corollary 4.6 (the all-original-cut retrospective relation).** Under the
actual finite-completion condition of Lemma 4.2, the union of the intervals
(RCA.4) covers every original lawful finite primitive cut. Its prefix
decoder handles all of the following simultaneously on one history:
unfinished IN latches, partial source packets, uncommitted requests,
committed unsealed events, partial row construction and partial copies,
including original complete successors.

Proof. Choose $s\succeq h$ by Lemma 4.2 and select $k=|h|$ in its
constructed interval. Lemma 4.5 returns precisely $h,X(h)$. At an
IN-before-WR stop its last consumed bit is represented in $Q$, not an
already written cell. Before commitment a complete or partial request
remains in $D$ and busy work; after commitment the event may be in $P$.
At source overwrite the represented raw bits and unavailable phase need
not decode as a tree. At a copy stop the represented destination may be
unreadable even when all payload bits have been written. These are the
complete coordinates of AIF replay, with no physical read of the old
unavailable object. The next prescribed original transition has AIF's
complete actual successor, not just the same semantic source tree. ∎

## 5. Explicit paid selector routines and their entry contracts

**Definition 5.1 (native cost convention).** Each displayed RD, WR, move,
EX, BF, TOK or CTL is one PR57 primitive, one receipt, one tick and two
fee units. A successor edge itself costs no additional CTL. Separate CTL
occurrences are counted. Every routine below requires constructed cells,
proper ownership, distinct input/test latches and heads at its stated
entry positions. No formula is an empty-port setup allowance.

**Definition 5.2 (unary comparison).** Given sealed $U(x),U(y)$ with heads
at their first symbols, use the following finite control. Read the current
left symbol; read the current right symbol in a distinct latch. If both
are one, move both heads right and return to the first read. Otherwise
exit as equal for $(0,0)$, smaller for $(0,1)$ and larger for $(1,0)$.
The newly read bit tests are native successor choices. No value is read
through a mathematical index.

**Proposition 5.3 (comparison count).** This graph performs exactly
$4\min(x,y)+2$ primitives. It leaves both heads at the first unequal or
joint terminating position.

Proof. Exactly $\min(x,y)$ pairs of ones execute two reads and two moves.
The next two reads determine the exit and execute no moves. The stated
head positions and results follow from the unary encodings. This includes
$x=0$, $y=0$ and equality. ∎

**Definition 5.4 (unary copy and fresh increment).** The destination head
is at its existing right marker; its writer token is held. The source is
a sealed $U(x)$ followed by a constructed delimiter, with head at its
first bit. A copy reads each bit, applies `EX; WR(bit); MR(destination)`,
and moves the source head once, including after its zero. Its final-zero
branch exits at that move. A fresh increment instead copies each leading
one with the same five-row body, then reads the source zero and appends
one and zero without moving the source again. It retains the old version.

**Proposition 5.5 (copy and increment counts).** The copy has exactly
$5(x+1)$ primitives and constructs $x+1$ data cells. The increment has
exactly $5x+7$ primitives and constructs $x+2$ data cells. Appending a
delimiter adds three primitives and one cell. A separate seal adds one
CTL. All positioning, containing-record construction and handoffs are
additional.

Proof. Each leading source one uses RD, EX, WR and two moves. The copy's
zero uses the same body. The increment's terminal stage uses RD and two
three-row appends. EX is the sole ordinary-cell constructor in these
bodies. Delimiter append and seal have their displayed primitive effects. ∎

**Definition 5.6 (paid enumeration of a certified interval).** The native
enumerator's inputs are actual sealed $U(a),U(b)$ and sealed binding
material $\eta=(v,\xi,\text{backing reference})$, all legally readable.
They are not arguments supplied by a mathematical theorem. Obtain a fresh
copy of $U(a)$ as current $K$. At each iteration position $K,U(b)$ at
their first bits and run Definition 5.2. If greater, seal the end marker
and return. Otherwise construct a fresh framed record containing $\eta$
and a copy of $K$, seal it, and construct the incremented $K$ by
Definition 5.4. Repeat. Each output record is physically appended; issuing
a reference is not an unbounded native output instruction.

One fixed public port assignment uses consumer ports 40–47 for the two
bound copies, current-version stream, output arena, descriptor stream,
Base counter, binding material and auxiliary copies, respectively.
Temporaries of the unary library use its designated public arithmetic
ports 0–15. All these ports must have their actual reserved ownership
tokens at entry. The input backing itself is read through its existing
owner's read token. The routine makes no source-port assignment.

**Theorem 5.7 (actual selector acquisition, conditional on constructed
bounds).** Definition 5.6 is one finite native control graph, uniform in
$a,b,\eta$. It terminates and issues exactly the ordered references in
(RCA.4) when its actual inputs are those of Theorem 4.4. It creates no
permission from the reference bits. An absent invocation permission
remains absent.

Proof. Sequence and looping use PR57.D1's finite expansion. The outer
graph has one comparison branch, one finite record-copy graph, one fresh
increment graph and an exit. Its input lengths are tape data, never
native-state indices. The represented current value starts at $a$ and
increases by one. For $a\le b$ exactly $b-a+1$ output iterations precede
the greater result at $b+1$. For $a>b$ there is no output iteration.
Every copy, comparison, home, seek, framing and append has finite
constructed bounds. Old versions remain retained. Theorems 4.4 and 5.5
give correct bound denotations and copied numeral values. Authenticity is
the input backing's actual provenance followed by these writer/copy
transitions. The graph changes its own tapes, tokens and meter; it does
not change any live source or archive permission merely by writing its
description into $\eta$. ∎

**Definition 5.8 (placement and complete selector bill).** New blocks use
the actual Base/Allocate discipline. For an arena with $r$ existing data
cells and head at distance $h$ from its left marker, Base begins with
Home and one MR, then scans to the old right marker while appending one
counter mark per cell and its final zero. Its body, including this Home
and initial move, has

$$
2h+1+1+5r+4=2h+5r+6
\tag{RCA.5}
$$

primitives, provided its distinct output counter head is already at R.
Its output head positioning is otherwise an added actual routine. Named Allocate
requires a real length $U(\ell)$ at its first bit and the arena head at
R. Its zero-fill loop costs $5\ell+1$; its full contract already includes
the following delimiter. Count that delimiter once, then construct its descriptor from actual owner/port codes,
the Base result, $U(\ell)$, flags and its actual block-creation ordinal.
The latter is a maintained unary block counter, not an assumed receipt
number. Numeric receipt identities, when required by a different record
type, need their own authorized closed-cut scan.

Descriptor fields are copied by AppendWord, with body five per copied
symbol. Fixed literal fields use three per appended symbol. Home costs
$2h+1$ at the actual entry distance; Seek costs $2h+3r+3$ for the actual
constructed offset, plus positioning that offset's own head. Descriptor
blocks are found by a full origin scan of the sealed delimited descriptor
stream, counting blocks by the unary copy/increment/compare graphs, until
the acquired block ordinal is reached. The stream's sealed end is a
bound. This avoids an infinite regress of assuming a free descriptor seek.

Let $P_i$ be the word of all these positioning, Base, length construction,
allocation, descriptor, binding-copy, framing and seal occurrences for
enumeration iteration $i$, expanded exactly as above. Let $k_i=a+i$.
The count of a completed enumeration is

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{\rm enum}={}&C_{\rm initial\ copy/placement}
 +\sum_{i=0}^{b-a}
 \bigl(|P_i|+4k_i+2+5(k_i+1)+5k_i+7\bigr)\\
&+C_{\rm final\ positioning}+4b+2+C_{\rm end\ seal},
\qquad a\le b.
\end{aligned}
\tag{RCA.6}
$$

Here the final comparison is $(b+1,b)$ and its count is $4b+2$.
The copy and increment bodies in (RCA.6) are excluded from $P_i$.
Every $P_i$ is a specified finite recipe evaluated at its actual heads,
stream lengths and descriptors, not a constant granted to the service.
If reference framing needs another copy of a field, that copy belongs to
$P_i$ and is counted there. The equation does not price the computation
of $a,b$, the eventual decoder, or transport as enumeration.

## 6. Frozen targets, transport and the native consumer specification

**Definition 6.1 (immutable mathematical package).** For a fixed authentic
backing $B_s$ and accepted $a\le k\le b$, a selected package payload
contains the complete version/entrance data, $\xi$, actual seed origin,
the acquired seed, the selected input $I(s[0:k])$, $U(k)$, and its actual
cover/naming provenance. An alternative shared-backing object contains a
frozen $B_s$ descriptor and the same fixed target $U(k)$ with read/call
authority to that backing. A mutable variable called latest-cover is
neither payload nor backing. A physical callable package additionally
needs an actual writer, seal/copy chain and installed admitted decoder.

**Proposition 6.2 (extension-stable target and coherent restriction).**
For two authentic covers of the same actual original history, the
selected historical image at every common covered $k$ is equal. A frozen
package keeps that target under later covers and later inputs. For
$k\le\ell$ within one cover, restricting the decoded length-$\ell$
history to $k$ gives the decoded length-$k$ history.

Proof. Lemma 4.5 selects exactly the actual consumed input prefix and
actual chronology through $k$, independently of an unused later suffix.
The acquired seed, version and absolute target are fixed. Restriction
of prefixes in one word gives the coherence assertion. A later cover
changes none of the frozen fields or immutable backing. Physical copy
origin metadata may differ across two independently created packages;
the equality asserted is of their decoded historical images, not of
the complete later package objects or their construction costs. ∎

**Proposition 6.3 (actual paid transfer after materialization).** If an
authorized writer has actually materialized and sealed a payload of
$\ell\ge1$ bits with the binding of Definition 6.1, the existing serial
channel can deliver it to its authorized client. With the AIF six-row
length-tested copy implementation, the two copy stages together have
exactly $12\ell+6$ loop/test/seal/handoff primitives. All initialization,
length preparation, descriptors and positioning remain additional actual
graphs. Before the final receiver seal the destination is unavailable.

Proof. At each stage a sealed immutable source, initialized distinct
destination and actual $U(\ell)$ enter at their first payload/counter
symbols. One iteration is counter RD, source RD, destination WR, source
MR, destination MR, counter MR. The terminal counter RD, seal CTL and
TOK add three per stage. Thus each stage costs $6\ell+3$. The first
copy's seal and token enable the second; repositioning its actual source,
destination and length heads is a separate graph. PR57.L4 proves bit and
origin equality at the final seal. No zero-cost bulk copy, source alias or
unsealed destination read occurs. ∎

**Definition 6.4 (native consumer specification).** A
complete realization must supply one fixed finite PR57 graph

\operatorname{decode}(\operatorname{Run}_G(B_s,U(k)))

**Proposition 6.5 (what existing effective simulation does not close).**

## 7. Repeated service: a precise extension criterion and its gap

**Definition 7.1 (a candidate conservative frame extension).** Retain all
five original source-frame tags and payload languages. Reserve tags 101,
110 and 111 for the following additional canonical frames; all frames
still have the original three-bit tag, $U(d)$ and exactly $d$ payload bits.

| Tag | Payload and installed post-acknowledgement action |
| --- | --- |
| 101 | Empty payload: create a Cover for the preceding post-acknowledgement Ready before this frame's admission, using the immutable complete predecessor-frame prefix. |
| 110 | Either $0U(c)$ for Next in cover $c$, or $1U(c)U(k)$ for Select in cover $c$ at $k$. These are distinct complete payloads. |
| 111 | $U(p)$: Call an actual already published package number $p$. |

Original ReadE, Rho, TagRead and graft semantics are unchanged. These new
frames are a declared model extension, not original source operations or
reinterpreted literal TagReads. Frame syntax is prefix-free after its
length field, including well-typed payload validation. Every actually
consumed service bit is retained through its corresponding WR and its
whole canonical frame thereafter. The new handler adds no further IN,
random bit, independently chosen allocator or schedule. Output delivery
and return are deterministic mandatory work.

Package and cover numbers are paid maintained unary creation counters.
They name actual immutable archive records with owner tokens. An unknown
number returns its distinct executed failure. A correct number alone
never grants a missing current token. A Cover freezes its predecessor
backing and bound; later Select and Call do not consult latest-cover.
The version identifies the entire actually installed extension graph,
boot, ownership rules and descriptor language.

The finite-string handlers have these stopping rules. Cover
replays the complete predecessor frame list to its final ready PC, writes
the resulting bound and initializes its enumeration cursor at the
acquisition bound. Next looks up that immutable cover, compares its cursor
to the bound, and either issues the cursor's selected key and increments
the cursor or returns an end marker. Select uses its actually supplied
index instead and does not advance another cover's cursor. Both use
strict prefix selection and publish only the selected input prefix, seed,
version and provenance. Call looks up an already published immutable key,
replays its finite cutoff and writes the historical image. Unknown
records and out-of-range indices take their separate failure branches.
All branches finish their deterministic publication/acknowledgement and
return before admitting another frame. These are finite-string algorithms;
their primitive lowering is specified in §§11–16.

**Lemma 7.2 (source-only retention is insufficient for this extension).**
If service frames are omitted from replay input, equality of the seed,
consumed original source frames and actual ordinal is insufficient for
full-state recovery on the extended domain.

Proof. At one common ready state, start a tag-110 Select payload of
declared length four. Choose the valid equal-length payloads
$1U(0)U(1)=1010$ and $1U(1)U(0)=1100$.
They share the same tag, length, allocation schedule and first payload
bit. At the IN of their second payload bit they consume different bits.
Both partial frames are extendible to the displayed canonical payloads;
unknown cover numbers may later fail without making their syntax illegal.
Immediately after that IN their raw input latches differ, while the seed,
consumed original source input and actual primitive count agree. The
primitive reader's schedule through that IN is value independent. Full
states therefore differ. Even a well-typed later `CutIndexReject` cannot
erase the earlier acquired bit. ∎

**Definition 7.3 (realization certificate for repeated service).** A
candidate graph $G$ for Definition 7.1 has a repeated-service certificate
when it satisfies all of the following concrete properties.

1. Its finite literal code and canonical boot are fixed independently of
   the source. Its true finite description is acquired and retained as
   public data when needed for replay. Code description and installation
   are paid; the interpreter need not contain a literal copy of itself.
2. Its primitive transition simulator executes one represented native row
   at a time, including represented receipts, dynamic code, descriptors,
   copies and permissions. A represented IN reads the next retained input
   bit by paid local RD. No simulated Call invokes a semantic recovery
   oracle or an actual live source operation.
3. Every new frame is retained as in Definition 7.1. The graph implements
   the displayed handler algorithms, with unique prescribed successors.
   Its elementary bounded scans, unary arithmetic, finite representation
   updates, allocation, delivery and return helpers terminate on their
   declared finite inputs. Termination of a whole handler containing
   boundary-stopped replay is not assumed in this item.
4. A Cover refers only to the complete predecessor prefix. Every accepted
   target is at or before that frozen bound, and every invoked package
   was already published. The historical cutoff is strictly before the
   service fragment computing its recovery.
5. All actual ownership, placement, entry heads, field lengths, seals and
   numeric identifiers satisfy the contracts of the chosen primitive
   routines, with complete current successors and an opcode accounting.
   Handler reads are of acquired public material and handler writes are
   to its owned work/record/output intervals. It exercises no live
   private-source port and grants no live token from represented data.

Items 2, 3 and 5 require the actual graph and its proofs. They are not
inferred from the abstract handlers in Definition 7.1. Definition 6.4 is
part of this certificate. Sections 11–16 construct a graph satisfying it
for the explicitly installed extension.

**Theorem 7.4 (conditional closure, without a self-replay oracle).** For
any fixed graph having a repeated-service certificate, the full lawful
prefix class of its canonical version is closed under every prescribed
non-IN successor and exactly its canonical-extendible IN successors.
Every finite lawful cut, including a cut inside an earlier recovery or
package copy, has a finite-completion-conditional later Cover and exact
historical call. Frozen packages retain their old target. This theorem
does not assert that a graph with the certificate has been constructed.

Proof. Syntax determines the admissible IN bits exactly as for AIF's
canonical language, now with the three additional frame languages.
Serial admission prevents a second frame before handler return and
acknowledgement. At non-IN phases the prescribed successor preserves the
unfinished finite routine. At IN phases extendibility is necessary and
sufficient for remaining in the lawful prefix class. This proves the
closure assertion with physical raw IN distinguished from task admission.

To avoid circular termination, induct on the number of completed frames.
The original source fragments terminate by their suppliers. Consider a
new handler after a finite prefix of completed frames. Its frozen
predecessor consists entirely of those earlier completed fragments.
Boundary-stopped replay of it is finite by the induction hypothesis and
the certified row simulator. A Select or Next has a finite constructed
interval. A Call has a finite previously published key and finite cutoff
at or before a prior cover. Replaying that cutoff follows represented
native rows; even when they are rows of an earlier recovery, each is a
single finite representation update, not a recursive oracle answer.
The certificate's output/copy/return graphs terminate on the resulting
finite strings. Thus the next frame completes, closing the induction.

For an unfinished lawful cut, supply a finite canonical remainder and
finish its already admitted finite fragment. The next Cover freezes a
complete predecessor containing that exact cut. All added input choices
are retained, so the same boundary-stopping and prefix-selection argument
applies to the actual extended graph, not merely to $M_0$'s source rows.
The resulting endpoint includes the earlier recovery's work, cells and
permissions. Freezing the package fields gives Proposition 6.2's target
stability. All physical reads and live successors are those in the
certificate; historical permissions remain represented data. ∎

**Definition 7.5 (two different version correspondences).** For a graph
installed from boot, its ordinal $k_G$ counts that actual boot and every
added primitive. It is not an original $M_0$ ordinal.

A different possibility is an authorized paid late installation at an
original ready boundary $s$. It requires an explicit new owner-local
loader/admission edge, bitwise installation of the finite description,
retention of the activation choice and its actual boundary, and a
deterministic return convention. The old prefix through $s$ is literally
unchanged, but the suffix uses a new version. Such a loader is not
provided by merely declaring a total mathematical decoder. The paid late-loader
realization is specified in §14; no boot-installed clock identification is used.

**Proposition 7.6 (source projection and ordinal correspondence).** Suppose
a certified boot-installed extension runs any sequence of the original
source frames with arbitrary admitted service frames between them. Erasing
the service frames preserves the actual source request/response sequence,
whole guarded acceptance/rejection and source tree evolution from $u$.
It need not preserve complete endpoints, reference numerals or clocks.

If a particular finite lowering maps each logical edge $e_i$ to an actual
nonempty native word $w_i$, logical cut $j$ corresponds to

$$
\theta(j)=|w_{\rm entrance}|+\sum_{i<j}|w_i|,
\qquad
\theta(j+1)-\theta(j)=|w_j|>0.
\tag{RCA.8}
$$

A cut inside $w_j$ is specified by its additional actual prefix length.
Historical references must use that version and those actual lengths.

Proof. The service graph has no private-source operation. Each original
source fragment therefore sees the tree left by the preceding original
fragment and has its unchanged literal or whole-guarded semantics. Induct
on those fragments. Added work changes storage, placement and meter, so
there is no stronger complete-state conclusion from this induction.
Concatenation of the actual finite words gives (RCA.8); nonemptiness makes
it strictly increasing. Restriction to an interior prefix gives its exact
microcut. The formula gives no acquired numeral until the words' lengths
have been computed and serialized by paid operations. ∎

Theorem 7.4 isolates a sufficient repeated-service criterion. AIF alone
does not discharge it. Lemma 7.2 rules out using only old source input.
The late-installed realization and its repeated-service proof are given
in §§14–16. Neither adds immediate busy admission to the original graph.

## 8. Resource correspondence and separate lower bounds

**Definition 8.1 (one-execution accounting).** On an actually implemented
run, partition primitive occurrences into original boot/acquisition,
original finite completion, added installation/admission, selector ingress
and retention, authentic backing access, boundary-stopped replay and
tallies, selection/validation, prefix replay, payload framing/allocation,
transport, invocation/output and return. Each occurrence has one purpose
and its original opcode. An allocation inside an interpreter body belongs
to exactly one such class; counting its cell as storage is a different
coordinate. Current time and fee are the sum and twice the sum of these
actual primitive counts.

Known exact components are the native counts of §5 and the two-stage
copy body of Proposition 6.3. A real bound-driven replay outer loop alone
has $2k+1$ RD/move occurrences: test each leading one of $U(k)$, perform
its body, move its bound head, and execute the final zero test. It must
add the actual simulated-row body, initialization and output work. It
does not make $k$ simulated primitives into $k$ physical primitives.

A tally constructed as one Append(1) per simulated primitive and a final
Append(0) has $3(k+1)$ append-body occurrences. A separate tally copy at
the acquisition boundary, its descriptor, head restoration and seals
have their own costs. The tally's physical receipts are later physical
work, not extra represented original primitives.

**Proposition 8.2 (selector, transfer, working storage and retained
backing differ).** For one cover of $m=b-a+1$ eligible targets, the
conditional selector-width lower bound is
$\lceil\log_2m\rceil$. A unary target has $k+1$ payload bits. Neither
number equals the payload capacity or physical storage of its callable
backing. On any actual PR57 implementation cut $z$ the simultaneous
storage is

$$
S(z)=S_0+b_{\rm ctl}(z)+8A(z)+8r(z),
\tag{RCA.9}
$$

where $A$ counts all constructed ordinary cells and $r$ all actual
receipts, including installation, decoder work, copies and retired versions.

Proof. Theorem 3.3 counts distinct target choices conditional on common
backing. Unary representation length is its definition. A callable object
also needs its actual seed/input backing, permissions and implementation;
removing those while keeping the pointer leaves no source of its payload.
PR57's constructors give one eight-bit ordinary cell per ordinary EX and
one eight-bit receipt per actual primitive. Finite control/token/latch
bits are counted separately. This is (RCA.9), with sender, mailbox,
receiver, tally versions and simulation workspace counted together at
the same cut. Separate-run peak maxima are not added. ∎

**Definition 8.3 (requirements on a full price).** An
expression containing an unspecified interpreter or loader constant is
not a complete acquisition/delivery price. The recurrences in §§11 and 16
include virtual-state parsing, cell and native-rule lookup,
replacement/append, dynamic code, numeric origin construction, all
Base/Allocate/descriptors, actual head positions, control tests,
framing, seals, acknowledgements and return. The known copy-body and
selector-body formulas cannot substitute for these terms.

For a canonical frame with payload length $d$, the actual input is
$3+(d+1)+d=2d+4$ bits. Thus the new empty Cover frame, if installed,
requires four real INs; a Select payload $1U(c)U(k)$ has length
$c+k+3$ and requires $2c+2k+10$ real frame INs. These are bit counts,
not full ingress prices. Header/length construction, matching WRs,
allocation, delimiters, descriptors and positions remain paid. The
final AIF setup price has its own entrance and is unnecessary for these identities.

**Definition 9.1 (falsifiers for the finite models).** The local finite

for their quantified proofs.

**Proposition 9.2 (boundary witnesses).** The following exact witnesses
distinguish the relevant claims.

1. The five-row reader with $d=0$ performs its one zero test and no payload

2. In a capacity-one accepted $\rho\alpha=\beta$ write, packets 01 and 10

3. Fix a cover with bound $b$ and request $k>b$. Later request processing

4. If a package instead stores a pointer to mutable latest-cover, replacing
   that cover can change its target binding. Storing its immutable cover

5. Lemma 7.2's equal-length service frames give different latch states at

Proof. Items 1 and 2 follow by the displayed local transition counts and

the later meter. Item 4 follows by substituting two different cover values

## 11. A finite word compiler with physical entry and exit relations

**Definition 11.1 (representation and the reserved lanes).** The service
uses verifier lanes 32–39, in this order: bank A, bank B, Base/ordinal
scratch, length scratch, descriptor stream, installation manifest, retained
frame stream, and service archive. The five source handlers and the
no-update acquisition invoke producer, actor, arithmetic and channel
routines, not PR57.D8's verifier. In the standard PR57 port assignment,
32–39 therefore have no accesses on the original source-frame call graph.
This is a syntactic call-graph fact: the original five dispatch arms lead
to LJA's generators, producer, counter, row and actor-delivery routines;
none leads to the exhaustive verifier. Public arithmetic ports 0–15 are
not service scratch. No token or position on them is borrowed by the word
compiler. Optional verification is outside $\mathcal R_H$'s five-frame
language; it is not silently inserted between its frames.

The compiler below uses Next through its own word program. The independent
selector of §5 on ports 40–47 is not invoked by this version and its body
prices are not added to the compiler's word-instruction prices.

Original $q,g,e,j$ and $E,A_P,P$ retain LJA's original event language.
A memory-service frame, like the supplier's paid Fetch, is primitive work
with its own typed outcome, not an extra producer source event. Completed
frame count, cover/package count and descriptor count are separately typed
unary words. They never substitute for source-event count or primitive time.

The installation of §14 transfers the eight unused verifier-lane tokens
to $O$. The service reads the seed and ingress only after their authorized
copies. It has no producer-lane token. There are still exactly 64 ports.
The extension does not change the source handlers' access to the installed
private interval. After activation, source requests run their original
handler graphs, with the new frame dispatch and retention work outside
those graphs. Their tree semantics is unchanged; their absolute clocks
and later allocation origins are those of the extended run.

A word program has finitely many bit-string registers $x_0,\ldots,x_{r-1}$.
It uses only assignments $x_i:=\epsilon$, $x_i:=x_j$,
$x_i:=b x_j$, $x_i:=\operatorname{tail}(x_j)$, and tests for empty or
first bit. Tail of empty is defined to be empty. Sequence, finite case,
and while have their usual labelled finite-flow meaning. A finite word
constant is a finite chain of cons assignments. No register stores an
unbounded integer in finite control. A natural used in a word program is
the actual word $1^m$; its wire form is $U(m)=1^m0$.

At a word-instruction boundary, one of A and B has the active complete
bank

$$
x_0\,\mathsf{sep}\;x_1\,\mathsf{sep}\;\cdots\;
x_{r-1}\,\mathsf{sep}\;\mathsf F.
\tag{RCA.10}
$$

Each bank tape initially receives a literal F sentinel. Later versions are
appended, never substituted for preceding versions. F is outside each
version's $\ell=\sum_i(|x_i|+1)$ payload cells. A completed version has an
actual descriptor on port 36 and a seal occurrence. The parity of the
active bank is one finite control bit. The other bank is the next append
destination. All port heads are actual marks; the boundary relation places
no head by a mathematical offset. Scratch and descriptor streams are
fixed owner-local work ports, like AIF's template and Base-counter ports;
they are not exported allocated data intervals. Their raw cells and old
versions are nevertheless counted and replayed.

**Definition 11.2 (complete positioning schemata).** These are native
schemata, not unit-cost word instructions. A symbol test is the testing
RD of PR57.D1. Every displayed append is EX; WR; MR.
Unless a row explicitly names another latch, these public routines use
latch 4 for RD and its matching latched-bit WR. A multi-symbol alternative
uses that same read followed by finite testing CTLs on latch 4. Latch 5
is the ingress length-test latch and latch 6 the ingress input latch.
The compiler, imports and metadata copies use none of latches 0–3 or 7.
There is no live unbounded register or return address in a latch.

1. `LastBank(p)` executes Tail; ML; ML, then RD and, unless the symbol is
   F, ML and repeat. At the preceding F it executes MR. It has reached
   the active version's first field. The final F and preceding sentinel
   or bank F already exist. From entry distance $d$ to R, on a bank with
   $\ell$ payload cells it costs $2d+2\ell+5$.
2. `Field(p,i)` executes LastBank, then skips $i$ fields. A skip executes
   RD; MR until and including that field's sep. The number $i<r$ is a
   static finite compiler choice, so those $i$ skips are unrolled. If the
   preceding fields have lengths $l_j$, its extra cost is
   $2\sum_{j<i}(l_j+1)$. No dynamic index selects a native state.
3. Scratch words have format $U(m)\mathsf{sep}$; scratch head returns to
   R after each append. `LastScratch(t,e)` starts with Tail; ML, at the
   last sep. Repeat the following exactly $e+1$ times: ML, then RD; on
   sep stop this backwards scan; otherwise testing CTL for L; on L stop
   this scan, otherwise ML and repeat its RD. After the final scan MR.
   Here $e$ is a fixed finite call-site offset (0, 1 or 2 in these
   recipes), unrolled into control. Every skipped earlier word exists. Thus all marker and L
   tests are paid, including a first word whose preceding boundary is L.
4. `SegmentCopy(p,t)` starts with $p$ at its first bit and $t$ at R.
   RD tests for sep. On a bit it executes EX(t); WR(t,latched bit);
   MR(t); MR(p) and repeats; on sep it exits without copying it.
   The exact count is $5l+1$. `SegmentCount` uses Append(t,1) in place
   of the bit append, with the same count. A unary copy includes the
   terminal zero and has count $5(m+1)$, as in §5.
5. `ScratchStart(t)` executes Tail, so appending to nonempty scratch
   never assumes that its head was already at R. Every public stream
   scan executes Home; MR, then RD; MR through its actual delimiter or
   R. Each test and move is a primitive. Finite alternatives to distinguish
   L, R, sep, F, 0 and 1 use one testing RD followed by the needed testing
   CTLs, in that displayed order. No such alternative is a free test.

Home; MR in this schema reaches the first ordinary cell. A stream's
leading F sentinel is still that cell. The full manifest-field importer
has Definition 13.2's explicit second MR before its field loop; it does
not identify either Home's L or that first ordinary F with a field bit.

The active-bank scan need test only F: its payload alphabet is
$\{0,1,\mathsf{sep}\}$ and a preceding F is guaranteed. In contrast,
scratch backwards scans really do need the displayed L alternative.

**Definition 11.3 (one assignment's actual expansion).** To update bank
parity $p$ to the other bank $t$, perform the following finite recipe.
All scratch words remain retained. At entry the source version is sealed;
no partially constructed version is treated as active.

1. Execute a CTL entering the register-operation purpose. Tail on Base
   scratch precedes Base(t). Base executes Home(t); MR(t), then
   RD(t); Append(1); MR(t) for each existing data cell, followed by
   RD(R); Append(0). Append sep to its result and seal it. Its full
   body with arena entry head $h$ and $b$ existing data cells is
   $2h+5b+6$, before the sep, seal and scratch positioning. This is
   the actually measured starting offset $U(b)$.
2. Tail on length scratch. Perform a dry emission of the new bank fields.
   For each unchanged field use Field(p,i) and SegmentCount. For a copy
   field use Field(p,j). For a cons field count one extra symbol before
   that same field. For tail, read the source field; if nonempty move
   past its first bit, then count its remainder; if empty emit nothing.
   Count each field sep as one additional mark. These dry emissions
   append exactly $1^\ell$; append 0, sep and a sealing CTL. They do
   not change the bank or issue a simulated primitive.
3. Tail(t). Repeat precisely the same finite emission, now copying the
   actual bits and appending an actual sep after every field. Empty
   assignment emits only its field sep. Cons appends its literal bit;
   tail uses the same empty test and first-bit move as in step 2.
   Append F and execute the bank seal CTL. There is no Allocate call:
   each emitted symbol's EX constructs its own initialized data cell.
   A delimiter is appended exactly once. This append construction
   does not use Allocate's zero-fill-body price as a full helper price.
4. Construct the creation ordinal $U(c)$ by scanning descriptor port 36
   from L to its current R, appending one 1 to Base scratch for each
   completed descriptor terminator F, then 0, sep and a seal. The new
   ordinal is the number of preceding actual descriptors, not a receipt
   index. No descriptor is appended until this scan ends.
5. Tail(36). Append the unary literal owner and bank port, copy $U(b)$
   from the preceding Base-scratch word, copy $U(\ell)$ from the latest
   length word, append flags 10, copy the latest $U(c)$, append F and
   seal this descriptor. Field copies use LastScratch and the complete
   unary-copy graph, including terminal zeros. Literal bits and F use
   Append. Change parity and restore the caller's purpose by one CTL.

The descriptor is exactly owner, port, actual base, payload length,
initialized/mutability flags and actual creation ordinal. The written
10 means initialized and no longer mutable, not an extra acquired right. It describes the
payload before F, not the other bank or a mathematical string. In step 4
there is one newly appended Base scratch word after $U(b)$, so the base
is found with offset 1 and the ordinal with offset 0. Counting descriptors
requires no descriptor of the descriptor stream. Its closed end is the
current R, and it is not extended during that scan. No old bank is erased.

A field read for an empty test uses Field and RD(test sep). A first-bit
test uses Field and RD(test 0), on the nonempty branch of its preceding
empty test. Its two successors are static native labels. Neither test
changes bank parity. They leave their actual head and latch successors;
subsequent routines reposition from those actual heads.

**Proposition 11.4 (compiler and entry theorem).** Every finite word program
has a specified finite PR57 expansion using these eight owned ports. At
every word boundary its represented registers equal the word program's
registers, its parity identifies the most recent completely sealed bank,
and its descriptors name the actual payloads. Each word step has a finite
nonempty paid expansion. No stored length, head position, code fetch or
call/return is an extra atomic operation.

Proof. Assign variables their indices in first-occurrence order, after
hygienic expansion of the finite acyclic helper list of Definition 12.1.
Allocate no native states indexed by a variable's value. Unroll the finite
field numbers in Field. Sequence joins finite exits; tests use the displayed
RD rows; while joins the final edge to its testing entry. Fixed calls are
inlined, as PR57.D1 requires. Thus the result is one finite graph.

For an assignment, the old source bank is immutable throughout both
passes. Each copied field therefore has exactly the intended old value,
even for self assignment, self cons and self tail. The first pass counts
one physical payload cell per symbol that the second pass constructs.
Base was measured before either pass appended to the destination. Only
after payload, F and seal does parity change, and its descriptor copies
those actual counted words. Induction over assignments and tests proves
the register and descriptor assertions. LastBank stops at its existing F;
Field consumes a fixed finite number of finite fields; Base and descriptor
counting have closed unchanged ends; both emissions consume sealed source
fields. All these loops terminate by those explicit bounds. The argument
also establishes the actual entry positions required by every append and
copy. Partially built banks, scratch words and descriptors remain real
busy-state data at interior cuts. ∎

The initial empty bank is constructed without calling a word assignment.
After the two literal bank sentinels, Base(A) obtains its actual start.
Construct the literal $U(r)$ on length scratch by $r$ unrolled Append(1)
rows, Append(0), sep and seal; append $r$ literal seps and F to A and
seal it. Count preceding descriptors and append its full descriptor exactly
as in Definition 11.3. One CTL sets parity A. All registers then really
are empty and the compiled Initialize can run. The $r$ here is a finite
syntactic compiler constant, whose every written bit/cell is counted;
it is not an unacquired data-dependent bound. In particular no uninitialized
source bank is assumed in order to construct the first bank.

**Definition 11.5 (a computable full cost recurrence).** Costs are vectors
indexed by primitive opcode and purpose; the scalar cost is their sum.
Use the following grammar, with the actual entry heads retained in its
state. It is both an expansion rule and a recurrence for its cost.

| Schema | Cost and successor prescription |
| --- | --- |
| Primitive row $o$ | Unit vector at $o$, with PR57.D1's actual local successor and receipt. |
| Sequence | Add the first cost to the second cost evaluated at the first complete successor. |
| Testing RD or CTL | Its unit vector, then the cost of its actual encoded successor. |
| Home or Tail | RD; on the sought marker exit, otherwise ML or MR and recurse. |
| Append | Unit EX + unit WR + unit MR; one new data cell. |
| Field, LastBank, LastScratch | The finite scans of Definition 11.2, from their actual heads. |
| Copy or count segment | Unit RD; at sep exit; otherwise EX, WR, two MRs and recurse on the next source cell. |
| Unary copy | The same five-row body, stopping only after copying and moving past its actual 0. |
| Base | Home; MR; RD; at R Append(0), otherwise Append(1); MR; repeat. |
| Descriptor count | Home; MR; RD; at R Append(0); at F Append(1); MR and repeat; at another symbol MR and repeat. |
| Assignment | Steps 1–5 of Definition 11.3, with the specified dry and real emissions. |
| Word while | Cost of its actual test, plus zero on exit or its body and the recurrence at the next bank state. |

An emission has a fixed list of $r$ field choices. Each choice invokes
the stated Field, copy/count, optional cons or tail, and sep append;
there is no uninterpreted per-field term. Its terminal segment test is
included. Purpose changes, flags, delimiter, descriptor seal and parity
change are their displayed CTLs or appends. This recurrence can be
evaluated from finite inputs while executing the specified graph; it is
not the length of an unspecified interpreter run. Its unbounded variables
are actual words and actual marked-head positions. Termination for a whole
consumer is proved in §15, rather than built into this cost definition.

## 12. The literal row simulator and its complete word program

**Definition 12.1 (acyclic word macros).** The following finite list is a
precise word-program specification convention. Inputs and outputs are word
registers; temporaries are fresh per static call site. A loop over a word
copies it to a temporary and removes its first bit each iteration. A
natural loop uses the word $1^m$, not a semantic integer. These macros
are expanded in this order; none calls itself or a later macro.

| Macro | Word instructions after finite expansion |
| --- | --- |
| Reverse($x$) | $t:=x,y:=\epsilon$; while $t$ nonempty, test its first bit, cons that bit to $y$, and tail $t$. |
| Cat($x,y$) | Reverse $x$ into $t$; copy $y$ to $z$; consume $t$, consing each first bit to $z$. |
| Length($x$) | Copy $x$ to $t$, clear $n$; consume $t$, consing 1 to $n$. |
| Equal($x,y$) | Copy both; while both nonempty compare first bits by finite tests and tail both; differing bits or one empty returns false, joint empty returns true. |
| Frame($x$) | Length $x$ gives $n$; Cat($n,0$) gives $U(|x|)$; Cat that word with $x$. |
| Take($n,s$) | Copy $1^n$ to counter, clear reverse result; consume one source bit per counter bit, cons it to result, tail source and counter; Reverse result. Empty source before counter exhaustion returns typed parse failure. |
| ReadUnary($s$) | Clear a counter; consume one leading source 1 per cons 1 to that counter; require and remove its 0; return the natural word $1^m$ and suffix. Missing 0 is parse failure. |
| ReadFrame($s$) | ReadUnary gives the length counter and suffix; Take that many bits; return the word and remaining source. |
| ReadList($s$) | The list grammar is $0$ or $1\operatorname{Frame}(w)s'$. Inspect and remove its first bit; on 1 use ReadFrame for its item and retain the suffix as the remaining list. |
| Fold or Map | Copy the finite encoded list to a cursor; use ReadList until its terminal 0. Cons each framed result to a reverse item list; Reverse the item order by another ReadList traversal and Cat. |
| Nth($s,n$) | Traverse ReadList, tailing the actual unary index once per skipped item; return the item when the index is empty, or typed missing at list end. |
| Replace($s,n,w$) | Traverse the whole list; at the selected index emit $w$, otherwise its old item; emit the terminal 0. Return missing if no selected item existed. |
| AppendItem($s,w$) | Traverse the list and emit all its items, then $w$, then terminal 0. |

The phrase "emit an item" means Cat of literal 1 and Frame(item) to the
accumulated output; thus it uses only earlier macros. Reverse item order
can also be effected by prepending each complete framed item to an
accumulator. It is not bit reversal of the framed concatenation. A fixed
arity tuple is a list of exactly that many fields. Nth and Replace implement
all tuple projections and updates. Finite tuple indices are literal words;
variable indices are acquired or computed unary words. Every wire $U(m)$
field is decoded with ReadUnary before use as the internal natural $1^m$;
every emitted numeric field appends its 0 explicitly. In particular Prefix
does not count its wire target's terminal 0 as another simulated primitive. List cardinality is
a Fold consing one 1 per item. No host integer operation occurs in these
macros. Their loop variants are the unconsumed finite word, list or index.

**Definition 12.2 (the represented complete state).** A simulated state is
a fixed tuple of the following encoded words and finite lists:

- Installed native row description, bootstrap description and finite
  control/token bit vector; actual PC; eight symbol latches; phase and
  purpose bits. Code has literal row order and literal finite effect
  annotations for the bootstrap and CTL variants.
- The 64 ports in their fixed order. An ordinary port is a finite list of
  eight-bit cell records, including L and R, ordinary head, append/read
  marks, seal and reserved bits. A not-yet-constructed boot port has the
  empty list. Meter read and append heads use their distinct tracks.
- Remaining typed boot material and consumed-input cursor; the chronological
  native event list; unary primitive tally; the retained acquisition tally.

A cell identity is its port and creation position in that port's append
chain, including the old R replaced by EX. Counting the preceding actual
cell records constructs its unary wire identity. All descriptors, source
and scratch bits, dynamic code, counter versions, old banks, frame and
row prefixes, mailbox and receiver states are ordinary cells in this
carrier. They are not reconstructed by separate semantic approximations.
Represented permissions are bits in this tuple; live tokens remain outside
it. No represented source operand controls a physical producer operation.

The new description's exact wire grammar is a framed list of rows, a
framed initial finite-control/token word, and framed distinguished-entry
labels. A row item is its seven concatenated PR57.03 unary fields followed
by its framed finite-effect description. The native graph uses those
seven fields with their specified finite local effects; outer wire framing
is public code material, not an additional dynamic fetch. Write
$\Delta(G)$ for this archival description and $E(G)$ for the owning graph's
literal native boot encoding. They describe the same expanded graph but
are not identified as bit strings. The finite serializer for $E$ traverses
the row list in its order, decodes each unary field and emits its PR57.03
wire word, then emits the local-effect/initial finite-control bit fields
in their fixed installation order. Length Frames and archival tuple/list
tags are removed by ReadFrame/ReadList. All effect bits are the fixed
finite local constructors already specified for the actual CTL/WR variants;
no extra interpreted opcode is introduced. This finite word transducer
is also the definition of the native installation image of the expansion.
The code/frame reservoir for a bootstrap contains $E(G)$; Step's lookup
uses $\Delta(G)$, while its raw code-store coordinate contains the actual
partly or completely installed $E(G)$. The original $v_0$ description
uses its own fixed supplied codec, converted to this row-list grammar by
consuming its finite unary fields. Each consumed bit and converted field
is word work. Effect descriptions use the following finite constructors:
no change; set a designated finite control bit to a literal or designated
latched bit; seal the designated current or immediately preceding marker;
enter the specified bootstrap stage; halt; and select F for this receipt.
An effect is a finite list of such constructors, in written order.
Finite control-bit locations are actual unary fields in the description.
Replace implements each indicated virtual bit change. Bootstrap advance
moves its designated finite control-store cursor; its current and next
slots are existing fields, scanned and updated by the same list operations.
All newer CTLs are purpose entry/restore, parity selection, seal, Ready,
bootstrap entry/NEXT/advance/DONE or halt from these constructors. A row's
finite variant also identifies literal versus latched-bit WR, its actual
source latch on that branch, public/private IN kind, and ordinary versus
control-bit boot write. These are finite annotations to PR57's already
distinct local variants, not an extra string-operation opcode. The fixed
bootstrap's slot selection is part of its finite phase/cursor encoding. Original
CTL effect descriptions are the original supplier's fixed local effects,
encoded in this same finite bit/marker convention. No CTL effect contains
an unbounded state transformation, history append or semantic recovery.

During either bootstrap, preserve the actual raw partly written control
store separately from its last complete decoded table. Lookup uses the
fixed active bootstrap rows until DONE. Only DONE enables the completely
written new table. Thus a mid-installation image includes actual partial
control bits without treating an invalid partial description as executable
main code. Its capture-controller and service-table transitions are the
actual two version changes. This raw/active distinction is part of the
complete state codec.

Native rows use PR57.03's seven unary fields. A CTL or bootstrap row also
has its literal finite local-effect tag, drawn from the fixed finite table
for that graph: seal at its designated current marker, phase/purpose update,
commit, halt, control-bit write/advance, or boundary constructor. These tags
specify the finite local effects already part of the owning graph, not an
unbounded instruction. Without these tags PR57.03 alone would not describe
a seal versus an ordinary testing CTL. The acquired description includes
their finite bit encodings. A port, symbol, latch or Boolean-table field
outside its finite range is rejected by the description parser.

**Definition 12.3 (row lookup, cell lookup and replacement).** To obtain
the current row, traverse the complete finite row list. Decode each of its
seven Frame/unary fields; compare its state field to the stored PC with
Equal; retain the unique matching row. Decode its effect tag as a finite
case. The installed-description validator rejects absent or duplicate
state numbers, invalid successors and malformed finite tags before entry.
It does not assume a native-code random access instruction.

To read a virtual port, use Nth on the 64-port list with the decoded unary
port field. To locate its head, traverse the whole cell list, testing the
appropriate mark bit by fixed tuple/bit positions; retain the marked item,
its actual preceding-item unary count, and its predecessor/successor items.
At a legal reached state there is precisely one appropriate mark. To change
one cell or latch use Replace. To append a cell use AppendItem. To move
between adjacent existing cells, construct the selected index's successor
by cons 1, or its predecessor by tail, on the nonzero branch. An absent
neighbor or disabled token gives a typed simulated-illegal result; it is
never treated as a source reject. Each of these instructions expands into
Definition 12.1 and then Definition 11.3. No scan stops at an address whose
numeral was never constructed.

**Definition 12.4 (one simulated primitive).** The following finite table
is the complete instruction selection for `Step`. Every field operation,
list traversal, fixed-bit selection and update in the table has exactly
the word expansion just given. Finite cases over 64 ports, eight symbols,
eight latches and 256 Boolean tables are unrolled. The old state is copied
to a working word before its updates, so old-value dependencies are not
lost. At its end it appends the event and meter update below.

| Native opcode | Word transformation before the common receipt stage |
| --- | --- |
| RD($i,j$) | Find the ordinary marked cell of port $i$ (read-marked cell on the meter); copy its three symbol bits into latch $j$. Test equality of that newly read symbol to the row's immediate; choose its specified successor PC. |
| WR($i,s$) | Check the represented writer token and writable current cell; select the literal symbol or the represented latched bit by the finite row variant; replace only the symbol bits. Choose the equal successor. |
| ML/MR($i$) | Check the represented enabled boundary and token conditions; clear the old ordinary mark (meter read mark on the meter) and set
  that same track at the appropriate existing adjacent cell. The old R/L crossing failure is simulated-illegal. |
| EX($i$) | On an ordinary port require ordinary-marked R; replace its symbol by blank, retaining the ordinary mark there; append a fresh R with zero ordinary mark. The old R's creation identity is retained. |
| BF($f$) | Read operand subfields 0–2, select one of the eight literal bits of truth table $f$, and replace result subfield 3. No physical source latch or private port is used. |
| IN($i$) | Pop the next bit from the appropriate retained typed reservoir; replace the represented input latch only. No represented cell write or head move occurs. |
| OUT($j$) | Read the represented latch bit and append that bit to the represented public-output event; transmit no live bit at this stage. |
| TOK($a,b$) | Check and replace exactly the represented finite token bits prescribed by that row. Do not execute a physical TOK from these data. |
| CTL | Perform its literal finite local-effect tag, including any designated seal/head mark, finite phase/purpose bits or halt, then its testing or equal PC successor. |
| Boot EX/WR variants | Construct the designated one boundary cell or write the one designated finite-control bit; advance the represented finite installation cursor by its encoded finite local rule. |

For all rows, keep all other finite bits and cells. If there is no test,
$q_0=q_1$. Ordinary native reading of its already installed row is the
finite PC mechanism of PR57.D1; ordinary dynamic code fetch remains a
represented RD and its surrounding rows. Step does not insert a virtual
dynamic fetch on top of a native row.

The common receipt stage replaces the meter append R by the selected
ordinary or F receipt, moves its append mark to one newly constructed R,
and preserves its read mark at its current cell. This applies when the
core row is a meter RD or read-head move. None of the expanded controller
rows performs a core EX or WR on the meter; those variants are not an
admitted way to alter its append track. Its only extensions are receipts. A receipt at the old append R
retains that cell's creation identity. Append one 1 to the represented
primitive tally and append the complete native event, including its actual
port/cell operand identities and newly read, written, supplied or emitted
symbol, to the represented history. Tick/fee are 1 and 2 for this event;
their cumulative represented wire values can be built by unary copy or
doubling. At the distinguished acquisition fence copy the tally to its
retained acquisition register. Complete marks, finite-control bits and
receipt contents use the owning graph's bit convention, not just their
mathematical counts.

The HistoricalImage wire codec is this complete physical-state tuple,
its complete encoded native event list and its source/version/cut binding.
The target-version field is the represented active version/installation
phase at the stop, not the later consumer version. Thus an old target
before Install has $v_0$ in (RCA.1); a target inside a bootstrap retains
that actual bootstrap/raw-table phase. The package separately binds the
later $D_1$ service and the complete activation chain. These tags do not
identify two versions' different clocks.
Reservoir cursors, loop counters, the virtual event accumulator and tally
are simulator bookkeeping, separate from the physical-state projection.
They do not invent extra cells in the represented old endpoint. Their
actual current implementations are cells of the later consumer's banks,
and are counted there. Only the represented port cells, actual finite
control stores, heads, latches and live-token bits project to $X(h)$.
Logical event words and pending/busy interpretation have the following
explicit redundant-field construction. The finite row annotations distinguish
original request admission, original response commitment and original row
seal from service reserve, output seal and Ready. During a represented
frame ingress accumulate precisely its IN bits, parse its complete frame
with ReadUnary/Take and the finite tag cases, and retain that actual request.
Do not accumulate the source clone's subsequent RD(38) as another external
frame. An original response-commit annotation identifies its actual public
response block by the supplier's stored port/base/length fields. Extract
that virtual block by Nth on the virtual port, cancellation of the unary
base and Take over its cell symbols; parse its exact response codec. These
are virtual list operations on acquired replay data, not a live private read.

At this commit append the parsed request/response pair to $E$ and set
$P$ to its singleton. At the corresponding original row seal append that
same pair to $A_P$ and clear $P$. Until commit the request remains only
in the current-frame/busy record. Service frames never append to $E$.
The initial lists are empty at the original entrance; acquisition TagReads
are handled by their same source-commit rows. Generation and attempt
fields come from their represented actual counter cells, by the same
block extraction; their increments are not inferred from service counts.
Original reply variants alpha/beta/branch/absent, numerical ReadE,
accept and reject have distinct finite codec tags. Invalid raw packets
are never passed to the tree-response decoder. At an unfinished response
or row, its actual raw cells and stage remain in the physical tuple;
only a committed pair enters the semantic list. The pending data thus
satisfy the original $E=A_P\mathbin{+\!+}P$ convention by an explicit
word fold, with actual provenance fields copied from the represented
rows and descriptors. Readable-prefix status is the represented final
seal/token case, not equality of payload bytes. These word computations
and their output appends are paid. An old invalid source
packet is always encoded as its raw cell list, with its unavailable phase.
No decoder silently replaces it with an old or candidate valid tree.

The next IN reservoir is determined by represented phase: the canonical
private installation packet reconstructed from the acquired seed; fixed
public initialization and native-description bits in the actual manifest;
or the chronological retained frame stream. A represented control-boot
IN is thus distinct from a frame IN. Exhaustion before a requested IN is
an explicit incomplete-reservoir result, never guessed to be an idle cut.

**Proposition 12.5 (actual native-row correspondence).** Starting with the
encoded actual pre-row state and its authentic reservoir (including the
recorded activation selection of Definition 14.1 at that boundary), the compiled
Step terminates with the encoded actual complete successor and native
event, including its one receipt. It is source uniform. In particular,
a represented IN-before-WR, partial private overwrite, row prefix or
unsealed copy has its actual full type and raw bits. No live authority is
recovered from historical permission bits.

Proof. At the one activation occurrence the explicit recorded instrument
case selects its actual bounded bootstrap-entry CTL. Otherwise row lookup
terminates on the sealed description and selects the actual prescribed row. Each operand lookup is Nth or a finite marked-cell scan; each
replacement preserves the other items. For RD the actual mark selects
the same symbol, giving the same newly read latch and encoded branch.
WR changes only the same symbol subfield. The adjacent-item ML/MR and
EX cases give exactly their old-cell and new-cell effects. The finite BF
truth table has the same operand index and output. IN changes only the
specified latch and consumes exactly its next actual reservoir bit.
OUT records the same emitted bit. TOK and CTL change their explicitly
specified finite fields, with historical tokens confined to word data.
The two boot cases are single-cell/single-bit constructors. Applying the
common receipt rule after the core effect also handles meter-head overlap;
it preserves the updated core read head, rather than its pre-row value.
The tally and event append are encodings of those effects, not additional
virtual native primitives.

All the loops just used consume finite words, row lists or cell lists.
Their intermediate new lists are not their scan sources. The compiler
therefore gives finite actual paid work for each case. Induction on a
finite native prefix gives the complete encoded endpoint and chronology.
This is an implementation of AIF's literal replay map; AIF supplies its
authentic actual-image identification, which is not claimed as a new
information-sufficiency theorem. ∎


## 13. The consumer, its service frames and its actual output

**Definition 13.1 (the finite consumer program).** All structured fields
below are encoded lists and Frames of Definition 12.1. The persistent
registers are manifest, completed-frames, covers, cursors and packages.
Finite temporaries used by the displayed macros and Step are registers
of the same compiler. Their number is the finite syntactic number after
inlining, independent of $H$, source, ordinals and number of calls.

The manifest contains the original public initialization, $H,\xi$, the
actual acquired seed and its delivery origin, original graph/boot version,
new graph/boot description, activation record of §14, and the corresponding
actual copy origins. A cover record contains the manifest binding, frozen
complete predecessor-frame word, $U(a)$ and $U(b)$. Its cursor is a separate
versioned register item. A package contains manifest, selected consumed
frame-input prefix, fixed $U(k)$, and the immutable cover/creation binding.
The actual archive on port 39 contains the corresponding sealed records.
The package list in the banks is another owned, copied physical backing,
not a pointer whose width stands for the payload. Both are counted.

Here is the full finite handler flow. Lookup is Nth on the corresponding
list; replacing a cursor is Replace. AppendItem updates a persistent list.
Ordinal creation is its actual list cardinality in unary. Parse failures
have an explicit finite exit; they do not enter Step with forged code.

| Entry | Word algorithm and complete result |
| --- | --- |
| Initialize | After the native empty-bank construction, run ManifestPrefix(37) of Definition 13.2 and use its ordered field list as the actual manifest. Clear the three record lists and cursors, and import the old completed frame word from its prescribed field. Validate finite tuple syntax and native-row fields. Require tag 101 in field zero, strip those three bits, ReadUnary its actual length and Take exactly its $\Delta(D_1)$ payload; require field exhaustion before row validation. Initialize the actual frame reader; then enter Ready. |
| Cover, tag 101 | Require empty payload. Freeze the present completed-frames word $J$; Boundary($J$) below constructs $a,b$. Append the immutable cover with its constructed creation number $c$, append cursor $a$, and Publish(CoverRef,$c,a,b$). |
| Next, tag 110 payload $0U(c)$ | Lookup cover $c$ or Publish(NoCover). Let $k$ be its actual cursor. If $k>b$, Publish(End,$c$). Otherwise execute Issue($c,k$), then replace that cursor by consing 1 to its unary natural. No other cover's cursor changes. |
| Select, tag 110 payload $1U(c)U(k)$ | Lookup the actual cover or Publish(NoCover). Cancel unary ones to test $a\le k\le b$. If false, Publish(CutIndexReject,$c,k$). If true, Issue($c,k$); do not change a cursor. |
