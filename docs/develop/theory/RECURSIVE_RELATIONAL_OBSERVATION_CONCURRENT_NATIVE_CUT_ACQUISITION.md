# Concurrent native cut acquisition and its temporal boundary

## 1. One actual source and the complete acquisition target

**Definition 1.1 (native source and literal observation).** The source algebra is
the set $\mathcal T$ of all nonempty finite ordered full binary trees, freely
generated by $\alpha,\beta$ and pairing. Different bracketings and occurrences
are not identified. Its global update is

$$
\mathcal T::=\alpha\mid\beta\mid\langle\mathcal T,\mathcal T\rangle,\qquad
\rho\alpha=\beta,\quad \rho\beta=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,\quad
\rho\langle x,y\rangle=\langle\rho x,\rho y\rangle.
\tag{CNA.1}
$$

Write $\varepsilon$ for the root and $\operatorname{Pos}(u)$ for its present
finite $L/R$ addresses. The primitive $\operatorname{Read}(p)$ accepts a
complete finite literal word. At one atomic event it returns
$r_u(p)\in\{\alpha,\beta,\mathsf{branch},\mathsf{absent}\}$: the constructor
at $p$ if present, otherwise absence. It reveals neither children handles
nor subtree equality. Address submission, retention and local computation
are separate resources. These are the source and port of
[NAR, Definition 1.1][NAR], with actual definitions in
[GenealogicalFiberTransport][GFT] and [ActualTreeReadoutAcquisition][ACQ].
A tree in a proof is not an additional observer input.

**Definition 1.2 (entire cut row).** Fix any public finite prefix-free
$P\subseteq\{L,R\}^{*}$. Reuse exactly [NAR, Definition 1.2][NAR]:
$O=I_P(u)=P\cap\operatorname{Pos}(u)$;
$C_P(u)$ is the literal ordered exterior obtained by replacing every
$u|_p$, $p\in O$, by the address-marked hole $\square_p$; and

$$
R_P(u)=(C_P(u),\pi_P(u)),\qquad
p\sim_{\pi_P(u)}q\ \Longleftrightarrow\ u|_p=u|_q
\quad(p,q\in O).
\tag{CNA.2}
$$

The partition is complete: different classes assert disequality.
Every $p\in P\setminus O$ is blocked by a concrete strict-prefix leaf of
the exterior. These labels, brackets, reached addresses and blocked guards
are all retained information. Classes may be named by canonical ordinals
in lexicographic hole order. This is the supplied *literalRecord* /
*canonicalRow_record_iff* equivalence in [ActualCutPartitionDecoder][ACPD],
not a vector of replies at $P$.

Empty cuts, root cuts, unreached addresses and mixed depths are allowed.
$R_\varnothing(u)$ contains the entire tree $u$ and the empty partition.
$R_{\{\varepsilon\}}(u)$ is the constant single root hole with its singleton
partition. An empty reached set for a nonroot cut still retains the concrete
tree and its blocking guards.

**Definition 1.3 (joint configuration and legal histories).** A configuration
contains one actual register $U\in\mathcal T$, observer control and retained
finite records $c$, and the internal writer's active or halted control.
At invocation $U=u$ is arbitrary; observer initialization depends only on
$P$ and one fixed effective program. The writer acts on this same register
and root, not an independently chosen marginal source. Its only
source-changing event is the atomic assignment $U\leftarrow\rho U$.
It may halt after any finite number of updates or remain active.

Observer events are source-free local transitions, atomic literal Reads,
and a separate Return event. A Read leaves $U$ unchanged; its literal and
continuation are selected from actual retained records and control.
Return follows finite local preparation of its output and leaves $U$
unchanged. Writer events may occur before the first Read, between Reads,
during local computation, and after the last Read before Return.
They supply no observer event, count, timestamp or notification.
A larger action class also permits an observer-issued atomic
$U\leftarrow\rho U$ with the single source-free acknowledgement $\star$;
only Theorem 6.3 uses that larger class.

A legal history is a finite or natural-number-indexed interleaving
respecting each process's order. Every finite prefix contains finitely
many events and a finite source. A finite burst may be arbitrarily long,
but no Read is preceded by infinitely many events at one event position.
The semantic number $k$ of updates gives $U=\rho^k u$; it is a proof
witness, not observer data. There is no reset, rerooting, local rewrite,
source swap, saved source version, lock, snapshot, version field, size
or epoch advice, source-walk port or scheduler observation. Finite trees
reconstructed from paid replies are permitted records; they are not
supplied historical copies of the register.

This is a concurrent extension. [Sparse History, Convention 1.3][SLH]
and [NativeSerialMachine][NSM] give a single controller its own recorded
Read/Update sequence; they did not already permit an unrecorded independent
writer. An acknowledgement $\star$ in the larger class does not transmit
a hidden epoch.

**Definition 1.4 (progress and safety quantifiers).** Observer progress
means that a pending observer can perform its next finite local transition
or atomic operation after finitely many intervening events. A finite
calculation of finitely many such transitions therefore completes.
There is no delay bound. A fair infinite history gives a pending observer
infinitely many transitions and gives an indefinitely active writer
infinitely many transitions. A halted writer has no progress obligation.
A completed call can be extended with a halted observer; fairness requires
no further observer work after Return.

Universal coherent safety for $P$ means that on every legal history
where the observer returns $R$, some actual version between invocation
and Return has complete row $R$.
Validation safety is stronger: a success identifies an in-call Read $v$
and returns a finite tree $T=U_v$, hence $R_P(T)=R_P(U_v)$.
Invocation safety requires $R=R_P(U_{\rm in})$.
Return safety requires $R=R_P(U_{\rm ret})$ at the separate Return event.
Neither follows from validation safety.

A finite certificate is sound if, for every legal history realizing its
authentic ordered observer transcript, acceptance implies the asserted
validation equality. Deciding a transcript predicate does not establish
that a handed-in string really came from this register.
Finite-total-interference termination quantifies over every $u,P$ and
every observer-progressing history with finitely many writer updates
during the pending call, without supplying their number or last position.
A budget $B$ means at most $B$ total updates during that call.
Unrestricted fair termination also includes histories with infinitely
many updates.

The missing composition is executable concurrent acquisition and a
transcript certificate producing one actual entire tree version, with
finite-interference progress and paid resources. Abstract fiber criteria,
stationary traversal, stationary row services and post-acquisition
overlap recovery are reused, not new content here.

**Theorem 2.2 (native validation facts within the concurrent model).**
For an originally present $p$, its subtree after $k$ updates is
$\rho^k(u|_p)$. Present addresses persist, as do branch replies.
If $r_u(p)=\ell\in\{\alpha,\beta\}$, then

$$
r_{\rho^k u}(p)=\ell\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k=0.
\tag{CNA.3}
$$

Two authentic equal leaf replies at the same literal therefore enclose
zero global updates, including updates during intervening local work.
Equal branch or equal absent replies have no such implication.

**Proof.** The subtree statement is the supplied
[NativeDepthPatterns][NDP] *original_present_transport*.
Homomorphism preserves ancestors and every pairing. At a reached alpha
leaf successive root replies are alpha, beta, then branch forever;
at a reached beta leaf they are beta, then branch forever.
This proves (CNA.3). A joint-history segment is exactly an iterate,
whose exponent counts all global updates in it, independently of their
actor. No exponent need be decoded to infer that it is zero.
For the negative claims, the root of $\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle$
stays branch after one update; $L$ is absent both at alpha and its
one-step beta successor. These are consequences of native transport
and constructor evolution; the concurrent certificate is their consumer.
$\square$

## 3. A first-leaf certificate that tolerates earlier updates

**Definition 3.1 (first-leaf acquisition).** Start an attempt with an empty
record and a stack containing only $\varepsilon$. Pop $p$, issue
$\operatorname{Read}(p)$, and retain $(p,r_U(p))$ in order.
On branch, push $pR$ then $pL$, giving left-first depth-first traversal.
On a leaf, add no children. Save the first leaf pair $(q,\ell)$.
An absent reply rejects a purported transcript and is never reconstructed
as a leaf.

When the stack is empty the recorded constructor grammar determines a
finite tree $T$. Issue one additional $\operatorname{Read}(q)$,
designated $v$. Accept $T$ and this attempt's transcript if the reply
is $\ell$. Otherwise discard the attempt's guard, stack and description
and restart. After acceptance compute the complete $R_P(T)$ by literal
exterior extraction and structural comparison of acquired subtrees,
then Return. No failed-attempt archive or failure count is needed to retry.

This is one uniform effective read-only procedure. Pending literals,
guard, labels, partial tree and control are actual executable records.
It neither selects a leaf by consulting the unknown source nor predicts
a traversal length from an unprovided size bound.

**Definition 3.2 (finite first-leaf transcript check).** Given one claimed
successful attempt's ordered address/reply records and final validation
record, start with the prescribed stack, check each successive literal,
expand exactly the recorded branches, admit only alpha/beta/branch during
traversal, and require exact stack exhaustion with no extra traversal
records. Find its first leaf $(q,\ell)$ and require final record
$(q,\ell)$. Reconstruct $T$ and its entire row. If a proposed output
row is supplied, compare it to this result. Attempt and validation
delimiters are finite syntax checked in order.
Neither update counts nor source-state annotations enter the check.

**Theorem 3.3 (whole-tree validation from a possibly moving traversal).**
Every accepted first-leaf transcript is sound on every legal history
realizing it. If $s$ is its first-leaf Read and $v$ its validation Read,
there are no updates between $s$ and $v$, and

$$
T=U_s=U_v,\qquad R_P(T)=R_P(U_v).
\tag{CNA.4}
$$

The source is constant throughout that interval. Earlier Reads may
cross updates. This holds for every finite source and every finite
prefix-free cut, including all cases in Definition 1.2.

**Proof.** Equal leaf replies imply zero updates between $s$ and $v$.
Before $s$ every traversal reply was branch, by definition of first leaf.
Every such branch persists to $v$. All other traversal replies occur
in the stationary interval and are exact replies of $U_v$.

Every queued address exists when queried. The root exists; children
are queued only after their parent was read as branch, and persistence
keeps them present. No address is queried twice within the traversal:
each nonroot address has one parent, which queues it only once.
These invariants hold under interleaving, without stationarity advice.

For a completed grammar, induction from its leaves to its root shows
that its finite tree is $U_v$. At a recorded leaf the actual subtree
is exactly its label; at a recorded branch the two actual children
have their recursively reconstructed values. Stack exhaustion excludes
an omitted child or unexamined subtree. This uses the existing stationary
reconstruction argument after proving that every retained constructor
reply agrees with one actual version.

Structural comparison of acquired subtrees now determines equality
and disequality on this same $T$. Exterior and blocked guards also
come from $T$. The proof works for every legal realization of the
checked transcript, without inspecting its generating schedule. $\square$

**Proposition 3.4 (crossing updates and an incoherent complete row).**
Theorem 3.3 does not imply whole-traversal stationarity. Omitting its
validation can yield a complete row that occurred at no in-call version.

**Proof.** Start at $\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle$, Read root as branch,
apply one writer update, Read $L$ and $R$ as beta, and validate $L$
as beta. The accepted tree is $\langle\beta,\beta\rangle$.
Traversal crossed an update before the first leaf, although the
first-leaf-to-validation interval was stationary.

Instead Read root and $L$ before the update, then $R$ after it.
The replies branch, alpha, beta assemble $\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle$.
For $P=\{L,R\}$ its hidden partition has two classes, whereas both
actual versions have a single class. Every individual reply is true,
but the assembled complete row is wrong at every actual version.
Another root branch Read would not detect this; the required reread
of $L$ does. $\square$

## 4. A certificate for an entirely stationary fresh traversal

**Definition 4.1 (separate guard and bracketed traversal).**
Start an attempt by Reading the root and continuing only to the left
child on each branch reply. Stop this guard search at its first leaf
$(q,\ell)$, with $q=L^d$, and retain that paid pair.
Start a fresh left-first full traversal from $\varepsilon$, recording
its complete grammar as in Definition 3.1. Then Read $q$ at validation
$v$. Accept only on the same reply $\ell$; otherwise discard the attempt
and begin a new guard search. Compute the row only after acceptance.

The finite check validates the guard-search prefix
$\varepsilon,L,\ldots,L^d$, with all earlier replies branch and the last
a leaf, then the complete fresh traversal grammar and final identical
guard. It never substitutes a guard-search reply for a fresh traversal Read.

**Theorem 4.2 (stationary traversal certificate).** Every accepted bracketed
transcript certifies zero updates from its guard leaf Read $g$ through
validation $v$. All Reads and local steps of the entire fresh traversal
are within that stationary interval, and its tree is $U_g=U_v$.
The finite check is sound in every legal realization of the transcript.

**Proof.** Apply (CNA.3) to the two paid guard observations.
Every fresh-traversal event lies between them, so the stationary
reconstruction supplier applies directly, or use the grammar induction
of Theorem 3.3 with no preceding branch exception.
Guard search itself stays legal under concurrency by persistence of
branches and their left children. Its branches before $g$ may cross
updates, but they are not part of the fresh traversal. Every decision
uses retained finite data. $\square$

**Proposition 4.3 (root shortcut and its limit).** A separate zero-Read
procedure can return $R_{\{\varepsilon\}}$ under unlimited interference.
It does not acquire the entire tree or provide a tree certificate.
If a tree certificate is required, either full-tree procedure applies
unchanged to the root cut. Accepted transcripts have the respective
guarantees of Theorems 3.3 and 4.2; under finite total writer interference
and observer progress, termination and the paid resource bounds follow
from Theorems 5.2–5.4.

**Proof.** The row always hides the whole nonempty tree with one class.
Alpha and beta have that same row and different trees, so the constant
row is not a tree certificate. $\square$

## 5. Finite interference, failed attempts and the same construction's costs

**Definition 5.1 (analytical parameters and accounting model).** For the
invocation source $u$ and total interference bound $B$, set

$$
V_B=\rho^B u,\quad
N_B=|\operatorname{Pos}(V_B)|,\quad
H_B=\max_{p\in\operatorname{Pos}(V_B)}|p|,\quad
A_B=\sum_{p\in\operatorname{Pos}(V_B)}|p|,\quad
M=|P|,\quad L=\sum_{p\in P}(|p|+1).
\tag{CNA.5}
$$

The algorithms receive none of $u,V_B,B,N_B,H_B,A_B$.
For completed attempt $j$, let $N_j$ be its actual number of traversal
Reads, $A_j$ their literal-length sum, and $d_j=|q_j|$ its guard depth.
These definitions include failed attempts, whose reconstructed tree
need not be an actual source version.

Count raw Reads and submitted address letters separately.
For local work use finite records with explicit address strings,
node records, indices, stack and output buffers in a random-access model.
A character operation or bounded-index cell operation costs one logical
unit; its bit width is billed below. A cut trie is constructed in $O(L)$
units. Retain an explicit equality matrix on reached holes, including
every false entry. Exact comparisons walk acquired finite tree records;
allocation identity is not tree equality. This representation asserts
no space, query or work optimality.

**Theorem 5.2 (finite interference and disjoint failure charging).**
Both full-tree procedures terminate on every observer-progressing history
with finite total writer interference. Under a total budget $B$, at most
$B$ attempts fail and at most $B+1$ attempts are made. Each failure can
be assigned a distinct actual update strictly between its guard leaf
Read and validation Read.

**Proof.** Fix a finite bound $B$ for the actual history.
Every visited or queued address is present in $V_B$, by persistence.
A traversal visits distinct addresses, hence makes at most $N_B$ Reads.
The separate guard search follows distinct left prefixes, hence has
at most $H_B+1$ Reads. It must reach a leaf: a branch at depth $H_B$
would force a deeper child in $V_B$. The same finite-tree bound forces
a first leaf in the first procedure. Finite processing between Reads
and observer progress therefore finish each attempt and its validation.

A failed validation entails an update in its guard interval: zero
updates would give the same leaf reply. Such intervals are disjoint
and lie in successive attempts. Charge the first update in each one.
No event is charged twice, so at most $B$ failures occur. An infinite
succession of completed attempts would contradict that bound.
Once accepted, the tree, row, finite check and Return preparation
require only finite local work.

If only finiteness is promised, use the actual finite update count $b$
as the proof's bound. The observer neither receives $b$ nor recognizes
the final writer event. $\square$

**Theorem 5.3 (Reads and addresses, including all failures).**
A completed first-leaf attempt pays exactly $N_j+1$ Reads and $A_j+d_j$
letters. A completed bracketed attempt pays exactly $N_j+d_j+2$ Reads
and $A_j+d_j(d_j+1)/2+d_j$ letters. Thus the entire call has bounds

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 & \text{Reads} & \text{submitted letters}\\ \hline
\text{first leaf}&(B+1)(N_B+1)&(B+1)(A_B+H_B)\\
\text{bracketed}&(B+1)(N_B+H_B+2)&
(B+1)(A_B+H_B(H_B+1)/2+H_B).
\end{array}
\tag{CNA.6}
$$

In both cases the maximum queried literal length is at most $H_B$,
and at most $N_B$ different addresses are queried in the entire call.
On an accepted attempt, $N_j=N(U_v)$ and $A_j=A(U_v)$.
Its first-leaf bill is $N(U_v)+1$ Reads; its bracketed bill is
$N(U_v)+d_j+2$ Reads.

**Proof.** Traversal pays once per visited address and validation once
at the guard. Separate guard search reads exactly the $d_j+1$ literals
$L^h$, $0\le h\le d_j$, whose lengths sum to $d_j(d_j+1)/2$.
Each is separately submitted from the root; this is not a source-walk
port. Traversal addresses form a subset of $\operatorname{Pos}(V_B)$,
without repetition, so $N_j\le N_B$, $A_j\le A_B$ and $d_j\le H_B$.
Sum over at most $B+1$ attempts. Persistence also puts the union of all
queried addresses in that finite position set. An accepted complete
grammar is the validated tree, proving the final equalities. $\square$

**Theorem 5.4 (work, retained certificates and index bits).** There are
implementations of these same procedures, extracting a row only after
acceptance and performing one subsequent finite certificate check,
with logical-work bounds

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{\rm first}
&=O\bigl((B+1)(N_B+A_B+H_B+1)+L+M^2N_B\bigr),\\
W_{\rm bracket}
&=O\bigl((B+1)(N_B+A_B+H_B^2+H_B+1)+L+M^2N_B\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{CNA.7}
$$

Peak observer storage, including public cut input, current attempt,
retained accepted tree and certificate, row output, blocked guards,
partition matrix and comparison workspace, is

$$
S_B=O(N_B+A_B+L+M^2)
\tag{CNA.8}
$$

constant-alphabet characters and bounded-index cells. Failed certificates
are discarded. A sufficient analytical index width, up to a fixed
additive constant, is

$$
w_B=\left\lceil\log_2\!\left(
2+(B+1)(N_B+A_B+H_B^2+H_B+2)+L+M^2
\right)\right\rceil .
\tag{CNA.9}
$$

On a logarithmic-cost random-access model charging $O(w_B)$ bit work per
indexed operation, conservative bit bounds are $O(w_BW)$ work and
$O(w_BS_B)$ storage, for the corresponding expression $W$ in (CNA.7).
Indices grow dynamically; (CNA.9) is not installed source-dependent
word-size advice.

**Proof.** Keep explicit words on a depth-first stack and append an
address/reply record per Read. Tree construction, parsing and checking
use $O(N_j+A_j+d_j+1)$ units per first-leaf attempt.
For a bracketed attempt add $O(d_j^2+d_j+1)$ for all guard prefixes.
Ordinary node indices and continuation frames build the acquired tree
without repeatedly copying entire subtrees. Discarding a failed attempt
costs at most its allocated length and is included in this sum.

After acceptance, construct the public cut trie and traverse it with
the acquired tree, retaining the exterior and a subtree index or
concrete blocking leaf for each cut address. Work is $O(N_B+L)$;
hole-address copies total at most $L$.
For each pair of reached holes, directly compare their ordered finite
subtrees. Each comparison visits at most $O(N_B)$ node records with
a bounded-depth stack, and supplies its equality or disequality bit.
There are at most $M^2$ pairs. Canonical class names, if wanted, follow
by scanning the matrix in hole order. Recomputing and checking the row
costs at most another constant multiple. This gives (CNA.7).

A traversal's explicit addresses cost $O(N_B+A_B)$ storage; its tree
and stack fit the same bound. Guard-prefix storage adds
$O(H_B^2+H_B+1)$. A longest source path already contributes
$H_B(H_B+1)/2$ to $A_B$, so the addition is $O(N_B+A_B)$.
Input, exterior, guards and matrix give the other terms in (CNA.8).
The chosen finite implementation uses only indices, lengths and optional
cumulative Read counters bounded by a constant multiple of the quantity
inside (CNA.9). Constant extra bits cover that multiple.
It maintains no hidden writer count.

All retained data, comparison, checking and output materialization
are charged. Keeping earlier certificates, failed attempts or multiple
captured trees adds their actual lengths. The source register's storage
and global-update work are separate from observer work.
Atomicity is the declared primitive contract, not a claim of constant
physical time for global substitution. $\square$

**Proposition 5.5 (size bounds and absence of a final-update signal).**
The analytical parameters also satisfy

$$
H_B\le H(u)+B,\qquad
N_B\le (N(u)+1)2^B-1,\qquad A_B\le N_BH_B.
\tag{CNA.10}
$$

There is no source-size bound from $B$ alone. Finiteness alone, or a
positive budget without evidence that it is exhausted, does not exclude
an update after successful validation. A zero remaining budget does
exclude it.

**Proof.** An update increases height by at most one and at most doubles
the number of leaves. A full tree with $\ell$ leaves has $2\ell-1$ nodes.
This gives the first two inequalities; the third is immediate.
Already at $B=0$, the initial source may be arbitrarily large.
For the last claim, when an unused update is still permitted, compare
a successful prefix with itself followed by an update in the source-free
response gap. Both have accepted the same certificate. The promise $B=0$,
or evidence of $B$ distinct already spent updates, instead excludes more
updates. For example, $B$ failed attempts provide such evidence by
Theorem 5.2. A positive upper bound by itself is not a count of spent
updates. $\square$

## 6. The unrestricted-interference classification

**Theorem 6.1 (branch saturation and disjoint complete-row trajectories).**
For every $u\in\mathcal T$ and $d\ge0$, $\rho^{2(d+1)}u$ has branch
at every address of length at most $d$, and those replies persist.
For every finite prefix-free $P\ne\{\varepsilon\}$ there are finite
$x_P,y_P$ such that

$$
\{R_P(\rho^a x_P):a\ge0\}
\ \cap\
\{R_P(\rho^b y_P):b\ge0\}
=\varnothing.
\tag{CNA.11}
$$

Separation holds even across different epochs.

**Proof.** Within two updates every current leaf becomes a branch with
two nonempty children. Minimum terminal depth therefore increases by
at least one every two updates. After $2(d+1)$ updates it exceeds $d$,
so every address through depth $d$ is a branch. Persistence follows
from Theorem 2.2.

For separation suppose first $|P|\ge2$. Complete its finite prefix trie
to a full binary skeleton by adding missing sibling leaves. Every
$p\in P$ is a skeleton leaf. Fill those leaves with alpha for $x_P$;
use identical other leaves for $y_P$, changing exactly one selected
$P$-leaf to beta. All cut addresses are reached at every epoch.
Every $x_P$ row has all hidden subtrees equal. Every $y_P$ row has its
selected hole unequal to every other hole, because $\rho$ is injective.
Native injectivity is supplied by [SourceTransportCentralizer][STC]
and the internal injectivity proof in [GenealogicalFiberTransport][GFT];
it concerns literal trees, not leaf counts. Thus the complete partitions
separate every cross-epoch pair. Exterior evolution cannot remove that
separation.

For $P=\varnothing$, take $\alpha$ and $\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle$.
Both are primitive: alpha is never a root image; a pair of alphas is
neither $\rho\beta=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle$ nor the image of a pair,
since alpha has no predecessor. If iterates of these two trees coincided,
cancel the common smaller exponent using injectivity. Equal exponents
would identify the distinct trees; unequal exponents would put one
primitive tree in the image. This is the primitive-orbit uniqueness
supplied, including private *primitive_index* and *normal_root*
arguments, by [IntrinsicSubstitutionCoordinates][ISC].
Empty-cut rows are the entire trees.

For $P=\{p\}$ with $p\ne\varepsilon$, let $q$ be the root child opposite
the first letter of $p$. Use the same other child in both sources,
and put alpha at $q$ in one and $\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle$ there
in the other. The whole $q$-subtree stays visible outside $P$.
Homomorphism and the disjoint primitive orbits separate it at any
pair of epochs. Prefix freedom leaves only these cases and the excluded
root singleton. $\square$

**Theorem 6.2 (read-only classification on every fair history).**
For fixed finite prefix-free $P$, there exists an initially source-blind
deterministic read-only observer that is universally coherently safe
and terminates on every fair history with unrestricted interference
if and only if $P=\{\varepsilon\}$.
For any other cut, every universally safe observer has a fair
nonterminating actual history. No bounded-memory assumption is imposed
on the observer.

**Proof.** The root shortcut proves sufficiency.
Otherwise use $x_P,y_P$ from Theorem 6.1. Run the observer from its
common control in two actual histories. Before each selected
$\operatorname{Read}(p)$, allow a finite writer burst of at least
$2(|p|+1)$ updates in both histories, then perform the Read.
Both replies are branch. Burst length depends only on the requested
finite literal, itself determined by the common retained observer record.
It supplies no source advice to the observer. Every finite source version
is still finite.

By induction the observer's controls, requests, replies and local
computations agree in the two histories. A finite return would give
the same row in both. Coherent safety would put it in both disjoint
trajectory ranges in (CNA.11), even if its purported instants had
different epochs, a contradiction.

Thus this common execution does not return. Interleave an extra writer
step between successive observer local steps whenever necessary.
Every observer transition and requested Read occurs after a finite
delay, and an active writer performs infinitely many steps.
If only finitely many Reads occur and local computation continues
forever, this interleaving still gives fairness. An observer unable
to produce its next transition already fails the effective
progress/termination requirement. Hence this is a fair nonterminating
history, not just an unbounded family of finite running times.

Every finite prefix can alternatively be extended by halting the writer.
A return on such a prefix would already violate safety in one of those
finite-total-interference extensions. The argument never substitutes an
infinite actual tree for a finite source version. $\square$

**Theorem 6.3 (separate extension permitting observer Updates).**
The classification of Theorem 6.2 remains true if the observer may also
issue genuine global $\rho$ with the source-free acknowledgement
$\star$, selected from retained control. The positive root procedure
still issues no Update.

**Proof.** Keep the same paired sources and schedule. At an observer
Update, apply $\rho$ in both registers and return $\star$ in both;
observer controls remain equal. These events only advance the registers
along their own trajectories. Equation (CNA.11) separates them at every
pair of total exponents, including updates by both processes.
The writer can still supply a finite saturating burst before every Read.
Interleave writer steps with local or observer Update events as needed
for fairness. The same output contradiction follows.

An acknowledgement carrying a count, size, saved view or source-dependent
duration would be a different port. This theorem extends the
unrestricted-interference obstruction; it does not extend the
read-only endpoint obstructions below. $\square$

**Proposition 6.4 (constant subdomains and degenerate rows).**
Nonroot rows can be constant along particular trajectories without
contradicting the all-source classification.

**Proof.** At $P=\{L,R\}$ and source $\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle$,
every row is the two-hole pair with a single equality class.
At a root cut such constancy holds for all sources.
An empty cut exposes the whole changing tree: its right clock increases
at every update. A cut blocked at alpha retains a concrete alpha guard,
which changes to beta at the next update even if no hole is reached.
Constant root rows, empty partitions, blocked rows and trajectory-specific
constant rows therefore have distinct meanings. Theorem 6.2 includes
the separating pair for its fixed cut among its all-source inputs. $\square$

## 7. Invocation, validation and a separate Return

**Theorem 7.1 (one-update endpoint obstructions for all nonroot cuts).**
Let $P\ne\{\varepsilon\}$. No deterministic initially source-blind
read-only procedure, correct on all sources and terminating on
quiescent inputs, can guarantee the invocation row if one hidden update
before its first Read is permitted. In the same comparison class it
cannot guarantee the separate Return-time row if one hidden update
after its last Read and before Return is permitted.
Each obstruction uses at most one writer update during the call.

**Proof.** For every such $P$, the row on alpha is the concrete alpha
tree with empty reached set and partition; the row on beta is the
distinct concrete beta tree. This includes $P=\varnothing$.

For invocation, compare entry alpha followed by a hidden update before
the first Read with entry beta and no update. All later source observations
and observer control agree, while the required invocation rows differ.
Termination on the stationary beta input supplies the common finite
response. A response before any Read is already impossible by the
same all-source distinction.

For Return, run the observer on stationary alpha until its finite response.
Insert one update after its last Read and before the distinct Return.
No later observation distinguishes the two histories; their output agrees
but the required Return row changes from alpha to beta. If there was
no Read, use the invocation-to-Return gap. The update may occur during
source-free output preparation too. Both histories have fair extensions
with the writer halted. $\square$

**Theorem 7.2 (entry obstruction without pre-first-Read updates, for an
uncovered ray).** Suppose the finite cut $P$ leaves an infinite binary
ray uncovered: none of its elements is a prefix of that ray.
Even if writer updates before the first Read are forbidden, no
read-only observer correct on all sources and terminating on quiescent
inputs can guarantee the invocation row when one hidden update after
its first Read is permitted.

**Proof.** Let $D=\max(\{0\}\cup\{|p|:p\in P\})$.
The depth-$D$ prefix $w$ of an uncovered ray is incomparable with
every member of $P$, and $wR^\infty$ is also uncovered.
Follow that ray to its terminal leaf in a finite tree. Sum weight one
per $L$, two per $R$, and finally zero for alpha or one for beta,
obtaining $J_w(u)$. All constructors needed lie in the literal exterior;
an early blocking leaf also ends the computation. Thus $J_w$ factors
through $R_P(u)$.

This is the visible right-clock factor of [NAR, §11 and Theorem 12.3][NAR].
Its one-step law is also direct: alpha becomes beta, increasing the
sum by one; beta becomes the pair beta,alpha, so the ray's next edge
contributes either $1+1$ or $2+0$ in place of the old one. Therefore

$$
J_w(\rho u)=J_w(u)+1,\qquad R_P(\rho u)\ne R_P(u).
\tag{CNA.12}
$$

The observer's first literal $a$ is fixed by source-blind initialization
and its local control. A return without a Read cannot be universally
correct; never reaching a first Read violates quiescent termination.
Choose a finite $u$ giving branch at $a$, for example
$\rho^{2(|a|+1)}\alpha$. The first reply is branch both at $u$ and
at $\rho u$.

Compare entry $u$, first Read, then one update with entry $\rho u$
and no writer update. Neither has a pre-first-Read update. The first
replies agree, and after that gap the registers agree permanently.
The observer's remaining records and output therefore agree, and the
second history supplies quiescent termination. The entry rows differ
by (CNA.12). $\square$

**Proposition 7.3 (the uncovered-ray restriction is substantive).**
For $P=\{L,R\}$, if pre-first-Read updates are forbidden, a read-only
procedure recovers the invocation row on every finite source under
finite total interference and observer progress. Theorem 7.2 thus cannot
be extended to every nonroot cut.

**Proof.** First Read the root, which is the invocation constructor.
If it is a leaf, the entire invocation tree and row are known; return
that saved row regardless of later updates.
If it is branch, both cut addresses were reached at invocation.
Their exterior is the fixed two-hole pair, and their equality bit is
preserved in both directions by every update, using injectivity and
homomorphism of $\rho$.
Acquire a later validated row with either whole-tree procedure.
Finite interference gives termination; the acquired partition equals
the invocation partition, including disequality. This gives the
invocation row in every case. It supplies no all-fair-history
termination under infinite interference. $\square$

**Proposition 7.4 (endpoint and certificate distinctions).**
The validation event is a designated attainable observation endpoint,
not the separate Return event. A successful finite certificate proves
neither the invocation row nor absence of future updates.
A fused validation-Read/Return primitive or a supplied quiet response
gap would change the Return contract.

**Proof.** Theorem 3.3 gives equality at its named Read in every
compatible history. The first example in Proposition 3.4 gives identical
accepted transcripts for an entry $\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle$ with
an early update and entry $\langle\beta,\beta\rangle$ without one.
A subsequent unobserved update can change the source before Return,
as in Theorem 7.1, without changing the earlier validated equality.
A genuinely fused event has no intervening event position; a supplied
quiet gap preserves the source. Either new premise transfers validation
equality to Return. Neither is present in Definition 1.3. $\square$

## 8. From certified captures to relative epochs and retained memory

**Theorem 8.1 (concurrent capture supplies an actual row family).**
Consider finitely many successful calls on one actual register and root,
with pure $\rho$ as its only source change. Use their validation Reads
as chronological capture events $v_i$. A root shortcut may instead
designate any one declared event in its call. Put $t=U_{v_0}$ and
let $n_i$ count actual updates after $v_0$ and before $v_i$. Then

$$
0=n_0\le n_1\le\cdots\le n_{m-1},\qquad
U_{v_i}=\rho^{n_i}t,\qquad
R_i=R_{P_i}(\rho^{n_i}t).
\tag{CNA.13}
$$

These equalities supply the acquired-family premise of [NAR, §12][NAR].
The counts are semantic witnesses until a decoder recovers them.

**Proof.** Between chosen events the one source undergoes exactly
the counted number of updates; Reads and local work leave it fixed.
Induction over these events gives the source equality.
Each accepted certificate gives the entire tree at its validation,
hence the complete row there. The constant root-shortcut row is the
row at any chosen event. Compose these facts on this one actual history.

Each non-shortcut capture has a real stationary guard interval ending
at validation. A first-leaf traversal may start earlier, but Theorem 3.3
proves its output equals that stationary version. This supplies the
truthful row-value premise without retrospectively adding concurrent
writers to a serial execution theorem. $\square$

**Definition 8.2 (two later decoder interfaces).** A row-only decoder
receives exactly the ordered public cuts, ordered complete rows in
(CNA.13), and authentic common-root, pure-history and chronological-order
promises. It receives no $n_i$, source-dependent identity, full trees,
certificate strings, extra queried addresses, record lengths, retry
or Read counts, control residue, timing, pointers, archives or further
source-dependent information. A complete retention cut must make such
earlier fields inaccessible or functions of the declared row input.
This is an access and retention hypothesis, not free physical erasure.

A richer decoder may additionally receive retained validated trees $T_i$
or accepted transcripts reconstructing them. Their storage and retention
costs are those of §5, summed over the records kept. Syntax checking
is conditional on authentic transcripts and the common-history promise;
it does not create authentication.

**Theorem 8.3 (legitimate overlap application and its interface limit).**
For the row-only interface put
$D=\max(\{0\}\cup\{|p|:p\in P_i,\ i<m\})$ and

$$
S_i=\{w\in\{L,R\}^D:\nexists p\in P_i,\ p\preceq w\},\qquad
i\sim j\ \Longleftrightarrow\ i\ne j,\ S_i\cap S_j\ne\varnothing.
\tag{CNA.14}
$$

The supplied [NAR, Theorems 12.3 and 12.5][NAR] now apply:
differences within each connected component are uniformly effectively
recoverable; the entire vector $(n_i)$ is uniformly recoverable on the
all-source, all-chronological-epoch domain if and only if this overlap
graph is connected. A specified difference is uniformly recoverable
if and only if its indices share a component.
For the richer interface the positive implication remains valid, but
the row-only necessities do not transfer.

**Proof.** Theorem 8.1 supplies the actual family of NAR Assumption 12.1;
Definition 8.2 supplies its entire input restriction.
An edge's two acquired exterior clock factors subtract to $n_j-n_i$.
Summing along a spanning forest gives componentwise coordinates.
NAR Lemma 12.4 supplies the simultaneous whole-row collision for the
negative all-source assertions, preserving every partition and guard.

Every promised finite row family is attainable by pausing the writer
during each finite acquisition and executing the prescribed finite
bursts between captures. Thus this acquisition method followed by a
complete retention cut does not remove the collision inputs. This is
an application of the supplied uniform criterion, not a new overlap
theorem or a claim that every disconnected attained fiber is ambiguous.

For richer input, discard additional data to retain sufficiency.
For necessity, reuse the native right clock

$$
c(\alpha)=0,\quad c(\beta)=1,\quad
c(\langle x,y\rangle)=2+c(y),\qquad c(\rho^n t)=c(t)+n.
\tag{CNA.15}
$$

The last law is [DelayedOriginalReadout][DOR] *right_clock_iterate*,
with its private *clock_step* supplier. Validated trees determine
$n_i=c(T_i)-c(T_0)$ by finite local computation; accepted transcripts
first reconstruct those trees. This works even with only root cuts,
whose rows are constant and whose overlap graph is disconnected for
$m>1$. It uses paid descriptions, not a new clock port.
Row-only necessity is therefore false in the larger interface. $\square$

**Proposition 8.4 (disjoint-cut ambiguity and richer resolution).**
Let $T_a=\rho^a\alpha$, $P_0=\{R\}$, $P_1=\{L\}$,
$X=\langle T_2,T_6\rangle$ and $Y=\langle T_2,T_5\rangle$.
The authentic families $(X,\rho^2X)$ and $(Y,\rho^3Y)$ have the same
complete row input at these cuts, with lags two and three respectively.
Retained certified trees recover those different lags.

**Proof.** Reuse the attained collision of [NAR, Proposition 12.7][NAR].
First exteriors are both $\langle T_2,\square_R\rangle$; second exteriors
are both $\langle\square_L,T_8\rangle$. Reached sets are the indicated
singleton cuts with singleton partitions, so no guard or disequality
difference is omitted. Stationary successful calls separated by the
actual two or three updates acquire these families.
Equation (CNA.15) on the full trees returns the different lags.
This illustrates the interface boundary; the row collision itself
retains its existing supplier. $\square$

**Proposition 8.5 (relative memory recovery and the earlier-origin boundary).**
For one fixed cut, two authentic captured endpoint rows and their known
actual lag recover all intervening mathematical row values relative to
the first capture. A retained initial captured tree and known lag also
recover the entire intervening pure-$\rho$ tree sequence.
Neither conclusion authenticates a designated earlier origin, the count
before first capture, births relative to that earlier origin, full event
placement or physical duration.

**Proof.** Regard the first captured tree $t$ as relative origin.
Its row $R_P(t)$ computes $b_P(t)$ by [NBP, Theorem 4.2][NBP]:
already reached cut addresses have birth zero; each other cut address
is blocked by a concrete exterior leaf, whose label and remaining
literal suffix determine its future birth. The final captured row
and this vector, at the known lag $n$, are precisely the relative-origin
inputs of NBP Theorem 4.3. They recover every $R_P(\rho^j t)$,
$0\le j\le n$. The lag must have been recovered with the authentic
shared-history premise, or be independently authenticated; it is not
silently supplied for disconnected row-only inputs.
If $t$ itself is retained, direct finite computation of its iterates
gives the entire tree sequence. Neither method recreates unobserved
Read events or certifies their occurrence. Captures with different cuts
require the corresponding additional record conversion before applying
this fixed-cut theorem.

For the earlier-origin boundary, a first captured tree $t=\rho u$
and its subsequent evolution
are compatible with an origin at $t$ and no previous update, or an
origin at $u$ followed by one previous update. Every subsequent Read
may agree. Primitive ancestry is computable from an acquired full tree
by [Intrinsic Substitution Coordinates][ISC], but a designated origin
need not be primitive, and its authenticity is a separate premise.

Proposition 7.4 exhibits identical successful transcripts with different
pre-guard update placement and different entry trees.
Any increasing reassignment of physical delays to the same ordered
events also leaves every observation in this model unchanged.
Thus the transcript predicate and row input determine no physical
duration or full hidden event log.

NBP Theorem 4.3 at an earlier designated origin still requires its own
authenticated $n$ and birth record. A newly acquired current row alone
does not supply them. The endpoint reconstruction just proved also
differs from forward prediction using only the first row: that weaker
input has the conditional supplements and birth collisions identified
in [NAR, §§2–4][NAR]. Acquisition, retrospective relative memory,
forward prediction, earlier-origin recovery and authentication are
different tasks. $\square$

## 10. Exact invocation-cut classification from the first observation

**Assumption 10.1 (protected first Read, unprotected continuation).**
Use the source, complete row, deterministic read-only observer and separate
Return of Definitions 1.1–1.4. Fix any public finite prefix-free cut $P$.
The invocation source ranges over all of $\mathcal T$, including both
one-leaf trees. Observer initialization and every source-free transition
before the first Read depend only on $P$ and the fixed effective program.
No writer update is allowed before that first Read. Subsequently the writer
may perform any unknown finite number of global $\rho$ updates on the same
register and root, including during local computation and before Return.
Every pending observer has the progress of Definition 1.4.

An invocation solver must terminate on every such history and return
$R_P(u)$ for its invocation source $u$. The target remains $R_P(u)$ after
any later source change. No source-dependent initial record, literal,
program, size bound, epoch or scheduling observation is supplied.
A retained finite tree must be acquired by paid Reads. Correctness covers
the literal exterior, every reached address, every concrete strict-prefix
blocking guard, and the entire equality/disequality partition.

**Lemma 10.2 (the first literal and a lawful one-update comparison).**
Let $P\ne\{\varepsilon\}$. Any invocation solver under Assumption 10.1
must reach a first Read after finitely many source-free steps. Its first
literal is one fixed finite word $a$, independent of the source.
If there is $u\in\mathcal T$ such that

$$
r_u(a)=r_{\rho u}(a),\qquad R_P(u)\ne R_P(\rho u),
\tag{CNA.16}
$$

then no such solver exists. More generally, the comparison excludes any
observer with this first literal that is correct on all histories with
at most one later update and terminates on quiescent inputs.

**Proof.** Prefix freedom and $P\ne\{\varepsilon\}$ imply
$\varepsilon\notin P$. Thus $R_P(\alpha)$ and $R_P(\beta)$ have distinct
concrete root labels, empty reached sets and empty partitions.
A source-blind return without any Read has the same output on these
two quiescent sources and cannot be correct on both. An infinite
source-free computation before any Read violates quiescent termination.
The finite initial computation therefore selects a fixed literal $a$.
Its materialization and every continuation are actual observer work.

Given (CNA.16), run two calls with invocation sources $u$ and $\rho u$.
Their first Reads precede every writer update and have the same reply.
Immediately after that Read, let the first call's writer perform exactly
one update and halt. Let the second call's writer perform no update.
The two registers now contain exactly $\rho u$, while observer control,
retained records and the next selected operation agree. Both calls keep
their own original register and root throughout; neither history replaces,
resets or reroots a source.

Continue both calls with the same observer transitions and no further
writer events. At each local transition the records agree; at each Read
the selected finite literal and the source agree, hence the replies agree.
This is induction on the actual events, including address construction,
rereads, output preparation and Return. The quiescent call starting at
$\rho u$ terminates, so the paired call terminates with the same output.
Their required invocation rows differ. Both histories are finite and
observer-progressing, with respective update totals one and zero.
A separate Return leaves a legal update position even if the observer
returns immediately after its first Read. $\square$

**Theorem 10.3 (four-tree separation for every first literal).**
For every finite prefix-free $P\ne\{\varepsilon\}$ the following
source-specific alternatives hold.

1. If $a\ne\varepsilon$, then $u=\alpha$ satisfies (CNA.16).
2. If $a=\varepsilon$ and $P\ne\{L,R\}$, then
   $u=\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle$ satisfies (CNA.16).
3. If $a=\varepsilon$ and $P=\{L,R\}$, every $u$ with
   $r_u(a)=r_{\rho u}(a)$ instead satisfies $R_P(u)=R_P(\rho u)$.

Consequently, among nonroot cuts, absence of a one-step separation at
the first literal holds exactly for $(P,a)=(\{L,R\},\varepsilon)$.
The separating sources in the first two cases belong to the single set

$$
\{\alpha,\beta,\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle,
                         \langle\beta,\beta\rangle\},
\tag{CNA.17}
$$

independently of cut size, cut depth or first-literal length.

**Proof.** For a nonempty literal $a$, both one-leaf trees $\alpha$ and
$\rho\alpha=\beta$ give $\mathsf{absent}$. Their complete rows differ
at the concrete root, as in Lemma 10.2. This holds even when no cut
address is reached; absence of holes does not discard the exterior.

For a root literal, both
$u=\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle$ and
$\rho u=\langle\beta,\beta\rangle$ give $\mathsf{branch}$.
Since $P$ contains neither the root nor both children, choose
$q\in\{L,R\}\setminus P$. Here the assertion that it cannot contain
both children uses prefix freedom: every nonempty word extends one
of them, so containing both would force $P=\{L,R\}$.
Neither the root nor $q$ is a cut address. Thus the exterior at $q$
is the concrete leaf $\alpha$ in $u$ and $\beta$ in $\rho u$.
No descendant cut hides this strict-prefix leaf.

For completeness, the reached sets in this comparison are exactly
$O=P\cap\{L,R\}$ on both sources. If $O$ is nonempty, all its hidden
subtrees are equal on each source, so its complete partition has one
class in both rows. If $O$ is empty, both partitions are empty.
Every deeper cut word is blocked at its first letter; whenever such
a word extends $q$, that same strict-prefix guard changes label.
If no word extends $q$, the changed leaf remains a visible exterior
leaf without a blocked descendant. In either case the literal exteriors,
hence the complete rows, differ. No partition information is dropped
from this comparison.

Finally, equality of root replies before and after one update forces
$u$ to be a branch: $\alpha$ changes to $\beta$, and $\beta$ to branch.
For $u=\langle x,y\rangle$ and $P=\{L,R\}$ both holes are reached,
and the exterior is $\langle\square_L,\square_R\rangle$.
Homomorphism and the supplied native injectivity give
$x=y$ if and only if $\rho x=\rho y$.
Thus the complete partition, including its possible disequality, is
unchanged. These algebraic facts are the suppliers [GFT] and [STC]
used in Proposition 7.3; they are not new injectivity assertions.
This proves the three alternatives. $\square$

**Theorem 10.4 (universal complete invocation-row classification).**
Under Assumption 10.1, a deterministic initially source-blind effective
invocation solver for $P$ exists if and only if

$$
P=\{\varepsilon\}\quad\text{or}\quad P=\{L,R\}.
\tag{CNA.18}
$$

This is unchanged if the allowed interference is restricted to at most
one update after the first Read. Every solver for the nonconstant positive
target $P=\{L,R\}$ must make its first Read at $\varepsilon$.
More precisely, if a first Read at a specified literal $a$ is required,
a solver exists exactly when

$$
P=\{\varepsilon\}\quad\text{or}\quad
(P=\{L,R\}\ \text{and}\ a=\varepsilon).
\tag{CNA.19}
$$

A zero-Read solver exists exactly for the root cut.

**Proof.** For any nonroot cut a solver has a fixed first literal by
Lemma 10.2. If it is nonempty, the first alternative of Theorem 10.3
contradicts correctness. If it is the root and $P\ne\{L,R\}$, the
second alternative does so. Each contradiction uses the lawful
one-versus-zero-update histories of Lemma 10.2, not an infinite
interference limit or a chosen distribution on sources. It applies
without a bound on observer storage or the length of later literals.

For sufficiency reuse the zero-Read root shortcut of Proposition 4.3
and the root-first procedure of Proposition 7.3, with its effective
paid capture and costs made explicit in §11. They terminate under
unknown finite interference and give the full invocation row, so they
also solve every at-most-one-update history. A root-cut procedure required
to Read $a$ can materialize that public finite literal, perform the Read,
discard its reply and return the constant root row. This costs an actual
Read and the delivery of $a$; it is not the zero-Read shortcut.

The first alternative of Theorem 10.3 forces the root first for
$\{L,R\}$. The two leaf rows show that every other cut has a nonconstant
target, excluding a universally correct zero-Read return. The root row
is constant. These observations prove (CNA.19) and the final claim.
$\square$

**Mathematical consequence 10.5 (finite cuts, covering cuts and guards).**
The new necessity includes the gap left by Theorem 7.2: a finite cut
covering every infinite binary ray is still impossible unless it is
one of the two cuts in (CNA.18). Coverage of rays concerns the public
address tree, not the presence of those addresses in every finite source.
For example, $\{L,RL,RR\}$ covers every ray. On the branch sources in
(CNA.17), only $L$ is reached, and the concrete guard at $R$ changes
from alpha to beta. At $\{LL,LR,RL,RR\}$ no cut address is reached
on either branch source; both visible child guards change.

The empty cut is covered by the same proof: it retains the entire source.
A singleton nonroot cut leaves at least one child unhidden. Arbitrarily
deep and mixed cuts either hide both children, forcing $\{L,R\}$,
or expose the changed child of Theorem 10.3. These are applications of
that one universal separation, without additional shape or reachability
promises. Theorems 7.1 and 7.2 retain their respective pre-first-Read,
Return-endpoint and uncovered-ray assumptions; their conclusions are
not used outside those domains.

## 11. Executable sufficiency and its paid retained data

**Definition 11.1 (the supplied root-first observer as a finite program).**
For $P=\{L,R\}$ use Proposition 7.3 with the first-leaf capture of
Definitions 3.1–3.2. First materialize $\varepsilon$, Read it and retain
its constructor $b$. This is the invocation constructor by Assumption 10.1.
If $b=\alpha$ or $b=\beta$, materialize and save the complete row of
that one-leaf tree, with both cut addresses blocked at the root and an
empty partition, and Return it. No capture is needed in this case.

If $b=\mathsf{branch}$, retain that fact and start the full first-leaf
capture from its own empty record and root stack. In particular, its
first root Read is paid again; the earlier invocation Read is not silently
installed as part of a validated traversal. Each stack literal, constructor
reply, guard, failed-attempt decision and accepted finite tree is an
actual retained finite record. On acceptance, construct $R_P(T)$ from
the validated tree $T$, comparing its two full child subtrees structurally.
Return that row after finite local output preparation. No step asks for
an update count, a current epoch, subtree equality at the port, or a signal
that interference has ended.

The complete-row safety is precisely the supplied Proposition 7.3:
if the invocation tree is $\langle x,y\rangle$, then any accepted tree
is $T=\langle\rho^k x,\rho^k y\rangle$ for one actual $k\ge0$.
Theorem 3.3 supplies that equality at its validation Read; homomorphism
and injectivity from [GFT] and [STC] give

$$
x=y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \rho^k x=\rho^k y.
\tag{CNA.20}
$$

The observer computes the right side from its acquired tree; $k$ is only
a semantic witness. Thus it returns the original two-hole exterior and
its full partition, whether the children are equal or unequal.
The branch case cannot become a leaf. In the leaf case the saved original
row stays the invocation target even if the source later branches.
Updates after validation or during Return preparation cannot change that
target. This is an application of the existing positive construction,
not another capture primitive or a newly claimed positive theorem.

The finite-interference termination supplier is Theorem 5.2. Every attempt
finishes; every failed validation charges a distinct actual update; the
unknown finite update count bounds the number of failures. The observer
does not wait for a notification or choose an iteration bound from that
count. The initial Read and the final finite row computation add only
finite observer work, so the same progress premise suffices.

**Mathematical citation 11.2 (resource specialization of §§3–5).**
Use the analytical $B,N_B,H_B,A_B,L,M,w_B$ of Definition 5.1 and
Theorem 5.4, measured from the invocation source of this call.
They are not supplied to the observer. On the branch case of
Definition 11.1, $M=2$ and $L=4$. If capture completes $J$ attempts,
with the actual $N_j,A_j,d_j$ of Definition 5.1, the exact Read and
submitted-letter bills are

$$
\operatorname{Reads}=1+\sum_{j=1}^{J}(N_j+1),\qquad
\operatorname{Letters}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}(A_j+d_j),\qquad J\le B+1.
\tag{CNA.21}
$$

The leading Read is the invocation root Read, whose literal has length
zero. All capture root rereads and leaf validations occur in the sum.
Theorem 5.3 therefore gives bounds
$1+(B+1)(N_B+1)$ and $(B+1)(A_B+H_B)$ respectively.
An update between the invocation root Read and the start of capture
is included in $B$ even if no failed attempt charges it.
Every later visited address still belongs to $\operatorname{Pos}(\rho^B u)$,
so these bounds remain valid without restarting their analytical origin.

Theorem 5.4, with the extra retained constructor and initial operation,
gives logical work
$O((B+1)(N_B+A_B+H_B+1)+1)$ and peak storage
$O(N_B+A_B+1)$ characters and bounded-index cells.
These bounds include literal construction and delivery, all control,
retries and discards, traversal and comparison stacks, a retained accepted
full tree and certificate, optional certificate checking, and complete-row
output materialization. The two-child partition includes both its true
and false entries. Conservative bit bounds multiply these expressions
by $O(w_B)$ as in Theorem 5.4. Dynamically represented indices are paid;
no source-dependent word size is advice.

The leaf case costs one Read, zero submitted letters and constant work
and storage for these fixed cuts. The root shortcut costs no Read and
constant row-output work and storage. Validated trees and certificates
kept after the call continue to occupy their actual storage. Source-register
space and the writer's global substitution work remain separate costs.
These are upper bounds for this supplied implementation, with no claim
of sharp workspace or optimal bit work.

## 12. Interference promises and information boundaries

**Proposition 12.1 (a separate exactly-one-update variation).**
Consider a variation in which every observer must reach a first Read
after finite source-free work before it may Return. Require termination
under observer progress when exactly one writer update occurs after that
Read while the call is pending and the writer then halts. Require invocation
correctness on histories with exactly one such update before the separate
Return. Under this variation the classification (CNA.18), and the forced
root first for its nonconstant positive case, still hold.

**Proof.** Take the fixed first literal $a$ and a separating $u$ from
Theorem 10.3. Run the call from $u$, perform its first Read, immediately
apply one update, and halt the writer. By the stated termination premise
it has a finite observer continuation and Return. Starting instead from
$\rho u$, perform the same first Read and the same continuation with no
updates up through its last Read and all required output preparation.
The two observer records agree at each step: the first replies agree,
and the source used by every subsequent Read is $\rho u$.

Immediately before the second call's separate Return, apply its sole
update. This takes its register to $\rho^2u$ but supplies no observer
input. Return still has the same prepared output. Both complete histories
now have exactly one update after their first Read and before Return,
while their invocation rows differ. This excludes every negative case
and every nonroot first literal for $\{L,R\}$.

Definition 11.1 already works with finite interference. The root shortcut
can perform one root Read before returning its constant row to satisfy
the additional first-Read requirement. These give sufficiency. $\square$

This variation specifies finite first-Read progress expressly: restricting
correctness only to completed exactly-one histories must not give a
zero-Read program an empty correctness domain when pre-first updates
are forbidden. The main Assumption 10.1 has no such vacuity because
zero-update histories are included. The comparison in Lemma 10.2 uses
one versus zero updates; Proposition 12.1 instead needs the last-Read to
Return position to put one update in each history. A fused Read/Return
port removes that position and is outside Proposition 12.1.

A known zero-update budget is another model: stationary full traversal
recovers every $R_P(u)$, including the empty cut, by the suppliers of §2.
A positive upper bound is not an assertion that an update occurred.
Nor does the assertion that exactly one update will occur before Return
tell the observer when it has been spent. An authentic already-spent count
and a later acquired whole tree would supply different data; inverse
transport on its actual image is the [STC] supplier, not a free entry
snapshot. No such input is used in Theorem 10.4.

**Definition 12.2 (geometry, retained observations and the scope of recovery).**
A public cut can cover every infinite ray while a finite source ends
strictly before some of its addresses. The concrete leaf at such a stop
is part of the observed boundary, not a hidden hole. Theorem 10.3 detects
exactly this distinction at depth one: hiding both root children removes
all changing branch-case exterior labels; any other nonroot cut exposes
at least one possible changing child leaf. A first root observation also
identifies the entire invocation source when it is a leaf. These two
facts, together with the invariant hidden-child partition, explain the
two positive cuts without identifying ray coverage with source reachability.

On a restricted domain where a covering cut's every address is promised
present at invocation, the exterior is its fixed cut skeleton and all
hidden subtrees advance by the same injective $\rho^k$. The same transport
calculation (CNA.20) makes the complete row constant along that trajectory,
so the supplied coherent capture recovers the invocation row there.
This is a consequence of the existing capture and transport suppliers,
with an additional source-family promise. It excludes the early-blocking
sources in (CNA.17) for every deeper covering cut and therefore cannot
supply universal recovery under Assumption 10.1. For incomplete cuts,
even that reachability promise need not fix the exterior outside the cut.

The memory used by Definition 11.1 consists of the actually acquired root
constructor and later finite capture records. It determines an earlier
relational boundary for $\{L,R\}$ without determining how many intervening
updates occurred. On a fixed invocation branch that row is constant for
all update counts. This differs from the relative clock recovered from
two retained full trees in Theorem 8.3, and from the overlap criterion
for its strictly row-only interface. A retained full tree is a larger
input than its row and must be charged and declared separately.

The negative histories of Lemma 10.2 have identical complete observer
records after their first Read and identical future source values.
Thus retaining every later tree, certificate and local computation cannot
repair their different entry rows. This impossibility already permits
unbounded finite memory. It is stronger than an ambiguity created only
by a later deliberate row-only retention cut, and says nothing about
sharp minimum workspace on the positive cases.

## 13. Mathematical antecedents and the next recovery question

**Mathematical citation 13.1 (snapshot and prefix-tree correspondence).**
The history and linearization definitions of Herlihy and Wing cited in
§9 distinguish an invocation, a response and a permitted instant between
them. Lemma 10.2 uses equality of an observer's local history under two
actual executions. It does not infer equality of their invocation states
from a linearizable later scan. Proposition 12.1 additionally uses the
separate response event. These are source-specific indistinguishability
arguments, not new definitions of linearizability.

Afek, Attiya, Dolev, Gafni, Merritt and Shavit's atomic snapshot construction,
§§2–3 and §3.1 of the authors' manuscript cited in §9, makes unsuccessful
double collects reveal movement through sequence fields; its wait-free
completion can borrow a view stored by a writer. Here a raw reply has only
four possible constructor values, and the writer only applies $\rho$.
The positive call uses the existing native leaf-validation certificate
and its finite-interference progress theorem. The snapshot theorem
supplies neither an invocation-time scan nor those missing register fields.
The literature's arbitrary-writer-progress quantifier is also stronger
than the finite-total-interference termination used in Theorem 10.4.

Shannon, *A Mathematical Theory of Communication*, §9's second coding
construction and §10's binary example
([text, pp. 17–18](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/others/shannon/entropy/entropy.pdf)),
uses finite binary descriptions whose earlier codewords differ within
their own lengths from later ones; the recursive subdivision description
terminates at single-message groups. This gives the classical prefix-tree
interpretation relevant here. Its explicit code $\{0,10,110,111\}$ becomes
$P=\{L,RL,RRL,RRR\}$ under $0\mapsto L$, $1\mapsto R$.
These are terminal addresses of a finite full binary code tree and cover
every infinite ray. On $\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle$, however, the native
source stops at $R$ before the last three codewords, so the same cut has
the changing guard of Theorem 10.3. The code tree and the actual source
are different objects. Shannon's source probabilities and mean coding
lengths give neither a native-source reachability promise nor a concurrent
Read protocol. Only the prefix relation and finite code-tree interpretation
are used; no coding theorem is republished as a new cut-recovery result.

**Mathematical citation 13.2 (repository derivation and its limits).**
The classification and the uniform four-tree first-literal separation
are repo-derived additions. Their necessity is not the uncovered-ray
clock of Theorem 7.2: it applies also to complete deep prefix cuts, using
an exposed early leaf and a lawful merge of two histories. The prescribed
first-literal characterization and the exactly-one comparison retain
all source-blind control, full-row and endpoint conditions stated above.
The positive procedure, native injectivity and homomorphism, complete-row
encoding and finite-interference capture retain their suppliers
Propositions 4.3 and 7.3, Theorems 3.3 and 5.2–5.4, [GFT], [STC] and
[ACPD], including its *literalRecord* and *canonicalRow_record_iff*.

The public *transfer* of [NativeSerialDepth][NSD] transfers an actual
*NativeSerialMachine.Run* using truth of every recorded Read at its
recorded controller epoch. Its updates are that same controller's
*applyRho* actions. The private replay arguments and public
*parity_read_lower_bound* and *literal_parity_lower_bounds* of
[SparseLiteralParityReadLowerBound][SPR] likewise concern serial Runs
and their installed source families. They supply no unrecorded writer
transition, concurrent workspace optimum or entry-cut classification.
The event induction in Lemma 10.2 explicitly handles the extra writer;
matching notation alone would not justify invoking a serial Run theorem.

The mathematical statements here are prose consequences in the declared
native model. Literature attribution is limited to the stated primary
results and their ports and quantifiers; no worldwide originality or
new kernel certificate is asserted. Spatial cuts, relative time and
memory refer to these finite relational objects, not physical spacetime.

**Definition 13.3 (remaining mutual-recovery problem).**
Theorem 10.4 settles universal invocation-row acquisition in Assumption
10.1. The broader target of a smallest relational structure expressing
cuts, internal relative time, boundaries and memory remains separate.
A next question is to characterize which paid finite initial observation
records, beyond the one protected Read, suffice to recover a deeper
invocation cut when subsequent interference is unobserved. Such a result
must specify how that initial record is actually obtained and protected,
retain concrete early guards and the entire hidden partition, and charge
its literal delivery and storage. It cannot assume an entry snapshot
or replace the record by semantic ancestor witnesses.

Sharp acquisition space, exact finite-transcript languages, stronger
interference progress, authenticated designated origins and richer retained
interfaces remain the distinct questions in Definition 9.3. The present
classification supplies no minimal workspace theorem, physical duration,
full hidden event history or completion of that broader program.
