# QUANTUM ORIGIN: EXTERIOR VOLUME NETWORK ACTIVATION XV

## 166. A five-dimensional volume tensor supplying a ten-dimensional regular pencil

**Assumption 166.1 (The whole actual finite cone background).** Fix
$H=\mathbb C^{10}$ with its actual orthonormal computational basis. Take
$V=\operatorname{Herm}_{10}(\mathbb C)$ as a real vector space and
$W_N=V^{\otimes_{\mathbb R}N}\cong\operatorname{Herm}_{10^N}(\mathbb C)$
in the same actual tensor coordinates. For every finite $N\geq1$, let
$C_N\subset W_N$ be a closed, pointed, generating convex cone, with
$u_N=\operatorname{Tr}=u_1^{\otimes N}$ strictly positive on its nonzero
elements. Require

$$
C_1=\operatorname{PSD}_{10},\qquad C_2=\operatorname{PSD}_{100}.
\tag{166.1}
$$

Use the entire original state base $K_N=\{x\in C_N:u_N(x)=1\}$, all its
extreme points $P_N$, the complete positive dual $C_N^*$, the complete
proper effect interval $\mathcal E_N=[0,u_N]_{C_N^*}$, and every finite
unit-decomposition menu. For every finite actual split $a+b$, require

$$
C_a\otimes_{\min}C_b\subseteq C_{a+b}
 \subseteq C_a\otimes_{\max}C_b.
\tag{166.2}
$$

The minimal cone is the closed convex cone generated by positive simple
tensors. The maximal cone is the intersection of the nonnegative
half-spaces of every $f\otimes g$ with $f\in C_a^*$ and $g\in C_b^*$.
Every actual position permutation and its inverse is positive. Canonical
reassociation uses the same actual tensor space and the same cone;
noncontiguous splits are transported by those permutations. Pairings and
units multiply on simple tensors. Set $W_0=\mathbb R$, $C_0=\mathbb R_+$,
$u_0=\mathrm{id}$.
These are the whole hypotheses of [XIII, Definitions 164.1 and
165.1][XIII], specialized to $n=10$.

A real linear map $F:W_m\to W_k$ is **positive with all original
spectators** when

$$
(\mathrm{id}_{W_s}\otimes F)C_{s+m}\subseteq C_{s+k}
\qquad(s\geq0),
\tag{166.3}
$$

including permuted positions. The original single-leg congruences,
contraction budgets, two-leg effects, and whole-input slicing are the
provided interfaces of XIII164.3 and XIII165.3. No higher cone equality,
positivity of an arbitrary two-body CP map, grouped general linear
operation, or classification of higher original pure states is part of
this assumption. A vector dyad below has actual matrix rank one; this
does not presuppose that it is an extreme point of an unknown $K_N$.

**Definition 166.2 (Volume tensor and linear conventions).** Let
$U=\mathbb C^5$ with basis $e_0,\ldots,e_4$, embedded into the first five
coordinates of the first actual $H$. Write $e_{ij}=e_i\wedge e_j$ and
identify $B=\Lambda^2U$ with each of the other two actual copies of $H$
by the ordered orthonormal basis

$$
\mathcal B=(01,02,03,04,12,13,14,23,24,34).
\tag{166.4}
$$

Let $\omega\in\Lambda^5U^*$ satisfy
$\omega(e_0\wedge\cdots\wedge e_4)=1$. Define $\epsilon_{aijkl}$ by this
volume form, with value zero on repeated indices, and set

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_a(p,q)&=\epsilon_{aijkl}
 &&\bigl(p=(i<j),\ q=(k<l)\bigr),\\
w&=\sum_{a=0}^4\sum_{p,q\in\mathcal B}
       A_a(p,q)|a\rangle|p\rangle|q\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\tag{166.5}
$$

Thus $w$ is the coefficient tensor of the trilinear volume pairing
$(u,b,c)\mapsto\omega(u\wedge b\wedge c)$ in the specified bases.
The volume pairing is complex trilinear, not Hermitian.
A binary restriction of a chosen control leg means an arbitrary complex
linear map from that actual $H$ to the actual corner
$\operatorname{span}\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}\subset H$. Its two rows are
arbitrary linear bras; complex conjugation is not inserted in their
coefficients.

For matrices on an actual $H$, retain XIII164.2's convention

$$
|M\rangle\!\rangle
 =\sum_{t=0}^9|t\rangle_{\rm ref}\otimes M|t\rangle_{\rm out},
\qquad
(R\otimes L)|M\rangle\!\rangle=|LMR^{\mathsf T}\rangle\!\rangle.
\tag{166.6}
$$

**Proposition 166.3 (All complex single-copy binary restrictions miss
full data support).** The matrices in (166.5) are real symmetric and obey

$$
\operatorname{rank}A_a=6,\qquad
\sum_{a=0}^4 A_a^2=3I_{10},\qquad
\|w\|^2=30.
\tag{166.7}
$$

The one-leg flattening ranks of $w$, in order $(U,B,B)$, are $(5,10,10)$.
For every choice of control leg and every complex binary restriction,
neither remaining data leg has rank ten: when the $U$ leg is control,
both data ranks are at most nine; when a $B$ leg is control, the data
ranks are at most five and nine. These bounds persist under arbitrary
single-copy local filters and leg permutations. Consequently no such
single-copy restriction and filtering supplies XIII165.2 with full
ten-dimensional support on either data leg, or XIII164.2's regular
binary pencil.

Proof. Interchanging two degree-two factors has sign $+1$, so $A_a$ is
symmetric. Its nonzero entries above the diagonal are exactly the
following signed pairs of basis labels; the symmetric entries have the
same signs and every other entry is zero:

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
a&\multicolumn{3}{c}{(p,q;\ A_a(p,q))}\\ \hline
0&(12,34;+1)&(13,24;-1)&(14,23;+1)\\
1&(02,34;-1)&(03,24;+1)&(04,23;-1)\\
2&(01,34;+1)&(03,14;-1)&(04,13;+1)\\
3&(01,24;-1)&(02,14;+1)&(04,12;-1)\\
4&(01,23;+1)&(02,13;-1)&(03,12;+1)
\end{array}
\tag{166.8}
$$

Each $A_a$ exchanges the three complementary pairs in
$\{0,1,2,3,4\}\setminus\{a\}$, with signs. Its square is the diagonal
projection onto the six pairs not containing $a$. Each pair omits exactly
three values of $a$, proving the first two claims of (166.7). The five
matrices have disjoint sets of nonzero entries, and each has Hilbert--Schmidt
norm squared six. They are therefore linearly independent and give
$\|w\|^2=30$ and first flattening rank five. On either $B$ leg the
flattening Gram matrix is $\sum_a A_a^2=3I_{10}$, proving rank ten.

First choose the $U$ leg as control. For $u\in U$ write
$A(u)=\sum_a u_aA_a$. Nondegeneracy of the volume pairing between
$\Lambda^2U$ and $\Lambda^3U$ gives

$$
A(u)\eta=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u\wedge\eta=0.
\tag{166.9}
$$

For $u\ne0$, split $U=\mathbb Cu\oplus H_u$. The map
$u\wedge-:\Lambda^2H_u\to\Lambda^3U$ is injective, and hence
$\ker A(u)=u\wedge U$, of dimension four. If $u,v$ are independent,
a basis beginning with $u,v$ shows

$$
\ker A(u)\cap\ker A(v)=\mathbb C(u\wedge v).
\tag{166.10}
$$

Thus the stacked pair has rank nine. The horizontal pair has the same
rank, since the slices are symmetric. Dependent nonzero pairs have rank
six, and the zero pair has rank zero. This proves both data bounds for
every complex pair of control rows.

Now choose one $B$ leg as control. Its arbitrary rows correspond, by
the fixed coordinate duality, to $b,c\in\Lambda^2U$. The remaining
$U$ data leg has support at most five. On the other $B$ data leg its
joint two-slice flattening, after an invertible volume identification
$\Lambda^4U\cong U^*$ in each output block, is

$$
T_{b,c}:\Lambda^2U\longrightarrow\Lambda^4U\oplus\Lambda^4U,
\qquad \eta\longmapsto(b\wedge\eta,c\wedge\eta).
\tag{166.11}
$$

Both spaces have dimension ten. We prove that this map is singular
for every complex $b,c$, including degenerate pairs.

Here the odd alternating Pfaffian-kernel construction is used only as
an intermediate classical identity. The degree-$k$ volume construction
for a pencil in dimension $2k+1$ appears in [Kryński--Zelenko, §1.2,
(1.3)--(1.5) and Lemma 1.1][KZ]; alternating pencil canonical forms
are recalled in [Dmytryshyn--Dopico, Theorem 2.3, p.5][DD], with
attribution there to Thompson. The following exterior calculation
establishes the needed identity over $\mathbb C$ without a genericity
assumption or a choice of canonical form.

Define $\star:\Lambda^4U\to U^*$ by
$\star\xi(x)=\omega(\xi\wedge x)$. For
$d=\sum_{i<j}d_{ij}e_{ij}$ let $d^\#:U^*\to U$ be the alternating map

$$
d^\#\alpha=\sum_{i<j}d_{ij}
 (\alpha(e_j)e_i-\alpha(e_i)e_j)=-\iota_\alpha d.
\tag{166.12}
$$

Since $d^3=0$ in dimension five, contraction by any covector gives
$0=\iota_\alpha(d^3)=3(\iota_\alpha d)\wedge d^2$.
It follows that $\star(d^2)(d^\#\alpha)=0$ for every $\alpha$.
Skewness of $d^\#$ then implies

$$
d^\#\star(d^2)=0.
\tag{166.13}
$$

In coordinates, the entries of $\star(d^2)/2$ are the signed
four-by-four principal Pfaffians; the factor two comes from the two
orders of the degree-two factors. Set

$$
p=\star(b^2),\qquad q=2\star(b\wedge c),\qquad r=\star(c^2).
\tag{166.14}
$$

Thus $\star((b+\lambda c)^2)=p+\lambda q+\lambda^2r$.
Taking coefficients of (166.13), and suppressing the symbol $\#$ on
the maps only, gives precisely

$$
bp=0,\qquad bq+cp=0,\qquad br+cq=0,\qquad cr=0.
\tag{166.15}
$$

Suppose first that $p,q,r$ are independent. Let
$Q=\operatorname{span}\{p,q,r\}\subset U^*$.
This is a common isotropic three-space for the alternating forms $b,c$
on $U^*$. Indeed, pairings with $p$ vanish for $b$, pairings with $r$
vanish for $c$, and the two remaining off-diagonal pairings are

$$
q^{\mathsf T}br=-q^{\mathsf T}cq=0,\qquad
q^{\mathsf T}cp=-q^{\mathsf T}bq=0.
\tag{166.16}
$$

Diagonal pairings vanish by skewness. Put
$P=\operatorname{ann}Q\subset U$, so $\dim P=2$.
Extend a basis of $P$ to one of $U$. The vanishing of $b,c$ on
$Q\times Q$ says that all their coefficients using two of the three
complementary basis directions vanish. Equivalently,
$b,c\in P\wedge U$. For any nonzero
$\eta\in\Lambda^2P$ this gives $b\wedge\eta=c\wedge\eta=0$.
So $T_{b,c}$ is singular in this case.

To include every other pair, use the explicit pair

$$
b_0=e_{01}+e_{23},\qquad c_0=e_{12}+e_{34}.
\tag{166.17}
$$

Its three covectors (166.14) are
$2e_4^*,2e_2^*,2e_0^*$ respectively. For arbitrary fixed $b,c$ replace
them by $b(t)=b+tb_0$, $c(t)=c+tc_0$. Each of $p(t),q(t),r(t)$ has
degree at most two. The minor with rows in the order $(4,2,0)$ and
columns $(p(t),q(t),r(t))$ has leading coefficient $8t^6$.
It is a nonzero polynomial, so these covectors are independent for
all but finitely many $t\in\mathbb C$. Consequently
$\det T_{b(t),c(t)}=0$ at infinitely many $t$.
This determinant is itself a polynomial in $t$, hence is identically
zero. At $t=0$ it gives $\det T_{b,c}=0$. Therefore (166.11) always
has rank at most nine. Symmetry between the two $B$ factors proves
the bound for either of them as control.

Finally, a product of local linear filters cannot increase a one-leg
flattening rank. A control filter followed by a binary restriction is
another arbitrary pair of complex rows; data filters commute with that
restriction. This covers singular as well as invertible local filters.
More generally, if a filtered tensor merely has two-dimensional control
support, choose a binary map injective on that support. This preserves
its data ranks and reduces it to the case already proved. Permutations
only relabel the three cases. In particular, every binary output has
both data ranks below ten. This contradicts XIII165.2's required full
data rank, and a regular pencil would require both data ranks ten.
The rank interpretation is the usual matrix-pencil dictionary of
[Chitambar--Miller--Shi, §3, (3)--(6) and Lemma 2][CMS]. $\square$

**Proposition 166.5 (The exact volume triangle).** Put
$D_a(i,j)=L_i^{\mathsf T}A_aR_j$. With indices $0,\ldots,4$ on the
five-by-five matrix units, these matrices are

$$
D_0=I_5,\qquad D_1=0,\qquad
D_2=E_{00},\qquad D_3=E_{01},\qquad D_4=E_{02}.
\tag{166.21}
$$

The three contractions of Definition 166.4, applied to the ket

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal S&=\sum_{a=0}^4|S_a\rangle\!\rangle|a\rangle,\\
S_a&=\sum_{i,j=0}^4D_a(i,j)A_iA_j,\\
(S_0,S_1,S_2,S_3,S_4)
   &=(3I_{10},0,A_0^2,A_0A_1,A_0A_2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{166.22}
$$

Their ranks are $(10,0,6,3,3)$, their squared Hilbert--Schmidt norms
are $(90,0,6,3,3)$, and $\|\mathcal S\|^2=102$.

Proof. Write $\widetilde L_0=e_{12}$ and
$\widetilde L_i=L_i$ for $i>0$. The five ordered pairs in (166.19)
give

$$
\widetilde L_i\wedge R_j=\delta_{ij}e_{1234}.
\tag{166.23}
$$

Indeed, on the diagonal their wedges are respectively
$e_{12}\wedge e_{34}$, $-e_{13}\wedge e_{24}$,
$e_{14}\wedge e_{23}$, $e_{23}\wedge e_{14}$,
and $-e_{24}\wedge e_{13}$, all equal to $e_{1234}$.
Off the diagonal the factors share a basis index, so the wedge is zero.
The extra $e_{01}$ in $L_0$ has wedges with $R_0,\ldots,R_4$ equal to

$$
(e_{0134},-e_{0124},e_{0123},0,0).
\tag{166.24}
$$

Evaluating $\omega(e_a\wedge-)$ gives the single formula

$$
D_a(i,j)=\delta_{a0}\delta_{ij}
 +\delta_{i0}
  (\delta_{a2}\delta_{j0}+\delta_{a3}\delta_{j1}
                         +\delta_{a4}\delta_{j2}),
\tag{166.25}
$$

which proves all entries of (166.21), including the zeros and signs.

For fixed $a,p_1,q_2$, the coefficient left by the contractions is

 A_i(p_1,p_2)A_j(p_2,q_2)A_a(p_3,q_3)

\end{aligned}
\tag{166.26}

Because $q_2$ is the reference index and $p_1$ the output index,

If the data order were $(p_1,q_2)$, the matrix would instead be

Write $P_0=A_0^2$. By (166.8), it is the orthogonal projection onto

spectators).** Under Assumption 166.1, suppose the additional actual

\frac{\|r\|^2}{D}
 =\frac{32}{24300000}
 =\frac4{3037500}>0.
\tag{166.30}

Every step is positive with all original finite spectators and
nonincreasing in trace on the whole original input cone, including

\tag{166.31}

By $C_2=\operatorname{PSD}_{100}$ it belongs to the complete original

The minimal bounds in (166.2) put $\sigma^{\otimes3}$ in $C_9$.

The exact dyad is, by Proposition 166.5,

\frac{\mathcal S\mathcal S^*}{30^3\cdot10\cdot6\cdot5}

Its trace is
$102/8100000=17/1350000$. No assumption about extremality of

**Theorem 166.7 (Exact equivalence with the whole finite cone
hierarchy).** Under the whole Assumption 166.1, the following are
equivalent:

1. The explicit volume resource of Definition 166.2 satisfies
   $ww^*\in C_3$.
2. Some actual rank-one dyad in $C_3$ contains a regular binary
   pencil in the sense of XIII164.2.
3. For every finite $N\geq1$,
   $C_N=\operatorname{PSD}_{10^N}$ in the original actual coordinates.

Proof. Theorem 166.6 supplies
$r=|I_{10}\rangle\!\rangle|0\rangle
+|M\rangle\!\rangle|1\rangle$ with $M=P_0/3$ and $rr^*\in C_3$.
The two matrices are linearly independent: $P_0$ is nonzero of rank
six, whereas $I_{10}$ has rank ten. Thus $M$ is non-scalar and this
is precisely the regular binary resource required in XIII164.11.
This proves 1 implies 2. Apply that theorem, with its entire
background specialized to $n=10$, to obtain 2 implies 3. Its control
resources, finite polynomial calculus, exact finite unitary words,
and two-sided cone conclusion are reused as provided; the present
supplier does not assume any of them before constructing $r$.
Finally 3 at $N=3$ contains the actual PSD dyad $ww^*$, proving
3 implies 1. $\square$

**Proposition 166.8 (Given resources and existence conditions).**
The volume tensor $w$ cannot be converted by single-copy product
linear filters, even after permuting legs, into any binary tensor
with a full ten-dimensional data leg. In particular it cannot be
converted in this way into the $r$ of Theorem 166.6. Conversely,
$r$ cannot be converted by such filters into $w$. Nevertheless,
under Assumption 166.1, availability of $ww^*$, existence of a
XIII165.2 resource, and existence of a XIII164.2 resource are
equivalent conditions on the cone hierarchy.

Proof. The first claim is Proposition 166.3, including its argument
for a tensor whose control support becomes two only after data
filtering. The tensor $r$ has ranks $(10,10,2)$, since its identity
slice has full rank and its two slices are independent. Thus it
falls under that obstruction. In the reverse direction some leg of
every filtered or permuted $r$ has rank at most two, whereas every
leg of $w$ has rank at least five. The usual restriction relation is
the product-linear-map relation of [Christandl--Vrana--Zuiddam,
Definition 1.1.5][CVZ]. No assertion of equivalence of the given
tensors follows from an existence statement about the hierarchy.

For the last claim, Theorem 166.7 equates the explicit volume-resource
condition with XIII164.2's existence condition, and XIII165.9 equates
the latter with existence of its one-sided full-support binary
resource, on exactly the same background. This proves equivalence,
not logical independence of those existence conditions. The triangle
is a sufficient multi-copy supplier beyond the single-copy
restrictions just excluded; its use of three copies does not assert
minimality of that copy number. Both $w$ and $r$ already have all
three flattening ranks at least two. The supplied property is a regular
binary pencil on the original data legs. Mixed-state GME activation
from biseparable inputs, as defined in [Yamasaki et al., Abstract and
§1, p.1][YM], concerns a different property. $\square$

**Corollary 166.9 (The original state, effect, probability, tensor,

\mathcal E_N&=\{E:0\preceq E\preceq I_{d_N}\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{166.38}
$$

Every finite original menu is exactly a finite POVM
$E_a\succeq0$, $\sum_aE_a=I_{d_N}$, with

$$

\tag{166.39}
$$

Original composition, position permutation, and discard are the
ordinary tensor product, tensor-factor permutation, and partial
trace. For every finite $m,k$, the real linear maps
$F:W_m\to W_k$ positive with all original spectators are precisely
the usual completely positive maps,

$$
F(x)=\sum_\nu K_\nu xK_\nu^*,\qquad
K_\nu:\mathbb C^{10^m}\longrightarrow\mathbb C^{10^k}.
\tag{166.40}
$$

The original whole-input trace-nonincreasing and trace-preserving
conditions are respectively

$$
\sum_\nu K_\nu^*K_\nu\preceq I_{10^m},\qquad
\sum_\nu K_\nu^*K_\nu=I_{10^m}.
\tag{166.41}
$$

These conclusions describe mathematical states, effects, probabilities,
composites, and maps. They specify no event-saturation condition,
measurement-update choice, physical realization, physical clock,
continuous time law, or Hamiltonian.

Proof. Theorem 166.7 gives every whole-cone equality in the same
coordinates, so XIII164.12 applies directly, including its complete
dual and rectangular CP conclusions and the stated operational
boundaries. This uses neither a generated state subcone in place of
$K_N$ nor selected probes in place of the whole effect interval.
In particular the Kraus budget is the whole-input condition (166.41),
not a condition on the total trace of a Choi matrix. $\square$

**Definition 166.10 (Scope of the general supplier problem).** In
the background of XIII164.1 for an arbitrary fixed $n>2$, the
**general GME supplier problem** asks whether availability of any
nonzero actual three-leg dyad whose three flattening ranks are at
least two implies $C_N=\operatorname{PSD}_{n^N}$ for every finite
$N$. Theorem 166.7 settles the specified volume resource at $n=10$;
this general implication remains open here. It does not follow merely
from the existence of simultaneous two-dimensional restrictions in
[Christandl--Gesmundo--Zuiddam, Proposition 3.1(i),(ii)][CGZ], which
retain nontrivial flattenings without providing the ten-dimensional
identity slice (166.22). The background and the additional resource
condition $ww^*\in C_3$ remain distinct hypotheses; the theorem
makes no assertion that the background alone supplies $w$, or that
the full background has fewer independent assumptions.
