## 2. Kernel factorization with the complete signed gaps

**Proposition 2.1 (pure-support restriction and positive affine factor).**
Under Assumptions 1.1 and 1.3, suppose (RB.6) holds. Every pure
contact is in
$Y=\{x\in S^1:u\le0\ \text{or}\ f(x)\le0\}$.
There is a strictly positive affine circle function $L_*(x)$ such that

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_0(x)&:=P(x)+\ell(x)+\lambda u
       =(1-x_*\cdot x)L_*(x),\\
S_-(x)&:=P(x)+\ell(x)+\lambda u-\rho c(x)(w\cdot x)^2\\
 &=(1-x_*\cdot x)
       [L_*(x)-\rho c(x)(1+x_*\cdot x)],\\
L_*(x)-\rho c(x)(1+x_*\cdot x)&\ge0\quad(x\in S^1),\\
L_*(x_*)&\ge2\rho c(x_*)>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{RB.7}
$$

The function $S_-$ is the globally nonnegative smooth negative-height,
negative-report saturated signed gap. Weakly zero saturated height at
$x_*$ is allowed.

Proof. Let $x$ be a positive-height pure contact and choose its report
sign $\epsilon=\operatorname{sgn}u$, which is defined by FC20.1.
The pure positive-height test vanishes there. Its saturated version
is nonnegative by (RB.3), so $x^TR_+x\ge0$. Substitution of (RB.6)
gives $-2tf(x)-\rho(w\cdot x)^2\ge0$, hence $f(x)\le0$.
Every other pure contact has strictly negative height by FC20.1;
FC11.1 then gives $u<0$. This proves the full pure inventory is
in $Y$, without restricting any saturated contact.

The globally nonnegative degree-two trigonometric polynomial $F_0$
vanishes at $x_*$ by the inherited negative pure contact and its
left report sign. Its angular derivative vanishes there as well,
since a smooth nonnegative function has zero derivative at a zero.
Rotate coordinates so $x_*=(1,0)$, and write

$$
F_0(\theta)=A_0+A_1\cos\theta+B_1\sin\theta+
                         A_2\cos2\theta+B_2\sin2\theta.
\tag{RB.8}
$$

Value and derivative at zero give $A_1=-A_0-A_2$ and
$B_1=-2B_2$. Therefore

$$
F_0(\theta)=(1-\cos\theta)
 [(A_0-A_2)-2A_2\cos\theta-2B_2\sin\theta].
\tag{RB.9}
$$

Rotating back gives the first factorization of (RB.7), with affine
$L_*$. For $x\ne x_*$ its first factor is positive, so $L_*\ge0$
there. Continuity gives the same inequality at $x_*$.

The kernel relation makes $w$ perpendicular to the unit vector $x_*$.
In two dimensions this gives
$(w\cdot x)^2=1-(x_*\cdot x)^2$ on $S^1$. Substituting in the
globally nonnegative saturated test proves the second factorization.
Dividing away from $x_*$ and then taking the limit to $x_*$ proves
the bracket inequality everywhere and its strict lower bound for
$L_*(x_*)$.

If $L_*(x)=0$ elsewhere, then $F_0(x)=0$. Pure feasibility writes
this as a sum of nonnegative terms:

$$
0=F_0(x)=
[P-|\ell|-\lambda|u|]+[|\ell|+\ell]+\lambda[|u|+u].
\tag{RB.10}
$$

It forces a pure contact with $u\le0$, $\ell\le0$. The axis and
pure zero-height exclusions in FC20.1 make both inequalities strict.
This would be a second directed negative pure contact, contradicting
FC34.3. Thus $L_*>0$ everywhere. This conclusion uses the inherited
uniqueness, not a positive-contact inventory cap. $\square$

## 3. Full-moment snapping and a uniform contrast deficit

**Proposition 3.1 (contrast gap on every hypothetical rank-one face).**
Under the hypotheses of Proposition 2.1 every common multiplier
optimizer, including each endpoint representation of it, satisfies

$$
K<\sqrt\alpha-\frac1{24000}.
\tag{RB.11}
$$

Proof. Use the endpoint projection FC.110. It preserves every
$\Gamma$ coordinate and $K$. For an optimizer, FC Theorems 3.3
and 4.2 put each strict fraction in its compatible contact group,
so this projection also preserves $J_t$. In particular both columns
of every paired group remain separately available. No reversal is
merged across opposite height signs and no singleton is omitted.
Write the endpoint law as $\pi_{\rm end}=\rho_0\otimes\delta_0+
\rho_1\otimes\delta_1$, and set $S=\rho_1(S^1)$. From the same
failure moments and (RB.5),

$$
\frac{\mu}{t}=\int c\,d\rho_1>\frac{S}{25},\qquad
S<\frac{300}{t}\le\frac1{100}.
\tag{RB.12}
$$

The saturated measure is nonzero because $\mu>0$, which justifies
the strict integrated capacity bound. It includes the saturated
coordinate of $G_*$ and every mandatory or additional reverse group.

Put $a=\sqrt\alpha$, $b=\sqrt\beta$, and define

$$
H(u,v)=\frac14(1+u/a)(1+v/b),\qquad
d_a(u,v)=\big||u|-a\big|,\qquad
D_a=\int_{S^1}d_a^2\,d(\rho_0+\rho_1).
\tag{RB.13}
$$

The full live moments, including the first and mixed moments, give

$$
\int H\,d(\rho_0+\rho_1)=\frac14,\qquad
D_a=2a(a-K).
\tag{RB.14}
$$

Indeed the second identity expands
$\int(u^2-2a|u|+a^2)=\alpha-2aK+\alpha$ on the mass-one law.

For each point snap to $z=(\operatorname{sgn}(u)a,
\operatorname{sgn}(v)b)$, assigning the negative sign at zero.
On this four-point rectangle, $H(z)$ equals one at $(a,b)$ and
zero at the other three points. The circle identity gives

$$
\big||v|-b\big|=
\frac{|u^2-a^2|}{|v|+b}\le\frac{2d_a}{b}.
\tag{RB.15}
$$

Coordinatewise interpolation in the rectangle $[-1,1]^2$ and the
two partial derivatives of $H$ therefore yield

$$
|H(x)-H(z)|\le C d_a,\qquad
C=\frac{1+1/b}{4a}+\frac{2(1+1/a)}{4b^2}
<\frac{269725}{26877}<11.
\tag{RB.16}
$$

The strict comparison uses $a>93/100$ and $b>17/50$, which follow
by squaring these rationals in (RB.5).

If $x\in Y$ snaps to $(a,b)$, then $u,v>0$, and membership in
$Y$ requires $f(x)\le0$. Consequently $v/u\ge\chi>18/25$, so

$$
u^2<\frac{625}{949}<\frac{25}{36},\qquad
d_a>a-\frac56>\frac{29}{300}>\frac9{100}.
\tag{RB.17}
$$

Thus in this case $H(z)=1<(100/9)d_a$. In every other snapping
case $H(z)=0$. Combining both cases with (RB.16) proves
$H(x)\le24d_a(x)$ throughout $Y$. The whole circle also satisfies

$$
H(x)\le\frac14(1+1/a)(1+1/b)
<\frac{12931}{6324}<3.
\tag{RB.18}
$$

Neither inequality assumes $H\ge0$; negative values cause no change
to the upper bounds.

Proposition 2.1 confines the pure endpoint measure to $Y$, whereas
the saturated measure is unrestricted. Cauchy–Schwarz on the pure
part and (RB.12)–(RB.18) give

$$
\frac14
\le24\int d_a\,d\rho_0+3S
\le24\sqrt{D_a}+3S

\tag{RB.19}
$$

It follows that

$$

a-K=\frac{D_a}{2a}>\frac1{24000},
\tag{RB.20}
$$

where $a<1$. Projection preserved the original $K$, proving
(RB.11) for every optimizer. This argument uses the common full
law, not independently chosen angular and failure marginals. It
does not preserve or bound actual label count under projection.
$\square$

## 5. Degree-two coefficient control from the original dual target

**Proposition 5.1 (Toeplitz and failure-moment estimate for this dual).**
Under the rank-one hypotheses let $M_P=\max_{S^1}P$. Define
$\mathcal L_\alpha$ on real trigonometric polynomials of degree at
most two by its values on $(1,u,v,u^2,uv)$:
$(1,0,0,\alpha,0)$. Then

$$
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal L_\alpha(P)=y_0+\alpha y_3
=J_t+\lambda K-d_*m_v+\frac{\rho}{t}w^T\Lambda_fw,\\
0<M_P<\frac{25}{2}\mathcal L_\alpha(P)
<5+\frac{25}{2}\lambda+\frac{150\rho}{t}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{RB.27}
$$

Proof. Entire pure feasibility gives
$P\ge|\ell|+\lambda|u|\ge0$ on the full circle; $\lambda>0$
also ensures $P$ is not identically zero. Apply the classical scalar
Fejér–Riesz theorem in the form stated in Dritschel–Rovnyak,
*The Operator Fejér–Riesz Theorem*,
[§1](https://arxiv.org/html/0903.3639v1): a nonnegative scalar
Laurent polynomial on the entire unit circle has an analytic
polynomial factor of no greater degree. Zeros on the circle and
degree drops are allowed. This is a survey formulation of the
classical scalar theorem, not an operator-rank assertion. Here it
gives complex numbers $c_0,c_1,c_2$, not all zero, with

$$
P(\theta)=|c_0+c_1e^{i\theta}+c_2e^{2i\theta}|^2.
\tag{RB.28}
$$

The prescribed first and second angular moments give
$\mathcal L_\alpha(e^{i\theta})=0$ and
$\mathcal L_\alpha(e^{2i\theta})=2\alpha-1$.
For $\mathbf c=(c_0,c_1,c_2)^T$ the associated Toeplitz matrix is

$$
T_\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&2\alpha-1\\
0&1&0\\
2\alpha-1&0&1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\mathcal L_\alpha(P)=\mathbf c^\dagger T_\alpha\mathbf c.
\tag{RB.29}
$$

Its eigenvalues are $2\alpha,1,2\beta$, and its least is
$2\beta>6/25$ by (RB.5). Pointwise Cauchy–Schwarz in (RB.28)
therefore gives

$$
M_P\le3\|\mathbf c\|^2
<\frac{25}{2}\mathcal L_\alpha(P).
\tag{RB.30}
$$

The strict inequality follows because $\mathbf c\ne0$.

To identify this angular functional with the actual dual target,
use all three failure moments in (RB.2):

$$
y\cdot B=\mathcal L_\alpha(P)+\frac1t\operatorname{tr}(Q\Lambda_f)
=\mathcal L_\alpha(P)+d_*m_v-\frac{\rho}{t}w^T\Lambda_fw.
\tag{RB.31}
$$

The sign follows from $\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf F\Lambda_f)
=-d_*m_v$ and $Q=-t\mathsf F-\rho ww^T$.
Joint attainment now proves the first identity of (RB.27).
Since $d_*m_v>0$, $K\le1$, and
$w^T\Lambda_fw\le\operatorname{tr}\Lambda_f=\mu<12$,
(RB.23) and (RB.30) give its last strict bound. This controls
the dual polynomial's coefficients and maximum; it gives neither
a dependence of the augmented $(\Gamma,k)$ rows nor a quadrature
or actual-label bound. $\square$

$$

$$

\tag{RB.33}
$$

$t\ge37500$ and $t\ge14400000$, gives

$$
\rho<\frac{2505}{2}+3150(20000)<64000000.
\tag{RB.34}
$$

Proof. Differentiating (RB.1) on $u=\cos\theta$, $v=\sin\theta$
and using $A_\ell<B_\ell$ gives

$$
\|\ell''\|_\infty
\le2(B_\ell-A_\ell)+4a_\ell+l_u+l_v
<0.05948<\frac1{10}.
\tag{RB.35}
$$

Nonnegativity gives
$|\widehat P_j|\le(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}P
=\widehat P_0\le M_P$. Consequently

$$
\|P''\|_\infty\le\sum_{j=-2}^2j^2|\widehat P_j|
\le10M_P.
\tag{RB.36}
$$

This coefficient estimate is the direct Fourier integral inequality
inside this proof; it does not assume a sign for $P''$.

vanishes and has a minimum, including when $\eta_1(x_*)=0$.

and second derivative $2$ there: $x_*$ and its unit angular

$2c(x_*)$ at this point, with no derivatives of $c$ remaining.
The minimum condition gives

$$
2\rho c(x_*)\le P''(x_*)+\ell''(x_*)-\lambda u_*.
\tag{RB.37}
$$

Using $c(x_*)>1/25$, (RB.35), (RB.36) and $|u_*|\le1$
gives
$2\rho/25<10M_P+1/10+\lambda$, the first inequality of

Insert (RB.27) in that first inequality:

$$
\begin{aligned}

&<125\left(5+\frac{25}{2}\lambda+\frac{150\rho}{t}\right)
       +\frac{25}{2}\lambda+\frac54\\
&=\frac{2505}{4}+1575\lambda+\frac{18750\rho}{t}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{RB.38}
$$

For $t\ge37500$ its left coefficient after rearrangement is
at least $1/2$, proving (RB.33). Substituting
$\lambda<20000$ is valid only with the additional threshold
of Proposition 4.1. The resulting upper expression is
$63001252.5<64000000$, proving (RB.34) with both premises.
$\square$
