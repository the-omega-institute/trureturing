# Recursive relational observation: native edits and acquired current frontiers

## 1. Objects, consumers and entrance domains

**Definition 1.1 (native objects and the two output types).** Write FARG for
[Fibonacci atomic relation generation](FIBONACCI_ATOMIC_RELATION_GENERATION.md),
AA for [Atomic generation acquisition](RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_ATOMIC_GENERATION_ACQUISITION.md),
AAII for its [second volume](RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_ATOMIC_GENERATION_ACQUISITION_II.md),
and AAIV for its [fourth volume](RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_ATOMIC_GENERATION_ACQUISITION_IV.md).
Use FARG's free ordered objects

$$
t::=\alpha\mid\beta\mid\langle t,t\rangle,
\qquad P:\alpha\longmapsto\beta,
\qquad S:\beta\longmapsto\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle.
\tag{V.1}
$$

An addressed operation acts on a current logical leaf. There is no
associativity, commutativity, deletion or inverse edit. Encode beta by $0$
and alpha by $1$. The frontier replacements are $1\mapsto0$ and
$0\mapsto01$, respectively. Neither replacement is the whole-tree
homomorphism $\rho$, although applying the appropriate replacement once
at each old leaf implements $\rho$.

Put $s=(\sqrt5-1)/2$, $\varphi=1+s$. The selected seed of AAIV is

$$
f(i)=1-\big(\lfloor(i+2)s\rfloor-\lfloor(i+1)s\rfloor\big),
\qquad i\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{V.2}
$$

For an unknown selected origin $e$, the original task remains

$$
\mathcal R_H(e)=\big(\rho(a_e),\ldots,\rho(a_{e+H-1})\big),
\quad a_i=\begin{cases}\beta&f(i)=0,\\\alpha&f(i)=1.\end{cases}
\tag{V.3}
$$

Here $\rho(\alpha)=\beta$, $\rho(\beta)=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle$;
the $H$ output boundaries and the bracket in each beta output remain.
For a current ordered list of authentic leaf operands
$x_0,\ldots,x_{h-1}$, a different task is
$\mathcal C_h=(\rho(\operatorname{Tag}x_j))_{j<h}$ at a specified
current version. Versioned current-tree evaluation is a third type of
output. None is substituted for (V.3).

The quantity weights $q(\alpha)=2,q(\beta)=3$ and the calibrated gap
lengths $\ell(\alpha)=\varphi,\ell(\beta)=\varphi^2$ are distinct
maps. A new length chart assigns these lengths to current atoms; it
does not assert that material native growth preserves the old Euclidean
positions or physically produces those calibrated lengths.

**Definition 1.2 (finite native entrance).** An original instance is the
actual known-alpha-root installation of AA Assumption 20.1, at a returned
cut after any finite lawful word $w_0$. Its current tree is $t_0$ and its
actual ancestral tapes are those of AA §19, including the complete
prehistory $w_0$. Choose an epoch at this cut. Enumerate the logical
leaves of $t_0$ by a paid left-to-right source traversal. They are the
epoch seeds, with their actual references and paths; their count is $L$.
The enumeration and its actual Tag readings are retained in finite
backing. Their list has two genuine endpoints. Additional native events
can occur at every current logical leaf, including outside any requested
observation interval. Source and auxiliary rights, sufficient finite
reservation, finite progress, serial phases, End, local acknowledgement
and root Return are exactly the AA premises.

There is an entirely native selected-prefix entrance: take
$t_0=T_j=\rho^j(\alpha)$, $j\ge1$, constructed by its finite sequence
of addressed native operations. FARG §3 and AAIV (IV.123) give
$L=F_{j+1}$ and seed word $f[0,L)$. This entrance serves (V.3) at $e=0$
only for $H\le L$. It supplies neither a bilateral extension nor an
unknown phase. For general $t_0$, the epoch seed task is typed by its
actual finite seed list, without a golden-word promise.

**Definition 1.3 (bilateral source entrance).** A different entrance is
an ideal source-owned ordered family of seed roots $(r_i)_{i\in\mathbb Z}$,
seed boundaries $(\kappa_i)_{i\in\mathbb Z}$ and source-owned seed
headers $(h_i)_{i\in\mathbb Z}$. At the epoch, $r_i$ is the actual
one-atom material $C(a_i)$ of AA Definition 17.3; $h_i$ binds $r_i$,
$\kappa_i,\kappa_{i+1}$ and the previous/next seed headers. The
current frontier is initially the alternating adjacency

$$
\cdots\ \kappa_i\ r_i\ \kappa_{i+1}\ r_{i+1}\ \kappa_{i+2}\ \cdots.
\tag{V.4}
$$

The source supplies one authentic anchor header $h_e$ and its cut
$\kappa_e$, current source read rights and, separately, the native
rights used in any claimed edit. Reading or following these fields is
paid. There is no integer-to-reference conversion, exposed $e$, phase,
precomputed letter table or second replacement source. An integer $i$
in the definitions denotes the owner of a real binding, not an observer
address. Only finitely many headers, operands and record cells are
accessed in each finite execution.

For the original seven-request service on a selected root, require the
following additional entrance fact: that root is an actual AA installation
prepared from its own known alpha, with empty history when its epoch tag
is alpha and actual preparation $P_\varepsilon$ when its epoch tag is
beta. Its real tapes and root authority are retained. This is a premise
about the source's preparation, not a consequence of its flat labels.
There is no global generation history ordering all these preparations.
After selection of a root by actual paid header traversal, its original
request frames and root-relative history have exactly their original
meaning. In particular an epoch beta prepared this way has already been
promoted; it is not an unknown beta-born root treated as alpha-born.

Alternatively a genuinely beta-born root may be supplied with that birth
type. Its local descendant domain below is valid, but it is not an AA
known-alpha installation, and no original root-history service is claimed
for it without actual additional provenance. Inside every native tree,
left-born children are beta-born as usual.

For (V.3), use exactly the selected labels (V.2), lower convention and
the actual unknown anchor $h_e$. For a general mechanical phase one may
use Definition 2.1 instead; its seed task is then indexed by that phase,
not named $\mathcal R_H(e)$ unless (V.2) is the actual seed.

The countable source, its initial adjacency and any preparation authority
are explicit model premises. A finite $T_j$ does not construct them.
No existence of finite material realizing the entire bilateral source
or of a physical installation is asserted. Future adjacency correctness,
reading correctness and finite acquisition are proved from this entrance,
rather than included in it.

## 2. The fixed-slope family cannot contain a nonempty native edit

**Definition 2.1 (the complete fixed-slope family).** For
$\theta\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ define the bilateral sequences

$$
\begin{aligned}
d^-_\theta(n)&=\lfloor(n+1)s+\theta\rfloor-\lfloor ns+\theta\rfloor,\\
d^+_\theta(n)&=\lceil(n+1)s+\theta\rceil-\lceil ns+\theta\rceil,\\
x^\pm_\theta(n)&=1-d^\pm_\theta(n).
\end{aligned}
\tag{V.5}
$$

Let $\mathcal M_s$ contain both conventions for all phases. A finite
directed edit means a finite lawful sequence of (V.1), at arbitrary
integer locations and at any of their subsequent descendants. Flatten
the result and index it by fixing its agreement with the old left tail.
Each split adds one position; promotions preserve position count.
An alternative integer origin for the result is absorbed in its phase.

**Theorem 2.2 (no successful finite return to the mechanical family).**
For every $x\in\mathcal M_s$, the frontier after any nonempty finite
successful native edit is outside $\mathcal M_s$. This includes all
integer sites, both singular conventions and any final reindexing.

Proof. The following mechanical-coding facts are used internally.
The lower word $d^-_\theta$ codes rotation by $s$ with the interval
$[1-s,1)$; upper coding changes only endpoint membership. If two phases
$\theta,\eta$ are distinct, the two translates of this interval differ
on a nonempty open interval. Indeed a proper interval on the circle has
no nonzero translation symmetry: its two boundary points and its
oriented interior determine its starting point. The negative orbit and
the positive orbit of an irrational rotation are dense. Consequently
each tail contains a position with different codings, away from all
endpoints. Agreement on either entire sufficiently remote tail therefore
forces $\theta=\eta$ modulo one, even when endpoint conventions differ.

For a fixed phase the two conventions agree everywhere unless
$ks+\theta\in\mathbb Z$ at one integer $k$. Irrationality allows at
most one such $k$. Direct substitution into the floors and ceilings
then gives

$$
(d^-_\theta(k-1),d^-_\theta(k))=(1,0),\qquad
(d^+_\theta(k-1),d^+_\theta(k))=(0,1).
\tag{V.6}
$$

Thus the complementary words either coincide or differ by a balanced
adjacent exchange $01\leftrightarrow10$. These are classical
mechanical-word facts; the bilateral formulas and singular pair are
also given in Barbieri–Labbé–Starosta §3.1, with precisely this
floor/ceiling distinction. Their broader asymptotic-pair classification
is not needed here.

Let $b$ be the number of successful splits and $a$ the number of
successful promotions. Finitely many seed positions are touched, and
their descendants remain finite. Thus the result $y$ has

$$
y(n)=x(n)\quad(n\ll0),\qquad
y(n)=x(n-b)\quad(n\gg0).
\tag{V.7}
$$

If $y=x^{\delta}_\eta\in\mathcal M_s$, left-tail uniqueness gives
$\eta=\theta$. Right-tail uniqueness applied to (V.7) gives
$\eta=\theta-bs$ modulo one. Hence $bs\in\mathbb Z$, so $b=0$.
With no splits, every successful operation is $1\mapsto0$ at a
previously unchanged position. If $a>0$, $y\le x$ pointwise with a
strict inequality somewhere. This is neither equality nor the balanced
exchange (V.6). Therefore $a=0$ too, contradicting nonemptiness.
The choice of left-tail indexing involved no source operation; any
different integer indexing was already allowed in $\eta$. $\square$

**Corollary 2.3 (the obstruction is stronger than a forbidden factor).**
A local edit need not exhibit $11$ or $000$ for its departure from the
family to be certain. No choice of a new phase or a new singular
convention repairs any nonempty finite native edit. A synchronous
whole-source substitution has a different domain and is not covered by
the finite-support hypothesis of Theorem 2.2.

Proof. The theorem used tail agreement, the total insertion displacement
and directed promotion, not a particular short forbidden factor. A
whole-source substitution does not have the two finite-support tails
(V.7). $\square$

## 3. The exact edited source domain retains native brackets

**Definition 3.1 (one seed's descendants).** For a birth tag
$b\in\{\alpha,\beta\}$ let $\mathcal D_b$ be the finite ordered
terms with no alpha leaf at a nonempty address ending in $L$, and, if
$b=\beta$, with the additional exclusion of the singleton alpha.
The seed root has birth $b$, a left child has birth beta and a right
child has birth alpha. Branches retain their ordered brackets. Let
$\operatorname{fr}(t)$ denote only the frontier word of $t$.

An edited seed chart over a bilateral seed $x$ is a family $(t_i)$ with
$t_i\in\mathcal D_{a_i}$ and $t_i=a_i$ except at finitely many $i$.
Its current frontier is the ordered concatenation
$\cdots\operatorname{fr}(t_{-1})\operatorname{fr}(t_0)
\operatorname{fr}(t_1)\cdots$. It retains each seed boundary and
each internal native bracket, rather than identifying equal flat words.
For a finite epoch seed list use a finite family and its actual endpoints.

**Theorem 3.2 (exact finite-support completion).** The edited seed charts
are precisely the configurations obtainable by finite lawful native
edits from their indicated atomic seed roots. For each chart its possible
post-epoch successful histories are the linear extensions of the union
of its birth-typed native event orders, with different seeds independent.
Every prefix of such a history is lawful. Consequently the flat edited
domain is exactly the image of these charts under frontier concatenation;
it is not stipulated to be a new mechanical-word family.

Proof. At a born-alpha position the local prefix is empty, $P$, or
$PS$; at a born-beta position it is empty or $S$. Once split, construction
continues at its left beta-born and right alpha-born children. This gives
necessity of $\mathcal D_b$. Conversely, for a target in $\mathcal D_b$,
promote its root if it was born alpha and the target is not alpha; split
if the target is a branch; then recursively construct its two children.
The beta-born singleton-alpha case is the only impossible base case.
This is AA Theorem 17.2's construction with its root birth parameter
made explicit; no alpha-born formula is applied to a beta-born root.

Each event must follow the split creating its position, and a split of
an alpha-born position must follow its promotion. In a linear extension,
these prerequisites have occurred when an event is reached; no descendant
can already have consumed its operand, because that requires this split.
Thus its current guard is enabled. This is the same source-specific
argument as AA §17, applied within each component. Different components
have no common logical operand, so arbitrary shuffles of their lawful
histories are lawful. Finite support and finite component trees give a
finite event set. Every finite execution has exactly such a chart and
order. Taking the frontier image proves the last assertion without
asserting injectivity of flattening. $\square$

**Corollary 3.3 (one finite original tree, with no lost preparation).**
For the finite entrance $t_0$, all future current trees have the form
$t_0[t_i]$, substituting $t_i\in\mathcal D_{b_i}$ at each epoch leaf
of tag $b_i$. This describes every finite lawful continuation of that
actual $t_0$. Its full original generation history is $w_0$ followed by
the actual chosen extension, not a freshly initialized history at $t_0$.

Proof. Future edits affect exactly one epoch leaf's descendants. No native
operation deletes an old branch or changes the order of its children.
Apply Theorem 3.2 to these disjoint subtrees; prepend the actual $w_0$.
AA's retained tapes acquire the corresponding full extended history.
$\square$

## 4. A marker fibre with different original native behavior

**Proposition 4.1 (identical entire 00 field, different atoms and guards).**
Suppose a seed beta at an epoch leaf $p$ is immediately followed by an
epoch alpha. There are two lawful finite continuations, with equal split
count, equal promotion count, identical final ordered bracket shape and
identical $00$ indicator at every current adjacent pair, whose current
Tag, native growth guard, original atomic consumer and history replies
differ. The example occurs on an actual finite known-alpha instance.

Proof. On the beta subtree execute the common three splits
$S_pS_{pL}S_{pLL}$. Its current tree and word are

$$
u=\langle\langle\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,\alpha\rangle,
\alpha\rangle,\qquad \operatorname{fr}(u)=0111.
\tag{V.8}
$$

For the first continuation append $P_{pR}$, and for the second append
$P_{pLR}$. Their words in that block are respectively $0110$ and
$0101$. Both have no internal $00$, both start with zero, and the next
seed letter is one. Thus the left interface is unchanged, the right
interface is unmarked in both, and every other adjacent pair is
unchanged. Equal split counts give the same left-tail indexed positions
and equal current list length. The same cuts created by the common
splits can be identified between the two instances. Equal event counts
also give equal public version ordinals if only that ordinal is added.

At $pR$ the first Tag is beta and the second is alpha. Accordingly
$P_{pR}$ is wrong-type refused in the first and succeeds in the second;
$\rho(\operatorname{Tag}(pR))$ is an ordered pair in the first and
a singleton beta in the second. FetchGrowth at the index of the final
promotion returns different literal addresses. Next at that index uses
these different historical addresses on the respective current trees:
it executes $S_{pR}$ and $S_{pLR}$, respectively. Both are actual beta
leaves at that time, and their successors differ.

For a finite native realization, build
$T_3=\langle\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,\beta\rangle$ by
$P_\varepsilon S_\varepsilon S_L$, and take $p=L L$.
Its next epoch leaf is $LR$, of tag alpha. The two continuations just
given are all legal there, with the full preparation retained. For a
bilateral mechanical seed, a factor $01$ exists at every phase by
irrational rotation, so the same replacement can be made at one such
seed. $\square$

**Corollary 4.2 (more marker comparisons do not solve this fibre).**
On the source class of Proposition 4.1, no decoder of the old marker
menu's complete records, even of arbitrarily many comparisons and paid
Resumes, uniformly supplies all current atomic or native guard answers.
This remains true when comparison counts and final version ordinals are
also disclosed without event addresses or tags.

Proof. Couple source-independent policies, grants and coins. Every
comparison reads the same Boolean field at the same current position;
the common insertion history permits the same cursor and anchor cuts.
Induction on the complete attempted menu proves identical outputs,
stops, Resume acknowledgements and cursors. A requested answer at $pR$
is different by Proposition 4.1. Already the entire Boolean field is
insufficient, so no longer finite prefix repairs it. $\square$
