# Recursive relational observation: native edits and acquired current frontiers

## 1. Objects, consumers and entrance domains

**Definition 1.1 (native objects and the two output types).** Write FARG for
[Fibonacci atomic relation generation](FIBONACCI_ATOMIC_RELATION_GENERATION.md),
AA for [Atomic generation acquisition](RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_ATOMIC_GENERATION_ACQUISITION.md),
AAII for its [second volume](RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_ATOMIC_GENERATION_ACQUISITION_II.md),
and AAIV for its [fourth volume](RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_ATOMIC_GENERATION_ACQUISITION_IV.md).
Use FARG's free ordered objects

$$
t::=\alpha\mid\beta\mid\langle t,t\rangle,
\qquad P:\alpha\longmapsto\beta,
\qquad S:\beta\longmapsto\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle.
\tag{V.1}
$$

An addressed operation acts on a current logical leaf. There is no
associativity, commutativity, deletion or inverse edit. Encode beta by $0$
and alpha by $1$. The frontier replacements are $1\mapsto0$ and
$0\mapsto01$, respectively. Neither replacement is the whole-tree
homomorphism $\rho$, although applying the appropriate replacement once
at each old leaf implements $\rho$.

Put $s=(\sqrt5-1)/2$, $\varphi=1+s$. The selected seed of AAIV is

$$
f(i)=1-\big(\lfloor(i+2)s\rfloor-\lfloor(i+1)s\rfloor\big),
\qquad i\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{V.2}
$$

For an unknown selected origin $e$, the original task remains

$$
\mathcal R_H(e)=\big(\rho(a_e),\ldots,\rho(a_{e+H-1})\big),
\quad a_i=\begin{cases}\beta&f(i)=0,\\\alpha&f(i)=1.\end{cases}
\tag{V.3}
$$

Here $\rho(\alpha)=\beta$, $\rho(\beta)=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle$;
the $H$ output boundaries and the bracket in each beta output remain.
For a current ordered list of authentic leaf operands
$x_0,\ldots,x_{h-1}$, a different task is
$\mathcal C_h=(\rho(\operatorname{Tag}x_j))_{j<h}$ at a specified
current version. Versioned current-tree evaluation is a third type of
output. None is substituted for (V.3).

The quantity weights $q(\alpha)=2,q(\beta)=3$ and the calibrated gap
lengths $\ell(\alpha)=\varphi,\ell(\beta)=\varphi^2$ are distinct
maps. A new length chart assigns these lengths to current atoms; it
does not assert that material native growth preserves the old Euclidean
positions or physically produces those calibrated lengths.

**Definition 1.2 (finite native entrance).** An original instance is the
actual known-alpha-root installation of AA Assumption 20.1, at a returned
cut after any finite lawful word $w_0$. Its current tree is $t_0$ and its
actual ancestral tapes are those of AA §19, including the complete
prehistory $w_0$. Choose an epoch at this cut. Enumerate the logical
leaves of $t_0$ by a paid left-to-right source traversal. They are the
epoch seeds, with their actual references and paths; their count is $L$.
The enumeration and its actual Tag readings are retained in finite
backing. Their list has two genuine endpoints. Additional native events
can occur at every current logical leaf, including outside any requested
observation interval. Source and auxiliary rights, sufficient finite
reservation, finite progress, serial phases, End, local acknowledgement
and root Return are exactly the AA premises.

There is an entirely native selected-prefix entrance: take
$t_0=T_j=\rho^j(\alpha)$, $j\ge1$, constructed by its finite sequence
of addressed native operations. FARG §3 and AAIV (IV.123) give
$L=F_{j+1}$ and seed word $f[0,L)$. This entrance serves (V.3) at $e=0$
only for $H\le L$. It supplies neither a bilateral extension nor an
unknown phase. For general $t_0$, the epoch seed task is typed by its
actual finite seed list, without a golden-word promise.

**Definition 1.3 (bilateral source entrance).** A different entrance is
an ideal source-owned ordered family of seed roots $(r_i)_{i\in\mathbb Z}$,
seed boundaries $(\kappa_i)_{i\in\mathbb Z}$ and source-owned seed
headers $(h_i)_{i\in\mathbb Z}$. At the epoch, $r_i$ is the actual
one-atom material $C(a_i)$ of AA Definition 17.3; $h_i$ binds $r_i$,
$\kappa_i,\kappa_{i+1}$ and the previous/next seed headers. The
current frontier is initially the alternating adjacency

$$
\cdots\ \kappa_i\ r_i\ \kappa_{i+1}\ r_{i+1}\ \kappa_{i+2}\ \cdots.
\tag{V.4}
$$

For joint use with AAIV, write $P(i)=\lfloor(i+1)s\rfloor+i\varphi$
for its selected point coordinate. The source owner additionally binds
its original selected gap $[P(i),P(i+1)]$ to this very $r_i$ at the epoch, with
AAIV40's orientation and calibration. The original marker handle and
the native entrance share that authentic source binding and anchor;
they do not point to two merely equal words. The marker test reads the
tags of these actual adjacent operands. This initial attachment is an
explicit entrance premise, not a consequence of equality of flat words.
It grants no native write right by itself. There is no assumption here
about attachment after an edit; that maintenance is Theorem 5.4.

The source supplies one authentic anchor header $h_e$ and its cut
$\kappa_e$, current source read rights and, separately, the native
rights used in any claimed edit. Reading or following these fields is
paid. There is no integer-to-reference conversion, exposed $e$, phase,
precomputed letter table or second replacement source. An integer $i$
in the definitions denotes the owner of a real binding, not an observer
address. Only finitely many headers, operands and record cells are
accessed in each finite execution.

For the original seven-request service on a selected root, require the
following additional entrance fact: that root is an actual AA installation
prepared from its own known alpha, with empty history when its epoch tag
is alpha and actual preparation $P_\varepsilon$ when its epoch tag is
beta. Its real tapes and root authority are retained. This is a premise
about the source's preparation, not a consequence of its flat labels.
There is no global generation history ordering all these preparations.
After selection of a root by actual paid header traversal, its original
request frames and root-relative history have exactly their original
meaning. In particular an epoch beta prepared this way has already been
promoted; it is not an unknown beta-born root treated as alpha-born.

Alternatively a genuinely beta-born root may be supplied with that birth
type. Its local descendant domain below is valid, but it is not an AA
known-alpha installation, and no original root-history service is claimed
for it without actual additional provenance. Inside every native tree,
left-born children are beta-born as usual.

For (V.3), use exactly the selected labels (V.2), lower convention and
the actual unknown anchor $h_e$. For a general mechanical phase one may
use Definition 2.1 instead; its seed task is then indexed by that phase,
not named $\mathcal R_H(e)$ unless (V.2) is the actual seed.

The countable source, its initial adjacency and any preparation authority
are explicit model premises. A finite $T_j$ does not construct them.
No existence of finite material realizing the entire bilateral source
or of a physical installation is asserted. Future adjacency correctness,
reading correctness and finite acquisition are proved from this entrance,
rather than included in it.

## 2. The fixed-slope family cannot contain a nonempty native edit

**Definition 2.1 (the complete fixed-slope family).** For
$\theta\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ define the bilateral sequences

$$
\begin{aligned}
d^-_\theta(n)&=\lfloor(n+1)s+\theta\rfloor-\lfloor ns+\theta\rfloor,\\
d^+_\theta(n)&=\lceil(n+1)s+\theta\rceil-\lceil ns+\theta\rceil,\\
x^\pm_\theta(n)&=1-d^\pm_\theta(n).
\end{aligned}
\tag{V.5}
$$

Let $\mathcal M_s$ contain both conventions for all phases. A finite
directed edit means a finite lawful sequence of (V.1), at arbitrary
integer locations and at any of their subsequent descendants. Flatten
the result and index it by fixing its agreement with the old left tail.
Each split adds one position; promotions preserve position count.
An alternative integer origin for the result is absorbed in its phase.

**Theorem 2.2 (no successful finite return to the mechanical family).**
For every $x\in\mathcal M_s$, the frontier after any nonempty finite
successful native edit is outside $\mathcal M_s$. This includes all
integer sites, both singular conventions and any final reindexing.

Proof. The following mechanical-coding facts are used internally.
The lower word $d^-_\theta$ codes rotation by $s$ with the interval
$[1-s,1)$; upper coding changes only endpoint membership. If two phases
$\theta,\eta$ are distinct, the two translates of this interval differ
on a nonempty open interval. Indeed a proper interval on the circle has
no nonzero translation symmetry: its two boundary points and its
oriented interior determine its starting point. The negative orbit and
the positive orbit of an irrational rotation are dense. Consequently
each tail contains a position with different codings, away from all
endpoints. Agreement on either entire sufficiently remote tail therefore
forces $\theta=\eta$ modulo one, even when endpoint conventions differ.

For a fixed phase the two conventions agree everywhere unless
$ks+\theta\in\mathbb Z$ at one integer $k$. Irrationality allows at
most one such $k$. Direct substitution into the floors and ceilings
then gives

$$
(d^-_\theta(k-1),d^-_\theta(k))=(1,0),\qquad
(d^+_\theta(k-1),d^+_\theta(k))=(0,1).
\tag{V.6}
$$

Thus the complementary words either coincide or differ by a balanced
adjacent exchange $01\leftrightarrow10$. These are classical
mechanical-word facts; the bilateral formulas and singular pair are
also given in Barbieri–Labbé–Starosta §3.1, with precisely this
floor/ceiling distinction. Their broader asymptotic-pair classification
is not needed here.

Let $b$ be the number of successful splits and $a$ the number of
successful promotions. Finitely many seed positions are touched, and
their descendants remain finite. Thus the result $y$ has

$$
y(n)=x(n)\quad(n\ll0),\qquad
y(n)=x(n-b)\quad(n\gg0).
\tag{V.7}
$$

If $y=x^{\delta}_\eta\in\mathcal M_s$, left-tail uniqueness gives
$\eta=\theta$. Right-tail uniqueness applied to (V.7) gives
$\eta=\theta-bs$ modulo one. Hence $bs\in\mathbb Z$, so $b=0$.
With no splits, every successful operation is $1\mapsto0$ at a
previously unchanged position. If $a>0$, $y\le x$ pointwise with a
strict inequality somewhere. This is neither equality nor the balanced
exchange (V.6). Therefore $a=0$ too, contradicting nonemptiness.
The choice of left-tail indexing involved no source operation; any
different integer indexing was already allowed in $\eta$. $\square$

**Corollary 2.3 (the obstruction is stronger than a forbidden factor).**
A local edit need not exhibit $11$ or $000$ for its departure from the
family to be certain. No choice of a new phase or a new singular
convention repairs any nonempty finite native edit. A synchronous
whole-source substitution has a different domain and is not covered by
the finite-support hypothesis of Theorem 2.2.

Proof. The theorem used tail agreement, the total insertion displacement
and directed promotion, not a particular short forbidden factor. A
whole-source substitution does not have the two finite-support tails
(V.7). $\square$

## 3. The exact edited source domain retains native brackets

**Definition 3.1 (one seed's descendants).** For a birth tag
$b\in\{\alpha,\beta\}$ let $\mathcal D_b$ be the finite ordered
terms with no alpha leaf at a nonempty address ending in $L$, and, if
$b=\beta$, with the additional exclusion of the singleton alpha.
The seed root has birth $b$, a left child has birth beta and a right
child has birth alpha. Branches retain their ordered brackets. Let
$\operatorname{fr}(t)$ denote only the frontier word of $t$.

An edited seed chart over a bilateral seed $x$ is a family $(t_i)$ with
$t_i\in\mathcal D_{a_i}$ and $t_i=a_i$ except at finitely many $i$.
Its current frontier is the ordered concatenation
$\cdots\operatorname{fr}(t_{-1})\operatorname{fr}(t_0)
\operatorname{fr}(t_1)\cdots$. It retains each seed boundary and
each internal native bracket, rather than identifying equal flat words.
For a finite epoch seed list use a finite family and its actual endpoints.

**Theorem 3.2 (exact finite-support completion).** The edited seed charts
are precisely the configurations obtainable by finite lawful native
edits from their indicated atomic seed roots. For each chart its possible
post-epoch successful histories are the linear extensions of the union
of its birth-typed native event orders, with different seeds independent.
Every prefix of such a history is lawful. Consequently the flat edited
domain is exactly the image of these charts under frontier concatenation;
it is not stipulated to be a new mechanical-word family.

Proof. At a born-alpha position the local prefix is empty, $P$, or
$PS$; at a born-beta position it is empty or $S$. Once split, construction
continues at its left beta-born and right alpha-born children. This gives
necessity of $\mathcal D_b$. Conversely, for a target in $\mathcal D_b$,
promote its root if it was born alpha and the target is not alpha; split
if the target is a branch; then recursively construct its two children.
The beta-born singleton-alpha case is the only impossible base case.
This is AA Theorem 17.2's construction with its root birth parameter
made explicit; no alpha-born formula is applied to a beta-born root.

Each event must follow the split creating its position, and a split of
an alpha-born position must follow its promotion. In a linear extension,
these prerequisites have occurred when an event is reached; no descendant
can already have consumed its operand, because that requires this split.
Thus its current guard is enabled. This is the same source-specific
argument as AA §17, applied within each component. Different components
have no common logical operand, so arbitrary shuffles of their lawful
histories are lawful. Finite support and finite component trees give a
finite event set. Every finite execution has exactly such a chart and
order. Taking the frontier image proves the last assertion without
asserting injectivity of flattening. $\square$

**Corollary 3.3 (one finite original tree, with no lost preparation).**
For the finite entrance $t_0$, all future current trees have the form
$t_0[t_i]$, substituting $t_i\in\mathcal D_{b_i}$ at each epoch leaf
of tag $b_i$. This describes every finite lawful continuation of that
actual $t_0$. Its full original generation history is $w_0$ followed by
the actual chosen extension, not a freshly initialized history at $t_0$.

Proof. Future edits affect exactly one epoch leaf's descendants. No native
operation deletes an old branch or changes the order of its children.
Apply Theorem 3.2 to these disjoint subtrees; prepend the actual $w_0$.
AA's retained tapes acquire the corresponding full extended history.
$\square$

## 4. A marker fibre with different original native behavior

**Proposition 4.1 (identical entire 00 field, different atoms and guards).**
Suppose a seed beta at an epoch leaf $p$ is immediately followed by an
epoch alpha. There are two lawful finite continuations, with equal split
count, equal promotion count, identical final ordered bracket shape and
identical $00$ indicator at every current adjacent pair, whose current
Tag, native growth guard, original atomic consumer and history replies
differ. The example occurs on an actual finite known-alpha instance.

Proof. On the beta subtree execute the common three splits
$S_pS_{pL}S_{pLL}$. Its current tree and word are

$$
u=\langle\langle\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,\alpha\rangle,
\alpha\rangle,\qquad \operatorname{fr}(u)=0111.
\tag{V.8}
$$

For the first continuation append $P_{pR}$, and for the second append
$P_{pLR}$. Their words in that block are respectively $0110$ and
$0101$. Both have no internal $00$, both start with zero, and the next
seed letter is one. Thus the left interface is unchanged, the right
interface is unmarked in both, and every other adjacent pair is
unchanged. Equal split counts give the same left-tail indexed positions
and equal current list length. The same cuts created by the common
splits can be identified between the two instances. Equal event counts
also give equal public version ordinals if only that ordinal is added.

At $pR$ the first Tag is beta and the second is alpha. Accordingly
$P_{pR}$ is wrong-type refused in the first and succeeds in the second;
$\rho(\operatorname{Tag}(pR))$ is an ordered pair in the first and
a singleton beta in the second. FetchGrowth at the index of the final
promotion returns different literal addresses. Next at that index uses
these different historical addresses on the respective current trees:
it executes $S_{pR}$ and $S_{pLR}$, respectively. Both are actual beta
leaves at that time, and their successors differ.

For a finite native realization, build
$T_3=\langle\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle,\beta\rangle$ by
$P_\varepsilon S_\varepsilon S_LP_R$, and take $p=LL$.
Its next epoch leaf is $LR$, of tag alpha. The two continuations just
given are all legal there, with the full preparation retained. For a
bilateral mechanical seed, a factor $01$ exists at every phase by
irrational rotation, so the same replacement can be made at one such
seed. $\square$

**Corollary 4.2 (more marker comparisons do not solve this fibre).**
On the source class of Proposition 4.1, no decoder of the old marker
menu's complete records, even of arbitrarily many comparisons and paid
Resumes, uniformly supplies all current atomic or native guard answers.
This remains true when comparison counts and final version ordinals are
also disclosed without event addresses or tags.

Proof. Couple source-independent policies, grants and coins. Every
comparison reads the same Boolean field at the same current position;
the common insertion history permits the same cursor and anchor cuts.
Induction on the complete attempted menu proves identical outputs,
stops, Resume acknowledgements and cursors. A requested answer at $pR$
is different by Proposition 4.1. Already the entire Boolean field is
insufficient, so no longer finite prefix repairs it. $\square$

## 5. Paid cut material on the actual native source

**Definition 5.1 (incidence backing and persistent cuts).** A current
frontier cell binds an actual current logical leaf reference, its native
root binding, its epoch seed header and its left and right cut references.
Both children of a split inherit the old cell's seed header. Each cut has incidence
fields for its immediately left and right frontier cells, with null at
a finite endpoint. These are separate owned auxiliary objects; they are
not source edges of $C(t)$ and do not replace the ancestral tape header.
An association list, searched by paid reference equality, binds a logical
source vertex to its two subtree-boundary cuts. A branch entry also binds
its actual logical children. No expected constant-time lookup is assumed.
Old source vertices and old cell objects are retained; a cell removed
from the current list is marked retired. A retired cell is never a
current leaf merely because an earlier sample names it.

In the finite entrance, a paid recursive arity traversal of $C(t_0)$
allocates $L+1$ cut objects, $L$ frontier cells and entries for its
logical nodes. At a terminal it records alpha, at a unary node beta,
and at a binary node its two recursively traversed subtrees. The unary
terminal marker is not enumerated as a logical leaf. Return from a
subtree supplies its actual first/last cuts. The traversal also retains
each epoch seed's header and read tag. Its bracket map comes from these
actual links, not from parsing a flat word.

In the bilateral entrance the source-owned initial incidences in (V.4)
are supplied along with those initial headers and cuts. Additional
node entries, retirement fields, samples and scratch are allocated only
as accessed. Before the first post-epoch edit in a seed component,
enrollment reads its still-atomic root's actual Tag and writes a permanent
one-bit epoch-label record bound to that header. A first read can enroll
it earlier. All post-epoch edits, whether externally addressed or internal
to Next, must pass this enrollment and the maintenance below. There is
no other writer. Hence an unenrolled component has not yet changed since
the epoch. Enrollment does not infer that a current beta was born beta;
its native preparation/tapes, if required, have their separate provenance
in Definition 1.3.

**Definition 5.2 (version material).** One owned header binds the current
source version token $v$. An initial token marks the epoch. Each successful
post-epoch native event allocates one fresh token with its predecessor
reference, source binding, actual edited operand reference and event kind.
It becomes current only after source, original tapes and incidence
maintenance complete. Queries and refusals allocate no new source version.
For a bilateral family this orders post-epoch edits across the finitely
touched components; it supplies no invented order on their preparations.
Native history decoding still uses the original tapes, not these tokens.
Token references, fields, retention and every access are paid. The token
is an acquired epoch/version label, not a phase or a physical clock.

The live source handle binds this owned header and the same native
source throughout. Its separately granted current-access authority
covers reading the header and the maintained incidences; it is not
created by a sample token or by the old marker handle. Each transaction
actually reads the header's current token before sampling. If an entry
explicitly requires an older expected token, mismatch yields RejectEntry
without a sample. Reading the current token is a paid field read,
not reacquisition of a new source. Old sample tokens stay immutable;
maintenance changes only the live header's current-token field.

**Definition 5.3 (maintenance at an actual edit).** Reserve the original
native material, tape append material and all required auxiliary cells
before editing. Let the actual guarded logical leaf be $z$ with current
cuts $l,r$.

For $P_z$, execute AA's attachment of the unary marker. Keep the frontier
cell and cuts, whose logical root remains $z$; its actual tag is now beta.

For $S_z$, execute AA's source template. The old unary marker becomes the
new left logical beta root, after its own terminal marker is attached;
the new right logical root is alpha. Read these actual post-edit child
bindings. Allocate one new cut $m$ and two new frontier cells, binding
the left child to $(l,m)$ and the right child to $(m,r)$. Retire the
former leaf cell. Set $l$'s right incidence to the left new cell, $m$'s
two incidences to the two new cells, and $r$'s left incidence to the right
new cell. Other incidences are unchanged. The entry for $z$, now a
branch, keeps boundaries $(l,r)$ and binds its two real children.
Ancestor entries keep their boundaries. Neighbor cells still use the
same shared cuts. No old cut is deleted or identified with $m$.

Finish AA's actual ancestor tape appends. Retain the actual native
continuation and maintenance subphases throughout. Publish the new
version token only after both kinds of maintenance, then construct the
original answer. No other ingress, observation or query is admitted in
this busy interval. Print, End, local ACK, root Return and scratch cleanup
complete the transaction. The extra steps refine a busy native phase;
they do not create a new original refusal or suppress an old success.

**Theorem 5.4 (derived correspondence, including brackets and operands).**
At every returned cut the current incidence list is exactly the ordered
logical-leaf frontier of the same actual native source; each current
cell's reference and stored root binding name that very leaf. A node's
retained cuts bound precisely its current descendant frontier. Every
epoch seed cut remains the left boundary of that seed's descendants.
Every other retained cut remains a genuine current boundary. The epoch
label record, when present, equals the original seed tag. These assertions
hold after every finite lawful native history, not only after applying
$\rho$ once to a complete source.

Proof. For the finite entrance, induction on the actual arity traversal
proves initialization. A binary node concatenates the already obtained
left and right lists at their shared cut. A unary node contributes just
its root, of tag beta, not its material marker. This proves the asserted
operand map as well as bracket correspondence. The bilateral entrance
supplies precisely the same atomic base locally by (V.4).

Induct on successful events. Enrollment reads an unchanged epoch atom
before its first mutation, so its recorded bit is the original one. An
already enrolled bit is never overwritten. The actual $P$ template
changes only the tag at the same logical leaf root, exactly matching its
unchanged cell. The actual $S$ template replaces one logical leaf by its
ordered two logical children. The new field bindings are read from that
template's actual objects. Its list replacement is therefore exactly
that one frontier replacement. It introduces precisely one boundary and
keeps both old exterior boundaries. Each old ancestor's descendant
interval changes only by this internal replacement, so its boundaries
remain correct; disjoint nodes are untouched. No unary terminal is
mistaken for a logical child before its genuine reuse by $S$.

Version publication occurs after these updates and the original tape
updates, so a returned token labels a coherent source/tape/list state.
Nonmutating requests preserve it. Induction proves the result, including
arbitrary operations inside different seed components and outside the
currently observed interval. $\square$

**Proposition 5.5 (no finite-native endpoint is filled by notation).**
The finite construction, started at the actual known alpha and any
finite $w_0$, has two real current endpoints after every finite
continuation. In particular it is an actual native realization of the
finite case of Theorem 5.4. The bilateral construction requires the
separate source premise of Definition 1.3.

Proof. AA's initialization constructs a terminal alpha with actual
reference. Every $P$ adds one source vertex and every $S$ adds two,
as in AA §20. Finite repetition constructs the finite source and tapes.
The traversal above is finite. The list has $L+1$ cuts initially;
a split inserts an interior cut and never supplies an exterior neighbor
to either endpoint. Thus endpoints remain null on their exterior sides.
The native preparation of each $T_j$ is finite: at a stage enumerate
its current leaves, and execute the appropriate $P/S$ at each enumerated
prefix-free address. Other old addresses persist, so this constructs
the next $T_{j+1}$ with its full real history. This gives the selected
finite example of Definition 1.2 without an infinite-source argument.
$\square$

## 6. Current cursors, acquired samples and a genuinely paid return

**Definition 6.1 (current observation state).** Park two owned cursor
slots $c_L,c_R$ at the anchor cut, together with the real source binding,
current rights and a shared marker latch. A cursor cut denotes the
current site immediately to its right, if present. Also retain the
original seed anchor header and cut, comparison counters and the complete
actual observation record $O$. The native driver retains its own root
cursor and returns it to Ready; these parked slots are additional paid
state, not permission to navigate the source without its rights.

A current right marker query resolves the current cell $x$ right of
$c_R$ and its next current cell $y$, reads their actual native tags and
returns $b=1$ exactly for beta-beta. It moves $c_R$ to $x$'s right cut,
so the new site is $y$. A left query compares the current right cell
$x$ of $c_L$ with the cell $y$ left of that cut, and moves $c_L$ to
$y$'s left cut. The sample records source binding, current version,
direction, both cut/cell/operand bindings, their tested $00$ reply,
and the successor cursor. The counters count completed comparisons.
An active successful comparison sets the latch to $b$. At a finite
endpoint where the required pair does not exist, return Endpoint with
no comparison, no invented beta or zero, no counter increment and no
cursor/latch change.

While latched, ordinary raw queries reject without a sample or movement.
Resume has exactly AAIV §45's latch-only effect: a successful paid Ack
clears it and preserves the actual cursors, anchor, source, version and
old records. Active refusal, denied grant, mismatched entry, absent
authority and wrong phase retain AAIV's distinct meanings. In particular
an edit does not silently clear a latch. Refusals, denials, Endpoint,
Resume and rejected attempts have their actual records too.

Before any edit or extra movement, on the bilateral selected entrance
the $c$th right cursor is the original seed site $e+c$ and the comparison
is exactly AAIV (IV.145); the left cursor similarly gives (IV.125).
After edits the counters are not site ranks. The retained cut, together
with current incidence, is the cursor's meaning.

**Definition 6.2 (source-owned reads).** The additional operation
ReadCurrent at a cursor resolves its right cell, follows that cell's
actual operand binding, and obtains its current alpha/beta tag by the
original arity test. It records the tag as well as its version, source,
cut, cell and operand bindings. At an endpoint it records Endpoint.
This is an additional source-access observation beyond the Boolean
marker menu. Where the original native Tag right is already held it
uses that very paid test; the old marker handle alone does not supply
it. A current operand path for an original addressed request is acquired
by reading real parent/child fields back to its bound native root,
testing which child was traversed and reversing the finite path in
backed scratch. Every edge must be a logical binary edge. Equality to
a known root terminates the walk. This does not cast a leaf rank, a
bitstring or a returned historical value to a source reference.

ReadEpoch at an enrolled seed header reads its protected epoch-label
record. At an unenrolled seed it first performs enrollment's actual
root Tag and records that tag. Its result is explicitly an epoch value,
with its acquisition version and its original seed-header binding. It
is not a current tag if that component has changed. The original seed
links permit paid traversal between original headers after their
descendants have grown. They refer to retained boundaries of the same
source, not to another immutable word supplied for free.

**Definition 6.3 (movement beyond Resume).** A paid WalkRight moves one
cursor from its current cut, across its right current cell, to that
cell's right cut; WalkLeft does the corresponding left move. They test
actual incidence and rights, record every step and return Endpoint
without movement if there is no such cell. They do not compare tags or
clear the marker latch. The native Tag service and these source-access
transactions are distinct from ordinary latched raw queries. Every
extra retained reference and temporary traversal slot has supplied
rights and is charged.

Seek to a retained same-source cut is a finite procedure using these
walks and reference equality. It is not a primitive teleport. First test
whether the cursor is already at the target. Otherwise save the
starting cut in an owned slot. For $n=1,2,\ldots$, walk up to $n$ steps
right, checking the target at each visited cut, then walk the actual

and return; increment the backed counter. An endpoint shortens an
excursion and its reverse. Stop immediately on the target. All traversals,
failed excursions, counters, fields, observations and cleanup are paid.

a primitive is disabled; a denied grant is not successful acquisition.

to an uncompleted output or an unexecuted source read.

## 8. Positive finite acquisition after edits

**Definition 8.1 (quiescent acquisition episodes).** An episode is a
finite sequence of actually granted source-access transactions, with no
native growth between its reads and walks. It may follow any finite
native history and any prior observation or movement. Versions stay
fixed during its current-source reads. Native requests may be interleaved
arbitrarily outside the episode; more generally each individual
transaction of §§5–7 has exact semantics even when no such episode is
supplied. The acquisition theorem's finite stopping claim is for these
episodes. It is not a nonquiescent decoder-convergence theorem.

To acquire a current interval, save the actual cursor cut if a return
is requested, Seek the retained chosen anchor cut, and for each requested
current site perform ReadCurrent and walk right to the next site when
another site is needed. Record each tag with its actual operand and
version. To acquire original seeds, Seek the retained seed anchor cut,
ReadEpoch at its authentic header, then follow its paid next-seed link
and walk through the current descendants to that next seed cut before
reading the next header. Stop at the requested number of seeds. The
next original seed header is identified by that real link, not by
counting current leaves. A requested return invokes paid Seek back to
the saved cut. No operation clears the marker latch unless a separate
successful Resume is actually supplied.

Finite endpoints stop these procedures with their actually acquired
prefix and Endpoint. They never manufacture a completion. Counters for
$H$, traversed cells and output construction have real finite backing;
they are not extra finite-control states of unbounded magnitude.

**Theorem 8.2 (post-edit acquired finite consumers).** Under the finite

1. every requested finite current interval lying within the actual

**Proposition 9.2 (quiescent marker continuation exists but is insufficient).**

is the original mechanical tail, with a finite insertion displacement.

selected irrational mechanical sequence. To see the finite-block
transfer, for the lower convention approximate the phase from its

five-comparison existence bound on that unchanged selected tail then
supplies a marker there. Reaching the unchanged tail from a current

The real $P/S$ template and ancestor-tape updates are unchanged. Persistence
in Lemma 13.2 and the cell-root argument in Lemma 14.2 replace incidence

same new version only after the native and tape writes finish. No query,
second ingress or other writer is admitted during this busy interval.
Edits at, before, after or across either cursor, or in another seed component,
therefore preserve the same cut identities and actual source successor.

All derived accesses expand into finite primitive reads/tests and backed
loops by Proposition 13.4. BuildAnswer, Print, End, local ACK, root Return
and clearing complete each transaction. Source rights remain current;
missing authority or wrong phase disables a primitive, a denied grant is
not acquisition, and pending ingress/output retains its actual prefix and
subphase. No record is attributed to an unexecuted read or uncompleted
transaction. Induction on acquired input bits and completed serialized
transactions proves the assertion, including arbitrary finite adaptive
interleavings and incomplete frames. Quiescence is needed for a common-version
multi-transaction acquisition and its stopping claim, not for making a
single allowed transition meaningful. $\square$

**Theorem 14.4 (the same finite acquisitions and paid return).** Under
Definition 8.1's quiescence, finite backing, granted progress and serialization,

A quiescent Seek to a retained same-source cut at current distance $D$
uses at most $2D(D+1)$ successful one-cell moves, besides tests, shortened
endpoint attempts, records and returns.

Proof. Consecutive walks are inverse moves along the actual current cut
order by Proposition 13.4, including both inward finite endpoint cases.
Canonical equality in Lemma 13.2 recognizes the saved target, regardless
of whether it is a pre-epoch midpoint, a post-epoch midpoint, an exterior

at most $4n$ moves per stage gives the stated bound; at $D=0$ the initial
equality test suffices. Each resolver and finite counter scan terminates.
An endpoint merely shortens an excursion and its actual inverse return.

Theorem 14.3 supplies the same acquired current operands and versions,
so the current read/walk counts and exact $\mathcal C_h$ follow as in

For epoch acquisition the next header is acquired from the same actual

Actual current-tree acquisition and optional native $\rho$ execution use

## 15. Ownership-specific reductions and their charges

**Definition 15.1 (cache and retained-representation coordinates).** Compare
the two installations from their respective setup on the same entrance
and the same finite native/observation schedule. Let

$$
K=(C,F_{\mathrm{cur}},F_{\mathrm{ret}},I,N)
$$

count only observer-allocated independent frontier cache: cut objects,
current cell objects, retired cell objects, cut-to-cell incidence field
slots, and logical-node association entries. $I$ counts two incidence
slots per observer cut, including null slots. Cell boundary/retirement
fields belong to their cell objects and are charged separately by width;
they are not counted again in $I$. An alias stored in a header, cursor
or record is retained representation material, not an independent cut/cell
object with its own mutable incidence fields.

Retain a separate vector $R$ of all actual descriptor/alias tags and
reference fields in headers, parked/saved slots, immutable records and
pending/scratch backing, together with every allocation, copy and test.
Let $M$ count their retained occurrences, including repeats. Their storage
charge is the sum of actual tag/reference widths over these occurrences;
$M$ is not a constant independent of the record. Seed/epoch backing,
source-owned supplied backing, native source/tapes, version objects,
record headers/links, request/output and work capacity have separate
coordinates. Eliminating $K$ does not set any of those coordinates to zero
or assert that $R$ is smaller than its incidence representation.

**Proposition 15.2 (finite ownership counts).** On the finite known-alpha
entrance with $L\ge1$ epoch leaves and $b$ post-epoch splits, the two
installations have

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_{\mathrm{inc,fin}}&=(L+1+b,\ L+b,\ b,\ 2(L+1+b),\ 2L-1+2b),\\
K_{\mathrm{der,fin}}&=(0,0,0,0,0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{V.14}
$$

Thus $C,F_{\mathrm{cur}},I,N$ strictly decrease for every finite entrance;
$F_{\mathrm{ret}}$ strictly decreases exactly when $b>0$. The finite epoch
headers' cut fields must be replaced by acquired descriptors: retaining
those fields as references to the original auxiliary cuts would retain
the cuts and would not attain (V.14).

Proof. The incidence counts are (V.9); each allocated cut has the two
specified incidence slots. The derived installation performs the same paid
initial tree traversal and epoch enrollment, but constructs only its $L$
seed-header records, epoch bits, paths, boundary descriptor fields and
required slots/workspace. It allocates no initial frontier cache. A finite
header's boundary is an acquired actual branch reference or an exterior
constructor rooted at the actual source, so it does not depend on any
of the omitted auxiliary cut objects. Its descriptor slots and reference
widths remain charged in $R$. At a split the derived installation obtains
the real children through the native template and allocates no cache.
Lemmas 13.2 and 14.2 supply every later requested alias without a retained
node/cut/cell table. Hence all five independent-cache coordinates are zero.
Positivity in the displayed finite incidence vector proves precisely the
stated strict coordinates. $\square$

**Proposition 15.3 (bilateral ownership counts and zero cases).** For a finite
bilateral execution let $b$ be its number of successful post-epoch splits,
$t$ the number of seed components in which at least one split occurred,
and $n$ the actual number of observer-allocated association entries in the
chosen incidence installation. All split components lie in the finite
touched header footprint; $0\le t\le b$. Using the source-owned initial
cells/cuts supplied by Definition 5.1, and allocating the two new cells
and one new cut at every split as in Definition 5.3, gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_{\mathrm{inc,bil}}&=(b,\ b+t,\ b-t,\ 2b,\ n),\\
K_{\mathrm{der,bil}}&=(0,0,0,0,0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{V.15}
$$

Here $n$ is counted from actual allocated entries, not from the size of
an infinite family or from the single-tree formula $2L-1+2b$. In a policy
that allocates an entry for each accessed initial seed root and both actual
children of every split, $n=m_0+2b$, where $m_0$ counts those initial-root
entries. A lazier permitted policy is compared using its actual $n$.

The coordinates $C,F_{\mathrm{cur}},I$ strictly decrease exactly when
$b>0$; $F_{\mathrm{ret}}$ strictly decreases exactly when $b>t$; $N$
strictly decreases exactly when $n>0$. With $b=n=0$ all five coordinates
are equal, even with an enrolled seed. All supplied source-owned material
remains allocated in both installations.

Proof. No initial supplied cut or cell is observer-allocated. Each split
allocates one observer cut and two observer cells. Of the $b$ retired
cells, exactly $t$ are supplied initial cells, namely the first split cell
in each split component. The remaining $b-t$ are observer cells. Hence
of $2b$ allocated observer cells, $b-t$ are retired and $b+t$ are current.
There are $2b$ incidence slots on observer cuts. Association entries have
their actual separate count $n$; enrollment alone does not imply two new
observer cuts. The derived construction never allocates these cache objects.
The displayed differences prove each strictness condition.

For an explicit source-backing footprint, take any finite set of touched
seed headers containing all edited components and all adjacent headers
actually read. Count its distinct supplied headers, seed roots, initial
cells, boundaries and incidence slots separately in both installations.
For $m$ consecutive components their two-sided initial boundary set has
$m+1$ distinct boundaries; for a nonconsecutive footprint use its actual
union. These supplied objects and their actual preparation/source rights
are retained, with no negative allocation credited to the derived observer.
The $t$ retired supplied cells remain material. Source-owned initial
incidences are not deleted or counted as observer cache. In the derived
installation they are initial backing, not a newly promised current-incidence
port; current neighbors are actually obtained by Definition 13.3. The
named current consumers use those paid resolutions and retain their
source/header rights. If a separate supplied-backing contract additionally
requires maintenance of its raw incidence fields, that backing and its
maintenance must also be retained and charged; no saving in that coordinate
is asserted here. Such a contract is not inferred from supplying initial
incidences in Definition 1.3.

In particular one enrolled unchanged component can have $b=n=0$, with its
boundaries already supplied. Equation (V.15) then gives equality, not an
invented strict cut saving. Conversely the first real split already gives
$b=t=1$ and positive observer cut/current-cell/incidence allocations in the
incidence installation, independently of any optional node-entry policy.
$\square$

**Proposition 15.4 (initialization, access, maintenance and record bill).**
The structural differences (V.14)–(V.15) preserve the same original task
and source domain with the following paid residual material and work.

| Coordinate | Derived installation's retained material or actual work |
| --- | --- |
| Source and original service | Same actual root(s), prehistory/preparations, source vertices, native template writes, ancestral tapes, original frames/replies and Next effects |
| Epoch and header access | Same actual finite enrollment/tag/path facts or bilateral source bindings and enrolled epoch bits; paid list/header scans, previous/next reads and root matching |
| Boundary/cell representation | Each occurrence of a typed descriptor/alias in headers, cursors, saved slots, records and pending state, including all reference widths and equality tests |
| Source resolution | Every arity and parent/child/header read, side comparison, null test, finite seed search and authentic operand-path acquisition |
| Work backing | Actual recursion/ascent frames, side bits, pending operands, counters, path reversals and comparison references; peak live space and all retained cleared capacity |
| Version and records | Initial token, one token per successful event, predecessor/source/operand/kind fields, current-token reads/publication, actual record allocation/fields/links/appends |
| Transactions and output | Actual rights/grants/denials, pending phases, reservation, output construction/Print/End, local ACK, root Return and clearing |

For example, for a finite run of resolver/alias operations indexed by $j$,
let $u_j,w_j,q_j,A_j$ be its actual primitive field-read, field-write,
test and work-allocation counts. The resolver bill is the vector

$$
\left(\sum_j u_j,\ \sum_j w_j,\ \sum_j q_j,\ \sum_j A_j\right),
\tag{V.16}
$$

besides its retained-width/capacity charges and unchanged native/record/output
bill. Every header scan and inverse-direction derivation is included.
These are the derived installation's counts, not an unsupported difference
from the incidence installation's own paid access bill. If a Seek's
successful one-cell moves each cost at most $(u_*,w_*,q_*,A_*)$ in this
coordinate convention, their contribution is at most $2D(D+1)$ times
that vector. Initial target equality, endpoint attempts, enrollment,
version/record work and return transactions are additional counted work.

Finite setup traverses the actual pre-epoch tree, reads its $L$ tags,
retains the same authentic epoch roots/paths and acquires its header-boundary
descriptors by actual parent/child reads. This may use more work than simply
retaining cuts from a traversal. It requires finite scratch/reservation,
not free access to all ancestors or a rank-to-reference map. Bilateral
setup reads the supplied anchor/header fields; later enrollment and
newly touched header retention are paid before their use or mutation.
An unenrolled seed is unchanged, as in Definition 5.1. No complete bilateral
table or indexed version-tree access is installed.

At a promotion, Definition 5.3 already preserves the old frontier cell
and cuts. There is no claimed strictly positive frontier-maintenance
saving for a $P$-only step. Both installations still pay enrollment when
needed, native writes, ancestor tape appends, tokens, records and returns.
At each split the incidence installation allocates one cut and two cells,
retires its old cell and writes the four cut incidences described in
Definition 5.3, together with its chosen node-entry maintenance. The derived
installation omits these independent-cache allocations and writes. This
is a real maintenance reduction when those writes belong to the declared
cache; supplied-backing work required by a separate contract is kept
separate. Arbitrary edits remain available because the reduced cache has
no new guard. There is no maintenance or observation outside its lawful
serialized phase.

Proof. Propositions 13.4 and 14.2 expand each claimed access into the stated
actual reads, tests and finite loops. The source and header rights are
already those used by §§5–8; an alias grants no new right. Each expansion
can store its finite path and counters in the backed lists of Proposition
10.3, with their actual allocations/copies/clearing. No bulk or inverse
access is hidden in (V.16). Lemma 13.2 requires no persistent frontier
update, so the indicated cache writes really are omitted at setup and
splits; it does not omit any native or tape write. Source growth and version
counts remain respectively AA's $1+P+2S$ for a whole original finite source
and $1+a+b$ for post-epoch tokens. Record counts and logical acquired facts
are the same, but their alias fields are charged according to their actual
representation, including repetition across old records. These observations
prove the simultaneous bill and the move contribution bound. $\square$

**Proposition 15.5 (already allocated material and optional migration).**
No physical storage reduction for an already installed incidence observer
follows by applying the alias correspondence. In particular no immutable
record, externally retained reference, supplied object or cleared buffer
is deallocated by (V.14) or (V.15).

Proof. Those equations count allocations in the two alternative installations
from setup. In an existing incidence installation every old cut/cell/entry
and its retained fields still occupies material. At a returned serialized
cut, a possible conversion reads each retained live slot and record
binding, acquires the boundary/cell descriptor through the actual source
and authenticated header associations, and writes additional representation
backing. Searching those finite old associations and matching supplied
boundary references to retained headers is paid; a boundary-to-header
inverse is not granted. Existing immutable records are kept, and any
translation for their old references requires additional retained bindings.
All old cache, old records, newly allocated translation material, workspace
and remaining raw-field obligations must still be counted. Without an
independently supplied reclamation operation and its rights, clearing or
ceasing to use those objects releases none of their material. Hence
conversion may permit later derived navigation, but gives no automatic
retroactive allocation saving. The positive comparison is the directly
installed alternative of Definition 14.1. $\square$

## 16. Reused suppliers and exact scope of the reduction

**Definition 16.1 (mathematical and source suppliers).** Huet's
[*The Zipper*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956796897002864), Journal of
Functional Programming 7(5), 549–554 (1997), supplies the classical
focus/context method for navigating and locally modifying a structured
tree. That method is reused, not asserted as a new abstract tree theorem.
Here the retained source itself supplies the actual parent/left/right
relations; it is not a newly available applicative copy, reset or graft
operation. The boundary/alias and ownership arguments above concern the
particular AA native source and its two AAV entrances.

Bille and Gørtz, [*Random Access in Persistent Strings*](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ISAAC.2020.48),
ISAAC 2020, LIPIcs 181, Article 48, treats access with a given complete
version tree and indexed edits. Its access assumptions are not source
acquisition, native operand authentication or a paid source-header inverse.
No complexity bound from that model is transferred here.

AA §§17–23, especially Assumption 20.1, supply actual retained source
fields, reference equality, birth typing, unary/logical distinctions,
ancestral tapes, original frames and the real native Next driver.
AAII §§95–96 supply paid traversal/encoding facts on their own installation;
their raw-ordinal journal does not replace the ancestral tapes here.
AAIV §§13–26 supply history/recorder/supplement distinctions, and §§40–51
supply the immutable marker/Resume/return and original consumer results.
AAV §§1–12 already supply the positive native-edit bridge, real epoch/Tag
access, versioning, source correspondence and finite acquired consumers.
Their conditional positive result is retained in full. Geometry, storage
atlases and existing formal suppliers keep their own domains; no new
formal-verification claim is attached to these prose deductions.

**Proposition 16.2 (attained structural reduction and remaining boundaries).**
For the same finite known-alpha and supplied bilateral entrances, the
source-derived installation preserves the complete stated original/current
service and actual native continuation. It removes independently maintained
observer frontier material in exactly the positive coordinates and cases
of Propositions 15.2–15.3, with the separately paid representation,
initialization, work and record bill of Proposition 15.4. In particular
it gives a genuine finite cache reduction and a bilateral post-split cache
reduction; it asserts equality in every zero coordinate specified there.

This does not prove a smaller total bit volume, total runtime, energy,
Pareto improvement or global minimum. It does not reduce epoch-label bits,
replace current Tag/read rights by a marker record, create a bilateral
source from finite native material, erase old allocated material, or make
nonquiescent acquisition converge. Recovery of physical elapsed time,
unrecorded request order and revoked source access is still outside the
proved conditions. The full objective of a minimal common relational
structure for space, time, boundary and usable memory remains open.

Proof. Lemma 13.2 gives one persistent identity for every actual cut,
including finite pre-epoch branch cuts. Proposition 13.4 supplies all
existing neighbors, including inward endpoints, by actual paid source
access. Lemma 14.2 preserves typed cell/operand aliases and old retirement
relations. Theorems 14.3–14.4 give the entire same-consumer correspondence,
finite quiescent acquisition and actual return without narrowing either
entrance. Propositions 15.2–15.4 establish the stated allocation and
maintenance differences while keeping supplied material and representation
charges separate. Proposition 15.5 precludes retroactive erasure. None
of these arguments compares the sum of all resource coordinates or
eliminates their hypotheses; Proposition 9.1 and the distinctions of
§11 continue to apply. $\square$
