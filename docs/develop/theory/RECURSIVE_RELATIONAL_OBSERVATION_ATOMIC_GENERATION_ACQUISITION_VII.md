# Recursive relational observation: actual native target coverage and attained cuts

## 78. Acquired native targets on the unchanged entrances

**Definition 78.1 (source domain and mathematical status).** Write AA for
[Atomic generation acquisition](RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_ATOMIC_GENERATION_ACQUISITION.md),
AAV for [Acquisition V](RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_ATOMIC_GENERATION_ACQUISITION_V.md),
and VI for [Acquisition VI](RECURSIVE_RELATIONAL_OBSERVATION_ATOMIC_GENERATION_ACQUISITION_VI.md).
Use precisely AA17's free ordered syntax and native rules

$$
t::=\alpha\mid\beta\mid\langle t,t\rangle,
\qquad P_p:\alpha\longmapsto\beta,
\qquad S_p:\beta\longmapsto\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle.
\tag{VII.1}
$$

The entrances are AAV1.2's actual finite known-alpha installation after
its retained finite prehistory, and AAV1.3's selected bilateral joint
source with its genuine separately empty/P-prepared known-alpha roots.
The fixed selected seed, actual anchor and original joint binding remain.
There is no independent seed product, common preparation word, source
replacement, associativity quotient, reset, deletion or reverse edit.
Every finite lawful local P/S continuation, every original query and
refusal, and genuine Next remain admitted under their original premises.

All statements below are conditional mathematics in theory prose. They
assert no new Lean verification. The actual source, tapes, versions,
actor/source bindings, current grants, enrollment, samples and record
endpoints retain AAV5–7,20–24 and VI58.1's semantics. A maintained source
cut has no unfinished source edit, tape append or unpublished successful
event. It need not specify the observation cursor, latch, record endpoint
or controller instant. Where a returned source cut is required, the
original Print, End, local ACK, Return and cleanup have really finished.
These different predicates on a full process cut are not identified.

**Definition 78.2 (authenticated finite footprint).** At an actually
acquired maintained version u, use VI59.1–59.2 to acquire a finite list
of source subtree bindings

$$
X=((c_1,p_1,x_1,b_1,t_1),\ldots,(c_k,p_k,x_k,b_k,t_k)).
\tag{VII.2}
$$

Here c is an acquired original component binding, including its actual
source F and original known-alpha root; p is its original-root-relative
logical address; x is the actual object reached by paid arity-tested
navigation; b is that logical position's authentic birth type; and t is
its complete ordered subtree at u. Within a component the p's are prefix
free. Repetitions are removed by actual component/path equality. The
empty list is allowed. Its future region consists of these same logical
roots and their descendants, not a fixed list of material terminal cells.

The finite entrance has one original root. Its epoch leaves are not new
original installations, and their prehistory is not discarded. Bilaterally,
obtain each component through actual header/root traversal, retaining the
finite connecting header interval when needed. Integers naming owners
are mathematical notation; they are not header references. Each selected
component keeps its own empty or actual root-P preparation. There is no
asserted chronological merge of preparations in distinct components.

A root of an original component is born alpha, including a selected epoch
beta root with an actual P preparation. A nonroot logical position is born
beta if its last side is L and alpha if its last side is R. A beta marker
before its owner's split is not a logical child at all. When S reuses it
as the left logical root, that new logical position is beta-born; the
owner's earlier promotion is not a promotion of the child. The acquired
original paths, arities and provenance establish these types.

Acquisition of t at u may be completed after later source growth. VI59.2
supplies the old complete tree and paid navigation to the identical old
objects. VI72–74 may instead supply a complete tree at its attained R,
which can serve as u once its actual version and bindings are retained.
Neither supplier's completion is strengthened here. In particular, the
initial availability of X is a hypothesis of the following use theorem;
that theorem does not make asynchronous whole-tree acquisition wait-free.

**Definition 78.3 (event support and finite lawful target).** An event
key is the triple (actual component binding, kind, original-root-relative
logical address). Within one component each such possible P or S occurs
at most once. The original successful history supplies its unique actual
occurrence when it exists. Keys are compared only with their authentic
component bindings; equal letters at unrelated roots are not equal events.

For a birth-typed subtree (b,t), let E_b(t) be the birth-typed event
support of NativeEndpointEventIdeals.events, using AA17.1's event rule
with the original address prefix and component attached. At a branch it
contains S at its root and also P there exactly when that root was born
alpha; recurse on the beta-born left and alpha-born right child. At a
leaf it contains P exactly when that position was born alpha and is now
beta. Thus the initial support B_X is the disjoint union of these supports
for (VII.2). It includes old promotions inside X, however early their
actual preparation or prehistory was. Creating splits strictly above X
are retained as provenance, outside this local support.

A target is a finite backed addressed word

$$
w=e_1\cdots e_n
\tag{VII.3}
$$

whose operations are lawful, in this order, on the acquired forest
T_u=(t_1,\ldots,t_k), all inside X. Its addresses are original-root-relative;
relative subtree addresses, if used in local computation, are explicitly
prefixed by their acquired p. Apply w only as a computation in owned finite
backing to obtain the intended ordered forest T_*. Put K={e_1,...,e_n}
and J=B_X\cup K. Lawfulness implies distinct e_i and K disjoint from B_X.
It also proves each required creating split and local promotion is either
in B_X or earlier in w. Unborn target positions carry structural paths,
not prescribed future allocation names; when a real event creates them,
only actual navigation through its new fields can acquire their references.
Lawfulness is a checkable finite target-domain condition, not an execution,
reference cast, new source read or promised conformance.

The proposition Covered_X(w,v) means that every e in K has actually
occurred in the same source by maintained cut v. It does not require that
this client caused it, that the client's replies were Growth, or that
T_v=T_*. The proposition Attained_X(T_*;a,b) means that at some actual
maintained source cut between acquired endpoints a and b, inclusive, the
restriction to the same X was exactly T_*, with its ordered brackets,
logical birth types and persistent actual object interpretation. Neither
predicate asserts equality of the complete observer or complete service.

## 79. Actual original requests supply the coverage consumer

**Definition 79.1 (finite native client).** Retain the acquired X, u,
checked word w and finite word cursor in owned backing. For i=1,...,n,
select e_i's actual original component through the existing paid binding
interface. Supply its unmodified original P or S frame, including its
original-root-relative address, to AA20.2/AAV7's service. Finish its actual
reply, Print, End, local ACK, Return and cleanup before advancing this
client's word cursor. Retain the literal reply and its ordinary record
binding wherever the installation records it. No new reply tag or source
primitive is introduced. A conclusion about coverage is a theorem about
these original replies, not an extra success response on their wire.

Arbitrary other lawful original transactions, including genuine Next,
may occur between these client transactions, before the first, and after
the last. They may act anywhere, including repeatedly inside X. Each
client transaction uses only the service's original admission and current
rights, with its original exclusion of another native edit or unfinished
tape maintenance during that admitted transaction. There is no exclusion
between target requests, reservation of all old leaves, extra lock,
freeze of X or assumed absence of relevant interference.

Supplying a finite script uses the existing input connection and backed
request bits; it does not insert an opcode inside Receive or bypass an
occupied latch. If the original client is already busy, its pending
continuation must finish through its own enabled path before this script
can start. This definition grants neither admission nor a completion edge
when a right, input suffix, material reservation or progress is missing.

**Theorem 79.2 (coverage under arbitrary lawful local growth).** Fix either
entrance, any authentically acquired X at u, and any finite lawful target
(VII.3). For every original future in which the first j client requests of
Definition 79.1 have completed, all e_1,...,e_j actually occurred by that
jth return, for 1<=j<=n. At j=0 the claimed coverage is empty and holds
at u without any new native invocation or Return. The following are the
only possible completed client outcomes, with their original literal meanings:

| Target request | Original returned outcome | Fact about the required event |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P at its alpha-born position | Growth with that exact kind/address | This transaction performed that P |
| P at its alpha-born position | Refusal, wrong type | The actual beta already required that P |
| P at its alpha-born position | Refusal, branch | That position's actual promotion preceded its split |
| S | Growth with that exact kind/address | This transaction performed that S |
| S | Refusal, branch | That same position already actually split |

In particular, for n>0, after n completions Covered_X(w,v) holds at the
last returned cut v, without a no-conflict or finite-relevant-interference
hypothesis. For n=0 coverage already holds at u and the empty client
performs no native call or manufactured Return. An absent-address refusal,
or wrong-type refusal to S, cannot occur under these hypotheses. Such an outcome would refute at least one
stated source/birth/acquisition/word-order premise; it is not converted
into coverage.

Proof. Use AA17's actual native lifecycle and the persistent-object result
AAV21.2, already justified by AA20.2's real templates. At a logical address
born alpha the statuses progress alpha, beta, branch; at a beta-born
address they progress beta, branch. Only S creates logical children, and
it never replaces its own logical root. P never removes an edge. Every
already created logical route remains bound to the same actual operands.
This applies equally to an event executed by original Next.

Induct on j. Assume the required first j-1 events actually occurred. Every
proper-prefix creating split needed by e_j was already in the acquired
forest or is one of those earlier required events. Those splits persist.
Consequently original Navigate reaches the actual required logical
position. A child not present at u is earned now by following the real
post-split parent/child fields; a computed hypothetical child is never
cast to a reference. Arbitrary additional events cannot remove or redirect
this route or change its component or logical birth type.

If e_j=P_p, lawfulness of w gives alpha birth at p. If p is currently
alpha, its original guard enables P and the completed transaction performs
that event. If beta, its unique P has already occurred. If branch, that
same alpha-born position passed through its P before its S. The latter
two cases are the service's original wrong-type and branch refusals,
respectively; they do not append a new event. If e_j=S_p and p was born
beta, it is currently beta or branch. If born alpha, its required P is
in B_X or earlier in w, so the induction excludes alpha now; it too is
beta or branch. Beta enables the actual split; branch proves that very
split already occurred and gives the original branch refusal. No fourth
constructor is possible at this existing position.

The original maintained source/tape/version invariants put a successful
event before its published version and completed return. A refusal keeps
those fields unchanged. In the refusal cases the witnessed occurrence is
strictly earlier than this transaction's guard, and remains in the actual
history by return. Already covered e_i persist under every intervening
lawful original event. This proves the induction, the table, and the
absence of the two impossible refusals. No event support has been merged
into the source by an abstract set operation. Every new event in the
argument is an original successful execution on its authentic binding.
$\square$

**Proposition 79.3 (precise limits of refusal information).** The table's
past-occurrence inferences require its authenticated birth, address and
predecessor facts. An original refusal alone has no uniform interpretation
as native growth or as past occurrence of its requested event.

Proof. A P request at the beta-born left child immediately after S receives
wrong type although P at that logical child never occurred and never can.
An S request at an actual alpha receives wrong type without an S there.
An absent descendant receives absent although its creating ancestors may
not have split. All are ordinary admitted original calls, and all return
Refusal without changing source or tapes. Missing rights and wrong phase
instead disable the primitive and return none of these words. A proper
frame prefix produces only its pending input state. Thus the narrower
inferences of Theorem 79.2 follow from the full domain proof, not from
renaming Refusal, disabled input or silence as Growth. $\square$

## 80. Finite progress and the resource boundary

**Theorem 80.1 (bounded client calls with original conditional progress).**
In Theorem 79.2 assume additionally that each next client request is
actually admitted after finitely many intervening original transactions,
its finite complete frame is supplied, its required current grants and
finite reservation/backing are available, and each of its original finite
phases makes the progress required by AA20.1–20.5/AAV7.2. Include the same
premises for each paid component-selection operation the client uses.
Then the finite client returns after at most n completed P/S calls and
covers K. It uses no retry for source conflict. No bound on the total
number of relevant intervening events is assumed for the surrounding
continuing future.

More precisely let d_i be the fixed original-root-relative depth of e_i.
The total client request payload, including original tags, is

$$
\sum_{i=1}^n(2d_i+4)\text{ bits}.
\tag{VII.4}
$$

Its literal replies have total length at most

$$
\sum_{i=1}^n(2d_i+5)\text{ bits}.
\tag{VII.5}
$$

If G is the subset of its calls returning Growth, the client's successful
source templates add at most 2|G| source vertices, at most |G| split tape
headers, and exactly sum_{i in G} d_i ancestor tape cells in AA's native
layout. Its own successful events add exactly |G| successor version tokens
in AAV's layout. The installation's additional incidence, enrollment,
record, bit, counter, path, reservation, acknowledgement and return work
remains charged at the original rates. Acquisition, component selection,
other actors' work and subsequent history certification are separate
finite costs, not included in these particular counts.

Proof. Each call of depth d has the actual prefix-free frame consisting
of its three-bit tag and A(p)=1^d0p of length 2d+1. Its Growth reply has a
three-bit tag, one kind bit and A(p), hence 2d+5 bits; a Refusal has five
bits and satisfies the same bound. Theorem 79.2 supplies the only outcome
cases. A successful P adds one source marker. A successful S reuses its
old unary marker as its left logical beta root and allocates that child's
marker and the right alpha root, two source vertices, plus the target's
empty split tape header. Maintain appends one side cell at each of the
d proper logical ancestors. A refusal allocates no successful-event source
template, tape append or version token, although its real request, output
and record work is still paid.

Navigate traverses the fixed acquired path of d_i sides; even if its last
operand has grown enormously below that path, this request does not walk
to a new descendant in place of that operand. Maintenance and native root
Return use the actual finite retained route. The original finite-service
premises therefore complete each admitted call. Finite admission delays
and induction through the finite word finish the client; Theorem 79.2
gives coverage at that actual return. This is a bound on the client's
calls and the displayed resource coordinates, not a new bound on physical
time, grants, selection, bit lengths of references or all process work.
$\square$

**Definition 80.2 (prefix progress and continued futures).** At the jth
completed client call put C_j={e_1,...,e_j}. These are n+1 finite monotone
milestones in actual event coverage, ending in K, regardless of whether a
particular completed call contributed a new source event. The logical
count n-j is a remaining-client-work bound, not an assertion that the
source was unchanged while the client waited. Other legal work can
continue after the last milestone, for arbitrarily many further P/S/Next
events in X and outside X under continued supply.

**Proposition 80.3 (what the progress premises cannot be dropped to).**
There is no unconditional wall-clock or schedule-step completion bound
from n and the acquired forest alone, and no completion assertion when
admission, finite input or the original progress conditions are absent.

Proof. Delay the next client admission for any specified finite number
of other lawful original calls, queries or waiting transitions. This
preserves the target's lawfulness at u and Theorem 79.2's coverage invariant
on completed prefixes, but exceeds the proposed schedule bound. After a
real split, repeated genuine in-range Next(1) supplies arbitrarily many
further successful growth calls with sufficient material; a quiescence
condition is not implicit. Withhold a required input suffix to keep
Receive pending, withhold the current edit right to disable the primitive,
or withhold progress at a pending maintenance/output edge. None produces
a completed client response. These examples do not refute Theorem 80.1,
whose finite admission, supply and per-call progress hypotheses exclude
exactly those failures. $\square$

**Theorem 81.2 (finite usable chronological data).** With the paid read

termination under the stated service premises, without a bound on growth

## 82. Exact attainment from coverage order and first extra event

**Definition 82.1 (interval test).** Let J be the target support from
Definition 78.3 and B the actually acquired support at a. Put
R=J\setminus B and use the acquired finite D to compute

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau&=\begin{cases}
0,&R=\varnothing,\\
\max\{j:d_j\in R\},&R\subseteq\{d_1,...,d_m\},\ R\ne\varnothing,\\
+\infty,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}\\
\sigma&=\min\big(\{j:d_j\notin J\}\cup\{m+1\}\big).
\end{aligned}
\tag{VII.8}
$$

Return true exactly when B is a subset of J, tau is finite and tau<sigma.
In particular the test is false whenever B is not a subset of J. These
positions are backed counts from a genuinely acquired list, not exposed allocation
ordinals, physical times or free absolute source indices. Empty R and
empty D are included. When the test is true retain a if tau=0 and the
actual token of d_tau otherwise as its historical source-cut witness.
The test is a finite local computation on paid acquired data, not a new
read port or original reply type. If the consumer additionally requests
every actual vertex/cut reference of the witnessed target, apply VI59.2
at that retained witness under its actual read rights and finite progress
premises. This separately paid recovery acquires those historical bindings;
the prefix test alone does not cast its target paths into references.

**Theorem 82.2 (necessary and sufficient actual attainment).** Under the
domains and earned acquisition hypotheses above,

$$
\operatorname{Attained}_X(T_*;a,b)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B\subseteq J,\quad \tau<+\infty,\quad \tau<\sigma.
\tag{VII.9}
$$

When it holds, the witness in Definition 82.1 is an actual maintained
source cut in [a,b] with the required ordered tree and genuine persistent
source interpretation. It is the first such X-event prefix in that
interval. The full set of local chronological prefix lengths attaining
the target is exactly

$$
\{j:\tau\le j<\sigma\},\qquad 0\le j\le m.
\tag{VII.10}
$$

Thus coverage by b alone is insufficient. Existence of a compatible
causal ideal in the birth-typed local final event order is also insufficient.
Neither is substituted for the acquired chronological-prefix test.

Proof. Use the existing AA17.2 endpoint reconstruction and the birth-typed
NativeEndpointEventIdeals.cut_events/execution_adequacy correspondence.
For completeness about its application, at a present root the presence
of S distinguishes branch from leaf. In its absence, alpha birth and P
membership distinguish alpha from beta; beta birth requires beta. At a
branch recurse on its actual ordered beta-born left and alpha-born right
positions. Hence on the authentic domain equality of these local event
supports is equivalent to equality of the entire ordered birth-typed
forest, not just its frontier word. Original paths and AAV21.2's actual
navigation identify its operands with this very source; reconstruction
of syntax alone would not acquire that identity.

By Theorem 81.2 every maintained source cut of [a,b] has, inside X, the
support of one actual prefix of D, and each listed prefix is actually
attained: prefix zero at a and positive prefix j at d_j's published
maintained cut. Other source events and controller work do not change
that restriction. Therefore the desired assertion is exactly existence
of 0<=j<=m with

$$
B\cup\{d_1,...,d_j\}=J.
\tag{VII.11}
$$

If B is not a subset of J, monotonicity makes (VII.11) impossible. Suppose
it is. To include all of R, every required event must be present in D and
j must be at least tau. Distinctness and disjointness from B ensure no
other membership issue is hidden by a repeated key. To avoid any event
outside J, j must be strictly less than sigma. These are simultaneously
possible exactly under (VII.9), and then j=tau is allowed. The same two
inequalities give all and only (VII.10). A maintained witness is supplied
by the actual retained token of that prefix, not by executing a synthetic
linear extension or assuming a controller checkpoint there. $\square$

**Corollary 82.3 (coverage and attainment for the native client).** Suppose
the finite client of Theorem 80.1 completes, and subsequently acquire a
version b at or after that completion, together with the interval [u,b]
as in §81. With a=u, B=B_X and R=K, coverage guarantees finite tau in
(VII.8). For nonempty K, the intended target actually occurred in that
interval if and only if no event of X outside B_X union K preceded the
last required event. For empty K, the target is already attained at u and
the test is true. This is the exact additional relation still needed after
the coverage guarantee; it is computed from the actual paid native history.

Proof. For nonempty K, each required event actually occurred after u and
by the last client return: none was in B_X and events are unique. For
empty K, T_*=T_u, witnessed by u itself. VI59's frozen-prefix
acquisition therefore finds them by b, even if they were caused by other
actors or genuine Next and the client received refusals. Theorem 82.2
reduces to tau<sigma. Its criterion tolerates arbitrary outside-X growth,
and arbitrary extra X growth after tau. Such later growth can make the
current forest strictly larger without undoing the past attainment.
It also permits actual target events in another lawful order; if the
stronger question is whether the literal target word w was executed as a
specified contiguous or projected word, compare that additional acquired
sequence explicitly. Equality of an ordered source tree does not certify
a chosen linear extension. $\square$

**Proposition 82.4 (final equality, compatible ideal and actual cut).**
If the final local tree at b equals T_*, then the target is attained at b.
If only its event support contains J, J is a compatible lower set in the
final birth-typed causal order restricted to X, with its already acquired
external creating history fixed as context, but it need not be an attained
prefix of the actual chronology. If J is attained and a later X event
occurs outside J, the final local tree differs from T_* although attainment
stays true.

Proof. The first assertion uses b itself. For the second, w is lawful on
the actual forest at u, so every event prerequisite of J lies in J. Local
lifecycle and creating-split dependencies are exactly the native causal
order by NativeEndpointCausalOrder.result; actual unrelated later branches
do not introduce new predecessors of an old event. For a proper subtree
root, its creating ancestors strictly above X are already supplied by the
authentic u history. They are fixed contextual predecessors, not omitted
requirements for realizing an ideal of the entire original component.
Thus containment makes J a supported lower set. NativeEndpointEventIdeals already supplies a
legal realization of such an ideal, not a proof that this realization was
an actual prefix of this source's chosen history. Theorem 82.2 supplies
the missing chronological condition. For the third assertion, an extra
actual event adds a key permanently, so support reconstruction excludes
final equality while the retained earlier witness remains. The next
section gives both truth values with an identical final tree/support.
$\square$

## 83. Authentic same-endpoint executions with different attainment

**Proposition 83.1 (an attained target and a compatible unattained one).**
On each unchanged entrance there are two actual finite original executions
from the same acquired footprint and the same lawful target w, ending
with identical ordered source trees and generation-event support, one
with target attainment and one without. They can both include a genuine
Next. Their original tapes, acquired chronological histories and complete
service records remain distinct.

Proof. In the finite entrance use the actual known-alpha original root r,
execute and fully return from its $P_\varepsilon$ and $S_\varepsilon$, and acquire
u with $t_u=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle$ by §78's paid supplier. Let X be this actual whole
component. Retain the real preparation/prehistory word
$P_\varepsilon S_\varepsilon$ and its tapes. Use the lawful target

$$
w=S_LP_{LR},\qquad
T_*=\langle\langle\beta,\beta\rangle,\alpha\rangle.
\tag{VII.12}
$$

The second event's operand is the alpha-born right child actually created
by the first split; it is not available by treating the old left beta's
unary marker as that right child. Consider these two future words:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &: S_L,\ P_{LR},\ P_R,\ S_{LL},\\
B &: P_R,\ S_L,\ P_{LR},\ S_{LL}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{VII.13}
$$

Each displayed operation is lawful at its displayed time. For the last
event in both executions, actually call original Next(1), not an S frame.
Its internal FetchGrowth(1) recalls the original root P. At that point
the root is branch, its left child is branch, and LL is the beta-born
beta leaf. The original current left descent therefore executes S_LL
and returns the actual old/new pairs (P,epsilon),(S,LL). Its tape/version
maintenance and outer Print/End/ACK/Return are completed normally. Thus
(VII.13) records actual successful event words, with the last event earned
through genuine original Next. The external invocation histories still
contain that Next frame and its complete reply, not a replacement S call.

## 87. Downward closure of the actual occupied code set

**Theorem 87.1 (Boolean ideality and the maximal prefix cylinder).** For
every finite reachable native endpoint $t$, the actual set $B_t$ is a
nonempty Boolean order ideal of cardinality $F(t)$. For every
$0\le d\le\ell_t$ and every $q\in\{0,1\}^d$,

$$
|\{x\in B_t:q\preceq x\}|\le z_t(d).
\tag{VII.16}
$$

The occupied set at an endpoint is independent of permutations within
a group of equal lower width. This assertion concerns the occupied
set only; the assignment of its words to the labelled histories remains
the exact assignment in Definition 86.1.

**Proof.** For a fixed output width, distinct equal-length lower words
have disjoint sets of allowed leaves. Tokens with the same lower word
successively take the first, second and subsequent missing leaves of
one allowed subtree. Hence changing the within-width order leaves the
final occupied set unchanged, with every repeated lower token still
counted. It can change which complete history receives which leaf.
This proves exactly the stated occupied-set independence.

Induct on $N(t)$. At alpha, $B_\alpha=\{\varepsilon\}$ is a nonempty
ideal. At a noninitial endpoint fix its prescribed common width
$L=\ell_t$. Allocation feasibility is IV Lemma 18.2, independently of
any ideality assertion. Process the lower-width groups in their
prescribed decreasing order. Initially the occupied subset $S$ of the
length-$L$ cube is empty and is an ideal.

Suppose $S$ is an ideal before a group of lower width $l$. For each
$l$-bit word $b$ write

$$
S_b=\{w\in\{0,1\}^{L-l}:bw\in S\},\qquad
 a_b=\sum_{\substack{e:u\to t\\\ell_u=l}}\mathbf1_{\{b\in B_u\}}.
\tag{VII.17}
$$

The second expression is the exact number of this group's tokens
whose lower word is $b$: each predecessor table is injective, while
all labelled incoming edges remain in the sum. Each section $S_b$
is a suffix-cube ideal. If $b'\le b$, then $S_{b'}\supseteq S_b$
and, by predecessor ideality, $a_{b'}\ge a_b$.

Two elementary facts about filling missing elements apply. In a finite
linearly ordered universe, let $A\supseteq B$ and let $a\ge b$ be
nonnegative integers for which both fillings are feasible. Adjoining
the first $a$ missing elements to $A$ contains the result of adjoining
the first $b$ missing elements to $B$. Indeed an element of the latter
result already in $A$ is included; otherwise its rank among missing
elements of $A$ is at most its rank among missing elements of $B$,
which is at most $b\le a$. Also, adjoining an initial lexicographic
segment of the missing words to a Boolean ideal preserves ideality:
every coordinatewise smaller distinct missing word is lexicographically
earlier, so it has already been adjoined.

Apply these facts in each common suffix universe. By occupied-set
independence, the group replaces $S_b$ by $S_b$ together with its first
$a_b$ missing suffixes. The new sections are ideals and remain nested
under $b'\le b$. Consequently, if $bw$ is newly occupied and
$b'w'\le bw$, section nesting first gives $b'w$ and suffix ideality
then gives $b'w'$. Thus the whole group preserves ideality. Every group
is feasible, so induction through all groups proves that $B_t$ is an
ideal. The original allocation gives exactly one distinct leaf to each
history, proving nonemptiness and cardinality $F(t)$.

Finally the map $qw\mapsto0^dw$ is an injection from the $q$ section
of $B_t$ into its $0^d$ section: the target is coordinatewise smaller
and hence belongs to the ideal. This proves (VII.16), including $d=0$
and the length-zero cube. $\square$

## 88. Exact scalar recursion for width and congestion

**Theorem 88.1 (zero-prefix profile recursion on every native endpoint).**
The widths and profiles of the original $J$ are determined by

$$
\ell_\alpha=0,\qquad z_\alpha(0)=1.
\tag{VII.18}
$$

For a noninitial $t$ put $m_t=\max_{e:u\to t}\ell_u$ and, for every
integer $d\ge0$, define

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_t(d)&=\sum_{\substack{e:u\to t\\\ell_u\ge d}}z_u(d),\\
b_t(d)&=|\{e:u\to t:\ell_u<d\}|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{VII.19}
$$

In particular $A_t(d)=0$ for $d>m_t$. For $0\le d\le m_t$ it is
positive, and the exact recursion is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell_t&=\max_{0\le d\le m_t}
       \left(d+\left\lceil\log_2A_t(d)\right\rceil\right),\\
z_t(d)&=\min\{2^{\ell_t-d},\ A_t(d)+b_t(d)\}
                  &&(0\le d\le\ell_t),\\
F(t)C(t)&=\max_{0\le d\le m_t}2^dA_t(d).
\end{aligned}
\tag{VII.20}
$$

Here $C(t)$ is precisely IV Definition 19.4's congestion; at alpha its
separate convention is $C(\alpha)=1$. Every predecessor has smaller
native grade, so (VII.18)–(VII.20) are a terminating finite recursion.
They compute the widths and these scalar profiles without choosing
new codebooks or discarding any history multiplicity.

**Proof.** A nonempty ideal of width $w$ contains $0^w$. A predecessor
of maximal width therefore proves $A_t(d)>0$ at every $d\le m_t$.
For a depth-$d$ prefix $q$, predecessors of width below $d$ contribute
nothing to IV19.4's $D_t(q)$. Apply Theorem 87.1 separately to every
other predecessor to obtain

$$
D_t(q)\le D_t(0^d)=A_t(d)\qquad(0\le d\le m_t).
\tag{VII.21}
$$

There are no prefixes of lower words at depths greater than $m_t$.
Substitution into IV.40 proves the first line of (VII.20), and
substitution into the definition of $C$ proves the third. The depth
$m_t$ term also proves $\ell_t\ge m_t$.

Fix $d\le\ell_t$. All tokens of lower width at least $d$ are processed
before shorter ones. Exactly $A_t(d)$ of them have lower prefix $0^d$;
each of these must and does occupy one leaf of the output cylinder
$0^d\{0,1\}^{\ell_t-d}$. Every other such token stays outside.
For $d>m_t$ this first group is empty, consistently with $A_t(d)=0$.

For a predecessor of width $w<d$, ideality and injection imply that
exactly one of its lower words can enter that cylinder: its word $0^w$.
A nonzero $w$-bit lower word disagrees with the cylinder before depth
$d$. Hence exactly $b_t(d)$ remaining tokens can enter. For each such
zero token the cylinder is the lexicographically first block of its
allowed leaves. It takes a leaf there if and only if the cylinder has
not yet filled. No other remaining token changes that occupancy.
The final count is therefore its initial count $A_t(d)$ plus one per
eligible shorter token until the capacity $2^{\ell_t-d}$ is reached.
This is the second line of (VII.20).

At $d=0$ there are no shorter tokens, and the formula gives
$z_t(0)=A_t(0)=\sum_eF(u)=F(t)$; IV18.2 ensures enough capacity.
At $d=\ell_t$ it gives occupancy one, as also follows from ideality.
Width-zero noninitial endpoints are included: they have
$m_t=0$, $A_t(0)=1$ and $z_t(0)=1$. In particular the sole predecessor
of beta is the forced root promotion from alpha, so beta has the same
width-zero profile as alpha. $\square$
