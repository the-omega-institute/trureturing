# Signed spanning capacity and perfect Lee-ball boundaries

## 1. Signed coefficients and invariant factors

**Definition 1.1 (signed spanning and perfection).** Write $\mathbb Z_q=\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z$ for $q\ge1$. Let $G$ be a finite abelian group, $m,s\ge1$, and let $a_1,\ldots,a_m$ be distinct elements of $G$, with underlying set $A$. Define

$$
B_m(s)=\left\{x\in\mathbb Z^m:\sum_{i=1}^m|x_i|\le s\right\},
\qquad f_A(x)=\sum_{i=1}^m x_i a_i.
$$

The set $A$ is signed $s$-spanning if $f_A(B_m(s))=G$, and is signed $s$-perfect if the restriction $f_A:B_m(s)\longrightarrow G$ is bijective. These definitions are invariant under enumeration of $A$. In particular, uniqueness refers to integer coefficient vectors, not to words in the generators. For $m=2$ write the generators as $a,b$ and the coefficient map as $f(x,y)=xa+yb$. A spanning pair need not have the regular form $\{(1,u),(1,v)\}$ in an invariant-factor presentation.

The mathematical source for the additional capacities and perfect-set deductions below is Haobo Ma and Wenlin Zhang, *Bajnok–Beatty Conjectures 4.1 and 1.6: classification and extremal noncyclic order*, revision 2, 3 November 2026, sections “The lower ranges and completion”, “Residue counts and Conjecture 1.6”, and “Coverage and verification”; adapted with attribution under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The posing source is B. Bajnok and W. K. Beatty, [arXiv:2406.04045v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04045v1), especially Theorems 1.7–1.9, 3.2–3.3 and Conjecture 1.10. The published bibliographic record is [doi:10.2140/cnt.2025.14.1](https://doi.org/10.2140/cnt.2025.14.1); the numbered comparisons here refer to the original v1.

**Lemma 1.2 (coefficient-residue fibres).** Let $c\ge2$, $k\ge1$, and $G=\mathbb Z_c\times\mathbb Z_{ck}$. Suppose $f:\mathbb Z^2\to G$, $f(x,y)=xa+yb$, is surjective. Then $\ker f\subseteq c\mathbb Z^2$. The induced map $(\mathbb Z_c)^2\to G/cG$ is an isomorphism. Each coefficient residue modulo $c$ corresponds to exactly one coset of $cG$, containing $k$ elements. Consequently, if $a,b$ are signed $s$-spanning, every coefficient residue class contains at least $k$ points of $B_2(s)$.

Proof. The induced map is surjective. Both its domain and codomain have $c^2$ elements: the first factor of $G/cG$ has order $c$, and the quotient of $\mathbb Z_{ck}$ by its multiples of $c$ has order $c$. Hence this map is injective as well. A kernel vector therefore has both coordinates divisible by $c$. Also $|cG|=k$. Fix a residue class and its corresponding coset of $cG$. Every element of that coset has a representative in $B_2(s)$ by spanning, and injectivity of the induced map forces that representative into the fixed coefficient residue. Distinct group elements require distinct coefficient vectors, giving the claimed lower count. This is the same coefficient-kernel mechanism as [LeeSquareCompression.kernel_coordinates_dvd](../../../D5/S3/FiniteGroups/LeeSquareCompression.lean).

The usual invariant-factor classification applies to every noncyclic finite abelian group generated by two elements: it is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z_c\times\mathbb Z_{ck}$ with $c\ge2,k\ge1$. Indeed a surjection from $\mathbb Z^2$ presents the group by a full-rank relation lattice; Smith reduction gives two positive invariant factors $d_1\mid d_2$. Noncyclicity is precisely $d_1\ge2$, so take $c=d_1$ and $k=d_2/d_1$. Signed covering and perfection transport through this isomorphism. This standard quotient-lattice description is the intermediate framework of Dougherty and Faber, *The degree-diameter problem for several varieties of Cayley graphs, I: The Abelian case*, [doi:10.1137/S0895480100372899](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895480100372899), Proposition 2 in [arXiv:math/9412223v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9412223v2).

## 2. Even first invariant factor, including small radii

**Theorem 2.1 (even capacity and equality divisibility).** Let $c\ge2$ be even and $k,s\ge1$. Every signed $s$-spanning pair in $\mathbb Z_c\times\mathbb Z_{ck}$ satisfies

$$
|G|=c^2k\le2s^2.
$$

If equality holds then $c\mid s$. If $s$ is odd, the stronger conclusion is $|G|\le2s^2-2$. There is no regularity hypothesis on the pair.

Proof. For $s\ge3$ reuse the existing fixed-invariant-factor bound [LeeSquareCompression.arbitrary_pair_upper_bound](../../../D5/S3/FiniteGroups/LeeSquareCompression.lean): for any covering pair it gives

$$
c^2k\le2s^2
\quad\text{or}\quad
\left(c\text{ odd},\ c\mid2s+1,\ 2c^2k\le(2s+1)^2-c^2\right).
$$

The second alternative contradicts even $c$, so the first applies. This uses the existing core upper bound without repeating its lattice-compression proof.

It remains to justify the positive radii outside that theorem's range. At $s=1$, the coefficient residue $(1,1)$ modulo any even $c\ge2$ has minimum possible $\ell^1$-norm $2$, so is absent from $B_2(1)$. Lemma 1.2 makes spanning impossible. At $s=2$, if $c\ge4$, the residue $(1,2)$ has minimum norm $1+2=3$, so is again absent. Thus only $c=2$ can occur. In that case the residue $(1,0)$ has exactly the two representatives $(1,0),(-1,0)$ in $B_2(2)$: its first coordinate must be odd, and its second even; a nonzero even second coordinate already makes the norm at least $3$. Lemma 1.2 gives $k\le2$, hence $|G|\le8=2s^2$. If equality holds in this case, $c=2$ divides $s=2$.

For equality at any $s\ge3$, $c^2\mid2s^2$. For every odd prime $q$, taking prime valuations yields $2v_q(c)\le2v_q(s)$, hence $v_q(c)\le v_q(s)$. At $q=2$ the inequality is $2v_2(c)\le1+2v_2(s)$; since the valuations are integers, it too implies $v_2(c)\le v_2(s)$. Thus $c\mid s$. Finally, $4\mid c^2k$, whereas for odd $s$, $2s^2\equiv2\pmod4$. The greatest allowable multiple of $4$ is at most $2s^2-2$, as asserted. The equality-divisibility condition is necessary; no sufficiency for each $c,k,s$ is asserted.

This extends the even-$c$ bounds of Bajnok–Beatty, Theorems 1.8 and 3.2(1), from their specified regular pairs to arbitrary pairs. Its $s\ge3$ baseline is already contained in the cited repository theorem; the additional conclusions here include the complete positive-radius boundary and the divisibility refinement.

## 3. Odd first invariant factor and residue capacities

**Theorem 3.1 (three residue capacities).** Let $c\ge3$ be odd, $k,s\ge1$, and write uniquely $s=ct+r$ with $t\ge0$ and $0\le r<c$. If $\mathbb Z_c\times\mathbb Z_{ck}$ has a signed $s$-spanning pair, then

$$
k\le F_c(s):=
\begin{cases}
2t^2,&0\le r<(c-1)/2,\\
2t(t+1),&(c-1)/2\le r<c-1,\\
2t(t+1)+1,&r=c-1.
\end{cases}
$$

In particular $|G|\le c^2F_c(s)$, without a regularity assumption on the pair.

Proof. Introduce $U=x+y$, $V=x-y$. Over the integers this bijects $B_2(s)$ with

$$
\{(U,V): -s\le U,V\le s,\ U\equiv V\pmod2\},
$$

because $x=(U+V)/2$, $y=(U-V)/2$ are integral exactly at equal parity, and $|x|+|y|=\max(|U|,|V|)$. Modulo odd $c$ the transformation has invertible determinant $-2$. Thus fixing residues of $U,V$ is equivalent to fixing one coefficient residue of $x,y$. For $i\in\mathbb Z_c$, let $E_i,O_i$ count its even and odd representatives in $[-s,s]$. A residue pair $(i,j)$ therefore contains exactly

$$
E_iE_j+O_iO_j
$$

diamond points, and this number must be at least $k$ by Lemma 1.2.

Take $i=s+1\pmod c$. Its representatives in $[-s,s]$ are exactly $s+1-\ell c$ for

$$
1\le\ell\le\left\lfloor\frac{2s+1}{c}\right\rfloor.
$$

Successive representatives have opposite parity since $c$ is odd. If $r<(c-1)/2$, then $0<2r+1<c$, so the upper limit is $2t$. Hence $E_i=O_i=t$, and the residue pair $(i,i)$ has $2t^2$ points.

If $(c-1)/2\le r<c-1$, then $c\le2r+1<2c$, so $i$ has $2t+1$ representatives. The residue $j=s+2\pmod c$ has precisely the representatives $s+2-\ell c$ with $1\le\ell\le\lfloor(2s+2)/c\rfloor=2t+1$: the lower index is $1$ because $2\le c$, and $c\le2r+2<2c$. The first representatives for $i$ and $j$ differ by $1$, so their parity-majority classes are opposite. Their $(E,O)$ counts are $(t,t+1)$ and $(t+1,t)$ in one order. The residue pair $(i,j)$ has $2t(t+1)$ points.

If $r=c-1$, then $\lfloor(2s+1)/c\rfloor=2t+1$. Taking $(i,i)$ gives $t^2+(t+1)^2=2t(t+1)+1$ points. In each of the three cases the displayed coefficient residue must cover a coset of $cG$ of size $k$, giving the stated inequality. The argument includes $t=0$: a zero capacity means no spanning pair exists.

For example, $c=5,s=6$ gives $t=r=1$ in the first case, hence $k\le2$ and $|G|\le50$, improving the baseline $2s^2=72$. These are necessary residue capacities; a converse spanning construction for every allowed triple is not assumed.

## 4. Complete noncyclic small-radius closure

**Corollary 4.1 (radii one and two).** No noncyclic finite abelian group has a signed radius-one spanning pair. The maximum order of a noncyclic finite abelian group with a signed radius-two spanning pair is exactly $9$, attained by $\mathbb Z_3^2$.

Proof. A spanning pair generates $G$, so the invariant-factor presentation following Lemma 1.2 applies. At radius $1$, even $c$ is excluded by the proof of Theorem 2.1. For odd $c\ge3$, one has $t=0$ and $F_c(1)=0$: for $c=3$ this is the middle case, and for $c\ge5$ the first case. Theorem 3.1 contradicts $k\ge1$.

At radius $2$, even $c$ gives order at most $8$. If $c=3$, then $t=0,r=2=c-1$ gives $k\le1$ and order at most $9$. If $c=5$, $t=0,r=2=(c-1)/2$ gives $k\le0$. For every odd $c\ge7$, $t=0,r=2<(c-1)/2$ again gives $k\le0$. Thus the only odd first invariant factor permitted is $c=3,k=1$. Conversely take $a=(1,0),b=(0,1)$ in $\mathbb Z_3^2$. Every coordinate has a representative in $\{-1,0,1\}$, so every element has a coefficient vector of norm at most $2$. The pair is distinct, and the group is noncyclic since every nonzero element has order $3$ while its order is $9$.

For comparison with the original source, the lower family of Bajnok–Beatty Theorem 1.7 and the maximum statement of Theorem 1.9 require the radius-one exception just proved. This does not alter their positive-radius constructions in the applicable range $s\ge2$. The complete $\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_{2k}$ classification is already [BajnokBeattyAllRegimes.result](../../../D5/S3/FiniteGroups/BajnokBeattyAllRegimes.lean), and the all-noncyclic sharp maximum for $s\ge3$ is already [BajnokBeattySharpMaximum.sharp_maximum](../../../D5/S3/FiniteGroups/BajnokBeattySharpMaximum.lean), using [BajnokBeattyFiniteUpper.finite_noncyclic_upper](../../../D5/S3/FiniteGroups/BajnokBeattyFiniteUpper.lean). Their core proofs are not repeated here.

## 5. Perfect signed sets and the exact elementary boundaries

**Proposition 5.1 (Lee cardinality and boundary equivalences).** For the signed-perfect predicate of Definition 1.1,

$$
|B_m(s)|=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(m,s)}2^j\binom mj\binom sj.
$$

In particular, perfection implies $|G|=2s+1$ for $m=1$, $|G|=2m+1$ for $s=1$, and $|G|=2s^2+2s+1$ for $m=2$. More precisely, for $s\ge1$, a singleton $\{a\}$ is signed $s$-perfect if and only if $G$ is cyclic of order $2s+1$ and $a$ is a generator. For $m\ge1$, an $m$-element set $A$ is signed $1$-perfect if and only if

$$
G=\{0\}\ \sqcup\ A\ \sqcup\ (-A).
$$

Equivalently in this radius-one case, $G$ has odd order $2m+1$ and $A$ selects exactly one element of each nonzero negation orbit. These elementary boundaries are already stated in Bajnok–Beatty; the proofs are included to fix the signed predicate and its parameter endpoints.

Proof. Choose the $j$ nonzero coordinates, their $2^j$ signs, and their positive absolute values with sum at most $s$. For $j\ge1$, the number of such positive $j$-tuples is $\binom sj$, by adjoining a nonnegative slack coordinate to a positive composition, or summing $\binom{h-1}{j-1}$ over $j\le h\le s$. For $j=0$ there is the single zero vector. This proves the formula. Substitution of $m=1$, $s=1$, and $m=2$ gives the displayed cardinalities, including $s=1$ in the $m=2$ formula.

If $m=1$ and perfection holds, surjectivity on $[-s,s]$ makes $a$ a generator, and bijectivity gives $|G|=2s+1$. Conversely, if $a$ has order $2s+1$, two distinct integers in $[-s,s]$ differ by a nonzero integer of absolute value at most $2s$, so cannot give the same multiple of $a$. There are exactly $|G|$ such integers, proving bijectivity.

For $s=1$, the coefficient vectors are exactly $0,e_1,-e_1,\ldots,e_m,-e_m$. Their images are distinct and exhaust $G$ exactly when the displayed disjoint union holds. This union forces $|G|=2m+1$ and chooses one element from every pair $\{g,-g\}$, $g\ne0$. Conversely, in a group of this odd order there is no nonzero element with $g=-g$: such an element would generate a subgroup of order $2$, contrary to Lagrange's theorem. Choosing one member of each pair gives precisely the disjoint union. Thus both directions and the intersection $m=s=1$ are accounted for.

The coefficient domain here differs from the unsigned restricted-basis domain in [BajnokBersonJustPerfectRestrictedBasis.IsPerfectRestrictedBasis](../../../D5/S3/FiniteGroups/BajnokBersonJustPerfectRestrictedBasis.lean), which uses sums of distinct subset elements. That predicate cannot replace bijectivity on an integer Lee ball.

**Theorem 5.2 (two-generator cyclicity at every positive radius).** If a two-element subset of a finite abelian group is signed $s$-perfect for any integer $s\ge1$, then the group is cyclic of order $2s^2+2s+1$.

Proof. Proposition 5.1 gives that order and supplies signed spanning. For $s\ge3$, the existing uniform consequence of BajnokBeattySharpMaximum.sharp_maximum says that every noncyclic finite abelian group with a distinct signed spanning pair has order at most $2s^2+2s-4$. This is smaller by $5$, contradicting perfection. At $s=2$, the perfect order is $13$, whereas Corollary 4.1 bounds every noncyclic example by $9$. At $s=1$, the perfect order is $5$, and a group of prime order is cyclic by Lagrange's theorem. These cases exhaust all positive radii.

## 6. Multiplier domain and higher-dimensional scope

**Proposition 6.1 (the multiplier-domain counterexample).** In $\mathbb Z_{13}$, the set $A=\{2,10\}$ is signed $2$-perfect. It equals $5\{2,3\}$ with $5\in(\mathbb Z_{13})^\times$, but equals none of $d\{2,3\}$ for the integer representatives $0\le d\le4$ of $\mathbb Z_5$. Thus the multiplier domain printed in Bajnok–Beatty Conjecture 1.10 cannot describe every perfect pair even at $s=2$.

Proof. The thirteen coefficient vectors in $B_2(2)$ have images

$$
0,\quad \pm2,\quad\pm10,\quad\pm4,\quad\pm20,\quad\pm12,\quad\pm8.
$$

Reduced modulo $13$, these are $0$ together with the six disjoint pairs $\{2,11\}$, $\{10,3\}$, $\{4,9\}$, $\{7,6\}$, $\{12,1\}$, $\{8,5\}$. They exhaust the group without repetition, proving the actual Lee-ball bijection. Multiplication by $5$ sends $\{2,3\}$ to $\{10,2\}$. In contrast, multipliers $d=0,1,2,3,4$ give respectively

$$
\{0\},\quad\{2,3\},\quad\{4,6\},\quad\{6,9\},\quad\{8,12\},
$$

none equal to $A$. The printed domain $\mathbb Z_{2s+1}$ is interpreted here by these standard integer representatives; it is not itself a natural scalar action on $\mathbb Z_{2s^2+2s+1}$. The example disproves that proposed bounded multiplier list. The candidate corrected assertion would allow $d\in(\mathbb Z_{2s^2+2s+1})^\times$ in $A=d\{s,s+1\}$. Neither cyclicity nor this example proves that universal shape assertion; it remains an unproved assertion in this source.

For clarity on the cited higher-dimensional exclusion, the bridge is purely mathematical. If $f_A$ is bijective on $B_m(s)$ and $K=\ker f_A$, every $z\in\mathbb Z^m$ has exactly one $b\in B_m(s)$ with $f_A(z)=f_A(b)$, and then exactly one $k=z-b\in K$. Thus

$$
\mathbb Z^m=\bigsqcup_{k\in K}(k+B_m(s)).
$$

Conversely such a lattice tiling makes the Lee-ball map to $\mathbb Z^m/K$ bijective. This explains why a signed-perfect set would give a lattice Lee-ball tiling. Chen, Huang and Luo, *The Golomb–Welch conjecture for lattice tilings*, [arXiv:2610.33983v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33983v1), Theorem 1.1, is the cited exclusion for $m\ge3,s\ge2$. Their *The Golomb–Welch conjecture for arbitrary centre sets*, [arXiv:2610.34291v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34291v1), Theorem 1.1, states the stronger arbitrary-centre exclusion. These are attributed external theorem statements; their long proofs are not supplied by the two-dimensional capacities above. The repository's [LeeBallTwoLatticeObstruction](../../../D5/S3/Arith/Coding/LeeBallTwoLatticeObstruction.lean) concerns only its dimension-three, radius-two lattice obstruction and cannot supply this full parameter range. The corrected two-generator shape is a separate question, not a consequence of those higher-dimensional statements.
