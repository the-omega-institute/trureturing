# Event-driven first actual update: receiver work and the original owned boundary

A public rational-height prior, together with a genuinely paid exact initial scalar, a globally complete compatible class and an acquired depth cutoff, permits a receiver to recover the actual calibration, original depth, first acquired block and complete original owned boundary at that block's own Return. Exact temporal fibres give stable deadlines; a finite constrained Zeckendorf digit program finds them without enumerating a lifetime. Only polynomially many visits require substantial arithmetic. Every intervening real Step is delivered individually by an explicitly amortized sequential-tape driver.

The resulting bound is actual receiver work linear in complete actual traffic plus a polynomial in numerical depth, height and control width. The receiver's own binary counter still performs work proportional to the real waiting time; its separate amortization is dominated by the existing event traffic. This is not a polynomial bound on all elective arithmetic after literal subtraction of a fixed compulsory baseline. In particular it does not settle the stronger baseline-subtracted question in the parent volume's Problem 19.2. Producer, service, old history, backend and physical costs retain separate coordinates.

All proofs and algorithms below are ordinary, unexecuted conditional symbolics. No numerical experiment, executed mathematical verification, Lean/kernel certification, independent physical realization or external priority claim is asserted.

## 3. Finite quadratic arithmetic and first-change labels

**Lemma 3.1 (Powers, differences and joint affine rigidity).** Every nonzero integer power of $\alpha$ is irrational. If $d,e\ge0$ are different, then $\alpha^d-\alpha^e$ is irrational. For positive rational $q,r$, rational $c,c'$, nonnegative depths $d,e$ and $g,h\in\{1,2\}$, the two equalities

$$
c+q\alpha^d=c'+r\alpha^e,\qquad
c+q\alpha^{d+g}=c'+r\alpha^{e+h}
\tag{13}
$$

force $(q,c,d,g)=(r,c',e,h)$, including depth zero.

Proof. Conjugation fixes rationals and sends $\alpha$ to $\beta=-\phi$. A positive odd power has opposite conjugate sign, whereas a positive even power is below one and has conjugate modulus above one. Neither is rational; negative powers follow by reciprocal. Thus $q\alpha^u=r\alpha^v$ for positive rational factors forces $u=v$ and $q=r$.

For $d>e\ge0$, $w=\alpha^e-\alpha^d\in(0,1)$. Opposite parities give conjugate modulus $\phi^e+\phi^d>1$. Equal parities give $d-e\ge2$ and modulus at least $\phi^e(\phi^2-1)=\phi^{e+1}>1$. A rational number would equal its conjugate, a contradiction. Reversing the order changes only sign. This proves the difference statement also when one depth is zero; it does not follow merely from single-power irrationality.

Subtract (13) and use $1-\alpha^g=\alpha^{3-g}$ for both allowed block lengths. Then $q\alpha^{d+3-g}=r\alpha^{e+3-h}$. The power argument gives $q=r$ and equal exponents. Returning to the first equality, the difference argument forces $d=e$, whence $c=c'$ and $g=h$. Positivity of the unit and the two specified block lengths are essential. $\square$

Prepare one unchanged centre $z_0$ and the two labelled changed centres for every entry:

$$
\mathcal V=\{z_0\}\cup
\{c_e+q_e\alpha^{e+g}:(q_e,c_e,e)\in\mathcal A,\ g=1,2\}.
\tag{14}
$$

Every changed centre is strictly below $z_0$. Equal changed centres would, together with their equal initial scalar, contradict Lemma 3.1 unless their complete labels agree. Thus there are exactly $1+2|\mathcal A|$ distinct categories, with the unchanged scalar shared once.

**Lemma 3.2 (Closed-error unique classification).** Set

$$
n_c=D+9H+6,\quad \varepsilon_c=2^{-n_c},\quad
\gamma=2^{-(D+9H+3)}.
\tag{15}
$$

At any queried Return through the first actual update, a full scan using the closed tests $|a2^{-n_c}-z|\le\varepsilon_c$, $z\in\mathcal V$, has exactly one passing centre, which is the actual one.

Proof. Every centre has a height-$H$ representation with exponent at most $D+1$; the saved $z_0$ has an actual representation below $D$. For unequal $z,w$, clear their four rational denominators with $L\le M^4$. Then $L(z-w)\in\mathbb Z[\alpha]$. The norm of $u+v\alpha$ for integral coefficients is $u^2-uv-v^2$, a nonzero integer when the field element is nonzero. Moreover $|(z-w)'|\le4M2^{D+1}$ since $\phi<2$. Therefore

$$
|z-w|\ge\frac1{L^2|(z-w)'|}\ge\gamma.
\tag{16}
$$

The true centre is in (14) before and at the first update and passes every allowed closed-error reply. Two passing centres would have distance at most $2\varepsilon_c<\gamma$; also $4\varepsilon_c<\gamma$. Hence the pass is unique. No minimum-gap enumeration, preferred floor, independent error or nested interval is used. An extra saved centre with unrestricted exponent would invalidate this reasoning; the origin and exponent bound on every saved centre are explicit. $\square$

All tests have finite exact integer specifications. Represent $\alpha$ powers by the recurrence after (12); the absolute coefficient sum increases at most twofold per exponent, giving linear bit width. Clear positive denominators and convert a sign test to signed integers $u+v\sqrt5$. If $v=0$, use the sign of $u$; if $u=0$, use the sign of $v$; common nonzero signs give that common sign. If $u>0>v$, use the sign of $u^2-5v^2$; if $u<0<v$, use its opposite. Equality is retained. Gcd reduction uses Euclidean integer division; zero is normalized separately and denominators made positive. Ordinary multiplication, division and these sign cases cover all closed tests. No exact-real oracle is present. Only a fixed number of rational denominators is combined per operation, never the product over the whole class.

In particular the whole past satisfies $E_i[j]=\operatorname{Low}(N_v+j)$ for every $j<i$, but those bits need not be regenerated.

## 6. Complete finite constrained Zeckendorf maximum

**Lemma 6.1 (Greedy endpoints and numerical order).** The maximum legal no11 value supported on positions $[0,j)$ is $G_j-1$. Consequently the highest differing digit orders legal finite words in exactly their numerical order. Finite greedy expansion with descending consecutive weights gives the legal padded expansion of every nonnegative endpoint.

Proof. Call the maximum $M_j$. The bases are $M_0=0=G_0-1$ and $M_1=1=G_1-1$. For $j\ge1$, splitting on position $j$ gives

$$
M_{j+1}=\max(M_j,G_j+M_{j-1})=G_{j+1}-1
\tag{26}
$$

by induction: a one at $j$ forces the adjacent lower digit zero. Thus a high one at the highest difference has weight $G_j$, strictly larger than all possible lower contribution $G_j-1$. It dominates with the correct sign, proving lexicographic equals numerical order.

For greedy expansion, generate consecutive weights through the first one above the endpoint, then descend. Select a weight when it does not exceed the residual. For a selected $G_j$ with $j\ge1$, the preselection residual was below $G_{j+1}=G_j+G_{j-1}$, so the new residual is below $G_{j-1}$. The next adjacent digit must therefore be zero. At position zero the remaining unit is exhausted. Zero selects nothing and is padded with zeros. The resulting word is legal, has the prescribed value, and is $Z$ by finite uniqueness. Weights can be recovered backwards by subtraction of consecutive weights, without any negative-index case. $\square$

Here is the full finite maximum algorithm for (21). It is a specification, not an executed DP. Reject if $i>L_e$. Otherwise set $l=a_e$, $u=b_e-i$, and use

$$
R=D+3
\tag{27}
$$

padded positions $0,\ldots,R-1$. Every candidate has $s\le b_e<G_{e+1}\le G_D$ since $e<D$, so no nonzero digit at or above $D$ is possible. Lemma 4.1 gives $h\le d_0+1\le D$; all forced bits fit too. Greedily compute $Z(l)$ and $Z(u)$, padded to $R$. At positions $j<h$ force $d_j=v_j$. At the higher positions digits are free subject to no11. There is no extra high block-ending constraint: $v$ is a complete prefix, but the rest of $Z(s)$ is an arbitrary legal finite word.

Define a Boolean table $F(j,t,L,U)$. Its remaining positions are $j,j-1,\ldots,0$, $t\in\{0,1\}$ is the already fixed higher adjacent digit, $L\in\{\mathrm{equal},\mathrm{greater}\}$ is the high-prefix comparison with the lower endpoint, and $U\in\{\mathrm{equal},\mathrm{less}\}$ is the comparison with the upper endpoint. Failed comparisons are rejected rather than stored. The complete base is

$$
F(-1,t,L,U)=\mathrm{true}
\tag{28}
$$

for every nonfailed state. At $j\ge0$, try each $d\in\{0,1\}$ with the following exact transitions.

1. Reject a forced-bit violation, or $t=d=1$.
2. If $L=\mathrm{equal}$, reject $d<Z(l)_j$, keep equal on equality and change to greater on $d>Z(l)_j$. If already greater, keep greater.
3. If $U=\mathrm{equal}$, reject $d>Z(u)_j$, keep equal on equality and change to less on $d<Z(u)_j$. If already less, keep less.
4. A nonrejected transition is feasible exactly when $F(j-1,d,L',U')$ is true. Define $F(j,t,L,U)$ as the disjunction of these at most two transitions.

Fill the rows upward from $j=0$ for every state; then inspect $F(R-1,0,\mathrm{equal},\mathrm{equal})$. If false, return EMPTY. Otherwise reconstruct downward. At each position choose one if its allowed transition has a true lower completion; choose zero otherwise, which feasibility guarantees exists. Retain the updated flags and adjacent bit. Sum the selected stored weights to recover the integer $m_e$.

Proof of the algorithm. Induct on $j$. Given a higher prefix, the comparison flags record exactly its first unequal digit relative to each endpoint. Once that digit is strictly favourable, lower digits cannot reverse its numerical order, by Lemma 6.1. The recurrence lists exactly every legal next bit with every feasible lower completion. At $j=-1$ no position remains, so (28) accepts every nonfailed endpoint equality or favourable strict comparison. The initial outside high digit zero imposes the correct top boundary; there is no outside constraint below position zero. Induction proves the initial state true exactly when (21) is nonempty. Every allowed integer has its unique legal padded representation and hence a path in this table.

During reconstruction choosing the highest possible one preserves existence of a completion.
