# Tebni's A364822 congruence at odd primes

## Abstract

Integral hyperbolic EGF coefficients have residue two at every odd prime.

All indices are natural numbers. PowerSeries has rational coefficients. The inverse is the formal power-series inverse, and factorials and integer coefficients are cast to the displayed carrier. Natural subtraction in n-k is truncated subtraction. Finset.range n contains the indices k<n. Int.ModEq uses an integer modulus. The entry is unpaginated.

$$\operatorname{sinh} = \operatorname{PowerSeries}.\operatorname{mk}\left((n: \mathbb{N} \mapsto (\operatorname{if} (\operatorname{Even}\left(n\right)) \operatorname{then} 0 \operatorname{else} ((\operatorname{Nat}.\operatorname{factorial}\left(n\right): \mathbb{Q}))^{-1}))\right)$$

OEIS A364822, Name (unpaginated): "Expansion of e.g.f. cosh(x) / (1 - 2*sinh(x))." The series sinh has coefficient 1/n! at odd indices and zero at even indices.

**Theorem 1.3 (The normalized odd Taylor coefficients).**

$$\forall n: \mathbb{N}, \operatorname{IntegralEGFComposition}.\operatorname{eCoeff}\left(\operatorname{sinh}, n\right) = (\operatorname{if} (\operatorname{Even}\left(n\right)) \operatorname{then} 0 \operatorname{else} 1)$$

*Commentary.*

Factorial normalization cancels the Taylor denominator. Every even index gives zero and every odd index gives one; this coefficient identity also determines the sign of sinh under substitution of -X.

**Definition 1.5 (The conjecture).**

$$\operatorname{claim} \iff (\forall p: \mathbb{N}, (\operatorname{Nat}.\operatorname{Prime}\left(p\right)) \implies ((2 < p) \implies (\exists z: \mathbb{Z}, ((z: \mathbb{Q}) = \operatorname{a}\left(p\right)) \land (\operatorname{Int}.\operatorname{ModEq}\left((p: \mathbb{Z}), z, 2\right)))))$$

OEIS A364822, Comments (unpaginated): "Conjectures: For p prime (p > 2), a(p) == 2 (mod p)." The prime p is natural, z is integer, and z equals a(p) after the rational cast. The strict bound excludes p=2.

**Theorem 1.6 (Every coefficient is an integer).**

$$\forall n: \mathbb{N}, (\operatorname{Rat}.\operatorname{num}\left(\operatorname{a}\left(n\right)\right): \mathbb{Q}) = \operatorname{a}\left(n\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S1/Recurrence/Residue/TebniA364822.num_cast` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Commentary.*

Substituting 2 sinh into the geometric series preserves integral factorial-scaled coefficients. The binomial product formula then proves integrality after multiplication by cosh. The rational numerator therefore equals the rational coefficient after casting.

**Theorem 1.7 (The integer binomial recurrence).**

$$\forall n: \mathbb{N}, \operatorname{Rat}.\operatorname{num}\left(\operatorname{a}\left(n\right)\right) = (\operatorname{if} (\operatorname{Even}\left(n\right)) \operatorname{then} 1 \operatorname{else} 0) + (2) \cdot (\sum_{k \in \operatorname{Finset}.\operatorname{range}\left(n\right)} ((((\operatorname{Nat}.\operatorname{choose}\left(n, k\right): \mathbb{Z})) \cdot (\operatorname{Rat}.\operatorname{num}\left(\operatorname{a}\left(k\right)\right))) \cdot ((\operatorname{if} (\operatorname{Even}\left(n - k\right)) \operatorname{then} 0 \operatorname{else} 1))))$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S1/Recurrence/Residue/TebniA364822.num_recurrence` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Commentary.*

Multiplication by 1-2 sinh gives the recurrence. The k=n endpoint summand vanishes because sinh has constant coefficient zero. This recurrence also proves the congruence at powers of two.

**Theorem 1.8 (Residue two at every odd prime).**

$$\forall p: \mathbb{N}, (\operatorname{Nat}.\operatorname{Prime}\left(p\right)) \implies ((2 < p) \implies (\exists z: \mathbb{Z}, ((z: \mathbb{Q}) = \operatorname{a}\left(p\right)) \land (\operatorname{Int}.\operatorname{ModEq}\left((p: \mathbb{Z}), z, 2\right))))$$
