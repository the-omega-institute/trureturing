*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For the unique nonnegative integer sequence on positive indices with a(1)=a(2)=0 and a(3)=1, the recurrence a(n+1)=Round(sqrt(a(n)^2+a(n-1)^2+a(n-2)^2)) holds for every n at least 3. Its consecutive ratio tends to rho, the largest positive real root of x^6-x^4-x^2-1=0. Every denominator from the third positive index is positive.

The integer rounding threshold has strict error less than one half, so no tie convention changes any step. Monotonicity and the three-input square-root estimate give a(n+5) at least a(n+2)+1 in zero-based coordinates. Induction on blocks implies that the actual rounded terms tend to infinity.

Write lambda for the Tribonacci constant and b(n)=a(n+2)^2. The signed error e(n)=b(n+3)-b(n+2)-b(n+1)-b(n) satisfies |e(n)| at most 2 a(n+4). The positive projection T(n)=b(n+2)+(lambda-1)b(n+1)+b(n)/lambda obeys T(n+1)=lambda T(n)+e(n), with T(n) at least b(n+2). Hence e(n)/T(n) tends to zero and T(n+1)/T(n) tends to lambda.

The bounded coordinates v(n)=b(n+2)/T(n) lie in [0,1]. Their recurrence has limiting coefficients alpha=(lambda-1)/lambda and beta=lambda^(-3), both nonnegative with alpha+beta less than one. Put U=1/(1+alpha+beta). The deviations d(n)=v(n)-U satisfy d(n+2)=w(n)-alpha d(n+1)-beta d(n), where w(n) tends to zero because the coefficients converge and v is bounded.

For M(k)=max(|d(2k)|,|d(2k+1)|), the two-step estimate gives M(k+1) at most (alpha+beta)M(k)+(1+alpha)(|w(2k)|+|w(2k+1)|). The strict affine-contraction theorem yields M(k) tending to zero. Both parities are contained in these maxima, so v(n) tends to U over all natural indices. Since U is positive, b(n+3)/b(n+2) tends to lambda. Continuity of the real square root yields the original consecutive-ratio limit.

Finally rho^2=lambda converts the Tribonacci cubic into the sextic equation. Every positive sextic root x has x^2 greater than one and satisfies the Tribonacci cubic; cubic-root uniqueness forces x=rho. The shift sourceA(n)=a(n-1) gives the exact published indexing, and strong induction gives uniqueness on positive indices.
