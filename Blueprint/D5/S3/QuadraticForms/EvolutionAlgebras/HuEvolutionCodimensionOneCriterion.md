# A codimension-one minimal-perfect criterion

## Abstract

An exact all-parameter nilpotent and cyclic criterion for minimal perfect algebras with a square-zero hyperplane.

**Definition 1.1 (The square-zero hyperplane product).**

$$\forall m \in \mathbb{N},\; \forall a \in \mathbb{C},\; \forall c \in \operatorname{Coord}\left(m\right),\; \forall T \in \operatorname{Matrix}\left(\operatorname{Fin}\left(m\right), \operatorname{Fin}\left(m\right), \mathbb{C}\right),\; \forall x \in \operatorname{Coord}\left(m + 1\right),\; \forall y \in \operatorname{Coord}\left(m + 1\right),\; (\operatorname{codimOneProduct}\left(m, a, c, T, x, y\right)\left(0\right) = a \cdot x\left(0\right) \cdot y\left(0\right)) \land (\forall i \in \operatorname{Fin}\left(m\right),\; \operatorname{codimOneProduct}\left(m, a, c, T, x, y\right)\left(\operatorname{succ}\left(i\right)\right) = x\left(0\right) \cdot \operatorname{mulVec}\left(T, \operatorname{tail}\left(y\right)\right)\left(i\right) + y\left(0\right) \cdot \operatorname{mulVec}\left(T, \operatorname{tail}\left(x\right)\right)\left(i\right) + c\left(i\right) \cdot x\left(0\right) \cdot y\left(0\right))$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/QuadraticForms/EvolutionAlgebras/HuEvolutionCodimensionOneCriterion.codimOneProduct` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Coordinate zero is the quotient vector u; coordinate i.succ is the i-th coordinate of W. Here tail(x)(j)=x(j.succ). The law is u squared = au+c, uw=wu=Tw and W squared =0. Matrices act on column vectors. There is no assumption of associativity, a unit, a natural basis or a Jordan identity.

**Theorem 1.2 (The complete nilpotent and cyclic equivalence).**

$$\forall m \in \mathbb{N},\; (1 \le m) \Rightarrow \left(\forall a \in \mathbb{C},\; (a \ne 0) \Rightarrow \left(\forall c \in \operatorname{Coord}\left(m\right),\; \forall T \in \operatorname{Matrix}\left(\operatorname{Fin}\left(m\right), \operatorname{Fin}\left(m\right), \mathbb{C}\right),\; (\operatorname{det}\left(T\right) \ne 0) \Rightarrow \left(\forall mu \in \operatorname{Product}\left(m + 1\right),\; (\forall x \in \operatorname{Coord}\left(m + 1\right),\; \forall y \in \operatorname{Coord}\left(m + 1\right),\; mu\left(x, y\right) = \operatorname{codimOneProduct}\left(m, a, c, T, x, y\right)) \Rightarrow \left((\operatorname{minimalPerfect}\left(mu\right)) \Leftrightarrow ((\left(T - \frac{a}{2} \cdot 1\right)^{m} = 0) \land (\operatorname{det}\left(\operatorname{krylov}\left(T - \frac{a}{2} \cdot 1, c, m\right)\right) \ne 0))\right)\right)\right)\right)$$

In the displayed formula, 1 is the m by m identity matrix. The matrix krylov(N,c,m) has entry (i,j) equal to ((N raised to j.val).mulVec c)(i). Its columns are c,Nc,...,N raised to m−1 times c, in that order. The exponent is exactly m. Arbitrary c, including zero, and every complex subspace are included. The square of a subspace means the span of all pair products. Minimal perfect means V squared = V and every nonzero complex subspace U satisfying U squared = U equals V.

A nonzero perfect subspace contains a normalized vector u+z. Its intersection S with W is invariant under T and N, and contains c+2Nz. Cyclicity puts W inside S+NW. Repeated lifting gives W=S+N raised to m times W=S, so the subspace is the whole algebra.

Conversely, an invariant subspace S containing c+2Nz gives the subspace generated by u+z and S. It is closed under multiplication. Left multiplication by u+z is injective because a and T are invertible; its restriction is therefore surjective, proving equality with its full product span. The Fitting generalized-zero component supplies a proper such subspace when N is not nilpotent. When N is nilpotent but c is not cyclic, its finite orbit span supplies one instead.

The arbitrary-operator-to-Jordan product-preserving isomorphism, the idempotent obstruction, square-zero extensions and Hu–Wen Question 2.11 are separate assertions and do not follow as completion claims here.
