# Execution parsing, bilateral past states and return guards

## Abstract

Positive Fibonacci return lists have unique execution-word and color-history decodings. Bilateral letter pasts determine a unique bounded state, terminal-u words have unique positive-run decompositions, and independently occurring runs inherit their exact return guards.

**Theorem 1.1 (Maximal complete runs and original history injection).**

$$\left(\forall a \in Return, xs \in \operatorname{List}\left(Return\right),\; \operatorname{takeWhile}\left(\operatorname{executionWord}\left(\operatorname{cons}\left(a, xs\right)\right), isC\right) = \operatorname{replicate}\left(\operatorname{r}\left(a\right), c\right)\right) \land \left(\left(\forall a \in Return, xs \in \operatorname{List}\left(Return\right),\; \operatorname{takeWhile}\left(\operatorname{drop}\left(\operatorname{executionWord}\left(\operatorname{cons}\left(a, xs\right)\right), \operatorname{r}\left(a\right)\right), isU\right) = \operatorname{replicate}\left(\operatorname{m}\left(a\right), u\right)\right) \land \left(\operatorname{Injective}\left(executionWord\right) \land \left(\left(\forall xs \in \operatorname{List}\left(Return\right),\; \operatorname{wordWeight}\left(\operatorname{executionWord}\left(xs\right)\right) = \operatorname{listWeight}\left(xs\right)\right) \land \left(\forall model \in Model,\; \operatorname{Injective}\left(\operatorname{history}\left(model\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Coding/FibonacciFactorCompletion/Bilateral.complete_execution_word_parser` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

The initial maximal c-run has exactly r letters and the following maximal u-run has exactly m letters for every positive return. Recursing after those runs uniquely recovers the complete execution list. Its weight is exactly the original list weight with c charged 20 and u charged 6. The actual color blocks have different second colors, so their concatenation is injective. Reversing only the execution-letter word produces the external block order without reversing any color-block letters. Removing the fixed common stem and paid anchor proves history injection for each actual model.

*Commentary.*

The auxiliary letter maps are c: z maps to chi*z and u: z maps to A_H+rho*z, with the original high-side constants. The finite past at i composes the N letters immediately before i in chronological order. Its exact seed difference is g raised to their original 20/6 weight times the seed difference, bounded by rho^N. The zero-seed sequence is increasing and bounded in [0,h_H]; its supremum is the independently defined pastState. Every seed in that interval has the same limit. This state satisfies the letter transition law and is the unique bilateral solution confined to [0,h_H]. Two sequences agreeing on the last N past letters have states differing by at most h_H*rho^N. The all-u state equals h_H. In the formula, Sequence denotes the function of N and const(u) denotes the constant bilateral u sequence.

Matching-past stability gives continuity in the product of the discrete letter spaces. AuxiliaryLanguage forbids K+1 consecutive c letters and checks chi^(K-1)*d before a current c whose preceding K-1 letters are c. For K at least one and every real d, the all-u sequence belongs to this closed compact language. States commute with every integer shift, where shift(omega,j)(n)=omega(n+j), and membership is invariant under those shifts. This language is defined by the independent letter conditions and is not a completion image or an actual literal source.

**Theorem 1.4 (Independent occurrence guards on canonical complete runs).**

$$\forall K \in Nat, d \in Real, omega \in Int \to CuLetter, i \in Int, xs \in \operatorname{List}\left(Return\right),\; \left(\operatorname{le}\left(1, K\right) \land \left(\operatorname{member}\left(omega, \operatorname{AuxiliaryLanguage}\left(K, d\right)\right) \land \left(\forall k \in \operatorname{Fin}\left(\operatorname{length}\left(\operatorname{executionWord}\left(xs\right)\right)\right),\; \left(\operatorname{lt}\left(\operatorname{add}\left(\operatorname{val}\left(k\right), 1\right), \operatorname{length}\left(\operatorname{executionWord}\left(xs\right)\right)\right) \lor \operatorname{getElem}\left(\operatorname{executionWord}\left(xs\right), k\right) = c\right) \Rightarrow \operatorname{apply}\left(omega, \operatorname{add}\left(i, \operatorname{castInt}\left(\operatorname{val}\left(k\right)\right)\right)\right) = \operatorname{getElem}\left(\operatorname{executionWord}\left(xs\right), k\right)\right)\right)\right) \Rightarrow \operatorname{GuardTrace}\left(K, d, false, high, xs, \operatorname{pastState}\left(omega, i\right)\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Coding/FibonacciFactorCompletion/Bilateral.occurrence_run_guards` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

The bilateral sequence independently belongs to AuxiliaryLanguage. Its letters agree with the prescribed complete execution word at every position except that the final u may have been appended as a terminal fill. All c letters must still agree. Each run inherits the cap from the original forbidden K+1 c letters. At a run of length K, the original state before its last c is chi^(K-1) times its run-start state, so the independent before-Kth-c guard yields the weak return guard. When another return follows, every intervening u is an original occurrence letter and the exact transition law aligns its next start. No state alignment is claimed across an appended terminal u.

**Theorem 1.5 (Recursive guards at every execution split).**

$$\forall K \in Nat, d \in Real, strict \in Bool, j \in Side, execution \in \operatorname{List}\left(Return\right), D \in Real,\; \operatorname{GuardTrace}\left(K, d, strict, j, execution, D\right) \Leftrightarrow \left(\forall before \in \operatorname{List}\left(Return\right), a \in Return, after \in \operatorname{List}\left(Return\right),\; execution = \operatorname{append}\left(before, \operatorname{cons}\left(a, after\right)\right) \Rightarrow \left(\operatorname{le}\left(\operatorname{r}\left(a\right), K\right) \land \left(\operatorname{r}\left(a\right) = K \Rightarrow \left(\left(strict = true \Rightarrow \operatorname{lt}\left(d, \operatorname{execute}\left(j, before, D\right)\right)\right) \land \left(strict = false \Rightarrow \operatorname{le}\left(d, \operatorname{execute}\left(j, before, D\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Coding/FibonacciFactorCompletion/Bilateral.uniform_guard_trace_iff_split` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For every natural cap K, real threshold d, Boolean strictness, side, finite positive-return list and real initial state D, the recursive GuardTrace holds exactly when each split before ++ a :: after obeys a.r <= K and, when a.r = K, tests the state obtained by executing before from D. The test is d < state when strict is true and d <= state when strict is false; the displayed pair of implications states that Boolean conditional completely. Return carries positive natural r and m. No lower bound on K, restriction to an actual literal starting state, or bound on the list length is assumed. Induction aligns the tail's initial state with returnMap after its first return. This structural equivalence alone does not assert whole-record supply.

**Theorem 1.6 (Exact active costs at every execution split).**

$$\forall j \in Side, budget \in Real, execution \in \operatorname{List}\left(Return\right), D \in Real,\; \operatorname{StrictControl}\left(j, budget, execution, D\right) \Leftrightarrow \left(\forall before \in \operatorname{List}\left(Return\right), a \in Return, after \in \operatorname{List}\left(Return\right),\; execution = \operatorname{append}\left(before, \operatorname{cons}\left(a, after\right)\right) \Rightarrow \operatorname{lt}\left(\operatorname{subtract}\left(lam, \operatorname{multiply}\left(\operatorname{multiply}\left(\operatorname{power}\left(g, 2\right), \operatorname{power}\left(chi, \operatorname{r}\left(a\right)\right)\right), \operatorname{execute}\left(j, before, D\right)\right)\right), budget\right)\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Coding/FibonacciFactorCompletion/Bilateral.exact_control_iff_split` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For either side, every real budget and initial state, and every finite list of positive returns, StrictControl is equivalent to testing lam - g^2 * chi^(a.r) * execute(j,before,D) < budget at every split before ++ a :: after of the same execution. StrictControl recursively tests the current cost and continues at returnMap(j,a,D); execute follows that same return map. The constants are T2 = 1 - t and lam = T2 / 10, with the original Fibonacci t, g and chi. The empty list satisfies both sides. The proof uses unbounded list induction and imposes no actual-start, budget-interval or cap hypothesis. It proves no record-error, reset or rate assertion.

## Uncapped positive-return enumeration and guarded recurrence

### Original lists, execution order and half weight

The objects in this application are the original Return records a = (m,r,m_pos,r_pos), with m,r : Nat, m_pos : 0 < m and r_pos : 0 < r. Neither exponent has a cap. An execution is an ordered List Return. Its definitions are executionWord([]) = [], executionWord(a :: xs) = replicate(a.r,c) ++ replicate(a.m,u) ++ executionWord(xs), listWeight([]) = 0, and listWeight(a :: xs) = 6*a.m + 20*a.r + listWeight(xs). Thus each positive return contributes a complete c^r u^m run in execution order, and the list concatenates those runs without reversing them.

On List CuLetter, retain wordWeight([]) = 0, wordWeight(c :: w) = 20 + wordWeight(w), and wordWeight(u :: w) = 6 + wordWeight(w). Define halfWeight([]) = 0, halfWeight(c :: w) = 10 + halfWeight(w), and halfWeight(u :: w) = 3 + halfWeight(w). In particular halfWeight(w ++ v) = halfWeight(w) + halfWeight(v), wordWeight(w) = 2*halfWeight(w), and wordWeight(executionWord(xs)) = listWeight(xs). The last identity is the complete_execution_word_parser supplier in `D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Coding/FibonacciFactorCompletion/Bilateral`. The first two identities use List.foldr_hom with the additive letter steps: adding halfWeight(v) transports the append fold, and k ↦ 2*k transports the 10/3 fold to the 20/6 fold. No change of word or letter order is involved.

For each n : Nat, the actual carrier is ReturnFiber(n) := {xs : List Return // xs ≠ [] ∧ listWeight(xs) = 2*n}, and returnCount(n) := Nat.card(ReturnFiber(n)). This excludes the empty execution even at n = 0; it includes every nonempty positive-return list of that weight, with no GuardTrace, side, model, budget or supply condition. The finiteness assertion below concerns this subtype itself, separately from its natural cardinality.

The index n is half the execution weight. It is not the number of returns or the number of c/u letters. The original history is colorsD ++ colorsE ++ (xs.reverse.map(returnColors)).flatten ++ anchor(model), where returnColors(a) = flatten(replicate(a.m,colorsD)) ++ flatten(replicate(a.r,colorsE)), the original anchor is empty, and the anchored model adds colorsD ++ colorsE. Since colorsD and colorsE have lengths 6 and 20, a member of ReturnFiber(n) has history length 26 + 2*n in the original model and 52 + 2*n in the anchored model. The literal source uses the reversed packet order described in the source-address application below, and appends its prescribed finite tail. Its full sourcePrefix length includes that tail; its infinite source is eventually L0. Those lengths and addresses do not redefine the weight in ReturnFiber.

### Both endpoint directions and the actual parser inverse

Every nonempty executionWord(xs) starts with c because the first return has positive r, and ends with u because the last return has positive m. Hence it has the form c :: (w ++ [u]) for a unique literal middle word w. Conversely, for every w : List CuLetter the word c :: (w ++ [u]) ends in u. Apply the existing finite_run_decomposition in the Completion namespace of `D5/S3/ConceptDynamics/Coding/FibonacciFactorCompletion/Bilateral`. Its leading u run has length zero, since a positive leading u run would make the first letter u instead of c. The remaining execution list is nonempty, since its execution word starts with c. This gives an original positive-return list for every middle word, including w = [].

The inverse operation is the same maximal-run parser already described above: from c :: (w ++ [u]), read its maximal initial c run, then its maximal u run, record their positive lengths as (m,r), and continue on the remaining complete runs. The complete_execution_word_parser gives uniqueness of this list and injectivity of executionWord. Starting from a nonempty xs, delete exactly its first c and final u, namely take (executionWord(xs)).tail.dropLast, and parse after putting those two endpoints back. The recovered list is xs by that injectivity. Starting from w, parsing and then deleting the two endpoints returns w by cancellation of the common initial c and final [u]. This describes both inverse laws on the actual words and lists, rather than on their letter multiplicities.

For an arbitrary natural n, the endpoint correspondence therefore has carriers {w : List CuLetter // 13 + halfWeight(w) = n} and ReturnFiber(n). Its forward map parses c :: (w ++ [u]); its inverse strips the endpoints of executionWord(xs). Indeed halfWeight(c :: (w ++ [u])) = 10 + halfWeight(w) + 3 = 13 + halfWeight(w), so the parser's weight identity gives listWeight(xs) = 2*(13 + halfWeight(w)). In the forward direction the carrier equation gives xs ≠ [] and listWeight(xs) = 2*n. In the reverse direction xs ≠ [] supplies the endpoints and listWeight(xs) = 2*n gives 13 + halfWeight(w) = n, cancelling multiplication by 2 in Nat.

Writing the forward and inverse maps here as E_n and F_n, their graph equations are executionWord((E_n(w)).val) = c :: (w.val ++ [u]) and executionWord(xs.val) = c :: ((F_n(xs)).val ++ [u]). Their laws are F_n(E_n(w)) = w and E_n(F_n(xs)) = xs, including the subtype evidence by extensionality. If E_n(w) = E_n(v), the graph equations cancel to w.val = v.val; if two lists have the same execution word, the existing complete parser identifies them. This is a correspondence of the original ordered objects for every n, also when both carriers are empty.

### The literal guarded finite enumeration and both membership directions

Retain the recursive Finset of literal words, with all indices in Nat: middleWords(k) = (if k = 0 then {[]} else ∅) ∪ ((if 3 ≤ k then (middleWords(k-3)).image(List.cons u) else ∅) ∪ (if 10 ≤ k then (middleWords(k-10)).image(List.cons c) else ∅)). The recursion is on k. Each recursive call is made only under its displayed guard and then has strictly smaller index. The three branches enumerate the empty word, a first u followed by a middle word of half weight k-3, and a first c followed by one of half weight k-10. Finset image prefixes the actual list; it does not quotient by letter counts.

The complete membership statement is, for every k : Nat and every w : List CuLetter, w ∈ middleWords(k) ↔ halfWeight(w) = k. Its forward direction sends an enumerated word to its exact half weight; its reverse direction places every word of that half weight in the same literal Finset. For [] the two observations are both 0 = k. For u :: v they are both equivalent to 3 ≤ k ∧ halfWeight(v) = k-3, and for c :: v they are both equivalent to 10 ≤ k ∧ halfWeight(v) = k-10. The reverse implications use the guard to restore k from its natural subtraction; the forward implications obtain the guard from 3 + halfWeight(v) = k or 10 + halfWeight(v) = k.

More explicitly, put cost(c) = 10 and cost(u) = 3, and let the letter step send a predicate q on Nat to k ↦ cost(a) ≤ k ∧ q(k-cost(a)). Both w ↦ (k ↦ w ∈ middleWords(k)) and w ↦ (k ↦ halfWeight(w) = k) have the same empty-word predicate k ↦ 0 = k and the same letter step. List.foldr_hom identifies these two folds, evaluated at the same k. In unfolding the enumeration, membership of a cons in the empty-word branch is impossible, and a u-prefixed word cannot come from the c image or conversely. Thus the equivalence retains both membership directions for arbitrary words, rather than only for words of a chosen run form.

At k = 0, both positive-cost guards are false and middleWords(0) = {[]}. For 0 < k < 3 all three branches are empty. For 3 ≤ k < 10 only the u branch can contribute; at k ≥ 10 both prefixed branches are available, with the empty-word branch absent. At the guard equalities k = 3 and k = 10 the recursive argument is zero and its [] is legitimately prefixed. Below a cost, the corresponding difference would truncate to zero, but the branch is ∅, so it does not incorrectly prefix the zero-weight word. These cases also explain why an empty Finset at a positive k and the singleton {[]} at k = 0 have different meanings.

The membership correspondence {w : List CuLetter // halfWeight(w) = k} ≃ {w : List CuLetter // w ∈ middleWords(k)} is the identity on w in both directions: only the proof field changes, using the reverse membership implication for the forward map and the forward implication for its inverse. Consequently both inverse laws hold by subtype extensionality, and the underlying word is unchanged in each direction. This is also the precise correspondence at k = n-13, whether or not 13 ≤ n; the separate endpoint shift used next is the step that requires that inequality.

### Separate finite carriers and the complete all-natural count conjunction

First establish finiteness. For any k, Finset.Subtype.fintype makes {w // w ∈ middleWords(k)} finite. Transport it through the inverse of the identity-on-words membership correspondence using Finite.of_equiv. This gives Finite {w : List CuLetter // halfWeight(w) = k}, in particular at k = n-13. This argument uses the actual finite enumeration and does not infer finiteness from a value of Nat.card.

When 13 ≤ n, the equation halfWeight(w) = n-13 is equivalent to 13 + halfWeight(w) = n. Equiv.subtypeEquivRight changes only this proof field and leaves w fixed in both directions. Compose that guarded endpoint shift with the endpoint correspondence to obtain an equivalence from the finite half-weight carrier to ReturnFiber(n). Finite.of_equiv gives Finite(ReturnFiber(n)) before any cardinality equality is used. When n < 13, no w can satisfy 13 + halfWeight(w) = n. The inverse endpoint map therefore gives IsEmpty(ReturnFiber(n)), and its empty Fintype supplies finiteness in this branch as well.

Only after those finite carriers are established, Nat.card_congr transports their cardinalities. For 13 ≤ n the chain is Nat.card(ReturnFiber(n)) = Nat.card {w : List CuLetter // 13 + halfWeight(w) = n} = Nat.card {w : List CuLetter // halfWeight(w) = n-13} = (middleWords(n-13)).card. The last equality uses the identity-on-words membership equivalence and the cardinality of a Finset subtype. For n < 13 the actual fiber is empty and its cardinality is zero.

The complete conclusion, with no finiteness premise, is ∀ n : Nat, Finite(ReturnFiber(n)) ∧ returnCount(n) = (if 13 ≤ n then (middleWords(n-13)).card else 0). Equivalently its first conjunct is Finite {xs : List Return // xs ≠ [] ∧ listWeight(xs) = 2*n}, and its second conjunct has Nat.card of that exact same subtype on the left. Projecting either conjunct preserves the universal natural index; the finite statement is not merely a side condition for interpreting the second.

For all n < 13, ReturnFiber(n) is empty, including n = 0. Although n-13 = 0 then, the outer guard suppresses middleWords(0); omitting that guard would falsely count its empty middle. At n = 13 the guard is true and middleWords(0) = {[]} contributes one. Its endpoint word is [c,u], whose parser gives exactly the singleton list [a] with a.m = a.r = 1. This is a nonempty list of weight 26, so returnCount(13) = 1. For n > 13 an empty middle cannot have half weight n-13, and any zero count comes from an empty positive-weight enumeration. No identification of the empty middle with an empty Return list occurs.

### Disjoint empty/u/c partition and guarded cardinalities

For k : Nat write Z_k = (if k = 0 then {[]} else ∅), U_k = (if 3 ≤ k then (middleWords(k-3)).image(List.cons u) else ∅), and C_k = (if 10 ≤ k then (middleWords(k-10)).image(List.cons c) else ∅). These are exactly the three branches of the original definition, so middleWords(k) = Z_k ∪ (U_k ∪ C_k). The empty word cannot equal a cons; and a word in U_k and C_k would have both first letters u and c, which are distinct. Thus U_k and C_k are disjoint and Z_k is disjoint from their union, in every guard case including an empty branch.

For each letter a, List.cons a is injective: a :: v = a :: t implies v = t by List.cons.inj. Hence Finset.card_image_of_injective preserves the cardinality of each enabled prefixed branch. The branch cardinalities are card(Z_k) = (if k = 0 then 1 else 0), card(U_k) = (if 3 ≤ k then (middleWords(k-3)).card else 0), and card(C_k) = (if 10 ≤ k then (middleWords(k-10)).card else 0). Disabled branches have cardinality zero rather than the cardinality at a truncated index.

Apply Finset.card_union_of_disjoint to those two disjoint unions and reorder the three natural summands. For every k : Nat this gives (middleWords(k)).card = (if 3 ≤ k then (middleWords(k-3)).card else 0) + (if 10 ≤ k then (middleWords(k-10)).card else 0) + (if k = 0 then 1 else 0). Thus at k = 0 the two prefix terms vanish and only the empty-word pulse remains; at k = 1 or 2 everything vanishes; for 3 ≤ k < 10 only the u term is present; and at every k ≥ 10 both prefix terms are present. These are cardinalities of disjoint sets of literal words.

### Natural-index transport with both guards and the thirteen pulse

For n ≥ 13 substitute k = n-13 in this middle-word recurrence and use the finite/count conjunction. The natural-index identities n-13-3 = n-3-13 and n-13-10 = n-10-13 hold with truncated subtraction. The corresponding guarded identities, under this n ≥ 13 condition, are 3 ≤ n-13 ↔ (3 ≤ n ∧ 13 ≤ n-3) ↔ 16 ≤ n, and 10 ≤ n-13 ↔ (10 ≤ n ∧ 13 ≤ n-10) ↔ 23 ≤ n. These conjunctions retain both the outer recurrence guard and the inner endpoint threshold of the delayed count.

For the u term, if 3 ≤ n-13 then both 3 ≤ n and 13 ≤ n-3 hold. The count formula at n-3 and the first index identity identify (middleWords(n-13-3)).card with returnCount(n-3).
