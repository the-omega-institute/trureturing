# Dimension bound for finite-rank ternary comparison structures

## Abstract

A noncommutative, preconnected, locally compact group with a continuous injective homomorphism into an orthogonal group, a neighbourhood of the identity of which maps continuously and injectively into R^d, has d >= 3; a noncommutative ternary comparison structure of finite feature rank r on a preconnected, locally compact space with continuous transfer and reading, a neighbourhood of which maps continuously and injectively into R^d, has d >= 3 and r >= 5.

**Theorem 1.1 (Noncommutative orthogonal groups need dimension at least three).**

$$\forall d, n: \mathbb{N}, \forall G: \operatorname{Type}*, [\operatorname{TopologicalSpace}\left(G\right)], [\operatorname{Group}\left(G\right)], [\operatorname{IsTopologicalGroup}\left(G\right)], [\operatorname{LocallyCompactSpace}\left(G\right)], [\operatorname{PreconnectedSpace}\left(G\right)], \forall V: \operatorname{Set}\left(G\right), V \in \operatorname{nhds}\left(1\right) \Rightarrow \forall f: G \to \operatorname{EuclideanSpace}\left(\mathbb{R}, \operatorname{Fin}\left(d\right)\right), \operatorname{ContinuousOn}\left(f, V\right) \Rightarrow \operatorname{InjOn}\left(f, V\right) \Rightarrow \forall \rho: \operatorname{MonoidHom}\left(G, \operatorname{orthogonalGroup}\left(\operatorname{Fin}\left(n\right), \mathbb{R}\right)\right), \operatorname{Continuous}\left(\rho\right) \Rightarrow \operatorname{Injective}\left(\rho\right) \Rightarrow (\exists a, b: G, a \cdot b \neq b \cdot a) \Rightarrow 3 \leq d$$

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Let G be a topological group that is locally compact, preconnected and noncommutative, with a continuous injective homomorphism rho into the orthogonal group of size n. Suppose that some neighbourhood V of the identity admits a map f into Euclidean space of dimension d that is continuous and injective on V. Then d >= 3. No charts and no manifold structure are assumed; a group locally homeomorphic to Euclidean space of dimension d is a special case, since its charts give local compactness and the chart at the identity is such a map f. A real matrix X is a one-parameter generator of rho when exp (t X) lies in the image of rho for every real t; these generators form a real Lie subalgebra L of matrices. Because rho takes orthogonal values, every generator is skew: the product of the transpose of exp (t X) with exp (t X) is constant equal to 1, and its derivative at t = 0 is the transpose of X plus X. On a Lie algebra of skew matrices the trace form (X, Y) -> tr (X Y) is invariant, by cyclicity of the trace, and separating, since tr (X X) is minus the sum of the squares of the entries of X. A real Lie algebra of dimension at most two with an invariant separating bilinear form is abelian. If L were abelian, the exponential chart would map an open neighbourhood of 0 in L onto a neighbourhood of the identity in G whose elements commute pairwise; the subgroup generated by this neighbourhood is open, hence closed, hence all of the preconnected group G, so G would be commutative. Hence L has dimension k >= 3. The exponential chart is injective on an open neighbourhood of 0 in L, so composing it with f gives a continuous injection of a nonempty open subset of Euclidean space of dimension k into Euclidean space of dimension d; for d <= 2 < k this is excluded by the low-dimensional invariance of domain, so d >= 3. The argument uses the one-parameter subgroup method of von Neumann's theorem on closed linear groups, with local compactness of G in place of closedness of the image; the classical route through the theorem of Gleason, Montgomery and Zippin that a locally Euclidean group is a Lie group is not used. Noncommutativity is necessary: the circle group of rotations of the plane is commutative, compact and connected, embeds into the orthogonal group of size two, and a neighbourhood of the identity maps injectively onto an interval, so d = 1 there.

**Theorem 1.2 (Connected carriers give chain-connected readings).**

$$\forall M: \operatorname{Type}*, [\operatorname{TopologicalSpace}\left(M\right)], [\operatorname{PreconnectedSpace}\left(M\right)], \forall C: \operatorname{TernaryComparison}\left(M\right), (\forall x: M, \operatorname{Continuous}\left(\operatorname{kappa}\left(C, x\right)\right)) \Rightarrow \operatorname{ChainConnected}\left(C\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/FiniteRankDimensionBound.chainConnected_of_connected` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Let C be a ternary comparison structure on a preconnected topological space M whose reading kappa x is continuous for every x. Then C is chain connected: for every positive e, any two points are joined by a finite chain in which consecutive points a and b satisfy 1 - e < kappa a b. Fix e. Since kappa a a = 1 and kappa a is continuous, the points b with 1 - e < kappa a b form a neighbourhood of a, and by symmetry of the reading they also satisfy 1 - e < kappa b a. The relation of being joined by such a chain is an equivalence relation that relates every point to all points of a neighbourhood, so on a preconnected space it relates any two points.

**Theorem 1.3 (Dimension and feature rank of noncommutative structures).**

$$\forall d: \mathbb{N}, \forall M: \operatorname{Type}*, [\operatorname{TopologicalSpace}\left(M\right)], [\operatorname{LocallyCompactSpace}\left(M\right)], [\operatorname{PreconnectedSpace}\left(M\right)], \forall C: \operatorname{TernaryComparison}\left(M\right), \operatorname{Continuous}\left((p: M \times M \times M) \mapsto \operatorname{tri}\left(C, p.1, p.2.1, p.2.2\right)\right) \Rightarrow (\forall x: M, \operatorname{Continuous}\left(\operatorname{kappa}\left(C, x\right)\right)) \Rightarrow \operatorname{Noncommutative}\left(C\right) \Rightarrow \forall r: \mathbb{N}, \operatorname{HasFeatureRank}\left(C, r\right) \Rightarrow \forall o: M, \forall V: \operatorname{Set}\left(M\right), V \in \operatorname{nhds}\left(o\right) \Rightarrow \forall f: M \to \operatorname{EuclideanSpace}\left(\mathbb{R}, \operatorname{Fin}\left(d\right)\right), \operatorname{ContinuousOn}\left(f, V\right) \Rightarrow \operatorname{InjOn}\left(f, V\right) \Rightarrow 3 \leq d \land 5 \leq r$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/FiniteRankDimensionBound.three_le_dim_of_hasFeatureRank` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Let C be a ternary comparison structure on a preconnected, locally compact space M, with transfer (x, y, z) -> tri x y z continuous on M x M x M and reading kappa x continuous for every x. Suppose that C is noncommutative, that it has feature rank r, and that some neighbourhood V of a point o admits a map f into Euclidean space of dimension d that is continuous and injective on V. Then d >= 3 and r >= 5. The hypotheses are local compactness, preconnectedness and one neighbourhood with a continuous injective map into Euclidean space of dimension d; a connected manifold of dimension d is a special case, using the chart at o. The group at o, with product tri x o y and identity o, is a topological group because the transfer is continuous. A feature map phi of rank r, with kappa x y = <phi x, phi y>, satisfies |phi x - phi y| ^ 2 = 2 - 2 kappa y x, so it is continuous and injective, and its Gram kernel is left invariant because the reading is invariant under the transfer. The orthogonal representation of this invariant Gram kernel has the stabilizer of the family phi as its kernel, which is trivial since phi is injective. The group at o is noncommutative because C is, so the group bound gives d >= 3. The structure is chain connected because M is preconnected and the reading is continuous, so the feature-rank bound for noncommutative chain-connected structures gives r >= 5. Both bounds are attained together: the unit quaternions with transfer x y^-1 z and reading (1 + <x, y>) / 2 form a noncommutative structure of feature rank five, by the theorem quaternionModel_hasFeatureRank_five, on the unit three-sphere, a compact connected manifold of dimension three.
