# Feature rank of noncommutative ternary comparison structures

## Abstract

A noncommutative, chain-connected ternary comparison structure admits no feature rank below five; at feature rank five, with readings arbitrarily close to zero, it embeds into the unit quaternions.

**Theorem 1.1 (Noncommutative structures need feature rank at least five).**

$$\forall M \in \operatorname{Type}*,\; \forall C \in \operatorname{TernaryComparison}\left(M\right),\; \operatorname{Noncommutative}\left(C\right) \Rightarrow \left(\operatorname{ChainConnected}\left(C\right) \Rightarrow \left(\forall r \in \mathbb{N},\; \operatorname{HasFeatureRank}\left(C, r\right) \Rightarrow 5 \le r\right)\right)$$

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Let C be a ternary comparison structure on M whose transfer is order sensitive and whose reading is chain connected. If the reading is the Gram kernel of a map f from M into Euclidean r-space, that is, kappa x y equals the inner product of f x and f y for all x and y, then r >= 5. Fix a base point o (M is nonempty since the transfer is order sensitive) and let W be the span of the image of f. The image consists of unit vectors, since kappa x x = 1, with pairwise nonnegative inner products, since the reading is nonnegative. Left translations x -> tri g o x of the group at o preserve the reading, so they extend to linear isometries of W permuting the image. They therefore fix the point m of least norm in the closed convex hull of the image; m is nonzero, and the inner product of m with f x equals the squared norm of m for every x. The vectors f x - m span the orthogonal complement E of m in W, whose dimension is less than r, and their Gram kernel is kappa minus the squared norm of m. On E, left and right translations act by two commuting families of linear isometries. The left translations have no common nonzero fixed vector, and by chain connectivity the group they form is generated by translations whose readings against the identity are close to one; such a translation sends no nonzero vector to its negative. The right translations do not all commute, because the group at o is noncommutative. Suppose r <= 4. Then E has dimension at most three, so these facts make the left translations act irreducibly on E with the right translations in their commutant, and the quaternionic coordinates for such a pair force E to have dimension exactly four, a contradiction. Hence r >= 5.

**Theorem 1.2 (Feature rank five and the unit quaternions).**

$$\forall M \in \operatorname{Type}*,\; \forall C \in \operatorname{TernaryComparison}\left(M\right),\; \operatorname{Noncommutative}\left(C\right) \Rightarrow \left(\operatorname{ChainConnected}\left(C\right) \Rightarrow \left(\operatorname{TouchesZero}\left(C\right) \Rightarrow \left(\operatorname{HasFeatureRank}\left(C, 5\right) \Rightarrow \left(\exists u \in M \to \operatorname{unitary}\left(\mathbb{H}\right),\; \operatorname{Injective}\left(u\right) \land \left(\left(\forall x \in M, y \in M, z \in M,\; u\left(\operatorname{tri}\left(C, x, y, z\right)\right) = u\left(x\right) \cdot u\left(y\right)^{-1} \cdot u\left(z\right)\right) \land \left(\forall x \in M, y \in M,\; \operatorname{kappa}\left(C, x, y\right) = \frac{1 + \langle u\left(x\right), u\left(y\right) \rangle_{\mathbb{R}}}{2}\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Quantum/AURICOmega/TernaryComparisonRankFive.exists_unitQuaternion_embedding` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Let C be a ternary comparison structure on M whose transfer is order sensitive, whose reading is chain connected, takes values arbitrarily close to zero (for every positive e some reading is below e), and is the Gram kernel of a map into Euclidean 5-space. Then there is an injective map u from M into the unit quaternions with u (tri x y z) = u x * (u y)^-1 * u z for all x, y, z, and kappa x y = (1 + <u x, u y>) / 2 for all x and y, where the inner product is the real inner product of the quaternions u x and u y in H. In the notation of the feature-rank bound, the quaternionic coordinates on the four-dimensional space E give a product-reversing injective map q into the unit quaternions with kappa x y = a + (1 - a) * <q x, q y>, where a is the squared norm of m and a < 1. Readings arbitrarily close to zero give a <= 1/2. A unit quaternion p different from 1 has a power whose real part is at most minus the real part of p, so every reading between distinct points is at most 2 a; chain connectivity supplies readings between distinct points arbitrarily close to one, which gives a >= 1/2. Hence a = 1/2, and u x is the conjugate of q x, which turns the product-reversing map q into a map carrying tri x y z to u x * (u y)^-1 * u z.
