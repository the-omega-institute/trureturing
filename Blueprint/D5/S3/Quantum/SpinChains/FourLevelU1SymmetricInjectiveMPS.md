# A symmetric injective matrix-product family with four physical levels

## Abstract

Four physical levels with nonzero charge values admit a normalized, symmetric matrix-product family of bond dimension three that is injective at block length two.

*Commentary.*

Use bond dimension 3 and the identity change of physical basis. With matrix units Eᵢⱼ indexed by 0, 1, 2, set A₀ = (3/5)(E₀₁ + E₁₂), A₁ = (4/5)(E₁₀ + E₂₁), A₂ = (4/5)E₀₂ and A₃ = (3/5)E₂₀. Take the four generator eigenvalues to be (−1, 1, −2, 2). Each local matrix has a nonzero entry. Direct multiplication gives Σₖ AₖAₖ† = I: each diagonal entry is 9/25 + 16/25 = 1 and every off-diagonal entry vanishes. Each eigenvalue is nonzero, and the first two differ.

For real φ let V(φ) = diag(1, exp(iφ), exp(2iφ)) and V(φ)⁻¹ = diag(1, exp(−iφ), exp(−2iφ)). Their product is the identity because the exponents cancel in each diagonal entry. Entrywise multiplication gives V(φ)AₖV(φ)⁻¹ = exp(iφλₖ)Aₖ for each k. Induction on a list of physical labels extends this identity to its ordered matrix product: the empty product uses V(φ)V(φ)⁻¹ = I, and the induction step inserts V(φ)⁻¹V(φ) = I between two factors. Taking traces and using invariance under conjugation shows that the product of the scalar phases times the trace of the word equals the original trace.

At the identity change of basis the one-site action is diagonal. Its tensor power is diagonal as well, so its action on a word amplitude is multiplication by the product of exp(iφλₖ) over that word. The trace identity therefore fixes every periodic amplitude. Choosing θ = 0 establishes symmetry at every positive chain length.

For injectivity, the length-two products satisfy A₀A₀ = (9/25)E₀₂, A₁A₁ = (16/25)E₂₀, A₀A₃ = (9/25)E₁₀, A₃A₀ = (9/25)E₂₁, A₁A₂ = (16/25)E₁₂, A₂A₁ = (16/25)E₀₁, A₂A₃ = (12/25)E₀₀, A₃A₂ = (12/25)E₂₂, and A₀A₁ − A₂A₃ = (12/25)E₁₁. Suppose tr(x AᵢAⱼ) = 0 for every pair of physical labels. For any nonzero complex c, the identity tr(x cEᵢⱼ) = c xⱼᵢ forces xⱼᵢ = 0 when this trace vanishes. The first eight products force x₂₀, x₀₂, x₀₁, x₁₂, x₂₁, x₁₀, x₀₀ and x₂₂ to vanish. Subtracting the traces for A₀A₁ and A₂A₃ forces x₁₁ = 0, so x = 0.

If boundary matrices x and y give equal amplitudes for every word of length two, evaluate their amplitude functions on the word with labels i and j. Linearity of the trace gives tr((x − y)AᵢAⱼ) = 0 for every pair. The preceding argument gives x − y = 0, hence x = y. Thus block length two gives injectivity. Applying the proposed obstruction to these local matrices, the identity unitary and these eigenvalues contradicts that injectivity. The example has general integer generator eigenvalues and does not assert SU(2) symmetry or symmetry under the usual spin-three-halves generator.
