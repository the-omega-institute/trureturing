*Commentary.*

Let P be any Hermitian idempotent on a finite complex matrix carrier and let E=I-P. The task predicts the next outcome under repeated use of this fixed instrument; it does not recover complete histories, quantum states or responses under other gates. The initial state is an arbitrary density matrix, including coherent off-diagonal blocks. Put q=Re Tr(P rho), and assume 0<q<1. In the displayed formula, ofMatrixInverse(val(r)) is the raw matrix CStarMatrix.ofMatrix.symm r.val; card is Fintype.card, Sum is the disjoint sum, and contains is list membership.

Set x=1/phi, where phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2, and retain the relative phase zeta^(-3), where zeta=exp(3 pi i/5). The two Kraus branches are M0=sqrt(x) E and M1=x E+zeta^(-3) P. Their adjoint products sum to the identity. Branch states are M_i rho M_i*, and every outcome is retained in source order through runWord.

The mass of every finite Boolean history is the real trace of its branch state and is strictly positive. For n consecutive one outcomes the mass equals q+(1-q)x^(2n), and the conditional P weight is q divided by this mass. The next-zero probability is the ratio of the extended-history mass to the history mass.

After any zero, the posterior P weight is zero and the conditional next-zero probability is x. At every all-one history of length n, including n=0, it is x(1-q)x^(2n)/(q+(1-q)x^(2n)). The computation keeps both CP branches and their relative phase; it does not replace the instrument by a supplied scalar-law certificate.

For every 0 < epsilon < x/2 there is a first nonstrict cutoff N with p(n) <= 2 epsilon exactly when N <= n. A total predictor on Fin(N+1)+Unit attains N+2 states, and every correct predictor on every finite carrier has at least this many states. This includes N=0 and threshold equality. Correctness is tested on the actual conditional trace probabilities at every positive history; it does not assume the scalar law.

For epsilon >= x/2 a single state outputting x/2 suffices. At epsilon=0 no finite predictor is correct, because the positive age probabilities tend to zero and a finite total-update orbit must repeat. Only the state count is asserted. The natural ceiling definition of N is the ceiling of log((1-q)(x-2 epsilon)/(2 epsilon q))/(2 log phi), truncated at zero; Nat.ceil_le gives the nonstrict threshold, including integer ties. The following asymptotic and supplied-clock consequences are applications of this theorem and the cited pinned library results; they add no separate mathematical declaration.

For fixed q in (0,1), put L(epsilon)=log(1/epsilon), D=2 log phi and A(epsilon)=(1-q)(x-2 epsilon)/(2q). As epsilon tends to zero from above, A tends to the positive number (1-q)x/(2q), L tends to infinity, and the real argument f of the natural ceiling equals (L+log A)/D. Thus f/L tends to 1/D. The pinned Mathlib limit tendsto_nat_ceil_div_atTop gives ceil(f)/f tending to one. Consequently (N+2)/L tends to 1/D, which yields O(log(1/epsilon)) states. The constants depend on the fixed q; this is not a uniform q-endpoint estimate.

For m=N+2 let B=Nat.clog 2 m. The pinned Mathlib identity Real.natCeil_logb_natCast gives B=ceil(log(m)/log(2)). The positive state-ratio limit implies log(m)/log(L) tends to one. Applying the same ceiling limit gives B/log(L) tending to 1/log(2), hence O(log log(1/epsilon)) encoding bits. Function.Embedding.nonempty_iff_card_le and Nat.le_pow_clog supply an actual injection of Fin(N+1)+Unit into functions from Fin(B) to Bool. This counts state labels; it provides no bound on output installation, numerical precision, computation time, instrument construction or archive size.

With a separately supplied exact depth, define the flag of a history as its Boolean any-test for an outcome zero. The initial flag is false and the total update is oldFlag OR (currentOutcome=zero). List.any_append verifies this update at every history. Reading x when the flag is true and ageProbability(q,n) otherwise equals the actual conditional trace probability for every finite history, by native_predictor_minimum. Both outputs are in [0,1]. The flag has two states, while the complete depth-and-flag carrier is Nat times Bool and is infinite; depth increments on each outcome. This augmented readout receives n as an additional argument. Its unbounded clock is a supplied resource, and the finite fully charged predictor lower bound still applies to the original contract.

The degenerate q endpoints are context outside the fixed-q theorem and its asymptotic application. The explicit retained Born/CP interpretation remains the model premise; neither the asymptotic application nor the augmented clock construction establishes calibration or physical clock acquisition.

The explicit Born/CP retained-readout interpretation is a model premise. The preparation coefficients are sqrt(x) I and x(E+zeta P), and the reader matrix B_R has rows (-x, sqrt(x) zeta^(-1)) and (sqrt(x) zeta^(-1), -x zeta^3). Their composition equals zeta^2 times these Kraus operators in each record coordinate. The theorem also proves equality of the resulting CP branches, so this common phase does not alter the retained history law. A reconstruction for one designated preparation alone does not establish that interpretation for arbitrary inputs.
