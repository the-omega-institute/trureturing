# Quantum Kac for Actual Monitored Returns

## Abstract

Relevant-space unital CPTP channels have actual mean return time equal to the relevant dimension.

For a finite complex coordinate carrier n and an arbitrary branch index set A, write C(K,P) for the span of all original Kraus words applied to the support of P, including the empty word. The support is the orthogonal complement of the kernel. For Hermitian P it equals the range of P.

**Theorem 1.1 (Monitoring does not change the reachable space).**

$$\begin{aligned}\forall n, A: Type,\\{}[\operatorname{Fintype}(n)] [\operatorname{DecidableEq}(n)],\\\forall K: A \to \operatorname{Matrix}(n, n, \mathbb{C}), \forall P: \operatorname{Matrix}(n, n, \mathbb{C}),\\\operatorname{IsHermitian}(P) \Rightarrow \operatorname{reachableSpace}(a \mapsto (I-P) \operatorname{K}(a), P) = \operatorname{reachableSpace}(K, P)\end{aligned}$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Quantum/Measurements/RelevantSpaceQuantumKac.monitored_reachable_space_eq` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Citation.* P. Sinkovicz, Z. Kurucz, T. Kiss, and J. K. Asboth (2014). *Quantized recurrence time in iterated open quantum dynamics*. URL: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.0568v1>.

*Commentary.*

Each monitored branch is (I-P)K_a. The original and monitored branches differ by P K_a x, a vector in the initial support. Both spaces contain that support and are invariant under their generating branches. Induction on Kraus words therefore gives both inclusions.

When P is the projection onto a unit initial vector, these are the actual unread no-return branches of the monitored channel, and this is Appendix A equation (A9). No unitality or trace-preservation assumption is needed for this space identity. For general Hermitian P the identity is algebraic and does not assert that the branches form a physical instrument.

In the pure-state CPTP setting, the quantum Kac theorem further relates the sum of the actual no-return states to the orthogonal projection onto this common space. Its dimension is the relevant dimension; the space identity by itself is not a convergence or recurrence statement.

**Theorem 1.2 (The monitored operator series).**

$$\begin{aligned}\forall n, A: Type,\\{}[\operatorname{Fintype}(n)] [\operatorname{DecidableEq}(n)] [\operatorname{Fintype}(A)],\\\forall K: A \to \operatorname{Matrix}(n, n, \mathbb{C}), \forall P, J: \operatorname{Matrix}(n, n, \mathbb{C}),\\\operatorname{PosSemidef}(P) \Rightarrow P P = P \Rightarrow\\\operatorname{toEuclideanLin}(J) = \operatorname{starProjection}(\operatorname{reachableSpace}(K, P)) \Rightarrow\\\operatorname{weightedCP}(K, a \mapsto 1, J) = J \Rightarrow \operatorname{HasSum}(m \mapsto \operatorname{iterate}(\operatorname{weightedCP}(a \mapsto (I-P) \operatorname{K}(a), a \mapsto 1), m, P), J)\end{aligned}$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Quantum/Measurements/RelevantSpaceQuantumKac.relevant_projection_hasSum` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Citation.* P. Sinkovicz, Z. Kurucz, T. Kiss, and J. K. Asboth (2014). *Quantized recurrence time in iterated open quantum dynamics*. URL: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.0568v1>.

*Commentary.*

Let E(X) be the sum of K_a X K_a adjoint and let F(X)=(I-P)E(X)(I-P). The space C is generated by the original Kraus words from the support of P; Pi is its orthogonal projection. The result allows P to be a positive projection of any rank. Its only channel normalization assumption is E(Pi)=Pi.

The finite faithful-orbit theorem supplies an L and a positive mu for which the sum R of F^j(P), from j=0 through L, dominates mu Pi on C. Its support is C by the monitored-space identity. Orthogonal decomposition extends this inequality to the full carrier. The identity F(Pi)=Pi-P gives R=Pi-F^(L+1)(Pi). After reducing mu to at most one half, this is a strict contraction over a finite block. Positivity propagates the contraction, bounds the intervening iterates by a geometric sequence, and makes the residual tend to zero. The partial sums telescope to Pi minus that residual.

For a unit initial vector, P is its rank-one projection. Trace preservation is needed to interpret the trace lost at each step as the actual first-return probability. Physical time n+1 corresponds to Tr(P E(F^n(P))); the operator series alone does not state the normalization, weighted first moment or transport to a general Hilbert carrier.

**Theorem 1.3 (Normalization and the first moment of actual return times).**

$$\begin{aligned}\forall n, A: Type,\\{}[\operatorname{Fintype}(n)] [\operatorname{DecidableEq}(n)] [\operatorname{Fintype}(A)],\\\forall K: A \to \operatorname{Matrix}(n, n, \mathbb{C}), \forall P, J: \operatorname{Matrix}(n, n, \mathbb{C}),\\\operatorname{PosSemidef}(P) \Rightarrow P P = P \Rightarrow \operatorname{Tr}(P) = 1 \Rightarrow\\\operatorname{sum}(a \mapsto \operatorname{adjoint}(\operatorname{K}(a)) \operatorname{K}(a)) = I \Rightarrow \operatorname{toEuclideanLin}(J) = \operatorname{starProjection}(\operatorname{reachableSpace}(K, P)) \Rightarrow\\\operatorname{E}(J) = J \Rightarrow\\\operatorname{HasSum}(m \mapsto \operatorname{iterate}(F, m, P), J) \land (\forall m, 0 \leq \operatorname{Re}(\operatorname{Tr}(P \operatorname{E}(\operatorname{iterate}(F, m, P))))) \land\\\operatorname{HasSum}(m \mapsto \operatorname{Re}(\operatorname{Tr}(P \operatorname{E}(\operatorname{iterate}(F, m, P)))), 1) \land \operatorname{HasSum}(m \mapsto (m+1) \operatorname{Re}(\operatorname{Tr}(P \operatorname{E}(\operatorname{iterate}(F, m, P)))), \operatorname{finrank}(\mathbb{C}, \operatorname{reachableSpace}(K, P)))\end{aligned}$$

*Citation.* P. Sinkovicz, Z. Kurucz, T. Kiss, and J. K. Asboth (2014). *Quantized recurrence time in iterated open quantum dynamics*. URL: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.0568v1>.

*Commentary.*

Let n be any finite coordinate carrier, A any finite Kraus index set, and P a positive idempotent matrix with trace one. A unit vector v gives exactly such a matrix P=|v><v|. The completeness equation sum K_a adjoint K_a=I makes E trace preserving. C is the actual original Kraus reachable space from the support of P, and J is its orthogonal projection. Only E(J)=J is assumed.

Write E(X)=sum K_a X K_a adjoint, F(X)=(I-P)E(X)(I-P), and p(k)=Re Tr(P E(F^k(P))). Thus p(k) is the actual first-return probability at physical time k+1, including every preceding no-return measurement. The theorem asserts the operator sum J, nonnegativity of all p(k), total return probability one, and weighted first moment equal to complex finrank C.

Kraus completeness and cyclicity of trace give p(k)=s(k)-s(k+1), where s(k)=Re Tr(F^k(P)). Positivity gives p(k)>=0. Applying trace to the operator series makes s summable. For the nonnegative double family with value p(j) when i<=j and zero otherwise, row sums are s(i), while column sums are (j+1)p(j). Interchanging its two sums gives the first moment. The trace of the actual orthogonal projection is finrank C; no ambient dimension or unmonitored overlap enters this calculation.

**Theorem 1.4 (Quantum Kac on the original finite-dimensional complex carrier).**

$$\begin{aligned}\forall H, A: Type,\\{}[\operatorname{NormedAddCommGroup}(H)] [\operatorname{InnerProductSpace}(\mathbb{C}, H)] [\operatorname{FiniteDimensional}(\mathbb{C}, H)] [\operatorname{Fintype}(A)],\\\forall L: A \to \operatorname{ContinuousEnd}(\mathbb{C}, H), \forall v: H,\\\operatorname{norm}(v) = 1 \Rightarrow \operatorname{sum}(a \mapsto \operatorname{adjoint}(\operatorname{L}(a)) \operatorname{L}(a)) = I \Rightarrow\\P := \operatorname{rankOne}(\mathbb{C}, v, v), C := \operatorname{span}(\mathbb{C}, \operatorname{range}(w \mapsto \operatorname{foldr}(L, v, w)))\\J := \operatorname{starProjection}(C), E := X \mapsto \operatorname{sum}(a \mapsto \operatorname{L}(a) X \operatorname{adjoint}(\operatorname{L}(a)))\\F := X \mapsto (I-P) \operatorname{E}(X) (I-P)\\\operatorname{E}(J) = J \Rightarrow\\\operatorname{HasSum}(m \mapsto \operatorname{iterate}(F, m, P), J) \land (\forall m, 0 \leq \operatorname{Re}(\operatorname{Tr}(P \operatorname{E}(\operatorname{iterate}(F, m, P))))) \land\\\operatorname{HasSum}(m \mapsto \operatorname{Re}(\operatorname{Tr}(P \operatorname{E}(\operatorname{iterate}(F, m, P)))), 1) \land \operatorname{HasSum}(m \mapsto (m+1) \operatorname{Re}(\operatorname{Tr}(P \operatorname{E}(\operatorname{iterate}(F, m, P)))), \operatorname{finrank}(\mathbb{C}, C))\end{aligned}$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Quantum/Measurements/RelevantSpaceQuantumKac.pure_vector_quantum_kac` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Citation.* P. Sinkovicz, Z. Kurucz, T. Kiss, and J. K. Asboth (2014). *Quantized recurrence time in iterated open quantum dynamics*. URL: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.0568v1>.

*Commentary.*

H is any finite-dimensional complex inner product space, A any finite Kraus index set, L_a the original endomorphisms, and v the original unit vector. Endomorphisms are represented as continuous linear maps; every linear map on H is continuous. Define L_w v by folding the word from right to left, with the empty word giving v. C is the complex span of all these vectors, P=|v><v|, and J is the genuine orthogonal projection onto C. Assume Kraus completeness sum L_a adjoint L_a=I and only relevant-space unitality E(J)=J.

With E(X)=sum L_a X L_a adjoint and F(X)=(I-P)E(X)(I-P), the probability at physical time k+1 is Re Tr(P E(F^k(P))). The theorem proves HasSum of the original operator orbit to J, nonnegativity, total probability one, and weighted mean complex finrank C. Neither global unitality, adjoint invariance, irreducibility, primitivity nor assumed recurrence is added.

Choose an orthonormal basis internally. Its representation sends the original rank-one operator to its outer-product matrix and intertwines every original Kraus operator. The range of that matrix is the image of the line through v. Induction on original words and linear span proves that the matrix reachable space is exactly the image of C. The isometry transports its orthogonal projection, not merely its dimension. Matrix representation intertwines E and F, hence each actual monitored iterate, while trace preservation under coordinates identifies every return probability. Applying the inverse continuous linear coordinate map to the matrix operator series and using preservation of finrank yields all four conclusions on H.
