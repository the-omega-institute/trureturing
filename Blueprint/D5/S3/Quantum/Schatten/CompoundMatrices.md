# CompoundMatrices

## Abstract

Compound matrices and singular products.

The kth compound matrix has entries equal to kth order minors. Cauchy-Binet makes the compound construction multiplicative. Unitary diagonalization identifies its singular values with products over kth order subsets; sorting identifies the largest such product.

The source is Alex Meiburg's Apache-2.0 Physlib at revision b9043cc548ef6d63a28454cf3a57fb12a0c2e142. These results support the Schatten product Holder inequality. No mathematical originality is claimed.

Ordered Slater amplitudes give a further application of the rectangular cauchyBinet formula over the complex numbers and over complex polynomials. Let N and k be arbitrary natural numbers, let the sites be Fin N in their natural order, and let V be a complex N by k matrix with V†V = I. A Boolean occupation word x has occupied set S(x) = {a | x(a) = true}. If this set has cardinality k, write V[S(x)] for the square matrix whose rows are selected by S(x).orderEmbOfFin in increasing site order and whose columns retain their original order. Set ψV(x) = det(V[S(x)]) in this case and ψV(x) = 0 otherwise. The empty determinant is 1. These are coordinates in the normalized Boolean occupation basis, so no factor involving the square root of k factorial occurs.

Define ρV(x,y) = ψV(x) conjugate(ψV(y)) and P = VV†. Use exactly JordanWigner.fullC for each annihilator c(a). HardCoreCompression.fullC_entry identifies its entry from t to s: it vanishes unless t occupies a and s is t with a emptied; its nonzero value is (−1) raised to prefixCount(t,a), the number of occupied sites strictly below a in the original site order. The fullC_sparse and fullC_create_sparse identities in PeriodicSshExcessPseudoEntropyRefutation apply these same matrices to an arbitrary complex coordinate function. They supply the annihilation and creation actions without any assumption about Gaussianity or a covariance matrix.

For fixed sites i and j introduce the elementary matrix E(j,i), whose only nonzero entry is 1 in row j and column i. Over the polynomial ring in X set W(X) = (I + X E(j,i)) V, with all complex entries embedded as constant polynomials. Thus only row j changes: W(j,b) = V(j,b) + X V(i,b). Define ψW(X)(x) by the same ordered-minor rule. The coefficient of X in ψW(X)(x) is exactly the coordinate at x of c(j)† c(i) ψV.

This coefficient correspondence retains every occupation case. If j is absent, the selected minor is constant in X and the creation action is zero. If j is present, put R = S(x) with j erased. When i belongs to R, replacing row j by row i produces two equal rows and the determinant is zero; annihilation at the already occupied coordinate of the intermediate word is also zero. Otherwise the common coefficient is (−1) raised to (|{a in R | a < j}| + |{a in R | a < i}|), multiplied by the ordered minor on R with i inserted. The formulas include i = j. Words of the wrong cardinality give zero on both sides because this removal followed by insertion preserves cardinality.

For the sign comparison, the sorted_position_count and sorted_removeNth identities in PhysicalContractionRdmBridge transfer from finite subsets of the natural numbers through the order-preserving map Fin.val. Fin.cons_removeNth_eq_comp_cycleRange_symm moves the replaced row to the head; Matrix.det_permute and Fin.sign_cycleRange give its sign. Applying this once for the erased j and once for the inserted i gives the two counts above. Matrix.det_updateRow_add and det_updateRow_smul extract the linear coefficient, while det_updateRow_eq_zero handles the repeated row case. This uses neither a quotient of minors nor division by an amplitude.

Now set F(X) = det(V† W(X)). Rectangular cauchyBinet over complex polynomials gives F(X) as the sum over all Boolean words x of conjugate(ψV(x)) ψW(X)(x); Matrix.det_conjTranspose supplies the conjugated minor. Since V†V = I, the same polynomial is det(I + X V† E(j,i) V). Matrix.coeff_det_one_add_X_smul_one identifies its linear coefficient with trace(V† E(j,i) V). Matrix.trace_mul_cycle and trace_mul_single turn that trace into P(i,j). On the occupation side, finite coefficient extraction and the preceding correspondence give the sum of conjugate(ψV(x)) times the coordinate of c(j)† c(i) ψV. Matrix.mul_vecMulVec and trace_vecMulVec identify this with trace((ρV c(j)†) c(i)). Consequently K(i,j) = P(i,j), whereas the convention C(i,j) = trace((ρV c(i)†) c(j)) gives C(i,j) = P(j,i) = conjugate(P(i,j)). In matrix notation C is the transpose of P.

The occupation-basis and Slater conventions agree with Jan Dereziński, Clifford algebras and fermions, sections 7.1 and 7.2, equations (7.51)–(7.54). Theorem 8.1 and equation (8.103), giving the expectation of dΓ(h) as trace(πh), state the corresponding one-body expectation formula. The source is https://www.fuw.edu.pl/~derezins/clifford.pdf. The ordered determinant amplitudes and their two-point identity are standard applications; no mathematical originality is claimed for them.

All sums and polynomial identities are finite. The argument covers vacuum and full filling, equal sites, either ordering of distinct sites and arbitrary complex phases. N = 0 leaves no site indices to choose. No positive filling premise or strict inequality k < N is used. The construction concerns the specified determinant vector and the original Jordan-Wigner operators; it does not by itself identify a ground state of a chosen Hamiltonian or establish a spatial reduced spectrum or an entropy scaling law.

The same natural-order Slater amplitudes also admit the following compound-lift and prefix-locality application, used by the literal prefix spectrum and entropy derivation in PartialTraceMutualInformation.

Application to ordered Slater amplitudes. Fix natural numbers N and k with k at most N, and complex N by k matrices V and W. For each k-element subset S of Fin N, let d(S,V) be the determinant obtained by retaining the rows of V in increasing natural site order and leaving the column order unchanged. The rectangular cauchyBinet theorem, with the natural LinearOrder on Fin N and factors V adjoint and W, gives det(V adjoint W) = sum over S of conjugate(d(S,V)) times d(S,W). Thus V adjoint V = I gives sum over S of conjugate(d(S,V)) times d(S,V) = 1. The empty determinant is one, so this includes k = 0 without a factorial factor.

The ordering in this application needs an explicit comparison. The definition compoundMatrix fixes AllOrdered's classical order, whereas cauchyBinet accepts a supplied LinearOrder. For each S, let p(S) be the permutation of Fin k characterized by naturalEnum(S,i) = classicalEnum(S,p(S)(i)), and let epsilon(S) be its sign, cast to the complex numbers. For a complex N by N matrix U, let Cnat(U,k) use natural increasing rows and columns. Then Cnat(U,k)(S,T) = epsilon(S) epsilon(T) compoundMatrix(U,k)(S,T). Matrix.det_permute and Matrix.det_permute' give the two signs. They must be retained when using the frozen compound matrix in the ordered occupation basis.

For complex N by N matrices U and Q, applying cauchyBinet directly in natural order gives Cnat(U Q,k) = Cnat(U,k) Cnat(Q,k). Conjugate transpose commutes with this construction by Matrix.det_conjTranspose, and the compound of the identity is the identity. Consequently U adjoint U = I implies Cnat(U,k) adjoint Cnat(U,k) = I. For any complex N by N matrix B and N by k matrix V, the same rectangular formula gives d(S,B V) = sum over k-element T of Cnat(B,k)(S,T) d(T,V). This is the action on the actual determinant amplitudes, with no covariance or Gaussian-state premise.

Extend the natural compounds to the full Boolean occupation carrier. For words x and y on Fin m, define Gamma_m(B)(x,y) as the natural minor B[S(x),S(y)] when the two occupied subsets have the same cardinality, and as zero otherwise. This includes the vacuum sector, whose empty minor is one. Sectorwise cauchyBinet and the Boolean-word/subset bijection give Gamma(B C) = Gamma(B) Gamma(C), Gamma(B adjoint) = Gamma(B) adjoint, and Gamma(I) = I. The last identity uses a zero row when equal-size subsets differ and Matrix.submatrix_one when they agree. Thus U adjoint U = I gives Gamma(U) adjoint Gamma(U) = I on every occupation word.

Ordered-prefix locality holds for any complex ell by ell matrix U, without a unitarity hypothesis. Write B = U directSum I_n in Fin (ell + n) order and concatenate words literally with Fin.append. Then Gamma_(ell+n)(B)(ab,a'b') = Gamma_ell(U)(a,a') delta(b,b'). To see this in natural minor coordinates, the occupied subset of ab is S(a) mapped by Fin.castAdd n, union S(b) mapped by Fin.natAdd ell. These maps have disjoint images. Finset.orderEmbOfFin_unique identifies its increasing enumeration with the prefix enumeration followed by the tail enumeration. If b differs from b', a tail site missing from the other subset gives an identically zero row or column, so Matrix.det_eq_zero_of_row_eq_zero or its column version applies. If the tails agree, equal total particle count forces equal prefix particle count; reindexing by finSumFinEquiv gives a block matrix with the prefix minor and an identity tail block. Matrix.det_fromBlocks_zero₂₁ gives exactly the prefix determinant. Unequal total counts give zero by definition. No shuffle sign appears because every prefix site precedes every tail site.
