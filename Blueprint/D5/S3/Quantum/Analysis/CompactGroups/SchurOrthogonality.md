# Schur Orthogonality of Matrix Coefficients on Compact Groups

## Abstract

Let G be a compact group with a finite left-invariant measure mu, rho a representation of G on a finite-dimensional complex normed space with continuous orbit maps, sigma a representation of G on a complex vector space, both irreducible, and phi and psi linear functionals such that every coefficient g -> psi(sigma(g)(w)) is continuous. The integral of phi(rho(g)(v)) * psi(sigma(g^(-1))(w)) against mu vanishes when rho and sigma are inequivalent, and for sigma = rho it equals the total mass of mu times phi(w) * psi(v), divided by the dimension.

**Theorem 1.1 (Matrix coefficients of inequivalent irreducible representations are orthogonal).**

$$\forall G \in \operatorname{Type}*,\; \forall V \in \operatorname{Type}*,\; \forall W \in \operatorname{Type}*,\; [\operatorname{Group}\left(G\right)], [\operatorname{TopologicalSpace}\left(G\right)], [\operatorname{IsTopologicalGroup}\left(G\right)], [\operatorname{CompactSpace}\left(G\right)], [\operatorname{MeasurableSpace}\left(G\right)], [\operatorname{BorelSpace}\left(G\right)], \forall \mu \in \operatorname{Measure}\left(G\right),\; [\operatorname{IsMulLeftInvariant}\left(\mu\right)], [\operatorname{IsFiniteMeasure}\left(\mu\right)], [\operatorname{NormedAddCommGroup}\left(V\right)], [\operatorname{NormedSpace}\left(\mathbb{C}, V\right)], [\operatorname{FiniteDimensional}\left(\mathbb{C}, V\right)], [\operatorname{AddCommGroup}\left(W\right)], [\operatorname{Module}\left(\mathbb{C}, W\right)], \forall \rho \in \operatorname{Representation}\left(\mathbb{C}, G, V\right),\; \forall \sigma \in \operatorname{Representation}\left(\mathbb{C}, G, W\right),\; [\operatorname{IsIrreducible}\left(\rho\right)], [\operatorname{IsIrreducible}\left(\sigma\right)], \operatorname{IsEmpty}\left(\operatorname{Equiv}\left(\rho, \sigma\right)\right) \Rightarrow \left((\forall v \in V,\; \operatorname{Continuous}\left(g \mapsto \rho\left(g\right)\left(v\right)\right)) \Rightarrow \forall \phi \in \operatorname{Dual}\left(\mathbb{C}, V\right),\; \forall \psi \in \operatorname{Dual}\left(\mathbb{C}, W\right),\; (\forall w \in W,\; \operatorname{Continuous}\left(g \mapsto \psi\left(\sigma\left(g\right)\left(w\right)\right)\right)) \Rightarrow \left(\forall v \in V,\; \forall w \in W,\; \int \phi\left(\rho\left(g\right)\left(v\right)\right) \cdot \psi\left(\sigma\left(g^{-1}\right)\left(w\right)\right) \,\mathrm{d}\mu\left(g\right) = 0\right)\right)$$

*Commentary.*

Let G be a compact topological group carrying its Borel sigma-algebra and a finite measure mu that is invariant under left translations; in the formula these are the hypotheses IsMulLeftInvariant(mu) and IsFiniteMeasure(mu). Let rho be a representation of G on a finite-dimensional complex normed space V, and sigma a representation of G on a complex vector space W, which carries no topology. Both are irreducible: each is nonzero and has no invariant subspaces other than 0 and the whole space. Assume that there is no equivalence of representations from rho to sigma, that is, no linear isomorphism from V onto W intertwining rho(g) with sigma(g) for every g, that every orbit map g -> rho(g)(v) is continuous, and that phi is a complex linear functional on V and psi a complex linear functional on W such that every coefficient g -> psi(sigma(g)(w)) is continuous. The theorem states that for all v in V and w in W the integral over G, with respect to mu, of phi(rho(g)(v)) * psi(sigma(g^(-1))(w)) is 0. In the formula Dual(C, V) is the space of complex linear functionals on V, Equiv(rho, sigma) the type of equivalences from rho to sigma, and the integration variable is g.

For fixed v the map T sending x in W to the vector-valued integral of psi(sigma(g^(-1))(x)) rho(g)(v) is complex linear. The integrand is continuous on the compact group, hence integrable for the finite measure mu, and the integral exists because the finite-dimensional space V is complete. Left invariance of mu turns T(sigma(h)(x)) into the integral of psi(sigma((h g)^(-1))(sigma(h)(x))) rho(h g)(v) = psi(sigma(g^(-1))(x)) rho(h)(rho(g)(v)), and moving the linear map rho(h) out of the integral gives T(sigma(h)(x)) = rho(h)(T(x)); so T intertwines sigma with rho. Applying phi to T(w) gives the integral of the theorem. By Schur's lemma a nonzero intertwining map between irreducible representations is bijective, hence an equivalence; since sigma and rho are not equivalent, T = 0 and the integral vanishes. Only the left invariance and the finiteness of mu enter: mu need not be regular, positive on open sets or nonzero, and for the zero measure the conclusion holds trivially.

Neither irreducibility hypothesis can be dropped, nor inequivalence, once mu is not the zero measure, for instance for a Haar measure. If rho and sigma are both the trivial representation on C, with phi and psi the identity and v = w = 1, every hypothesis except inequivalence holds and the integral is the total mass mu(G), which is then positive. If rho is the trivial representation on C and sigma the trivial representation on C x C, the two are inequivalent because their dimensions differ and sigma is reducible; with phi the identity, psi the first coordinate, v = 1 and w = (1, 0) the integral is again mu(G). Exchanging the roles, with rho trivial on C x C and sigma trivial on C, shows the same for the irreducibility of rho.

In the classical unitary setting the statement takes a familiar form. If V and W are finite-dimensional complex inner product spaces, with the inner product conjugate linear in its first argument, and rho and sigma act by unitary operators, then sigma(g^(-1)) is the adjoint of sigma(g). Taking phi the functional x -> <v, x> and psi the functional x -> <w', x>, one has psi(sigma(g^(-1))(w)) = <sigma(g)(w'), w>, the complex conjugate of <w, sigma(g)(w')>, so the theorem applied to the vectors v' and w shows that the integral of <v, rho(g)(v')> times the complex conjugate of <w, sigma(g)(w')> is 0 for inequivalent irreducible unitary representations. This consequence follows by the substitution just described and is not among the formal statements here.

These are the orthogonality relations of Schur for matrix coefficients, in the form for compact groups in which the average over a finite group is replaced by the integral against Haar measure; the relations are classical and belong to the background of abstract harmonic analysis. The form given here uses arbitrary linear functionals in place of an invariant inner product, does not assume that the representations are unitary, and asks of the measure only left invariance and finiteness.

**Theorem 1.2 (The integral of a product of matrix coefficients of one irreducible representation).**

$$\forall G \in \operatorname{Type}*,\; \forall V \in \operatorname{Type}*,\; [\operatorname{Group}\left(G\right)], [\operatorname{TopologicalSpace}\left(G\right)], [\operatorname{IsTopologicalGroup}\left(G\right)], [\operatorname{CompactSpace}\left(G\right)], [\operatorname{MeasurableSpace}\left(G\right)], [\operatorname{BorelSpace}\left(G\right)], \forall \mu \in \operatorname{Measure}\left(G\right),\; [\operatorname{IsMulLeftInvariant}\left(\mu\right)], [\operatorname{IsFiniteMeasure}\left(\mu\right)], [\operatorname{NormedAddCommGroup}\left(V\right)], [\operatorname{NormedSpace}\left(\mathbb{C}, V\right)], [\operatorname{FiniteDimensional}\left(\mathbb{C}, V\right)], \forall \rho \in \operatorname{Representation}\left(\mathbb{C}, G, V\right),\; [\operatorname{IsIrreducible}\left(\rho\right)], (\forall v \in V,\; \operatorname{Continuous}\left(g \mapsto \rho\left(g\right)\left(v\right)\right)) \Rightarrow \forall \phi \in \operatorname{Dual}\left(\mathbb{C}, V\right),\; \forall \psi \in \operatorname{Dual}\left(\mathbb{C}, V\right),\; \forall v \in V,\; \forall w \in V,\; \int \phi\left(\rho\left(g\right)\left(v\right)\right) \cdot \psi\left(\rho\left(g^{-1}\right)\left(w\right)\right) \,\mathrm{d}\mu\left(g\right) = \frac{\operatorname{real}\left(\mu, \operatorname{univ}\right) \cdot (\phi\left(w\right) \cdot \psi\left(v\right))}{\operatorname{finrank}\left(\mathbb{C}, V\right)}$$

*Commentary.*

Let G be a compact topological group carrying its Borel sigma-algebra and a finite measure mu invariant under left translations. Let rho be an irreducible representation of G on a finite-dimensional complex normed space V such that every orbit map g -> rho(g)(v) is continuous. The theorem states that for complex linear functionals phi and psi on V and vectors v and w in V, the integral over G, with respect to mu, of phi(rho(g)(v)) * psi(rho(g^(-1))(w)) equals real(mu, univ) * (phi(w) * psi(v)) / dim V. Here real(mu, univ) is the total mass mu(G), a finite nonnegative real number, and dim V = finrank(C, V) is the complex dimension of V; both are read as complex numbers. No continuity hypothesis on psi is needed, because every linear functional on a finite-dimensional normed space is continuous.

As for inequivalent representations, the map T sending x in V to the integral of psi(rho(g^(-1))(x)) rho(g)(v) is linear and, by left invariance of mu, intertwines rho with itself, and phi(T(w)) is the integral of the theorem. Since V is finite-dimensional, rho is irreducible and the complex field is algebraically closed, Schur's lemma gives a complex number c with T(x) = c x for all x. To find c, take a basis b(1), ..., b(n) of V with coordinate functionals b*(1), ..., b*(n), where n = dim V. The trace of T is n c, and it is also the sum over i of b*(i)(T(b(i))), which is the integral of the sum over i of psi(rho(g^(-1))(b(i))) b*(i)(rho(g)(v)). By linearity of psi and rho(g^(-1)) this sum is psi(rho(g^(-1))(rho(g)(v))) = psi(v), a constant whose integral is mu(G) psi(v). Hence n c = mu(G) psi(v). An irreducible representation is nonzero, so n is positive, c = mu(G) psi(v) / n, and the integral is phi(c w) = mu(G) * (phi(w) * psi(v)) / n. For the zero measure both sides are 0, and the argument covers this case without change.

The total mass of mu enters as a factor: replacing mu by a positive multiple multiplies both sides by that number, and for a measure of total mass 1, such as a normalized Haar measure, the right side is phi(w) * psi(v) / dim V. The irreducibility of rho cannot be dropped once mu is not the zero measure. For the trivial representation of a compact group on C x C, which is reducible, with phi and psi the first coordinate and v = w = (1, 0), the left side is mu(G) while the right side is mu(G) / 2. Finite dimensionality of V is used for the completeness of V, the continuity of psi, the scalar form of Schur's lemma and the trace.

For a unitary irreducible representation on a finite-dimensional complex inner product space, with the inner product conjugate linear in its first argument, take phi the functional x -> <v, x> and psi the functional x -> <w', x>. Since rho(g^(-1)) is the adjoint of rho(g), psi(rho(g^(-1))(w)) is the complex conjugate of <w, rho(g)(w')>, and the theorem applied to the vectors v' and w gives the classical relation: the integral of <v, rho(g)(v')> times the complex conjugate of <w, rho(g)(w')> equals mu(G) <v, w> <w', v'> / dim V, which is <v, w> <w', v'> / dim V for a normalized Haar measure. This consequence follows by the substitution just described and is not among the formal statements here.

This is the second of the classical Schur orthogonality relations for compact groups. The statement for arbitrary linear functionals contains the unitary form and does not assume unitarity; only the left invariance and the finiteness of mu enter, and its total mass appears explicitly instead of being normalized to 1.
