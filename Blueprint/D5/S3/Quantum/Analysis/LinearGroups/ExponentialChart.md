# Exponential Chart of a Locally Compact Group in a Finite-Dimensional Algebra

## Abstract

For a locally compact topological group G with a continuous injective homomorphism rho into the units of a finite-dimensional real normed algebra A, the time-one map epsilon of the one-parameter subgroups is a continuous map from the Lie subalgebra L of one-parameter generators to G with rho(epsilon(X)) = exp(X), and its restriction to an open neighbourhood of 0 in L is an open embedding into G.

**Theorem 1.1 (Exponential chart at the identity).**

$$\forall A \in \operatorname{Type}*,\; [\operatorname{NormedRing}\left(A\right)],\ \forall G \in \operatorname{Type}*,\; [\operatorname{TopologicalSpace}\left(G\right)],\ [\operatorname{Group}\left(G\right)],\ [\operatorname{NormedAlgebra}\left(\mathbb{R}, A\right)],\ [\operatorname{FiniteDimensional}\left(\mathbb{R}, A\right)],\ [\operatorname{IsTopologicalGroup}\left(G\right)],\ [\operatorname{LocallyCompactSpace}\left(G\right)],\ \forall rho \in \operatorname{MonoidHom}\left(G, \operatorname{Units}\left(A\right)\right),\; \forall hc \in \operatorname{Continuous}\left(rho\right),\; \forall hi \in \operatorname{Injective}\left(rho\right),\; \exists epsilon \in \operatorname{generatorSubalgebra}\left(rho, hc, hi\right) \to G,\; \operatorname{Continuous}\left(epsilon\right) \land \left(\left(\forall X \in \operatorname{generatorSubalgebra}\left(rho, hc, hi\right),\; \operatorname{val}\left(rho\left(epsilon\left(X\right)\right)\right) = \operatorname{exp}\left(\operatorname{val}\left(X\right)\right)\right) \land \left(\exists U \in \operatorname{Set}\left(\operatorname{generatorSubalgebra}\left(rho, hc, hi\right)\right),\; \operatorname{IsOpen}\left(U\right) \land \left(0 \in U \land \operatorname{IsOpenEmbedding}\left(\operatorname{domRestrict}\left(U, epsilon\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)$$

*Commentary.*

Let A be a finite-dimensional real normed algebra, let G be a locally compact topological group and let rho be a continuous injective group homomorphism from G into the group Units(A) of invertible elements of A, with hc a proof of continuity and hi a proof of injectivity. Let L = generatorSubalgebra(rho, hc, hi) be the Lie subalgebra of one-parameter generators for rho, with the topology induced from A; val denotes the inclusion of Units(A) and of L into A, and exp is the exponential series of A. Then there is a continuous map epsilon from L to G such that val(rho(epsilon(X))) = exp(val(X)) for every X in L, and an open subset U of L containing 0 such that the restriction domRestrict(U, epsilon) of epsilon to U is an open embedding of U into G, that is, a homeomorphism of U onto an open subset of G.

Injectivity of rho makes epsilon unique: epsilon(X) is the only element of G whose image under rho is exp(X), namely the value at time 1 of the one-parameter subgroup with generator X, and in particular epsilon(0) is the identity. Two familiar local properties of the exponential map are immediate consequences of the open embedding: epsilon is injective on the neighbourhood U of 0, and epsilon maps every neighbourhood of 0 in L onto a neighbourhood of the identity in G, because it maps the open sets of L contained in U onto open sets of G.

The theorem is therefore a chart of G at the identity modelled on the real vector space L, whose dimension is finite since L is a subspace of A. Composing with left translations gives charts at every point, so G is locally Euclidean of dimension equal to the dimension of L. Neither a manifold structure nor a Lie group structure on G is assumed. The classical statement is the closed-subgroup theorem of J. von Neumann for closed subgroups of the general linear group and of E. Cartan for closed subgroups of Lie groups; here closedness of a linear group is replaced by local compactness of G together with continuity and injectivity of rho.

The proof has four steps. First, a compact neighbourhood V of the identity in G has a boundary whose image under rho is compact and misses 1, so a path in G starting at the identity whose image under rho stays close to 1 never leaves V. Second, epsilon is continuous on all of L: near X1, the path t -> epsilon(t X1)^(-1) epsilon(t X) on [0, 1] has image under rho uniformly close to 1, so it ends in any prescribed compact neighbourhood of the identity. Third, images of neighbourhoods of 0 are neighbourhoods of the identity. Choose a complement C of L in A. The map (Y, B) -> exp(Y) exp(B) from L x C to A has the sum map as derivative at 0, so by the inverse function theorem every element of A near 1 factors as exp(Y) exp(B) with Y in L and B in C small. If elements of G outside the image accumulated at the identity, their C-components B would be nonzero, and by the escape lemma of the generator theory, applied along a sequence, the directions B / norm(B) would accumulate at a one-parameter generator of norm one lying in C, which is impossible since L and C meet only in 0. Fourth, let S be a domain around 0 on which exp has a continuous local inverse log, and let W be the interior of the image under epsilon of the points of L in S, an open neighbourhood of the identity by the third step. On U, the set of points of L in S whose image lies in W, the map epsilon is injective, and for an open set O of L contained in U the image of O is the set of points g of W with log(val(rho(g))) in O, which is open.

Local compactness of G cannot be dropped from the conclusion. Let G be the additive group of rational numbers with its usual topology, A the real numbers and rho(q) = exp(q), a continuous injective homomorphism. A real X is a one-parameter generator only if t -> t X has a continuous lift into the rationals, which forces X = 0, since a continuous map from the real line into the rationals is constant. So the generators form the single point 0, and no map from it restricts to an open embedding of a neighbourhood of 0: the image would be a single point of the rationals, which is not open.
