# Varying contraction backgrounds and measurable evolution

## Abstract

Compact-time strong convergence of contraction backgrounds and fixed-vector L1 convergence of separately controlled measurable coefficients imply uniform strong convergence of the original nonautonomous evolution on the full closed ordered time triangle.

Let E be a complex normed space in any universe. The assertions about Dyson terms and evolution assume completeness; the compact-range integral assertion does not. The zero space is included. A contraction background is the original NonautonomousDysonPhillips.Contraction E, with complex-linear contraction operators on nonnegative times, identity at zero, the semigroup law and continuous vector orbits. Write A(h)=S.action(h)=S.operator(Real.toNNReal(h)), and A_n(h)=(Sseq n).action(h). Strong convergence on a compact time interval means that for every fixed x and epsilon>0 one eventual index controls norm(A_n(h)x-A(h)x) for every 0<=h<=T. There is no uniformity over the unit ball.

$$
\forall u \in \operatorname{Universe}\left(\right),\; \forall E \in \operatorname{Type}\left(u\right),\; [\operatorname{NormedAddCommGroup}\left(E\right)] [\operatorname{NormedSpace}\left(\operatorname{Complex}\left(\right), E\right)] [\operatorname{CompleteSpace}\left(E\right)] \forall S \in \operatorname{Contraction}\left(E\right),\; \forall B \in \operatorname{Real}\left(\right) \to \operatorname{ComplexCLM}\left(E, E\right),\; \forall x \in E,\; \forall t \in \operatorname{Real}\left(\right),\; \forall r \in \operatorname{Real}\left(\right),\; \operatorname{term}\left(S, B, 0, t, r, x\right) = \operatorname{action}\left(S, t - r, x\right) \land \left(\forall m \in \operatorname{Nat}\left(\right),\; \operatorname{term}\left(S, B, m + 1, t, r, x\right) = \operatorname{intervalIntegral}\left(\operatorname{volume}\left(\right), r, t, {s:\operatorname{Real}\left(\right)}\mapsto \operatorname{action}\left(S, t - s, B\left(s\right)\left(\operatorname{term}\left(S, B, m, s, r, x\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)

The displayed terms and vector Bochner integrals are exactly the original NonautonomousDysonPhillips.term. Coefficients are evaluated at absolute time s, and A(t-s) acts after B(s). The evolution is the original coefficient-indexed MeasurableNonautonomousDysonPhillips.measurableEvolution. Its existing realization identity identifies it with the original sum of these terms on every admissible ordered interval.

*Commentary.*

Let T>=0, J=Icc(0,T), and Delta(T)={(t,r):0<=r<=t<=T}. Every fixed-vector slice of B_n and B is AE strongly measurable for volume restricted to J. Each n has a separate real integrable control g_n on J; B has another control g. At every s in J these controls are nonnegative and bound the corresponding operator norms. A real C>=0 bounds every integral of g_n and the integral of g from 0 to T. For each fixed x, the integral from 0 to T of norm((B_n(s)-B(s))x) tends to zero. Under these hypotheses and compact-time strong convergence of the backgrounds, for every natural m, vector x and epsilon>0 there is one N such that all n>=N and all (t,r) in Delta(T) have term discrepancy less than epsilon.

For the limiting path v_r(s)=term(S,B,m,max(s,r),r,x), original joint continuity makes its range over J times J compact. The existing coefficient path error theorem controls the integral of norm((B_n(s)-B(s))v_r(s)) uniformly in r, using the separate mass bound for g_n+g. Apply the background integral assertion with h_t(s)=max(t-s,0). This continuous time choice lies in J for s,t in J. For r<=s<=t, both clamps agree with the original recurrence arguments.

The successor discrepancy is the integral of three contributions: A_n(t-s)B_n(s) times the previous-term difference; A_n(t-s)(B_n(s)-B(s)) times the limiting term; and (A_n(t-s)-A(t-s))B(s) times that same limiting term. Original term_integrable justifies subtraction of the two original Bochner integrals. Contractivity and the coefficient norm bound give g_n(s)*eta for the first contribution, whose integral is at most C*eta. The two uniform integral assertions control the other contributions. Choose eta>0 with C*eta<epsilon/3 and each other tolerance epsilon/3. This uses no division by C. The base term uses precisely the assumed compact-time background convergence.

*Commentary.*

Under exactly the finite-order theorem's hypotheses, every fixed vector x and epsilon>0 admit a single natural N that works for every n>=N and every pair of real times 0<=r<=t<=T. The norm of measurableEvolution(Sseq n,Bseq n,t,r,x)-measurableEvolution(S,B,t,r,x) is then less than epsilon.

For every contraction background U and coefficient Q with an admissible separate control of mass at most C, the original term_bound gives norm(term(U,Q,m,t,r,x))<=C^m/m!*norm(x). The exponential factorial series is summable, so the existing uniform-series theorem supplies a common tail bound over all these backgrounds, coefficients and endpoints. The finite head converges uniformly by the finite-order theorem. Two tails and the finite-head estimate give convergence of the original sums. Apply measurableEvolution_apply separately to the sequence background Sseq n with coefficient Bseq n and to the limiting background S with coefficient B.

The full closed triangle, its diagonal, T=0, C=0, x=0, zero controls and zero Banach spaces are included. No common integrable pointwise envelope for g_n and g, operator-norm convergence, finite dimension, separability, nonzero vector, generator comparison, forcing term or regularity outside J is assumed. The conclusion is uniform in the two endpoints for each fixed vector, with no assertion of operator-norm convergence or uniformity over unbounded horizons. The proof uses the original measurable Dyson construction, compact-path coefficient evaluation, dominated convergence and uniform summation; it makes no claim of literature priority.
