Countable Fredholm determinant. Let sigma be any positive trace-class density with countable eigenvalues lambda_i>=0 and sum_i lambda_i=1. For t>=0 define D_sigma(t)=product_i(1+t lambda_i). Since 0<=sum_i log(1+t lambda_i)<=t, the product is finite and well defined. Finite products increase to it. Expand each finite product, then use nonnegative convergence over increasing finite subsets to obtain D_sigma(t)=sum_{k>=0} e_k(sigma)t^k, with e0=1 and e_k the sum of products over increasing k-tuples. Expanding (sum_i lambda_i)^k shows k! e_k<=1, so the series converges absolutely and locally uniformly. This includes zero eigenvalues and infinite rank. Since e2=(1-Tr(sigma^2))/2, purity<=3/8 gives e2>=5/16. If also e3>=1/32, every higher e_k is nonnegative, hence D_sigma(t)>=1+t+(5/16)t^2+(1/32)t^3=(1+t/2)(1+t/4)^2=D_star(t) for every t>=0. Both premises must hold simultaneously on this sigma; separate attainable extrema do not suffice.

Moment integral with all endpoints justified. For 1<q<2 put c_q=q sin(pi(q-1))/pi>0. For x>=0, x^q=c_q integral_0^infinity (t x-log(1+t x)) t^(-q-1) dt. At x=0 both sides vanish. For x>0 substitute u=t x and integrate by parts: the integral at x=1 is (1/q) integral_0^infinity u^(1-q)/(1+u) du=pi/(q sin(pi(q-1))). The boundary terms vanish because u-log(1+u)=O(u^2) at zero and O(u) at infinity. Each integrand is nonnegative. Tonelli therefore permits summation over the countable spectrum and gives Tr(sigma^q)=c_q integral_0^infinity (t-log D_sigma(t)) t^(-q-1) dt. Moreover 0<=t-log D_sigma(t)<=min(t^2/2,t), using y-log(1+y)<=y^2/2 and sum lambda_i^2<=1. This bounds both endpoints and needs neither finite rank nor finite entropy. Determinant domination reverses the log-integral comparison and yields Tr(sigma^q)<=b_star(q) for every 1<q<2. The q=2 comparison is the separate purity premise.
