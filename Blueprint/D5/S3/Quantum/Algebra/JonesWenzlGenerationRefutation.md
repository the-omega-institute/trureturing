# Failure of Jones-Wenzl generation at a root of unity

## Abstract

Three two-strand Jones-Wenzl projectors at q = exp(pi i/6) give a corner not generated by its projected boundary generators.

*Commentary.*

Set r = sqrt 3 in the real numbers and include it in the complex numbers. Take q = exp(pi i/6) and σ = [2,2,2]. The exponential gives q = r/2 + i/2, q inverse = r/2 - i/2, q nonzero, and -q - q inverse = -r. The square of q is a primitive sixth root, so pbar q = 6. The Laurent sums give [0] = 0, [1] = 1, [2] = r. The recursion therefore makes the composite projector P = (1 + r inverse U₁)(1 + r inverse U₃)(1 + r inverse U₅), and its generating set is exactly {P, PU₂P, PU₄P}. Let W = U₂U₁U₄U₃U₂U₅U₄ and let Z = PWP.

Use the quadratic rational algebra with basis 1, omega and relation omega squared = 3. Five explicit nine-by-nine matrices A₀ through A₄ satisfy Aᵢ squared = -omega Aᵢ, AᵢAⱼAᵢ = Aᵢ for adjacent indices, and AᵢAⱼ = AⱼAᵢ for nonadjacent indices. All entries are rational pairs in this basis, so these equalities are exact finite computations. An explicit matrix B equals (1 + (omega/3)A₀)(1 + (omega/3)A₂)(1 + (omega/3)A₄).

The exact computations also give a vector v = (0, 3 - omega, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 2) with vB = v, (vA₁)B = (-omega/3)v, and (vA₃)B = (-omega/3)v. Seven successive multiplications by A₁, A₀, A₃, A₂, A₁, A₄, A₃ are specified by eight exact vectors. The sixth coordinate, numbered from zero, of the last vector multiplied by B is -2 omega/3.

The rational algebra homomorphism sending omega to the complex inclusion of r exists because r squared = 3. Apply it entrywise to the matrices. Their three relation families then give a complex algebra homomorphism from TL 6 (-r) to the nine-by-nine complex matrices, sending U at i + 1 to the image of Aᵢ. It sends P to the image of B, since the image of omega/3 is r inverse. The seven vector identities consequently give the sixth coordinate of v times the image of WP as -2 omega/3 before applying the scalar homomorphism.

Let ell be the complex vector obtained from v. Algebra homomorphisms commute with vector-matrix multiplication and scalar multiplication, so ell is fixed by the image of P and is a common left eigenvector of the images of PU₂P and PU₄P, with eigenvalue -r/3. The sixth coordinate of ell times the image of Z is -2r/3, which is nonzero.

The matrices M satisfying ell M = c ell for some complex c form a unital complex subalgebra: scalar matrices have this property, sums add the scalars, and products multiply them. The images of P, PU₂P and PU₄P all lie in it. Its inverse image under the algebra homomorphism therefore contains the subalgebra generated by {P, PU₂P, PU₄P}. But ell has sixth coordinate zero, so every matrix in this subalgebra has zero sixth coordinate after multiplication on the left by ell. The nonzero value for Z proves that Z is outside the generated subalgebra.

Finally, all three entries of σ are positive and 2 < 6 = pbar q. Applying the asserted universal generation property to this q, this list and t = W would place Z in that generated subalgebra, a contradiction. The total size is 6, so this example does not contradict the source's theorem under its strict total-size bound.
