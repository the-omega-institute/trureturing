# Trace-Kernel Function Polarization

## Abstract

A trace-zero matrix function with an ambient polynomial representative, mixed bidegree law and simultaneous special-unitary invariance admits a normalized coefficient polarization.

**Theorem 1.1 (Mixed polarization on the trace kernel).**

$$\forall m \in \mathbb{N},\; \forall n \in \mathbb{N},\; \forall N \in \mathbb{N},\; (1 \le N) \Rightarrow (\forall f \in K \to \left(K \to \mathbb{C}\right),\; \forall P \in \operatorname{MvPolynomial}\left(V, \mathbb{C}\right),\; ((\forall U \in \operatorname{SU}\left(N\right),\; \forall Z \in K,\; \forall W \in K,\; f\left(U \cdot Z \cdot (U)^{H}, U \cdot W \cdot (U)^{H}\right) = f\left(Z, W\right)) \land ((\forall Z \in K,\; \forall W \in K,\; P\left(\operatorname{entries}\left(Z, W\right)\right) = f\left(Z, W\right)) \land (\forall a \in \mathbb{C},\; \forall b \in \mathbb{C},\; \forall Z \in K,\; \forall W \in K,\; f\left(a \cdot Z, b \cdot W\right) = (a)^{m} \cdot (b)^{n} \cdot f\left(Z, W\right)))) \Rightarrow (\exists F \in \operatorname{MultilinearMap}\left(\mathbb{C}, J, M, \mathbb{C}\right),\; (\forall x \in J \to M,\; F\left(x\right) = \frac{\operatorname{coeff}\left(\operatorname{ones}\left(J\right), \operatorname{evalTwo}\left(\operatorname{C}, \operatorname{S}\left(x\right), \operatorname{Q}\left(P\right)\right)\right)}{\operatorname{factorial}\left(m\right) \cdot \operatorname{factorial}\left(n\right)}) \land ((\forall Z \in K,\; \forall W \in K,\; F\left(\operatorname{repeat}\left(Z, W\right)\right) = f\left(Z, W\right)) \land ((\forall T \in \operatorname{LinearMap}\left(\mathbb{C}, M, M\right),\; (\forall Z \in M,\; \forall W \in M,\; \operatorname{Q}\left(P\right)\left(\operatorname{entries}\left(T\left(Z\right), T\left(W\right)\right)\right) = \operatorname{Q}\left(P\right)\left(\operatorname{entries}\left(Z, W\right)\right)) \Rightarrow (\forall x \in J \to M,\; F\left(\operatorname{compose}\left(T, x\right)\right) = F\left(x\right))) \land (\forall U \in \operatorname{UnitaryGroup}\left(N\right),\; \forall x \in J \to M,\; F\left((j\mapsto U \cdot x\left(j\right) \cdot (U)^{H})\right) = F\left(x\right))))))$$

*Commentary.*

For each choice of m,n,N in the statement, the notation uses the full matrix space M, its trace-zero subtype K, the disjoint slot set J and the entry-variable set V. The superscript H denotes conjugate transpose; SU and UnitaryGroup denote the corresponding complex matrix groups. The map C embeds complex coefficients in the indicated polynomial ring; evalTwo is polynomial substitution. The symbol delta(i,j) is one when i=j and zero otherwise; identity(N) is the identity matrix. Writing a polynomial applied to an entry assignment means polynomial evaluation, and compose(T,x) sends each slot j to T(x(j)). The multi-index ones assigns exponent one to every slot, including the empty multi-index when both degrees vanish. The symbols t are the slot-polynomial variables. MultilinearMap(C,J,M,C) means complex multilinear maps with one full-matrix input per slot in J.

$M = \operatorname{Matrix}\left(\operatorname{Fin}\left(N\right), \operatorname{Fin}\left(N\right), \mathbb{C}\right); K = \{Z\in M|\operatorname{tr}\left(Z\right) = 0\}; J = \operatorname{Sum}\left(\operatorname{Fin}\left(m\right), \operatorname{Fin}\left(n\right)\right); V = \operatorname{Bool}\times(\operatorname{Fin}\left(N\right)\times\operatorname{Fin}\left(N\right)); \operatorname{q}\left(Z\right) = Z - \frac{\operatorname{tr}\left(Z\right)}{N} \cdot \operatorname{identity}\left(N\right); \operatorname{entries}\left(Z, W\right)\left((b,i,j)\right) = \begin{cases}W\left(i, j\right)&\text{if} b = \operatorname{true}\\Z\left(i, j\right)&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}; \operatorname{Q}\left(P\right) = \operatorname{evalTwo}\left(\operatorname{C}, (b,i,j)\mapsto X\left((b,i,j)\right) - \frac{\operatorname{delta}\left(i, j\right)}{N} \cdot \sum_{k\in \operatorname{Fin}\left(N\right)}X\left((b,k,k)\right), P\right); \operatorname{S}\left(x\right)\left((b,i,j)\right) = \begin{cases}\sum_{l\in \operatorname{Fin}\left(n\right)}x\left(\operatorname{inr}\left(l\right)\right)\left(i, j\right) \cdot t\left(\operatorname{inr}\left(l\right)\right)&\text{if} b = \operatorname{true}\\\sum_{l\in \operatorname{Fin}\left(m\right)}x\left(\operatorname{inl}\left(l\right)\right)\left(i, j\right) \cdot t\left(\operatorname{inl}\left(l\right)\right)&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}; \operatorname{repeat}\left(Z, W\right)\left(\operatorname{inl}\left(i\right)\right) = Z; \operatorname{repeat}\left(Z, W\right)\left(\operatorname{inr}\left(j\right)\right) = W$

For every m,n ≥ 0 and N ≥ 1, an arbitrary ambient entry-polynomial representative of a trace-zero matrix function satisfying the stated mixed bidegree law and simultaneous SU(N) conjugation invariance yields a multilinear coefficient polarization on all matrix slots.

The squarefree coefficient is linear in each slot: the derivative chain rule removes one slot and expresses its coefficient as a linear sum of that slot’s entries. The two block factorials normalize the repeated inputs. The theorem recovers the original function on every repeated trace-zero pair, and records invariance under every linear map preserving the projected polynomial and under every unitary conjugation. It is a partial source bridge: the intrinsic representative correspondence and original source witness construction remain open.
