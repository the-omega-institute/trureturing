# Simultaneous coordinates on an arbitrary complex Hilbert space

## Abstract

A nonzero Hilbert multiplicity space simultaneously diagonalizes and exchanges two anticommuting self-adjoint axes with positive scalar-square radii.

*Commentary.*

Let H be any nonzero complete complex inner product space. The bounded self-adjoint operators X and Y satisfy X squared equals r squared times the identity, Y squared equals s squared times the identity, and XY equals minus YX, with positive real r and s. There is a closed nonzero complex subspace K of this same H and a complex linear isometric equivalence U from the Hilbert product K plus K onto H. For each pair (u,v), U(u,v)=u+Y(v)/s, XU(u,v)=U(r u,-r v), and YU(u,v)=U(s v,s u). Thus the two actions use the same coordinates and retain their respective scalar radii.

Normalize the axes as n=X/r and m=Y/s. The multiplicity space is the kernel of n minus the identity. Its closedness gives completeness and the ordinary orthogonal decomposition H=K plus the orthogonal complement of K. Symmetry of n makes that orthogonal complement invariant. The vector z+n(z) lies both in K and in its orthogonal complement, so n(z)=-z there. Anticommutation then sends K into its orthogonal complement by m and sends that complement back into K. Symmetry and m squared equal to the identity make this restriction an onto isometry. Composing this restricted isometry with the Hilbert orthogonal decomposition constructs U. Surjectivity and nonzero H force K to be nonzero.

The Hilbert product uses the square-sum norm, rather than the maximum product norm. Closedness of K supplies its Hilbert completeness. No finite dimension, separability, spectral basis, compactness of either operator, or identification of the represented algebra with all bounded operators is required. The conclusion is a mathematical operator decomposition; it supplies no additional original response identity or physical operation.
