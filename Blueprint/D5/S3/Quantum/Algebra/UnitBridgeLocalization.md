# Square denominators for the same original relation

## Abstract

The original square-compatible relation constructs a nonlinear map on the actual evaluation localization, with inherited nested NN and saturated H-kernel translation.

Keep Original(T), its actual coefficient algebra A, and Raw(E) from the continuous spin geometry. Assume CS on the same D: multiplying the second input by f^2 through the original central Jordan multiplication multiplies the output by f^2, for every f in A and every pair of original inputs. Skewness gives the corresponding first-slot law. No additivity or general A-linearity of D is used.

Fix t in T. S is exactly the submonoid of f in A with f(t) nonzero. LocalVectors is the native module localization S^(-1)(A^3), so equality allows a saturation factor u in S and does not require A to be a domain. A representative x/s is treated as sx/s^2. For u s' x = u s x', multiplication by u s s' gives (u s')^2 sx=(u s)^2 s'x'. Applying the proved first-slot square law to these equal original inputs gives the numerator congruence. The second slot is treated separately. Native quotient lifting then constructs a total nonlinear map on LocalVectors.

*Commentary.*

The first clause is the square-denominator rule k(x/s^2,y/r^2)=K(x,y)/(s^2 r^2). The second clause is the inherited original NN relation: nesting the same localized map twice gives H_x(y)/(s^4 r^2), where H_x(y)=(x dot y)x-(x dot x)y. It is proved from the original nested K equality, with the intermediate denominator (sr)^2.

The third clause is a separate kernel-translation statement. For original coefficient vectors x,y,c sharing a denominator q^2, if H_x(c)/q^6 is zero in the actual localization, equality supplies u in S annihilating H_x(c). Multiplying once more by u gives H_x(u^2 c)=0. The original H-kernel translation applied to u^2 y and u^2 c, followed by CS, gives u^2 K(x,y+c)=u^2 K(x,y). This is exactly a native localized equality after the common denominator is restored.

The stated clauses quantify over all original numerator vectors and all denominators in S. A fourth clause gives a common square denominator for every triple of actual localized vectors, so the kernel clause applies to arbitrary triples after choosing these representatives. The statement does not assert the local-ring unit criterion, residue-field identification, or inherited localized skewness, real homogeneity and dot-area identities. Those additional localization properties, local plane orientation and the full original classification require further statements.
