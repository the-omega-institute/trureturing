# Actual Finite Rectangle Transport

## Abstract

Every finite rectangle menu on the positive five-mode fiber has an exact continuous transport realization.

**Theorem 1.1 (Exact realization by finite continuous controls).**

$$\begin{gathered}\forall b: \operatorname{Base}, \operatorname{ValidBase}\left(b\right)\Rightarrow\\{}\forall k_{0}\in \operatorname{J}\left(b\right), \forall A:\operatorname{List}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right),\\{}(\forall e\in A, \operatorname{fst}\left(e\right)\in \operatorname{J}\left(b\right))\Rightarrow\\{}\exists q:\mathbb{R}\to \operatorname{State}, \operatorname{Admissible}\left(b, \operatorname{Duration}\left(A\right), q\right)\land\\{}q(0)=(k_{0},0,0)\land\\{}\operatorname{kappa}\left(q(\operatorname{Duration}\left(A\right))\right)=k_{0}\land \operatorname{chi}\left(q(\operatorname{Duration}\left(A\right))\right)=0\land\\{}(\forall t\in [0,\operatorname{Duration}\left(A\right)], \exists p:\operatorname{InteriorProbability}\left(4\right), \operatorname{weight}\left(p\right)=\operatorname{probability}\left(b, \operatorname{kappa}\left(q(t)\right)\right))\land\\{}(\forall t\in [0,\operatorname{Duration}\left(A\right)], \operatorname{Compatible}\left(q(t)\right))\land\\{}\operatorname{phase}\left(q(\operatorname{Duration}\left(A\right))\right)=\operatorname{residual}\left(\operatorname{phaseWeight}\left(b\right), k_{0}, A\right)\end{gathered}$$

*Commentary.*

The base b is the triple (X,Y,Z), with 0 < Z < 1 and 0 < X,Y < 1-Z. Its open fiber J(b) has endpoints max(0,X+Y-(1-Z)) and min(X,Y). The five probabilities, in the mode order (0,1,2,3,13), are (1-Z-X-Y+k,X-k,Z,Y-k,k). Their strict positivity and total one use the existing InteriorProbability carrier.

The direction is (1,-1,0,-1,1). The information coefficient is the sum of the four reciprocal bottom probabilities. The phase weight w is the direction divided coordinatewise by probability times information. Compatible(q(t)) asserts that the middle phase is zero and that the direction paired with the lifted phases equals the real relation phase. The state of a history q consists of the probability coordinate k, the real lifted relation phase c, and five real lifted phases v. They use the original negative phase convention psi(s)=sqrt(p(s))*exp(-i*v(s)). The instantaneous weighted phase velocity has zero probability mean, because the sum of p(s)*w(s) is zero. Duration(A) is four times the length of the finite menu A; its phase target is the sum of a times (w(k)-w(k0)) over the entries (k,a).

Admissible(b,N,q) requires continuity of the actual real-time function q, probability coordinates in J(b) on the closed interval from zero to N, finite variation of c, and an affine chart on each unit-time leg. On every open leg and for every mode, the probability derivative equals the direction times the derivative of k, and the derivative of v equals w(k) times the derivative of c. It also requires integrability of every actual transport component and equality of its integral with the corresponding terminal minus initial lifted phase. Thus the supplied object is a piecewise linear control with its transported state lift and exact integral residual.

For each menu entry, the four successive vertices change k from k0 to k, change c from zero to a at k, return k to k0, and return c to zero at k0. The phase changes on the two phase legs are a times w(k) and minus a times w(k0). Concatenating these vertices gives the real-time interpolation; matching endpoints prove continuity, and affine charts give finite variation and the differential law.

All real coefficients, including negative and zero coefficients, repeated heights, either symmetry wall, and the empty menu are included. There is no uniform speed, energy or amplitude bound. This declaration realizes every supplied finite representation. The application below constructs representations on every pole stratum and then identifies the exact compact group.

**Theorem 1.2 (Middle gauge and unit relation charge).**

$$\forall b: \operatorname{Base}, \operatorname{ValidBase}\left(b\right)\Rightarrow \forall k\in \operatorname{J}\left(b\right), \operatorname{phaseWeight}\left(b, k\right)(2)=0\land \operatorname{phaseWeight}\left(b, k\right)(0)-\operatorname{phaseWeight}\left(b, k\right)(1)-\operatorname{phaseWeight}\left(b, k\right)(3)+\operatorname{phaseWeight}\left(b, k\right)(4)=1$$

*Commentary.*

At each interior height, the middle phase weight vanishes and the direction paired with the phase weight equals one. Strict positivity of the four bottom probabilities makes the information coefficient nonzero. These identities govern the transported gauge and relation phase on every finite rectangle and also apply pointwise to general histories.

Let T be finite, let k be continuous on the interval between zero and T with values in the same open fiber, and let alpha be interval integrable. Put H(i) equal to the integral of w(k(t))(i) times alpha(t), and put q equal to the integral of alpha. Each component of w composed with k is continuous: the positive affine probabilities and their positive reciprocal sum keep every denominator nonzero. Multiplication of an integrable function by a continuous function on a compact interval therefore proves that every component defining H is integrable.

Define V(b) by zero middle component, zero direction charge, equality of components 0 and 13 when X+Y=1-Z, and equality of components 1 and 3 when X=Y. Integrating the middle and charge identities gives H(2)=0 and H(0)-H(1)-H(3)+H(13)=q. On either wall the relevant affine probabilities and signs coincide, so the corresponding weights, and then their integrals, coincide. Subtracting q times w(k0), whose charge is one, shows that H-q*w(k0) belongs to V(b). This argument includes both walls simultaneously and does not require a finite affine chart decomposition.

For an absolutely continuous real phase lift chi, alpha is its derivative. The interval fundamental theorem of calculus identifies q with chi(T)-chi(0). Thus winding m gives H-2*pi*m*w(k0) in V(b), and zero winding gives H in V(b). If the five state-phase coordinates are absolutely continuous and satisfy the transport derivative law almost everywhere, the same fundamental theorem identifies H with their terminal minus initial values. Finite variation alone cannot replace absolute continuity in this endpoint statement.

For a continuous phase that is C1 on finitely many consecutive subintervals, choose arbitrary nondecreasing partition times from zero to T. On each open subinterval its derivative agrees with a continuous rate on the closed subinterval. That rate is integrable; changing values at partition endpoints does not change the interval integral. The ordinary fundamental theorem on each subinterval and telescoping of the finite endpoint sum give q=chi(T)-chi(0). Hence the same upper inclusion applies to finite piecewise C1 controls as well, without imposing unit-time legs or affine motion.
