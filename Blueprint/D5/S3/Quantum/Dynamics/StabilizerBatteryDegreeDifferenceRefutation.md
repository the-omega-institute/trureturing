# Equal degree differences do not determine stored work

## Abstract

Fourteen-qubit circulant stabilizer batteries with degree pairs (4,8) and (2,6) have the same degree difference but store different work at time π/4.

*Commentary.*

Equations (1) and (2) define the periodic circulant generators and their Hamiltonian sum. The mathematical symbols A, S and H denote the neighbourhood relation, its Pauli generators and that sum. The centre carries X, each neighbour carries Z, and every other site carries the identity. The maximum even degree is M(N). All arithmetic on natural degrees, including subtraction, is natural arithmetic; Nat.div is the integer quotient and Nat.mod is the remainder.

A State N is a complex vector on binary functions on Fin N, and an Operator N is a matrix on those indices. G(N,H,g) says that g has squared norm one and minimizes the real energy among all vectors v of squared norm one. The initial density is the outer product of g and its complex conjugate. The charging exponential and the real trace expression W are the unitary evolution and work of Section II, Figures of merit. Complex.ofReal explicitly embeds a real scalar into the complex numbers. The star on a vector is coordinatewise conjugation; the superscript H on a matrix is conjugate transpose.

The Section III remark reports unchanged work at fixed degree difference for the investigated sizes N at most ten outside multiples of four, and leaves the larger-size conclusion unresolved. The assertion displayed here quantifies over all sizes N greater than two not divisible by four, all four positive interior even degrees, all ground states g and h, and every nonnegative real time t. Interior means K + 2 at most M(N). Nat.dist is the absolute difference of two natural numbers.

Write a Pauli word as a scalar times X to a binary vector x followed by Z to a binary vector z, and let the binary adjacency matrix of the battery be Γ. Its graph-state expectation is a binary character sum. It vanishes unless the Z-part equals Γ applied to the X-part, that is z = Γx. For (4,8), every Qᵢ passes this test and its phase gives expectation -1. For (2,6), every Qᵢ fails the test and has expectation zero. The work is the sum over i of one plus the real part of this expectation, so the two work values are 0 and 14. Equal degree difference therefore does not suffice, even with both endpoint degrees excluded.
