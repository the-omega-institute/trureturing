# Rigidity for the Cosine Duplication Law

## Abstract

The unit fibre of a continuous duplication solution can determine its frequency.

**Theorem 1.1 (Return to one on the line).**

$$\neg \left(\exists p \in \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R},\; \operatorname{Continuous}\left(p\right) \land \left(p\left(0\right) = 1 \land \left(\left(\forall t \in \mathbb{R},\; \left|p\left(t\right)\right| \le 1\right) \land \left(\left(\forall t \in \mathbb{R},\; p\left(2 \cdot t\right) = 2 \cdot p\left(t\right)^{2}-1\right) \land \left(\forall t \in \mathbb{R},\; 0 < t \Rightarrow p\left(t\right) < 1\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Analytic/Dilation/DuplicationCircleRigidity.no_faithful_duplication_on_line` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

If the solution never returns to one on the positive ray, duplication excludes both minus one and zero there. Continuity then forces positivity. Every doubling increases the positive defect from one by a factor of at least two, which contradicts the range bound.

**Theorem 1.2 (The faithful solution on the circle).**

$$\forall p \in \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R},\; \operatorname{Continuous}\left(p\right) \Rightarrow \left(\operatorname{Periodic}\left(p, 2 \cdot \pi\right) \Rightarrow \left(\left(\forall t \in \mathbb{R},\; p\left(-t\right) = p\left(t\right)\right) \Rightarrow \left(\left(\forall t \in \mathbb{R},\; \left|p\left(t\right)\right| \le 1\right) \Rightarrow \left(\left(\forall t \in \mathbb{R},\; p\left(2 \cdot t\right) = 2 \cdot p\left(t\right)^{2}-1\right) \Rightarrow \left(\left(\forall t \in \mathbb{R},\; p\left(t\right) = 1 \Leftrightarrow \left(\exists k \in \mathbb{Z},\; t = 2 \cdot \pi \cdot k\right)\right) \Rightarrow p = cos\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Analytic/Dilation/DuplicationCircleRigidity.eq_cos_of_circle_duplication` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

The prescribed fibre at one determines the fibres at minus one and zero by duplication. On the half-circle these fibres and continuity determine which half of the arccosine range the angle occupies. Doubling followed by reflection into the half-circle doubles the absolute difference between the angle read from the solution and the original angle. This bounded difference therefore vanishes.

**Theorem 1.3 (The period lattice determines the frequency).**

$$\forall p \in \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R},\; \operatorname{Continuous}\left(p\right) \Rightarrow \left(\left(\forall t \in \mathbb{R},\; \left|p\left(t\right)\right| \le 1\right) \Rightarrow \left(p\left(0\right) = 1 \Rightarrow \left(\left(\forall t \in \mathbb{R},\; p\left(-t\right) = p\left(t\right)\right) \Rightarrow \left(\left(\forall t \in \mathbb{R},\; p\left(2 \cdot t\right) = 2 \cdot p\left(t\right)^{2}-1\right) \Rightarrow \left(\left(\exists e \in \mathbb{R},\; 0 < e \land \left(\forall t \in \mathbb{R},\; \left(-e < t \land t < e\right) \Rightarrow \left(p\left(t\right) = 1 \Rightarrow t = 0\right)\right)\right) \Rightarrow \left(\left(\forall s \in \mathbb{R},\; p\left(s\right) = 1 \Rightarrow \operatorname{Periodic}\left(p, s\right)\right) \Rightarrow \left(\exists T \in \mathbb{R},\; 0 < T \land \left(\left(\forall t \in \mathbb{R},\; p\left(t\right) = 1 \Leftrightarrow \left(\exists k \in \mathbb{Z},\; t = T \cdot k\right)\right) \land \left(\forall t \in \mathbb{R},\; p\left(t\right) = \operatorname{cos}\left(\frac{2 \cdot \pi \cdot t}{T}\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Analytic/Dilation/DuplicationCircleRigidity.eq_scaled_cos_of_discrete_fibre` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

If each point of the unit fibre is a period, the fibre is an additive subgroup. Isolation at zero rules out density. The return to one gives a nonzero group element, so the subgroup is generated by a positive period T. Rescaling this period to two pi gives the faithful solution on the circle.
