**Theorem 1.4 (The signed determinant is the negative second-order eigenvalue).**

$$\forall delta: \mathbb{R}, t: \mathbb{R}, \operatorname{negativeSecondDerivative}(\operatorname{positiveRateBranch}(delta), t) = \operatorname{reflectionPairSignedDeterminant}(delta) \cdot \operatorname{positiveRateBranch}(delta, t) \land \operatorname{negativeSecondDerivative}(\operatorname{negativeRateBranch}(delta), t) = \operatorname{reflectionPairSignedDeterminant}(delta) \cdot \operatorname{negativeRateBranch}(delta, t) \land \operatorname{negativeSecondDerivative}(\operatorname{reflectedGrowthSum}(delta), t) = \operatorname{reflectionPairSignedDeterminant}(delta) \cdot \operatorname{reflectedGrowthSum}(delta, t).$$

*Commentary.*

Both reflected exponential branches satisfy the same second-order equation. Applying the negative second derivative therefore multiplies each branch by the signed determinant minus delta squared.

The same identity holds for the branch-forgetting symmetric sum. This is a general scalar spectral statement and introduces no zeta or Riemann-hypothesis premise.

**Theorem 1.5 (The symmetric observer is first-order blind at the center).**

$$\forall delta: \mathbb{R}, \operatorname{iteratedDeriv}(1, \operatorname{reflectedGrowthSum}(delta), 0) = 0.$$
