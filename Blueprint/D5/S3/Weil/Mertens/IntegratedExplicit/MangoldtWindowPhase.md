# Bounded gaps between positive peaks

## Abstract

Recurrence of the normalized Mangoldt primitive and the first Li mass

Let U(x) be the complete Mangoldt remainder and write G(y) = exp(−y/2) U(exp y). Write M for the first Li mass, 2+γ−2 log(2√π). The first result samples G at nonnegative integer logarithmic times. The second tests the arithmetic remainder at all real points of a window.

**Theorem 1.1 (Returns to the first Li mass under RH).**

$$RiemannHypothesis \Rightarrow \left(\forall d \in \mathbb{R},\; 0 < d \Rightarrow \left(\exists L \in \mathbb{N},\; \forall n \in \mathbb{N},\; \exists k \in \mathbb{N},\; k \le L \land |\operatorname{G}\left(n+k\right)-M| < d\right)\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Weil/Mertens/IntegratedExplicit/MangoldtWindowPhase.bounded_gap_actual_G_peak` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

The phases of the actual zeros give a single rotation g on a product of circles. Under RH its continuous coefficient sum takes the value M at the identity. The nonnegative powers of g accumulate at every point in the closed subgroup generated by g. For each group element h in that subgroup, some k takes hgᵏ into the chosen open neighborhood of the identity. Compactness supplies a finite subcover, so one upper bound on k works for every starting exponent n.

The residual in the normalized explicit formula tends to zero. Give the phase sum and the residual half the tolerance each. Enlarging the gap bound by the initial cutoff includes n = 0.

**Theorem 1.2 (A coefficient below the first Li mass excludes cofinal windows).**

$$\forall C \in \mathbb{R},\; \forall b \in \mathbb{R},\; C < M \Rightarrow \left(1 < b \Rightarrow \neg(\forall R \in \mathbb{R},\; \exists Y \in \mathbb{R},\; \operatorname{max}\left(R, 2\right) \le Y \land \left(\forall x \in \mathbb{R},\; \left(Y \le x \land x \le Y^{b}\right) \Rightarrow \operatorname{U}\left(x\right) \le C\sqrt{x}\right))\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Weil/Mertens/IntegratedExplicit/MangoldtWindowPhase.no_windows_below_mass` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

If whole windows admitted coefficient C < M, they would also admit the larger coefficient M+1. The square-root window criterion would imply RH. For tolerance M−C, the first result then places a positive peak at time j between log Y and b log Y for every sufficiently large Y. Indeed, round log Y up to n and allow the fixed number of additional steps. At x = exp j one has U(x) > C√x, which contradicts the assumed whole-window inequality.

At C = M, the critical-gap theorem gives M√x − U(x) → +∞ under RH. Thus U(x) ≤ M√x on every sufficiently late window. Conversely, arbitrarily late windows with coefficient M also admit coefficient M+1, so the square-root window criterion gives RH.
