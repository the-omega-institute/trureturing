# Birth polynomials of adjacent honeycomb cuts

## Abstract

Adjacent-cut birth polynomials and their normalized honeycomb kernels.

For height a and cut d, retain nonnegative coordinates with x+y<a and g<2a−d. Sum one monomial per birth, whose exponent is its tick. The signed polynomial Δ(a,d) subtracts the result for cut d+1 from the result for cut d. Its coefficient at tick t sums the zero-extended differences of the two birth indicators.

At a positive multiple a of a dyadic strip width P, horizontal translation identifies the right strip with the height-P run after a delay of 2(a−P). The exterior-left birth indicators agree between the cuts there. Thus for c+1 ≤ P the adjacent-cut difference satisfies $\Delta\left(a, c\right) = z^{2 \cdot (a - P)} \cdot \Delta\left(P, c\right)$.

At neighboring cuts of an integral height, let τ=2a−d−1. The potential 2τ−g+y changes by at most one along an edge before nonnegative truncation. On the removed layer it equals the earliest possible touch time. This gives zero coefficients below τ and places any nonzero signed weight at time τ+j on a layer g=τ−i with i+y≤j.

Removing the delay gives the integer polynomial in equation (17). The kernel uses the least dyadic a above d, and the strip identity makes the coefficient independent of the choice of eligible dyadic enclosure.
