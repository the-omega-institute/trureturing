# Equal Gram fields outside one exact unitary orbit

## Abstract

Continuous equal-Gram matrix fields on the inverse-natural real compactum have explicit unitary approximants but no continuous multiplier or unitary polar factor.

The space X is the actual subspace of the real line consisting of zero and 1/k for positive natural k. Its inherited topology is used throughout. Mat(n) is Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) Complex, with conjugate transpose star and the complex Euclidean operator norm. The matrix norm is the L2Operator norm: Matrix.cstar_norm_def identifies it with the norm of Matrix.toEuclideanCLM. Mathlib's matrix C-star construction preserves the product topology and uniformity, so continuity is continuity of every complex entry.

Write S=OnePoint Nat, radius(k)=1/(k+1), point(k)=radius(k) in X, origin=0, and positivePoint(k)=1/k for k at least one. The map S to X sends infinity to origin and k to point(k). Its bijectivity follows from positivity and injectivity of radius; its continuity follows from tendsto_inv_atTop_nhds_zero_nat and OnePoint.continuous_iff_from_nat. Compactness and Hausdorffness give the proved homeomorphism literalHomeomorph. Countability and complete regularity give ZeroDimensionalSpace X through Mathlib's existing instance. No proxy space is assumed equivalent to X.

Zfirst and Zsecond in the display denote Z1 and Z2 in Lean. On S their diagonal entries are respectively zero at infinity and radius(k), (-1)^(k+1) radius(k) at k; the fields on X are transported by literalHomeomorph.symm. finiteHead(N) consists of point(k) with k<N, equivalently the positive points 1/k with 1<=k<=N. approximant(n,N) has phase (-1)^k times the identity at those positive points, and the identity on the complementary tail and at origin. The symbols identity, star, abs and norm denote the matrix identity, conjugate transpose, CFC.abs and that operator norm; scalar multiplication and matrix multiplication use the same displayed dot with their respective types. Natural denominators and radius are cast to the complex scalars in matrix formulas and to the reals in the error bound.

*Commentary.*

The theorem holds for every n>=1, hence for every finite n>=3. Both fields vanish at origin, are continuous, and have the stated values at every nonzero point. Their Gram matrices agree pointwise. The positive first field is exactly the CFC modulus of the second. The two nonexistence clauses quantify over all continuous matrix-valued multipliers, with no unitary assumption, and therefore also exclude continuous unitary multipliers and unitary polar factors.

For a hypothetical multiplier U, inspect its (0,0) entry after composing with literalHomeomorph. The equality at point(k), followed by cancellation of the nonzero radius(k), forces this entry to equal (-1)^(k+1). Continuity would give a limit at infinity. Along k=2j the entry is -1 and along k=2j+1 it is +1; both index maps tend to infinity. Uniqueness of limits in the complex numbers would identify -1 and +1, a contradiction. This is the obstruction to exact alignment, even though both original fields converge to zero.

Each finite head is clopen: its preimage in OnePoint Nat is finite, closed and open away from infinity. The phase of each approximant is eventually one, so it is continuous; its conjugate times itself in either order is one. On the head the error is zero. On the tail the scalar error is at most twice radius(k), bounded by 2/(N+1). The identity matrix has operator norm one in positive dimension, yielding the displayed uniform pointwise operator bound. The bound tends to zero independently of x and explains approximation in the uniform topology on these fields. A separate theorem packaged in the continuous-function-algebra supremum norm is not supplied.

The construction is the repository's inverse-natural counterexample, using the pinned Mathlib OnePoint, matrix, norm, positivity and CFC suppliers. It establishes this boundary between exact alignment and approximation. The general square-fiber orbit-closure theorem and the exact original PB classification are separate results and are not established here. No conclusion about the broader semifinite response classification or an arbitrary incomplete Jordan domain follows from this example.
