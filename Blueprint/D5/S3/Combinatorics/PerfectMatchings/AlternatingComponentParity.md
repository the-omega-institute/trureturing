# Cardinality of alternating matching components

## Abstract

Every finite component generated by two fixed-point-free matchings has cardinality twice one product orbit.

For permutations s and t with s²=t²=1 and no fixed points, Component(s,t,x) is the set of vertices reachable from x by a finite sequence of s- or t-steps. The existing orbit-splitting theorem identifies this component with two disjoint product orbits. The first matching maps one orbit bijectively onto the other, so the component cardinality is twice one orbit cardinality, and therefore has even cardinality. Finiteness is used only for Set.ncard.

**Definition 1.1 (The alternating matching component).**

$$\forall X \in Type,\; (\operatorname{Finite}\left(X\right)) \Rightarrow (\forall s \in \operatorname{Perm}\left(X\right),\; \forall t \in \operatorname{Perm}\left(X\right),\; \forall x \in X,\; \operatorname{Component}\left(s, t, x\right) = \{ y : X \mid (\operatorname{Connected}\left(s, t, x, y\right)) \})$$

*Commentary.*

Component(s,t,x) is exactly the set {y | Connected(s,t,x,y)}. It records the actual reachable component and introduces no graph embedding, orientation or perfect-matching assumption beyond the two permutations used to generate it.

**Theorem 1.2 (A component is twice one product orbit).**

$$\forall X \in Type,\; (\operatorname{Finite}\left(X\right)) \Rightarrow (\forall s \in \operatorname{Perm}\left(X\right),\; \forall t \in \operatorname{Perm}\left(X\right),\; ((\forall z \in X,\; s\left(s\left(z\right)\right) = z) \land (\forall z \in X,\; t\left(t\left(z\right)\right) = z)) \Rightarrow (((\forall z \in X,\; s\left(z\right) \ne z) \land (\forall z \in X,\; t\left(z\right) \ne z)) \Rightarrow (\forall x \in X,\; \operatorname{ncard}\left(\operatorname{Component}\left(s, t, x\right)\right) = 2 \cdot \operatorname{ncard}\left(\operatorname{RotationOrbit}\left(s \cdot t, x\right)\right))))$$

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

The existing component_split theorem supplies a disjoint union of the orbits of s*t through x and s(x), together with the image equality s '' Orbit(x) = Orbit(s(x)). Since s is injective, the two orbit cardinalities agree. The displayed equality is the new cardinality bridge; its factor of two supplies the parity input for the alternating-cycle argument.
