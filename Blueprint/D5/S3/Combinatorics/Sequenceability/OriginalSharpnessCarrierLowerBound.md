# A quadratic carrier lower bound from the original zero supports

## Abstract

The full original zero-support classification forces a quadratic lower bound on the finite carrier.

**Theorem 1.1 (At least m*(m+1) elements are necessary).**

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Let G be any finite commutative additive group, let m be a natural number with m at least two, and let f map the original Coordinate m into G. Write J = Fin(m-1), q and p for the two distinguished coordinates, and a_i,b_i for the paired coordinates indexed by J. Assume that f(q) has additive order exactly m. Assume also the complete coefficient-one zero-support classification: for every finite set C of original coordinates, C is nonempty and its f-labels sum to zero if and only if C is precisely {q,a_i,b_i} for some i in J. Then m*(m+1) is at most Nat.card G. No separate injectivity, nonzero-label or cyclicity hypothesis is imposed.

Put Q = f(q), P = f(p), A_i = f(a_i), B_i = f(b_i), and H = the additive subgroup generated by Q. Each prescribed triple gives Q+A_i+B_i = 0, hence A_i+B_i = -Q. The exact order assumption gives Nat.card H = m.

The full classification forces the cosets represented by 0, all m-1 values A_i, and P to be pairwise distinct in G/H. To exclude a collision, express the resulting relation as a residue multiple of Q and complete it with a suitable set of distinct a_j,b_j pairs and, when needed, q. The relation then produces a nonempty zero support outside the prescribed triple family. A collision involving the sentinel yields a forbidden support containing p; the A-coset collisions are ruled out by the corresponding paired completions. The argument includes the boundary case m = 2.

Thus Option(Option J) injects into G/H: none represents the zero coset, some none represents P, and some(some i) represents A_i. This domain has exactly m+1 elements, so Nat.card(G/H) is at least m+1. Translation by H partitions G into its orbits, namely these cosets, each of cardinality m. The finite quotient-cardinality identity therefore gives m*(m+1) at most Nat.card G.

The result applies to every finite commutative additive carrier satisfying the exact original support classification. The explicit cyclic construction of OriginalPolynomialCyclicOrderSharpness supplies a carrier with m*(6*m-7) elements under the same order and zero-support requirements. Together these pin quadratic growth for this complete support pattern. Merely attaining m-1 intersection-graph components in an arbitrary configuration does not supply the hypotheses of this lower bound. The exact smallest carrier size for general m remains undetermined.

At m = 3 the lower bound is attained without injective labels. Take G = ZMod 3 × (ZMod 2 × ZMod 2), with q labelled (1,(0,0)), p labelled (2,(1,1)), a_0 and b_0 both labelled (1,(1,0)), and a_1 and b_1 both labelled (1,(0,1)). The q label has additive order three, and the nonempty zero supports are exactly the two original triples. This group has twelve elements, equal to 3*(3+1), so equality holds at m = 3. This example establishes neither equality for every m nor a minimum among cyclic carriers.

**Theorem 1.2 (At least 2*m*m elements are necessary for a cyclic carrier).**

Lean statement: `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Sequenceability/OriginalSharpnessCarrierLowerBound.cyclic_carrier_card_lower_bound`

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Sequenceability/OriginalSharpnessCarrierLowerBound.cyclic_carrier_card_lower_bound` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

The cyclic strengthening assumes a finite commutative additive group G that is cyclic, a natural number m at least two, and an arbitrary labeling f of the original Coordinate m. Its hypotheses are exactly addOrderOf(f(q)) = m and the complete coefficient-one zero-support classification: for every finite set C of original coordinates, C is nonempty and its labels sum to zero if and only if C is one of the original triples {q,a_i,b_i}, indexed by Fin(m-1). The conclusion is 2*m*m at most Nat.card G, without a label-injectivity or separate nonzero-label assumption.

Put Q = f(q), P = f(p), A_i = f(a_i), B_i = f(b_i), H = the subgroup generated by Q, and K = G/H. The prescribed triples give A_i+B_i = -Q, and H has m elements. The quotient K is finite cyclic. The generic argument already separates the classes 0, P and all A_i: every A_i is nonzero, different indices have different A-classes, and P avoids 0 and every A_i.

The complete support classification also excludes P+A_i from H. If P+A_i = tQ with 0 <= t < m and t <= m-2, complete {p,a_i} with t distinct whole a_j,b_j pairs outside index i. Its sum is zero, but a support containing p cannot be a prescribed triple. If t = m-1, the forbidden zero support is {q,p,a_i}. Thus P avoids every negative A-class as well.

For distinct indices i,j, the same completion method excludes A_i+A_j from H. Such indices require m at least three. Write a putative sum as tQ with 0 <= t < m. If t <= m-3, adjoin t whole pairs outside i,j to {a_i,a_j}; the resulting zero support lacks q. If t = m-2, {b_i,b_j} has sum -mQ = 0. If t = m-1, {q,a_i,a_j} has sum mQ = 0. None is a prescribed triple, so no A-class can equal the negative of an A-class at a different index.

Consequently the signed set S = {0,P} union {A_i,-A_i : i in Fin(m-1)} has no collisions except possibly A_i = -A_i at an individual index. A finite cyclic quotient has at most one nonzero element fixed by negation; when present, this is its unique element of additive order two. If r counts the self-negative A-classes, then r <= 1 and S has 2+2*(m-1)-r elements, at least 2m-1.

Let n = Nat.card K. If n is even, n >= 2m-1 rounds up to n >= 2m. If n is odd, K has no nonzero element fixed by negation, so r = 0 and S itself has 2m elements. This includes m = 2, where there is only one A-class and no distinct-index case to consider. Translation by H partitions G into cosets, each of cardinality m. Hence Nat.card G = n*m is at least (2m)*m = 2*m*m.

The generic bound m*(m+1) applies to finite commutative additive carriers, whereas this stronger bound uses cyclicity. Both concern the original Coordinate and its complete nonempty zero-support family exactly equal to the original triples; attaining m-1 graph components alone does not provide these hypotheses. The prior explicit cyclic construction has m*(6*m-7) elements. These bounds do not determine the exact cyclic minimum for general m or the optimal constant in that construction.

An equality example at m = 2 uses ZMod 8 with labels q = 4, p = 1 and a_0 = b_0 = 2. Its eight-element carrier has size 2*2*2. An equality example at m = 3 uses ZMod 18 with labels q = 12, p = 5, a_0 = b_0 = 3, a_1 = 4 and b_1 = 2. Its eighteen-element carrier has size 2*3*3. At m = 4, ZMod 32 with q = 8, p = 3 and ordered pairs (a_i,b_i) = (1,23), (2,22), (12,12) attains 2*4*4 = 32. At m = 5, ZMod 70 with q = 14, p = 48 and ordered pairs (1,55), (9,47), (17,39), (63,63) supplies an upper witness of size 70, above the lower bound 2*5*5 = 50. In all four examples, the q label has additive order exactly m, the complete nonempty zero supports are precisely the original triples, and the labeling is noninjective. Equality holds at m = 2, 3 and 4; these examples alone do not establish equality for every m. The parameter-five cyclic minimum is determined below.

**Theorem 1.3 (At least m*(2*m+1) elements are necessary for an odd carrier).**

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Sequenceability/OriginalSharpnessCarrierLowerBound.odd_carrier_card_lower_bound` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Let G be any finite commutative additive group of odd cardinality, let m be a natural number at least two, and let f label the original Coordinate m in G. Write J = Fin(m-1), with distinguished coordinates q,p and paired coordinates a_i,b_i for i in J. Assume addOrderOf(f(q)) = m and the complete coefficient-one zero-support classification: for every finite set C of original coordinates, C is nonempty and its labels sum to zero if and only if C is exactly {q,a_i,b_i} for some i in J. Then m*(2*m+1) is at most Nat.card G. The theorem requires neither cyclicity nor label injectivity.

Put Q = f(q), P = f(p), A_i = f(a_i), H = the additive subgroup generated by Q, and K = G/H. The exact order of Q gives Nat.card H = m. The subgroup-index identity Nat.card G = Nat.card K * Nat.card H implies that Nat.card K is odd, since an odd product has odd factors.

The original support classification separates the classes 0, P and all A_i. Completing a putative subgroup relation with whole a_j,b_j pairs and, when needed, q also excludes P = -A_i and A_i = -A_j for distinct indices. These are the same forbidden-support completions used for the generic and cyclic bounds; a support containing p cannot be prescribed, and the remaining completions violate the exact triple classification.

An odd finite quotient has no nonzero class fixed by negation. Indeed, x = -x implies 2*x = 0, so its additive order divides two. A nonzero such class would have order two, forcing two to divide Nat.card K, contrary to oddness. Since every A_i is nonzero, this also excludes A_i = -A_i. Consequently the signed A-classes are all distinct, and the set {0,P} union {A_i,-A_i : i in J} has exactly 2+2*(m-1) = 2*m elements.

Thus Nat.card K is at least 2*m. Its odd parity improves this to Nat.card K at least 2*m+1. Multiplying by the subgroup cardinality m in Nat.card G = Nat.card K * m proves m*(2*m+1) at most Nat.card G. This argument applies to arbitrary finite commutative additive groups of odd cardinality, including m = 2 under the stated hypotheses.

Equality at m = 3 is realized in ZMod 21 with labels q = 7, p = 3, a_0 = 1, b_0 = 13, a_1 = 2 and b_1 = 12. The q label has additive order three, and among all sixty-four subsets of the original six coordinates the only nonempty zero supports are {q,a_0,b_0} and {q,a_1,b_1}. This odd carrier has 21 = 3*(2*3+1) elements, attaining the bound at this parameter. No equality statement for all m is asserted.

**Theorem 1.4 (Seventy elements are necessary and sufficient for a cyclic carrier at m = 5).**

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Sequenceability/OriginalSharpnessCarrierLowerBound.cyclic_five_carrier_card_lower_bound` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Let G be a finite commutative additive group that is cyclic, and let f label the original Coordinate 5 in G. Assume that f(q) has additive order exactly five and that, for every finite set C of original coordinates, C is nonempty and its labels sum to zero if and only if C is one of the four triples {q,a_i,b_i}, indexed by Fin 4. Then Nat.card G is at least 70. Neither label injectivity nor a separate nonzero-label assumption is required.

Put Q = f(q), H = the subgroup generated by Q, and K = G/H. The prescribed triples give f(a_i)+f(b_i) = -Q, while Nat.card H = 5. The earlier cyclic lower bound implies Nat.card K at least ten. The four classes A_i represented by f(a_i) are nonzero and occupy distinct orbits under negation.

The stronger obstruction uses three distinct pairs. More generally, when m is at least four, choose one original coordinate from each of three distinct pairs. Their label sum cannot lie in the subgroup generated by Q. To see this, write a putative sum as t*Q with 0 <= t < m. If t <= m-4, adjoin t whole pairs from the remaining indices. If t = m-1, adjoin q. If t = m-3, replace all three selected coordinates by their mates; if t = m-2, make that replacement and adjoin q. In each case the resulting nonempty zero support contains three singly selected pairs and is not one prescribed triple. Thus no signed sum of three A-classes at different indices is zero in K.

Suppose K had n elements with ten at most n and n at most thirteen. Identify the cyclic quotient with ZMod n and replace each A_i by its representative in the same negation orbit between one and n/2. Ordering the four distinct representatives gives 1 <= a < b < c < d and 2*d <= n. The integer inequalities force one of a+b=c, a+b=d, a+c=d, b+c=d, a+b+c=n, a+b+d=n, a+c+d=n or b+c+d=n. Each equality gives a forbidden signed sum of three distinct A-classes. Therefore Nat.card K is at least fourteen, and Nat.card G = 5*Nat.card K is at least seventy.

The lower bound is attained in ZMod 70. Label q by 14, p by 48, and the four ordered pairs (a_i,b_i) by (1,55), (9,47), (17,39) and (63,63). The q label has additive order five, and the only nonempty coefficient-one zero supports among all subsets of the ten original coordinates are the four prescribed triples. Combining this realization with the lower bound determines the smallest cyclic carrier at m = 5 to have seventy elements. It gives no minimum for arbitrary finite abelian carriers or for other parameters.
