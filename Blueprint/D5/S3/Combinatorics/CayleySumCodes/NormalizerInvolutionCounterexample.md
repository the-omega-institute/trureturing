# The order-thirty-two counterexample

## Abstract

The specified central product disproves normalizer-only translation in the same sum graph.

**Definition 1.1 (All thirty-two normal forms).**

Lean statement: `D5/S3/Combinatorics/CayleySumCodes/NormalizerInvolutionCounterexample.CentralProduct`

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Combinatorics/CayleySumCodes/NormalizerInvolutionCounterexample.CentralProduct` (`✓ std3`).

*Citation.* Haobo Ma; Reza Nikandish; Wenlin Zhang (2026). *Subgroup total perfect codes in Cayley sum graphs: a missing case and its consequences*. DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.23375144](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23375144). URL: <https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23375144>.

*Commentary.*

The elements are x^i y^j t^k with i,j in ZMod 4 and k in ZMod 2. Multiplication uses y^-1*x*y=x^-1, t central and t^2=x^2. The carries in the last coordinate contribute x^2. This is the entire finite group, with its group laws checked by the kernel.

H consists of the normal forms with last coordinate zero. It is the source group generated by x and y, with both generators of order four and y conjugating x to its inverse.

*Commentary.*

The map from H times the multiplicative ZMod 4 sends (h,u^r) to h*t^r. It is surjective and its kernel is exactly the cyclic subgroup generated by (x^2,u^2). The induced multiplication equivalence identifies the normal-form group with the revised manuscript's central quotient, including the embedding of H.

Y={z,t}, where z=x^2*y^2. The subset is normal. H has square set {1,x^2,y^2}, so z is a nonsquare of H. The involution b=x^-1*t normalizes H. These are the actual elements of Example 2.6.

*Commentary.*

H is a total perfect code for Y, but v has no open neighbor in Hb. Thus the normalizer-only assertion is false. The revision, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23375144, supplies this concrete quotient construction. The original version's statement about Yb and its centralization-alone product identity are not substituted for the corrected assertion. The centralizing-involution same-graph theorem remains a separate sufficient repair; no necessary-and-sufficient classification of translations is claimed.
