# Barry's Original Shifted Hankel Determinant

## Abstract

The original Barry A188475 shifted Hankel conjecture for the literal Catalan recurrence.

Barry's original OEIS A188475 assertion is ‘Hankel transform of A137398(n+1) (conjecture)’. A137398 originally had offset zero and later changed to offset one. In the current numbering its recurrence defines q(1)=0, q(2)=1, q(3)=2. The original shifted matrix therefore has entries q(i+j+2), with i and j from zero through n. No tabulated prefix defines q.

**Definition 1.1 (The literal source recurrence).**

$$\begin{aligned}\operatorname{q}\left(0\right) = 0, \operatorname{q}\left(1\right) = 0, \operatorname{q}\left(2\right) = 1, \operatorname{q}\left(3\right) = 2\\\forall n \in \mathbb{N}, \operatorname{q}\left(n + 3\right) = 2 \cdot \operatorname{q}\left(n + 2\right) + 2 \cdot \operatorname{q}\left(n + 1\right) + \sum_{k \in \operatorname{range}\left(n\right)} \operatorname{catalan}\left(k + 1\right) \cdot \operatorname{q}\left(n + 1 - k\right)\end{aligned}$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Combinatorics/CatalanPowerHankel/BarryA188475.q` (`✓ std3`).

*Citation.* Paul Barry (2011). *OEIS A188475, original shifted Hankel conjecture*. URL: <https://oeis.org/A188475/internal>.

*Commentary.*

All values lie in ℚ. The sum is over k in Finset.range n and is empty at n=0; hence the recursive clause also gives q(3)=2. The value at zero is auxiliary. For n≥1 the substitution r=n+3 and the change of summation index k↦k+1 give exactly the source's positive-index Catalan recurrence.

**Definition 1.2 (The exact shifted matrix).**

$$\forall N \in \mathbb{N}, \forall i \in \operatorname{Fin}\left(N\right), \forall j \in \operatorname{Fin}\left(N\right), \operatorname{hankel}\left(N, i, j\right) = \operatorname{q}\left(i + j + 2\right)$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Combinatorics/CatalanPowerHankel/BarryA188475.hankel` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Acknowledgement.* Paul Barry (2011). *OEIS A188475, original shifted Hankel conjecture*. URL: <https://oeis.org/A188475/internal>.

*Commentary.*

hankel(N) is a rational N by N matrix. In the theorem N=n+1, so its row and column indices are precisely 0,...,n. Every source index is at least two.

**Theorem 1.3 (The complete original conjecture).**

$$\forall n \in \mathbb{N}, \operatorname{det}\left(\operatorname{hankel}\left(n + 1\right)\right) = 1 + 2 \cdot \sum_{k = 0}^{n} k^{2} \land \operatorname{det}\left(\operatorname{hankel}\left(n + 1\right)\right) = \frac{2 \cdot n^{3} + 3 \cdot n^{2} + n + 3}{3}$$

*Commentary.*

Let C be the rational formal Catalan series, T=C², F=Σq(r)Xʳ and M=1+2F. The literal recurrence proves F(1−2X−2X²−X²T)=X². The Catalan equation proves T(1−2X−X²T)=1. Together they give M(1−2X²T)=1.

Use the monic family P₀=1, P₁=X and Pⱼ₊₂=(X−2)Pⱼ₊₁−βⱼPⱼ, where β₀=2 and βⱼ₊₁=1. The reversed-polynomial generating errors vanish below degree 2j and have leading coefficient two for j≥1. Coefficient extraction therefore gives all-index orthogonality with norms 1,2,2,... .

The monic coefficient matrix A has determinant one. Its finite Gram congruence is AᵀGA=diag(1,2,...,2). The adjugate product identity gives adj(G)=A adj(diag(1,2,...,2)) Aᵀ. The recurrence yields Pⱼ₊₁(0)=2(−1)ʲj, whose square is 4j². Thus the (0,0) cofactor equals 2ᴺ(1+2Σⱼ₌₀ᴺ⁻¹j²). Deleting row and column zero from G also gives twice hankel(N) entry by entry. Cancel the nonzero rational power 2ᴺ and set N=n+1. Induction evaluates the sum of squares to the cubic. The proof includes n=0 and assumes no moment representation or conditional identity for q.
