# Exact Length Products of Two Involutions

## Abstract

Exact-length products of two involutions retain parity-specific exponent bounds.

**Theorem 1.1 (Even and odd reachable exponent intervals).**

$$\forall G \in Type,\; Group\left(G\right) \Rightarrow \left(\forall u \in G,\; \forall v \in G,\; Involution\left(u\right) \Rightarrow \left(Involution\left(v\right) \Rightarrow \left(\forall m \in Nat,\; Equal\left(ExactPower\left(PairSet\left(u, v\right), Twice\left(m\right)\right), PowerInterval\left(Product\left(u, v\right), Negate\left(m\right), m\right)\right) \land Equal\left(ExactPower\left(PairSet\left(u, v\right), Successor\left(Twice\left(m\right)\right)\right), LeftPowerInterval\left(u, Product\left(u, v\right), Negate\left(m\right), Successor\left(m\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)$$

*Commentary.*

G is any group, u and v are any elements with u*u=v*v=one, and m is any natural number. Put S={u,v} and R=u*v. ExactPower(S,n) is the pointwise set power S^n: every element is an ordered product of exactly n letters from S; at n=zero it is the singleton identity.

PowerInterval(R,a,b) is the image of the closed integer interval [a,b] under k maps to R^k. LeftPowerInterval(u,R,a,b) is its image under left multiplication by u. The two equalities are simultaneous: the even interval is [-m,m], and the odd interval is [-m,m+one]. The latter upper endpoint is included.

The proof first inducts on m to characterize products of {one,R,R inverse} by bounded integer exponents. Squaring S gives this set. For odd length, left multiplication by u preserves the index and left multiplication by v shifts it by one, producing the stated union of intervals.

Neither distinctness of u and v nor infinite order of R is assumed. Trivial groups, finite orders, coincident powers and m=zero are included. The conclusion gives sets, not unique expressions or their cardinalities. It supplies the exact-length group step for terminal Kraus words, but proves no matrix rank, minimal polynomial, channel realization or physical environment claim.
