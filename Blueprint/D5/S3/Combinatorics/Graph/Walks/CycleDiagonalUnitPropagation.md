# Diagonal units propagate around a cycle

## Abstract

Two adjacent standard diagonal units in the real cycle algebra force every matrix unit, scalar complex commutants, and only the two scalar orthogonal symmetries.

Let L be any natural number at least three, with sites in Fin L and addition taken modulo L. Let A be the ordinary simple-cycle adjacency matrix, Delta its graph Laplacian, and V = Matrix.diagonal v for an arbitrary real potential v. Every vertex has two distinct neighbours, including on the triangle, so Delta = 2I - A. Write B = Algebra.adjoin real {Delta,V}, the real unital matrix algebra generated by those two matrices. The same algebra is generated by A and V. Write E(i,k) = Matrix.single i k 1 and C(M) = M.map Complex.ofReal for the entrywise complex image of a real matrix.

**Theorem 1.1 (Two adjacent diagonal units imply full algebra).**

$$\forall (L : \mathbb{N}), (3 \le L) \Rightarrow (\forall (v : \operatorname{Fin}\left(L\right) \to \mathbb{R}), (\exists (j : \operatorname{Fin}\left(L\right)), (\operatorname{E}\left(j, j\right) \in B) \land (\operatorname{E}\left(j + 1, j + 1\right) \in B)) \Rightarrow ((B = \operatorname{FullMatrixAlgebra}\left(\operatorname{Fin}\left(L\right), \mathbb{R}\right)) \land ((\forall (X : \operatorname{Matrix}\left(\operatorname{Fin}\left(L\right), \operatorname{Fin}\left(L\right), \mathbb{C}\right)), (\operatorname{Commute}\left(X, \operatorname{C}\left(Delta\right)\right)) \Rightarrow ((\operatorname{Commute}\left(X, \operatorname{C}\left(V\right)\right)) \Rightarrow (\exists (c : \mathbb{C}), X = c \cdot I))) \land (\forall (O : \operatorname{Matrix}\left(\operatorname{Fin}\left(L\right), \operatorname{Fin}\left(L\right), \mathbb{R}\right)), (O \in \operatorname{OrthogonalGroup}\left(\operatorname{Fin}\left(L\right), \mathbb{R}\right)) \Rightarrow ((\operatorname{Commute}\left(O, Delta\right)) \Rightarrow ((\operatorname{Commute}\left(O, V\right)) \Rightarrow ((O = I) \lor (O = -I))))))))$$

*Commentary.*

Assume E(j,j) and E(j+1,j+1) belong to B for some cyclic site j. If E(k-1,k-1) and E(k,k) belong to B, put Q = I - E(k-1,k-1). The column A E(k,k) is E(k-1,k) + E(k+1,k), and the corresponding row is E(k,k-1) + E(k,k+1). Multiplying by Q removes the k-1 component. Hence Q A E(k,k) and E(k,k) A Q are E(k+1,k) and E(k,k+1), whose product is E(k+1,k+1). All operations remain inside the real algebra. Induction in the number of cyclic steps starting at j gives every diagonal unit, without a bound on L.

For every graph edge from i to k, E(i,i) A E(k,k) = E(i,k). A walk from i to k exists because the cycle is connected. The successive edge units multiply to E(i,k), with the length-zero walk giving E(i,i). Thus every standard matrix unit belongs to B. Expanding an arbitrary real matrix as the finite sum of its entries times E(i,k) gives B = the full real matrix algebra.

Entrywise complexification is a real algebra homomorphism. A complex matrix commuting with C(Delta) and C(V) therefore commutes with the image of every element of B, in particular every complex standard unit. Matrix.mem_range_scalar_iff_commute_single' gives X = cI. Applying this to the complex image of a real orthogonal O and taking real parts gives O = aI with a real. Orthogonality gives a squared equal to one, so O is I or minus I. No distinct-value, generic-potential or two-valued-potential assumption is imposed.
