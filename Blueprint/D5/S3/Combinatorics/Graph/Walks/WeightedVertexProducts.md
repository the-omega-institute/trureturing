# Weighted Vertex Products and Closed Walks

## Abstract

On a connected graph over a commutative group with surjective squaring, symmetric edge weights factor into vertex products exactly when every even closed walk balances. For a bipartite graph over any commutative group, every specified spanning tree gives equivalent simple-cycle and fundamental-cycle tests.

Let V be an arbitrary vertex type, A a commutative group, G a simple graph, and w:Sym2(V)->A symmetric edge weights. For a walk p, alpha(w,p) is List.alternatingProd of its edge-weight list. Every occurrence is retained, including repeated edges and immediate backtracks. The empty walk has value one. Sol(w,G) means that there is b:V->A with b(u)b(v)=w({u,v}) on every edge.

**Theorem 1.1 (Even Closed Walks Are the Complete Obstruction).**

$$\forall V \in \mathrm{Type},\; \forall A \in \mathrm{Type},\; (\mathrm{CommGroup}\left(A\right)) \Rightarrow (\forall G \in \mathrm{SimpleGraph}\left(V\right),\; \forall w \in \mathrm{Function}\left(\mathrm{Sym2}\left(V\right), A\right),\; (\mathrm{SurjectiveSquare}\left(A\right)) \Rightarrow ((\mathrm{Connected}\left(G\right)) \Rightarrow ((\mathrm{Sol}\left(w, G\right)) \Leftrightarrow (\forall u \in V,\; \forall p \in \mathrm{Walk}\left(G, u, u\right),\; (\mathrm{Even}\left(\mathrm{length}\left(p\right)\right)) \Rightarrow (\mathrm{alpha}\left(w, p\right) = 1)))))$$

*Commentary.*

The square map a->aa is assumed surjective and G is connected. Along a walk from u to v, a vertex factorization gives alpha(p)=b(u)b(v)^(-(-1)^length(p)). At a root, either every closed walk is even, and its value can be set to one, or an odd closed walk fixes the square of the root value. Concatenating two odd loops shows that this constraint is independent of the chosen loop. Comparing two root-to-vertex walks through their closed concatenation proves path compatibility. Transporting the root value then constructs all vertex factors.

**Theorem 1.2 (Simple Cycles and Every Spanning Tree).**

$$\forall V \in \mathrm{Type},\; \forall A \in \mathrm{Type},\; (\mathrm{CommGroup}\left(A\right)) \Rightarrow (\forall G \in \mathrm{SimpleGraph}\left(V\right),\; \forall w \in \mathrm{Function}\left(\mathrm{Sym2}\left(V\right), A\right),\; (\mathrm{Colorable}\left(G, 2\right)) \Rightarrow (\forall T \in \mathrm{SimpleGraph}\left(V\right),\; (\mathrm{SubgraphOnSameVertices}\left(T, G\right)) \Rightarrow ((\mathrm{IsTree}\left(T\right)) \Rightarrow (((\mathrm{Sol}\left(w, G\right)) \Leftrightarrow (\mathrm{AllSimpleCyclesBalance}\left(w, G\right))) \land ((\mathrm{Sol}\left(w, G\right)) \Leftrightarrow (\mathrm{AllFundamentalCyclesBalance}\left(w, G, T\right)))))))$$

*Commentary.*

No square-root hypothesis is needed here. The graph is bipartite and T is any specified spanning tree on the same vertices. Tree paths construct vertex factors. A non-tree edge together with the tree path returning to its initial vertex is a fundamental simple cycle. Bipartiteness makes that returning path odd. Balancing its cycle is exactly the missing edge equation. Thus checking all simple cycles, or only the fundamental cycles of any T, is equivalent to Sol(w,G). Connectedness is carried by T.IsTree; no finiteness hypothesis is imposed.
