*Commentary.*

For every finite group G with two elements whose products differ, write z for the order of its center, n for the number of noncentral elements, C(x) for the centralizer of x and p for the least prime dividing |G|. Then z is at least 1, n is at least 3z, n is at least z+2 and at least 3, and z divides n. The common-neighbourhood energy of the commuting graph on the individual noncentral elements (commutingGraph(G) of the noncentral commuting-graph module) is at most twice the sum over noncentral x of max(|C(x)|-z-2,0), and that is at most n max(2(n+z)/p-2z-4,0). This last quantity is at most 2n((n+z)/p-z-1), and also at most n max(n-z-4,0), which is at most n(n-z-2), strictly below the complete-graph value 2(n-1)(n-2).

The per-vertex sum and the bound 2n((n+z)/p-z-1) are Corollary 4 of Ma and Zhang, Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24398996, and the strict bound n(n-z-2) < 2(n-1)(n-2) with n at least z+2 at least 3 is their Theorem 2. The repository sharpens the least-prime form to n max(2(n+z)/p-2z-4,0) and adds n at least 3z and z dividing n. The ring analogue is RingNoncentralBound.

For a noncentral x, the centralizer C(x) is exactly the disjoint union of the center, x itself and the graph neighbours of x, so deg(x) = |C(x)|-z-1; the noncentral commuting-graph module proves only the inequality. C(x) is a proper subgroup, so its index is a divisor of |G| exceeding one, hence at least p, and p|C(x)| is at most |G| = n+z. The center is a proper subgroup of C(x), so |C(x)| is at least 2z and |G| at least 4z; Lagrange also gives z dividing |G| and hence n. The neighbourhood-clique bound turns the degree identity into the per-vertex energy bound; the remaining steps use p at least 2 and |C(x)| at least z+1, and 2(n-1)(n-2)-n(n-z-2) = (n-2)^2+nz is positive.
