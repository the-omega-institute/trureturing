# Weighted gain reconstruction on chordal graphs and clique trees

## Abstract

Weighted predecessor means and translated weighted clique means bound gain residuals.

**Theorem 1.1 (Weighted simplicial reconstruction).**

$$\forall u \in \operatorname{Universe}\left(\right),\; \forall v \in \operatorname{Universe}\left(\right),\; \forall V \in \operatorname{Type}\left(u\right),\; [\operatorname{Finite}\left(V\right)], \forall G \in \operatorname{SimpleGraph}\left(V\right),\; (\operatorname{IsChordal}\left(G\right)) \Rightarrow (\forall k \in \mathbb{N},\; (2 \le k) \Rightarrow ((\forall C \in \operatorname{Set}\left(V\right),\; (\operatorname{IsClique}\left(G, C\right)) \Rightarrow (\operatorname{ncard}\left(C\right) \le k + 1)) \Rightarrow (\forall E \in \operatorname{Type}\left(v\right),\; [\operatorname{SeminormedAddCommGroup}\left(E\right)], [\operatorname{NormedSpace}\left(\mathbb{R}, E\right)], \forall g \in V \to \left(V \to E\right),\; \forall epsilon \in \mathbb{R},\; (0 \le epsilon) \Rightarrow ((\forall i \in V,\; \forall j \in V,\; \operatorname{g}\left(j, i\right) = -\operatorname{g}\left(i, j\right)) \Rightarrow ((\forall i \in V,\; \forall j \in V,\; \forall l \in V,\; (\operatorname{Adj}\left(G, i, j\right)) \Rightarrow ((\operatorname{Adj}\left(G, j, l\right)) \Rightarrow ((\operatorname{Adj}\left(G, i, l\right)) \Rightarrow (\operatorname{norm}\left(\operatorname{g}\left(i, j\right) + \operatorname{g}\left(j, l\right) - \operatorname{g}\left(i, l\right)\right) \le epsilon)))) \Rightarrow (\exists phi \in V \to E,\; \forall i \in V,\; \forall j \in V,\; (\operatorname{Adj}\left(G, i, j\right)) \Rightarrow (\operatorname{norm}\left(\operatorname{g}\left(i, j\right) - (\operatorname{phi}\left(j\right) - \operatorname{phi}\left(i\right))\right) \le (\operatorname{max}\left(\operatorname{real}\left(k\right) - 2, \frac{\operatorname{real}\left(k\right) - 1 + \operatorname{sqrt}\left((\operatorname{real}\left(k\right) + 3)^{2} - 16\right)}{4}\right)) \cdot epsilon)))))))$$

*Commentary.*

Reverse a simplicial deletion order. Every predecessor neighborhood is a clique of size at most k, since adjoining the inserted vertex gives another clique. The potential is zero at a vertex with no predecessors. Use the uniform mean of the earlier candidates for a smaller neighborhood. For a neighborhood of size k, give its newest predecessor weight t and the others weight s, with s equal to one divided by one plus the displayed radius and t equal to one minus the other weights.

The weighted residuals at an inserted vertex sum to zero. For an edge from the newest predecessor in a full neighborhood, the other predecessors lie in that vertex's own predecessor clique. When they exhaust that clique the earlier residuals cancel. When one vertex is omitted, subtract the old weighted zero sum to leave signed residual coefficients of total absolute mass at most twice s minus t.

The new triangle defects cost at most k minus one times s. The quadratic inequality for the displayed radical ensures that the residual cost and defect cost together stay within the radius. The real operator casts natural numbers to the reals, and norm is the seminorm. The coefficients at k equal to two, three, four and five are respectively one, the golden ratio, three plus the square root of thirty-three divided by four with the division applied to the entire sum, and three.

**Theorem 1.2 (Weighted clique-tree occurrence centers).**

$$\forall u \in \operatorname{Universe}\left(\right),\; \forall v \in \operatorname{Universe}\left(\right),\; \forall w \in \operatorname{Universe}\left(\right),\; \forall V \in \operatorname{Type}\left(u\right),\; [\operatorname{Fintype}\left(V\right)], \forall I \in \operatorname{Type}\left(w\right),\; [\operatorname{Finite}\left(I\right)], \forall T \in \operatorname{SimpleGraph}\left(I\right),\; \forall G \in \operatorname{SimpleGraph}\left(V\right),\; \forall K \in I \to \operatorname{Set}\left(V\right),\; (\operatorname{IsCliqueTree}\left(T, G, K\right)) \Rightarrow ((\forall a \in I,\; \forall b \in I,\; (\operatorname{Adj}\left(T, a, b\right)) \Rightarrow (\operatorname{Nonempty}\left(\operatorname{intersection}\left(\operatorname{K}\left(a\right), \operatorname{K}\left(b\right)\right)\right))) \Rightarrow (\forall pi \in I \to \left(V \to \mathbb{R}\right),\; (\forall a \in I,\; \forall x \in V,\; 0 \le \operatorname{pi}\left(a, x\right)) \Rightarrow ((\forall a \in I,\; \forall x \in V,\; (\neg (x \in \operatorname{K}\left(a\right))) \Rightarrow (\operatorname{pi}\left(a, x\right) = 0)) \Rightarrow ((\forall a \in I,\; \sum_{x \in V} \operatorname{pi}\left(a, x\right) = 1) \Rightarrow (\forall alpha \in \mathbb{R},\; \forall d \in \mathbb{R},\; (0 \le d) \Rightarrow ((\forall a \in I,\; \forall x \in V,\; \forall y \in V,\; (x \in \operatorname{K}\left(a\right)) \Rightarrow ((y \in \operatorname{K}\left(a\right)) \Rightarrow ((\operatorname{Adj}\left(G, x, y\right)) \Rightarrow (1 - \operatorname{pi}\left(a, x\right) - \operatorname{pi}\left(a, y\right) \le alpha)))) \Rightarrow ((\forall a \in I,\; \forall b \in I,\; (\operatorname{Adj}\left(T, a, b\right)) \Rightarrow ((1 - \frac{1}{\operatorname{real}\left(\operatorname{ncard}\left(\operatorname{intersection}\left(\operatorname{K}\left(a\right), \operatorname{K}\left(b\right)\right)\right)\right)}) \cdot (1 - \sum_{x \in \operatorname{intersection}\left(\operatorname{K}\left(a\right), \operatorname{K}\left(b\right)\right)} \operatorname{min}\left(\operatorname{pi}\left(a, x\right), \operatorname{pi}\left(b, x\right)\right)) \le d)) \Rightarrow (\forall D \in \mathbb{N},\; (\forall x \in V,\; \forall a \in I,\; \forall b \in I,\; (x \in \operatorname{K}\left(a\right)) \Rightarrow ((x \in \operatorname{K}\left(b\right)) \Rightarrow (\operatorname{dist}\left(T, a, b\right) \le D))) \Rightarrow (\forall E \in \operatorname{Type}\left(v\right),\; [\operatorname{SeminormedAddCommGroup}\left(E\right)], [\operatorname{NormedSpace}\left(\mathbb{R}, E\right)], \forall g \in V \to \left(V \to E\right),\; \forall epsilon \in \mathbb{R},\; (0 \le epsilon) \Rightarrow ((\forall i \in V,\; \forall j \in V,\; \operatorname{g}\left(j, i\right) = -\operatorname{g}\left(i, j\right)) \Rightarrow ((\forall i \in V,\; \forall j \in V,\; \forall l \in V,\; (\operatorname{Adj}\left(G, i, j\right)) \Rightarrow ((\operatorname{Adj}\left(G, j, l\right)) \Rightarrow ((\operatorname{Adj}\left(G, i, l\right)) \Rightarrow (\operatorname{norm}\left(\operatorname{g}\left(i, j\right) + \operatorname{g}\left(j, l\right) - \operatorname{g}\left(i, l\right)\right) \le epsilon)))) \Rightarrow (\exists phi \in V \to E,\; \forall i \in V,\; \forall j \in V,\; (\operatorname{Adj}\left(G, i, j\right)) \Rightarrow ((\operatorname{norm}\left(\operatorname{g}\left(i, j\right) - (\operatorname{phi}\left(j\right) - \operatorname{phi}\left(i\right))\right) \le (alpha + 4 \cdot \operatorname{real}\left(\operatorname{natDiv}\left(D + 1, 2\right)\right) \cdot d) \cdot epsilon) \land ((D \le 1) \Rightarrow (\operatorname{norm}\left(\operatorname{g}\left(i, j\right) - (\operatorname{phi}\left(j\right) - \operatorname{phi}\left(i\right))\right) \le (alpha + 2 \cdot d) \cdot epsilon)))))))))))))))$$

*Commentary.*

The tree T has clique bags K that cover the vertices and edges of G. The bags containing a fixed vertex contain all the bags on the tree paths between them. The distributions are indexed by the entire finite vertex type and vanish outside their bags. The sums use finite sets as their index domains, and natDiv denotes natural-number division.

Each bag potential is the weighted mean of vertex stars. Its error on an edge omits both endpoint weights. Translate the bag potentials by constants so the mean difference on every separator vanishes. Subtract the smaller of the two weights at each common star before bounding the difference of the two residuals. Averaging the resulting increments over a separator omits the zero diagonal term.

Choose a center in each vertex's occurrence subtree and use its translated value. Each endpoint of an edge moves by at most the product of its radius and the separator shift bound. At diameter one there are at most two occurrences of a vertex, so the midpoint of their translated values gives the second bound. At diameter zero every vertex has a unique occurrence.
