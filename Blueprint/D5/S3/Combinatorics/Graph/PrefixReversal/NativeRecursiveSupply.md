# Actual Anchored Recursive Supply

## Abstract

A full literal label tuple supplies actual recursive boundary ports, their native c edges, and their distinctness.

**Definition 1.1 (Independent Full Label Frames).**

$$\mathrm{SupplyFrame}\left(m, k\right)$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Graph/PrefixReversal/NativeRecursiveSupply.SupplyFrame` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

SupplyFrame(m,k) contains selected and base lists and a terminal label. The selected list has length k; selected followed by base has length m. The full tuple selected, base, terminal is nodup and contains every label in Fin(m+1). The frame does not contain a path, coverage premise, or recursive construction certificate.

**Definition 1.2 (The Actual Literal Anchor).**

$$\mathrm{Tuple}\left(\mathrm{anchor}\left(F\right)\right) = \mathrm{Append}\left(\mathrm{Append}\left(\mathrm{Selected}\left(F\right), \mathrm{Base}\left(F\right)\right), \mathrm{Singleton}\left(\mathrm{Terminal}\left(F\right)\right)\right)$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Graph/PrefixReversal/NativeRecursiveSupply.anchor` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

The nodup complete tuple determines an actual permutation of Fin(m+1), with precisely selected followed by base followed by the terminal as its literal list.

**Definition 1.3 (Actual Right Supply Ports).**

$$\mathrm{rightPort}\left(F, i\right) = \mathrm{Multiply}\left(\mathrm{anchor}\left(F\right), \mathrm{Power}\left(\mathrm{Multiply}\left(\mathrm{b}\left(m\right), \mathrm{c}\left(m\right)\right), i\right)\right)$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Graph/PrefixReversal/NativeRecursiveSupply.rightPort` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

R_i is anchor(F) multiplied on the right by (b_m c_m)^i. Its prefix rotates forward by i and its terminal label stays fixed.

**Definition 1.4 (Actual Left Supply Ports).**

$$\mathrm{leftPort}\left(F, i\right) = \mathrm{Multiply}\left(\mathrm{rightPort}\left(F, i\right), \mathrm{b}\left(m\right)\right)$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Graph/PrefixReversal/NativeRecursiveSupply.leftPort` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

L_i is R_i b_m. It reverses the entire prefix and keeps the terminal; the base is not independently reversed in the frame.

**Definition 1.5 (The Distinguished k Plus One Port Pairs).**

$$\mathrm{specialPort}\left(F, p\right)$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Graph/PrefixReversal/NativeRecursiveSupply.specialPort` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For p in Fin(k+1) times Fin(2), side zero selects R at p's first coordinate and side one selects L at that same coordinate.

**Theorem 1.6 (Every Supply Coordinate and Closing Seam Is Actual).**

$$\forall m \in \mathrm{Nat},\; 3 \le m \Rightarrow \left(\forall k \in \mathrm{Nat},\; \forall F \in \mathrm{SupplyFrame}\left(m, k\right),\; \left(\forall i \in \mathrm{Nat},\; \mathrm{Tuple}\left(\mathrm{rightPort}\left(F, i\right)\right) = \mathrm{Append}\left(\mathrm{Rotate}\left(\mathrm{Append}\left(\mathrm{Selected}\left(F\right), \mathrm{Base}\left(F\right)\right), i\right), \mathrm{Singleton}\left(\mathrm{Terminal}\left(F\right)\right)\right) \land \left(\mathrm{Tuple}\left(\mathrm{leftPort}\left(F, i\right)\right) = \mathrm{Append}\left(\mathrm{Reverse}\left(\mathrm{Rotate}\left(\mathrm{Append}\left(\mathrm{Selected}\left(F\right), \mathrm{Base}\left(F\right)\right), i\right)\right), \mathrm{Singleton}\left(\mathrm{Terminal}\left(F\right)\right)\right) \land \left(\mathrm{Multiply}\left(\mathrm{leftPort}\left(F, i\right), \mathrm{c}\left(m\right)\right) = \mathrm{rightPort}\left(F, \mathrm{Add}\left(i, 1\right)\right) \land \mathrm{Adj}\left(\mathrm{NativeCayleyGraph}\left(m\right), \mathrm{leftPort}\left(F, i\right), \mathrm{rightPort}\left(F, \mathrm{Add}\left(i, 1\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)\right) \land \left(\mathrm{Multiply}\left(\mathrm{leftPort}\left(F, \mathrm{Subtract}\left(m, 1\right)\right), \mathrm{c}\left(m\right)\right) = \mathrm{rightPort}\left(F, 0\right) \land \mathrm{Adj}\left(\mathrm{NativeCayleyGraph}\left(m\right), \mathrm{leftPort}\left(F, \mathrm{Subtract}\left(m, 1\right)\right), \mathrm{rightPort}\left(F, 0\right)\right)\right)\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Graph/PrefixReversal/NativeRecursiveSupply.native_anchored_supply_coordinates` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For every m at least three, every k, every full SupplyFrame(m,k), and every natural supply position i, the right tuple is rotate(selected ++ base,i) followed by terminal. The left tuple is the reverse of that same prefix followed by terminal. Multiplication by the actual c generator takes L_i to R_(i+1), and these configurations are adjacent in the actual Cayley graph generated by a, b and c. The final full-supply seam takes L_(m-1) to R_0 and is also a native edge.

The tuple and generator formulas are proved from the actual permutation coordinates. Full supplier closure uses the earned order-m prefix rotation. The c generator is nonidentity when m is at least three. No abstract boundary graph is supplied as a premise. This theorem does not assert a Hamilton cycle, a prior-cycle edge inventory, or the recursive all-size closure.

**Theorem 1.7 (Every Distinguished Actual Port Is Distinct When k Is Below m).**

$$\forall m \in \mathrm{Nat},\; 3 \le m \Rightarrow \left(\forall k \in \mathrm{Nat},\; \forall F \in \mathrm{SupplyFrame}\left(m, k\right),\; k < m \Rightarrow \mathrm{Injective}\left(\mathrm{specialPort}\left(F\right)\right)\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Graph/PrefixReversal/NativeRecursiveSupply.native_special_port_injective` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For m at least three and k strictly below m, the actual specialPort map on Fin(k+1) times Fin(2) is injective. The proof embeds these ports into the existing native supplier coordinate equivalence, so distinctness concerns actual configurations. The strict k < m condition is necessary: the full supplier returns to R_0 at index m.
