# The normalization at complex places

## Abstract

Trace metrics and fractional ideal slopes

Let K be a number field of degree d, with discriminant D and r complex places. Let I be a nonzero invertible fractional ideal of its full ring of integers, equipped with the standard Hermitian norm at each embedding. No cyclotomic or totally complex hypothesis is needed for the covolume calculation.

*Commentary.*

The mixed Minkowski space takes one real coordinate at each real place and one complex coordinate at each complex place. Its Euclidean realization carries the sum of squared coordinate norms. Multiplying each complex coordinate by the positive square root of two changes this squared norm to the sum of real squares plus twice the sum of complex squared norms. Thus both members of each conjugate pair are counted. The trace ideal lattice is the actual inverse image of the mixed Minkowski ideal lattice under the resulting linear coordinate equivalence.

The real determinant of the complex-coordinate rescaling is two to the power r. Applying Mathlib's NumberField.mixedEmbedding.covolume_idealLattice gives the mixed ideal covolume as the absolute ideal norm times the positive square root of the absolute discriminant, divided by two to the power r. The coordinate determinant cancels precisely this last factor. Consequently the trace lattice has covolume the absolute ideal norm times the positive square root of the absolute discriminant. Its measure-theoretic covolume also agrees with the square root of the Gram determinant used for Euclidean lattice slopes: expansion in an orthonormal basis expresses that determinant as the square of the coordinate determinant.

For the standard Hermitian ideal the Arakelov degree is minus the logarithm of the absolute ideal norm. Dividing minus the logarithm of the trace covolume by d therefore gives the normalized ideal degree minus log of the absolute discriminant divided by twice d. All quantities use real logarithms and the integer lattice has rank d.

The metric realization and the translation formula are derived here from the pinned Mathlib Minkowski fractional-ideal covolume theorem and real determinant change of variables. Grayson supplies the general Euclidean slope context. Gaudron–Remond's restriction-of-scalars formulas and Bost–Chen's extension-of-scalars theorem are classical results; the latter is not a restriction-of-scalars theorem. This statement concerns standard Hermitian ideals. It does not identify arbitrary higher-rank Hermitian modules with sums of these standard metrized ideals.

Assume now that K is a full cyclotomic field, with any positive cyclotomic order. The metric remains the standard trace-sum metric on the fractional ideal.

*Commentary.*

The cyclotomic-extension instance supplies a primitive root through IsCyclotomicExtension.zeta_spec; no chosen root is an additional hypothesis. Multiplication by this root is an integral isometry: each archimedean image of the root has absolute value one. Uniqueness of the Euclidean HN filtration fixes every step under this multiplication. The ring of integers is generated by the root, so every step is an order submodule. Its integral saturation also gives saturation for every nonzero order scalar: an algebraic scalar divides a nonzero rational integer, which reduces the assertion to integral saturation.

A fractional ideal has rank one over K. Given a nonzero element in a saturated order submodule and any element of the ideal, write their ratio with a nonzero denominator in the ring of integers. Clearing that denominator places a nonzero scalar multiple of the arbitrary element in the submodule. Saturation therefore makes the submodule the whole ideal. The first nonzero HN step is consequently the whole lattice, and the unique HN endpoint list has exactly one term.

Write d for the degree of K and Delta for its discriminant. The integral rank is d. The trace-sum covolume is the square root of the absolute discriminant times the absolute norm of I. In the normalization minus logarithmic covolume divided by integral rank, the slope is therefore minus log of the ideal norm divided by d, minus log of the absolute discriminant divided by twice d. This is the normalized Arakelov slope of the ideal minus the exact discriminant shift.

The argument applies to every positive cyclotomic order, including the rational cases. It uses the standard trace-sum metric on a line. It does not identify HN filtrations for arbitrary higher-rank Hermitian order lattices. The cyclotomic application is derived from the Euclidean uniqueness theorem and the fractional-ideal covolume formula. Grayson's work supplies the classical Euclidean filtration background; the particular combined statement is not attributed verbatim to that article.
