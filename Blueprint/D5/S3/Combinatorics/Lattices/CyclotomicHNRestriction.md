# Saturated modules over a monogenic order

## Abstract

Algebraic scalar actions on Euclidean HN sublattices

Let A be a commutative ring without zero divisors, algebraic over the integers and generated as an integer algebra by z. Let E be a finite-dimensional real inner product space with an A-module structure extending its integer scalar multiplication. Suppose multiplication by z agrees with a real linear isometry T, and T maps a discrete integral lattice L onto itself. The lattice need not span the ambient real space.

*Commentary.*

Every stage of any Euclidean HN filtration from zero to L is the restriction of an A-submodule of E. If x belongs to L, a is a nonzero element of A, and ax belongs to that submodule, then x belongs to it. The same assertion holds for the strict truncation above every real slope threshold, including thresholds equal to a factor slope. This is saturation inside L; no assertion is made about points outside L.

Uniqueness of the Euclidean HN filtration fixes each stage under T. Closure under z and under integer linear combinations extends to closure under A by induction on the generated algebra. For a nonzero a, algebraicity supplies c in A and a nonzero integer m with ac equal to m. Closure under c converts ax in the stage to mx in the stage. Its real span is closed under division by m, and integer saturation then gives x.

The result does not require that z have finite order, that A be integrally closed, or that L have positive rank. For a full cyclotomic field, take A to be its ring of integers and z a primitive root of unity. The cyclotomic generation theorem and an isometric realization of multiplication by z supply the hypotheses. The algebraic saturation argument is repo-derived; Grayson's work supplies the classical Euclidean HN background. This statement does not itself identify Arakelov degrees or compute the multiplicities of number-field slopes.

*Commentary.*

For these two equivalences, A need only be a commutative ring generated over the integers by z; no algebraicity or absence of zero divisors is required. The ambient finite-dimensional metric and integral isometry assumptions remain. For any two order-stable saturated sublattices F and G, Euclidean semistability of their interval is equivalent to testing its slope inequality only on order-stable intermediate sublattices. A common additive shift of all slopes has no effect on this equivalence. In the number-field normalization this shift is the logarithm of the absolute discriminant divided by twice the field degree, once the corresponding degree formula has been established for the subobjects.

Order stability implies invariance under T: stability gives T(F) contained in F. The isometry preserves rank, and rank is strictly increasing on saturated sublattices, so this inclusion is equality. Conversely, invariance under T implies order stability by generation. The first HN stage in the interval is therefore order-stable. Its slope is maximal among all intermediate sublattices, so testing that particular order subobject already bounds every integral subobject. This supplies the converse to the immediate restriction of the tests.

**Theorem 1.3 (The HN subobject chain is unchanged).**

*Acknowledgement.* Daniel R. Grayson (1984). *Reduction theory using semistability*. DOI: [10.1007/BF02566369](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02566369).

*Commentary.*

For a commutative integer algebra generated by z, between order-stable endpoints, a list of saturated integral sublattices is a Euclidean HN chain if and only if it is an order HN chain. The order chain requires its stages to be order-stable, tests semistability on order-stable intermediate subobjects, and adds the same real constant to every factor slope. Both directions include empty chains and arbitrary intervals. Strict decrease is unchanged by the common translation.

Induct on the endpoint list. In the forward direction, invariance under T makes its first stage order-stable, and the semistability equivalence applies to the first factor. The induction treats the tail. In the reverse direction the order chain already supplies stage stability, so the same equivalence recovers each Euclidean semistable factor. Identification with Arakelov slopes still requires the discriminant degree formula for each subobject and its rank over the field; the order chain here uses the integral rank in its normalized shifted slope.
