# Absorption and universal greatest support

## Abstract

Nonzero absorption exactly characterizes universal greatest support for finite additive-observation fibers over conical commutative coefficients.

**Theorem 1.1 (The exact original-coefficient equivalence).**

$$\forall K \in Typeu,\; [AddCommMonoid\left(K\right)], \left(\forall x \in K,\; \forall y \in K,\; add\left(x, y\right) = 0 \Rightarrow \left(x = 0 \land y = 0\right)\right) \Rightarrow \left(\left(\forall x \in K,\; x \ne 0 \Rightarrow \left(\exists e \in K,\; e \ne 0 \land add\left(x, e\right) = x\right)\right) \Leftrightarrow \left(\forall n \in Nat,\; \forall T \in Typeu,\; [AddMonoid\left(T\right)], \forall F \in AddMonoidHom\left(Fin\left(n\right) \to K, T\right),\; \forall p \in Fin\left(n\right) \to K,\; \exists r \in Fin\left(n\right) \to K,\; F\left(r\right) = F\left(p\right) \land \left(\forall q \in Fin\left(n\right) \to K,\; F\left(q\right) = F\left(p\right) \Rightarrow \left(\forall i \in Fin\left(n\right),\; q\left(i\right) \ne 0 \Rightarrow r\left(i\right) \ne 0\right)\right)\right)\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Transportation/AbsorptionGreatestSupport.result` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Fix any universe u and any K:Type u with its original AddCommMonoid structure. Conicality means x+y=0 implies x=0 and y=0. The left side A says that each nonzero x absorbs some nonzero e: x+e=x. The right side H quantifies over every natural number n, every T:Type u with an AddMonoid instance, every additive monoid homomorphism F:(Fin n→K)→+T, and every p. It returns r with F(r)=F(p) and, for every q with F(q)=F(p), every coordinate nonzero in q is nonzero in r. The zero, addition and equality in every clause are the original ones. In the displayed formula, Nat denotes ℕ and Typeu denotes Type u. The displayed add denotes this addition; applications p(i), F(p) have their usual meanings.

Empty powers n=0 and trivial coefficient monoids are included. There is no regularity, refinement, order, cancellation, division, idempotency, finite-coefficient or nontriviality premise. Targets need not be commutative. The conclusion is a greatest support, with no assertion of a greatest coefficient vector, uniqueness, an algorithm, or a running-time bound. The target universe is part of H: the reverse proof uses a quotient of Fin 2→K, and makes no converse assertion for a smaller target universe unable to contain that observer.

For A⇒H, choose e(i)=0 when q(i)=0, and otherwise choose a nonzero e(i) with q(i)+e(i)=q(i). Thus q+e=q and e has exactly q's support. Given F(p)=F(q), additivity gives F(p+e)=F(p)+F(e)=F(q)+F(e)=F(q+e)=F(p). Conicality gives supp(p+e)=supp(p)∪supp(q). This is the reusable fiber_support_union theorem, which allows arbitrary coordinate and target universes and does not require finite coordinates. In a finite power choose one feasible witness for each coordinate that can be nonzero. Feasible supports are directed by the binary union construction, so Directed.finset_le bounds this finite selected family by a feasible support. The original p supplies nonemptiness even when the coordinate set is empty.

For H⇒A, suppose x≠0 and x+e=x forces e=0. In K² let p=(x,0), q=(0,x), and S={p,q}. For every vector c, p+c∈S iff c=0, and q+c∈S iff c=0. In the equal-singleton case this follows from the assumed trivial absorption stabilizer; in the opposite-singleton case conicality would force x=0. Define the additive congruence a∼b iff, for every c, a+c∈S iff b+c∈S. Associativity and commutativity make this relation compatible with addition. It identifies p and q. For its quotient homomorphism F, any r with F(r)=F(p) belongs to S, by taking context c=0. But a greatest-support r must be nonzero in both coordinates because p and q are feasible. Neither member of S has that support, a contradiction. This is a universal-observer obstruction in the original coefficient monoid, with a quotient target in Type u.

For the annotated-relation application in Atserias and Kolaitis, Section 8, pages 15–17, begin with two finitely supported K-relations R(X), S(Y) and an existing consistency witness P. Let C be the finite ordinary join of the two marginal supports. Restriction to X and Y embeds C into the product of the two finite supports. Every consistency witness Q has support contained in C: a nonzero annotation contributes to each marginal sum, and conicality forces every summand of a zero finite sum to be zero. This uses the finite support required in the definition of each K-relation.

Restrict P to C and enumerate C by Fin n. Enumerate the finite marginal supports of R and S by Fin r and Fin s, respectively, allowing r=0 or s=0. Each marginal map is an additive homomorphism: its value at an indexed marginal tuple is the finite sum of annotations over the corresponding restriction fiber. Their product is one additive observer F:(Fin n→K)→+((Fin r→K)×(Fin s→K)). Its target (Fin r→K)×(Fin s→K) lies in Type u because Fin r and Fin s lie in Type 0 and K:Type u, irrespective of the ambient tuple universes. The finite-join restriction makes projected values outside the marginal supports zero, as in the preceding conicality argument, so this observer records exactly the required full marginals. Its fiber at P is exactly the set of restricted consistency witnesses. Apply A⇒H and extend the returned vector by zero outside C. This gives a finitely supported consistency witness containing the support of every Q. Feasibility is supplied by P; agreement of overlapping marginals alone is not asserted to produce P. This extends the sufficient coefficient hypothesis from regularity to A. Conversely, the quotient observer in H⇒A is an arbitrary additive observer, with no proof that it is a marginal map. The universal all-observer classification therefore does not settle the full marginal coefficient classification in AK25.

A includes conical nonregular coefficients. Take K=WithZero ℕ with the additive structure obtained by adjoining a new additive zero to the additive semigroup ℕ. Its elements are a new zero and symbols [n], with [m]+[n]=[m+n]; the new zero is an identity. It is conical. Every nonzero [n] absorbs the nonzero internal identity [0]. But [1]+y+[1]=[1] has no solution: y equal to the new zero gives [2]=[1], and y=[m] gives 2+m=1. This same original additive structure satisfies A and H and fails regularity. Thus the sufficient class is strictly larger than the conical regular class; this statement does not assert necessity for marginal observers.

A second application is a finite vector of original coefficients subject to arbitrary additive measurements. If one feasible vector is known and the coefficient monoid satisfies conicality and A, a feasible vector activates every coordinate attainable by any feasible vector. Several same-universe measurements combine into a product observer. The construction proves exact support feasibility and makes no optimization or complexity claim. The new equivalence is a repository synthesis; AK25 supplies the marginal problem and application context, rather than this universal equivalence. The pinned congruence and finite-directedness APIs supply infrastructure, rather than the absorption characterization.
