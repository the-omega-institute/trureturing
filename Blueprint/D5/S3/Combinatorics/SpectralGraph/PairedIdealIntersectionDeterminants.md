For any prime p, any natural k and any integer x, p^(2k) dividing x² implies p^k dividing x. If x=0 this is immediate. Otherwise v_p(x²)=2v_p(x) and the divisibility equivalence gives 2k≤2v_p(x). Over any field, let s,H,U,W be arbitrary with H≠0 and s-1≠0. If W²=(s-1)²[(2s-1)²H²U²+2s²(2s-1)²H⁴U+s⁴H⁶], then v=W/((s-1)H³) satisfies v²=F(s,U/H²). Expanding after multiplication by the nonzero denominator proves the assertion. These are the only two nonvanishing hypotheses; U and W may be zero and the characteristic is unrestricted.

For every prime p, every e≥1 and all integers H,U,c,s with p not dividing H or s-1, suppose D(s-1+p^eH,-p^eH+p^(2e)U,c,s)=0. Put P=p^e and W=2A(s-1+PH,-PH+P²U,s)c+B(s-1+PH,-PH+P²U,s). The quadratic identity gives d=W². The normalized expansion gives p^(6e) dividing W², so the preceding square-divisibility consequence gives p^(3e) dividing W. Choose an integer w with W=P³w. Since P≠0, cancel P⁶ to get w²=(s-1)²[(2s-1)²H²U²+2s²(2s-1)²H⁴U+s⁴H⁶]+PN(P,H,U,s). The first expansion yields the same integer equality by substituting h=PH and z=U/H² in the rationals; H≠0 follows from p not dividing H, so this substitution and cancellation are legitimate.

Reduce that integer equality in ZMod p. The cast of P is zero because e≥1, whereas H and s-1 cast to nonzero field elements by ZMod.intCast_zmod_eq_zero_iff_dvd. The arbitrary-field division just proved therefore supplies v in ZMod p with v²=F(s,U/H²), where each integer parameter is cast before field division. This is an actual square-or-zero witness. There is no positivity requirement, sign restriction, condition U≠0, or extra restriction on c or s.

For every prime p and integers U,H with p not dividing H, the rational q=U/H² is p-adically integral: 0≤padicValRat p q. If U=0, q=0 and the finite valuation is zero. Otherwise H≠0 and the rational valuation laws give v_p(q)=v_p(U)-2v_p(H)=v_p(U)≥0. Thus reduction uses a unit denominator and never an undefined residue of a rational expression.

Here is the full root-local consequence in the original variables. Let a,b,c,s be integers with a,b,c>0, let p be prime, put h=a+1-s and e=padicValInt p h, and assume p divides h, p does not divide s(s-1)(2s-1), D(a,b,c,s)=0, and either b+h=0 or 2padicValInt p h≤padicValInt p (b+h). Then 0≤padicValRat p ((b+h)/h²), and there exist actual integers H,U with h=p^eH, b+h=p^(2e)U, p not dividing H and (b+h)/h²=U/H² as rational numbers. Moreover there is v in ZMod p with v²=F(s,U/H²), using the casts of these same H,U. These witnesses fix the residue of the original rational quotient; they are not an unrelated choice of representative.

To see h≠0, set s=a+1 in the exact determinant supplier: D(a,b,c,a+1)=-a²(2a+1)b²c²<0, contradicting the root assumption. Hence the valuation decomposition gives e≥1 and h=p^eH with p not dividing H, even for negative h. The explicit zero disjunct or the divisibility equivalence gives b+h=p^(2e)U. Cancel the nonzero p^(2e) in the rational quotient to obtain (b+h)/h²=U/H²; this also holds for U=0. Rewrite a=s-1+p^eH and b=-p^eH+p^(2e)U. The good-prime hypothesis implies p does not divide s-1, so the scaled-root argument and the integral-quotient argument apply to these actual witnesses. No assumption s≥2, ordering of the tails, coprimality, or nonzero b+h is imposed.

These are necessary conditions on a determinant root. Passing a square test implies neither existence of an integer root nor an integral graph spectrum. Bad primes, other resonances and the general four-prime or higher-prime Q3 question are outside this consequence.
