# The sharp product and its two positive spectral rays

## Abstract

Sharp product equality on labelled K2m determines the entire native spectrum.

Every natural m at least three is allowed. The vertices are Fin (2+m), with two labelled hubs and m labelled leaves. Every one of the 2m hub-to-leaf conductances varies independently over strictly positive reals. The matrix is the original ordinary unnormalized wLap, lambda2 is the original mean-zero Rayleigh infimum, and totalRes is the original harmonic-current resistance summed over every unordered distinct vertex pair, including nonadjacent pairs. No total-conductance or symmetry assumption is imposed on competitors. Nonedge values are arbitrary.

**Definition 1.1 (The complete native eigenvalue multiplicities).**

$$\forall m \in \mathrm{Nat},\; \forall w \in \operatorname{Sym2}\left(\operatorname{Fin}\left(2 + m\right)\right) \to \mathrm{Real},\; \forall t \in \mathrm{Real},\; (\operatorname{FullSpectrum}\left(m, w, t\right)) \Leftrightarrow (let e := \operatorname{eigenvalues}\left(\operatorname{wLap}\left(\operatorname{K2m}\left(m\right), w\right)\right) in (\operatorname{card}\left(\operatorname{filter}\left((\lambda i \mapsto e\left(i\right) = 0), \operatorname{univ}\left(\operatorname{Fin}\left(2 + m\right)\right)\right)\right) = 1) \land ((\operatorname{card}\left(\operatorname{filter}\left((\lambda i \mapsto e\left(i\right) = t), \operatorname{univ}\left(\operatorname{Fin}\left(2 + m\right)\right)\right)\right) = m) \land ((\operatorname{card}\left(\operatorname{filter}\left((\lambda i \mapsto e\left(i\right) = (m) \cdot (t)), \operatorname{univ}\left(\operatorname{Fin}\left(2 + m\right)\right)\right)\right) = 1) \land (\forall i \in \operatorname{Fin}\left(2 + m\right),\; (e\left(i\right) = 0) \lor ((e\left(i\right) = t) \lor (e\left(i\right) = (m) \cdot (t)))))))$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Combinatorics/SpectralGraph/FiedlerResistanceK2mSharpSpectrum.FullSpectrum` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

The eigenvalues are those returned by Matrix.IsHermitian.eigenvalues for the actual matrix wLap (K2m m) w. FullSpectrum requires exactly one zero eigenvalue, exactly m entries equal to t, exactly one entry equal to m*t, and every entry equal to one of those three values. It imposes no ascending-index convention and introduces no surrogate matrix or assumed spectral list. When m is at least three and t is positive, the three values are distinct. The generic Hermitian spectral theorem makes this indexed enumeration the full spectrum with multiplicity.

**Definition 1.2 (One common sign across every leaf).**

$$\forall m \in \mathrm{Nat},\; \forall t \in \mathrm{Real},\; \forall epsilon \in \mathrm{Real},\; \forall w \in \operatorname{Sym2}\left(\operatorname{Fin}\left(2 + m\right)\right) \to \mathrm{Real},\; (\operatorname{Ray}\left(m, t, epsilon, w\right)) \Leftrightarrow (\operatorname{EdgeRestriction}\left(m, (\frac{t}{2}) \cdot (1 + (epsilon) \cdot (\operatorname{splitRatio}\left(m\right))), (\frac{t}{2}) \cdot (1 - (epsilon) \cdot (\operatorname{splitRatio}\left(m\right))), w\right))$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Combinatorics/SpectralGraph/FiedlerResistanceK2mSharpSpectrum.Ray` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For a real scale t and sign epsilon, Ray fixes each first-hub conductance to t/2*(1+epsilon*sqrt((m-2)/(m+2))) and each second-hub conductance to t/2*(1-epsilon*sqrt((m-2)/(m+2))). The same epsilon is used for all leaves. The theorem restricts t to positive values and epsilon to +1 or -1. The definition compares only labelled edges.

*Commentary.*

The conjunction contains the universal product bound C_m=(m+2)(m+1/m); equality if and only if a positive scale and one common sign give the edge restriction; existence and positivity of both rays at every positive scale; lambda2=t, totalRes=C_m/t and the full native spectrum on every such restriction; disjointness of the two labelled rays even when their positive scales differ; interchange of the rays under hub exchange; and invariance of lambda2 and totalRes under arbitrary changes on nonedges. Applying the equality characterization and the universal ray conclusion gives the complete spectrum at every equality case.

*Commentary.*

The product bound, trace equality, positive spectral lower bound, harmonic reciprocal-eigenvalue formula, zero multiplicity, equality parameters, quadratic parameter roots and extremal lambda2/resistance values are direct applications of the original frozen optimizer suppliers. The additional argument proves reciprocal equality rigidity: for the m+1 positive native eigenvalues r_i, put q_i=r_i/t-1. Trace equality gives sum q_i=m-1. Each difference q_i/(1+q_i)-q_i/m is nonnegative, and product equality makes their sum zero. Hence each q_i is either zero or m-1; a positive total forces exactly one nonzero term. Consequently exactly one native eigenvalue is m*t and exactly m are t. This relation is used for every positive point on either equality ray. The construction remains on the original native matrix and harmonic resistance carriers throughout. These are repository-derived consequences of the selected K2m theory; no literature novelty or external open-problem resolution is asserted.
