# Sun's finite sum and binomial translation

## Abstract

A finite polynomial translation preserves the complete Hankel determinant.

Nat denotes the nonnegative integers and Z the integers. Matrix.of is displayed with Fin-sized row and column binders and explicit val projections; det of the empty matrix is one. All floor quotients are natural division.

**Definition 1.1 (Multinomial moments).**

$$\forall r \in Nat,\; c\left(r\right) = (choose\left(2 \cdot r, r\right): \mathbb{Z}) \cdot (choose\left(3 \cdot r, r\right): \mathbb{Z})$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Hankel/SunA006077.c` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Acknowledgement.* Zhi-Wei Sun (2013). *On some determinants with Legendre symbol entries*. DOI: [10.48550/arXiv.1308.2900](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1308.2900). URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2900v5>.

*Commentary.*

The product of the two binomial coefficients is (3r)!/(r!)^3, with no division in the integer definition.

**Definition 1.2 (The original finite sum).**

$$\forall n \in Nat,\; w\left(n\right) = \left(\sum_{k = 0}\right)^{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor} \left(-1\right)^{k} \cdot 3^{n - 3 \cdot k} \cdot (choose\left(n, 3 \cdot k\right): \mathbb{Z}) \cdot (choose\left(2 \cdot k, k\right): \mathbb{Z}) \cdot (choose\left(3 \cdot k, k\right): \mathbb{Z})$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Hankel/SunA006077.w` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Acknowledgement.* Zhi-Wei Sun (2013). *On some determinants with Legendre symbol entries*. DOI: [10.48550/arXiv.1308.2900](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1308.2900). URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2900v5>.

*Commentary.*

This is the literal sum in Sun v5, Conjecture 3.16. The index runs from zero through floor(n/3), inclusive, so n-3k is nonnegative throughout; no truncated subtraction affects a summand.

**Definition 1.3 (The source Hankel determinant).**

$$\forall n \in Nat,\; W\left(n\right) = det\left((\operatorname{Matrix}.\operatorname{of}\left(\lambda (i: Fin\left(n + 1\right)) \mapsto (\lambda (j: Fin\left(n + 1\right)) \mapsto (w\left(\operatorname{Fin}.\operatorname{val}\left(i\right) + \operatorname{Fin}.\operatorname{val}\left(j\right)\right)))\right): Matrix\left(Fin\left(n + 1\right), Fin\left(n + 1\right), \mathbb{Z}\right))\right)$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Hankel/SunA006077.W` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Acknowledgement.* Zhi-Wei Sun (2013). *On some determinants with Legendre symbol entries*. DOI: [10.48550/arXiv.1308.2900](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1308.2900). URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2900v5>.

*Commentary.*

Rows and columns are numbered zero through n, so W(n) has size n+1.

**Definition 1.4 (The sparse moments).**

$$\forall n \in Nat,\; sparse\left(n\right) = ite\left(n \bmod 3 = 0, \left(-1\right)^{NatDiv\left(n, 3\right)} \cdot c\left(NatDiv\left(n, 3\right)\right), 0\right)$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Hankel/SunA006077.sparse` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Acknowledgement.* Zhi-Wei Sun (2013). *On some determinants with Legendre symbol entries*. DOI: [10.48550/arXiv.1308.2900](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1308.2900). URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2900v5>.

*Commentary.*

Only indices divisible by three survive. At index 3r the sparse moment is (-1)^r c(r).

**Definition 1.5 (Weighted binomial translation).**

$$\forall x \in \mathbb{Z},\; \forall b \in Nat \to \mathbb{Z},\; \forall n \in Nat,\; translate\left(x, b, n\right) = \left(\sum_{k = 0}\right)^{n} x^{n - k} \cdot (choose\left(n, k\right): \mathbb{Z}) \cdot b\left(k\right)$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Hankel/SunA006077.translate` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Acknowledgement.* Zhi-Wei Sun (2013). *On some determinants with Legendre symbol entries*. DOI: [10.48550/arXiv.1308.2900](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1308.2900). URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2900v5>.

*Commentary.*

The translation parameter is any integer. The index bound is inclusive and every exponent n-k is nonnegative.

**Theorem 1.6 (The exact source translation).**

$$\forall n \in Nat,\; w\left(n\right) = translate\left(3, sparse, n\right)$$

Deleting the zero terms and substituting j=3k identifies the literal finite sum with the weighted binomial transform.

**Theorem 1.7 (Weighted binomial invariance).**

$$\forall x \in \mathbb{Z},\; \forall b \in Nat \to \mathbb{Z},\; \forall N \in Nat,\; det\left((\operatorname{Matrix}.\operatorname{of}\left(\lambda (i: Fin\left(N\right)) \mapsto (\lambda (j: Fin\left(N\right)) \mapsto (translate\left(x, b\right)\left(\operatorname{Fin}.\operatorname{val}\left(i\right) + \operatorname{Fin}.\operatorname{val}\left(j\right)\right)))\right): Matrix\left(Fin\left(N\right), Fin\left(N\right), \mathbb{Z}\right))\right) = det\left((\operatorname{Matrix}.\operatorname{of}\left(\lambda (i: Fin\left(N\right)) \mapsto (\lambda (j: Fin\left(N\right)) \mapsto (b\left(\operatorname{Fin}.\operatorname{val}\left(i\right) + \operatorname{Fin}.\operatorname{val}\left(j\right)\right)))\right): Matrix\left(Fin\left(N\right), Fin\left(N\right), \mathbb{Z}\right))\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Combinatorics/Hankel/SunA006077.translate_hankel_det` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Citation.* Michael Z. Spivey and Laura L. Steil (2006). *The k-Binomial Transforms and the Hankel Transform*. URL: <https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL9/Spivey/spivey7.html>.

*Commentary.*

Apply the moment functional to products of the polynomials (X+x)^i. Their coefficient matrix is unit lower triangular, giving a congruence P H P transpose with determinant multiplier one. This is classical weighted binomial invariance.

**Theorem 1.8 (Reduction to sparse moments).**

$$\forall n \in Nat,\; W\left(n\right) = det\left((\operatorname{Matrix}.\operatorname{of}\left(\lambda (i: Fin\left(n + 1\right)) \mapsto (\lambda (j: Fin\left(n + 1\right)) \mapsto (sparse\left(\operatorname{Fin}.\operatorname{val}\left(i\right) + \operatorname{Fin}.\operatorname{val}\left(j\right)\right)))\right): Matrix\left(Fin\left(n + 1\right), Fin\left(n + 1\right), \mathbb{Z}\right))\right)$$
