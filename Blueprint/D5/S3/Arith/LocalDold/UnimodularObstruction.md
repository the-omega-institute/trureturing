# Unimodular coordinate obstruction

## Abstract

An integral unimodular action with a vanishing inverse coordinate forces unbounded prime-index violations of local Dold divisibility.

The local Dold condition in `D5/L/Scale/chuysurichay2026fibdold` requires every positive integer n to divide the integer Möbius sum of the p-parts of a(d), over positive divisors d of n. The repository derivation here supplies a sufficient obstruction for positive coordinates of integer unimodular dynamics.

**Theorem 1.3 (Unbounded prime-index violations).**

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Arith/LocalDold/UnimodularObstruction.unimodular_coordinate_obstruction` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For every natural d, A in GLInt(d), integral vector v and i in Fin d, assume (A inverse applied to v)(i)=0. Let a be a natural sequence such that, for every positive n, a(n)=(A^n v)(i) and a(n)>0, and assume a(1)=1. For every prime p and every natural bound H, there exists a prime q>H for which S=sum over d dividing q of μ(q/d) pPart(p,a(d)) satisfies 0<S<q and q does not divide S in the integers. Both p=2 and H=0 are included.

Reduction of A modulo p lies in a finite general linear group, giving a positive period T. In the commutative ring of integer polynomials, Mathlib SModEq.pow_pow_add_one gives (1+pX)^(p^j)-1 in the ideal generated by p^(j+1). Evaluating at the integral matrix C in A^T=I+pC gives A^(T*p^j)=I+p^(j+1)D. Polynomial.aeval allows this noncommutative target. This weak lift works at 2 and does not require an exact matrix order or an LTE formula.

Set r=pT-1 and e=valuation_p(a(r)). The actual inverse observation gives p dividing a(r); positivity gives a(r) nonzero and e>=1. For M=T*p^e, one has 0<r<M and gcd(r,M)=1, even when p divides T. The lifted period modulo p^(e+1) preserves the exact valuation e throughout the positive progression r+kM. Dirichlet supplies a prime in this class above H, r and p^e-1. Its Möbius sum is p^e-1, a positive integer smaller than q.

The source prints the prefixes T=(1,1,2,4,...) and B=(1,1,1,2,5,...). Interpreting them by the sum of the preceding three and four terms respectively, the native companion time map at u=1 acts on reversed states. T uses v=(1,1,0), with inverse state (1,0,0); B uses v=(1,1,1,-1), with inverse state (1,1,-1,0). In both cases the last coordinate is the observed positive-index sequence. The value -1 is only B's integer modeling extension at zero. Applying the generic obstruction to each source sequence requires these native coordinate identifications and positivity at every positive index; it requires no positive value at index zero.
