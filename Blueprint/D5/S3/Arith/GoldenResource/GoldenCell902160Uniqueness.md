# Uniqueness in the 902160 Cell

## Abstract

The order of three modulo 179 selects exactly one member of the scaled cell.

**Lemma 1.1 (An unbounded exponent characterization).**

$$\forall n \in \mathbb{N}, 3^{n} \equiv 2241 (\mathrm{mod} 179) \iff n \equiv 23 (\mathrm{mod} 89)$$

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

The order of three modulo 179 is 89: its eighty ninth power is one, three is not one, and 89 is prime. Its twenty third power and 2241 both have residue 93. Equality of powers therefore determines the exponent modulo 89. This statement applies to every natural exponent.

**Theorem 1.2 (The unique member is 1804320).**

*Commentary.*

Every listed index is divisible by 179. Projecting its power congruence to that modulus forces its exponent to be congruent to 23 modulo 89, which excludes the other five indices. The old prime exponent windows alone do not make these exclusions.

For the surviving index, lift the frozen congruence at 10080 to any nonzero multiple of its exponent. The residues modulo 32, 5 and 7 are one, and the residue modulo 9 is zero. Coprime CRT combines these with the congruence modulo 179. The large powers stay symbolic; only small certificates and exponent residues are normalized.

A separate application of Fermat and Euler gives finiteness for the whole single residual prime-power branch, without an upper bound on the prime or its exponent. Precisely, let n = c p^e with c = 2^a 3^b 5 7, 4 ≤ a ≤ 6, 2 ≤ b ≤ 3, p prime, 7 < p, and 1 ≤ e, and assume 3^n is congruent to 2241 modulo that same n. Then 5040 ≤ c ≤ 60480. Frobenius modulo p gives 3^(c p^e) = 3^c, so p divides 3^c − 2241. This positive integer is at most B = 3^60480 − 2241, and therefore p ≤ B.

Since p > 7, three is coprime to p. The totient of p^e is p^(e−1)(p−1), which divides n(p−1). Euler's theorem and the original congruence thus give 2241^(p−1) = 1 modulo p^e. Consequently p^e divides the positive integer 2241^(p−1) − 1 and p^e ≤ E = 2241^(B−1) − 1. Hence n ≤ 60480 E: every such index belongs to one finite interval. These enormous bounds stay symbolic and no factorization of them is required. This is an application of existing theorems, rather than another frozen declaration. It does not enumerate the prime factors of the six residual integers or extend finiteness to several residual prime factors.

The two divisibility conditions are also sufficient under exactly the same hypotheses. For natural a, b, p and e with 4 ≤ a ≤ 6, 2 ≤ b ≤ 3, p prime, 7 < p and 1 ≤ e, put c = 2^a 3^b 5 7. Then 3^(c p^e) is congruent to 2241 modulo that same c p^e if and only if p divides 3^c − 2241 and p^e divides 2241^(p−1) − 1. There is no upper cutoff on p or e and no added assumption that p does not divide 2241.

For the reverse direction, instantiate Mathlib's SModEq.pow_pow_add_one at the ideal generated by p in the integers. The first condition lifts 3^c = 2241 modulo p to (3^c)^(p^e) = 2241^(p^e) modulo p^e. The second condition gives 2241^(p−1) = 1 modulo p^e. Since p^e = 1 modulo p−1, reducing the exponent gives 2241^(p^e) = 2241 modulo p^e. Thus the full power congruence holds modulo the residual prime power.
