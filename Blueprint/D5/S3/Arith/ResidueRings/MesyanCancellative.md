# Units of additive-submonoid quantales of semirings

## Abstract

The cancellative-semiring classification in Mesyan's Question 36 and an elementwise criterion for semirings.

Definition 4 of Mesyan's paper takes all additive submonoids of the semiring. An element belongs to the product of S and T exactly when it is a sum of n terms sᵢtᵢ for some n > 0, with sᵢ in S and tᵢ in T. Mathlib's pointwise product gives this membership condition by induction from single products and addition. Conversely, each such finite sum belongs to the pointwise product by additive closure. The one-term product of two zeros already gives zero. The quantale unit laws quantify over all additive submonoids and preserve the order of the factors. `D5/L/ArithUnits/mesyan2026quantales`.

**Definition 1.1 (Cyclic-preserving multipliers).**

$$\forall (R: Type), [NonUnitalSemiring R], \forall (c: R), c \in C(R) \Leftrightarrow (\forall (x: R), (\exists (m: \mathbb{N}), m \cdot x = c \cdot x) \land (\exists (n: \mathbb{N}), n \cdot x = x \cdot c))$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Arith/ResidueRings/MesyanCancellative.cyclicPreservingMultipliers` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For an associative possibly nonunital semiring R, C(R) is the additive submonoid `cyclicPreservingMultipliers R`. For each x, m · x and n · x denote repeated addition, with independent natural scalars. The cyclic submonoid generated by x contains zero and is closed under addition, so distributivity gives the additive-submonoid laws for C(R).

**Theorem 1.2 (The unique candidate and its local fixing criterion).**

$$\forall (R: Type), [NonUnitalSemiring R], \forall (E: AddSubmonoid R), (\forall (M: AddSubmonoid R), E \cdot M = M \land M \cdot E = M) \Leftrightarrow E = C(R) \land (\forall (x: R), (\exists (l: R), l \in C(R) \land l \cdot x = x) \land (\exists (r: R), r \in C(R) \land x \cdot r = x))$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Arith/ResidueRings/MesyanCancellative.product_unit_iff_eq_cyclicPreservingMultipliers` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Commentary.*

Test the unit laws on the additive submonoid of natural multiples of x. Each multiplier from E acts by a natural multiple on either side. Distributivity collects the finite sum representing x into a single left or right multiplier from E that fixes x.

For c in C(R), choose e in E with ec = c. Cyclic preservation by c makes ec a natural multiple of e, so c belongs to E. Thus E = C(R). Conversely, cyclic preservation keeps every summand of a product with C(R) inside the other additive submonoid. The local fixers give the reverse inclusions on both sides.

**Theorem 1.3 (Classification for additively cancellative semirings).**

$$\forall (R: Type), [NonUnitalSemiring R], [IsLeftCancelAdd R], (\exists (E: AddSubmonoid R), \forall (M: AddSubmonoid R), E \cdot M = M \land M \cdot E = M) \Leftrightarrow (\exists (u: R), \forall (x: R), u \cdot x = x \land x \cdot u = x) \lor ((\forall (a: R), 0 < addOrderOf(a)) \land (\forall (p: Primes), \exists (u: R), (\exists (j: \mathbb{N}), p^{j} \cdot u = 0) \land (\forall (x: R), (\exists (j: \mathbb{N}), p^{j} \cdot x = 0) \Rightarrow (u \cdot x = x \land x \cdot u = x))))$$

*Commentary.*

Suppose a has no additive inverse. Cancellation makes its natural multiples distinct, since equality of distinct multiples would give a positive multiple of a equal to zero. Apply `D5/S3/Arith/ResidueRings/MesyanCancellative.product_unit_iff_eq_cyclicPreservingMultipliers` to obtain a cyclic-preserving left multiplier e fixing a. Since e² is a natural multiple of e, evaluation at a forces e² = e. Left multiplication by e sends a + x to an element with no additive inverse, as that image is a plus the image of x. Idempotence and cyclic preservation then force e to fix a + x, and cancellation shows that e fixes x. The right multiplier argument gives a global right identity, which coincides with the global left identity.

When every element has an additive inverse, choosing those inverses gives a nonunital ring on the same carrier. Its semiring reduct is definitionally the original structure, including its natural scalar action, so `D5/S3/Arith/ResidueRings/MesyanQuantaleClassification.product_unit_iff` applies directly.

A multiplicative identity yields a product unit by taking its natural multiples. In the torsion case, positive additive order supplies an additive inverse for each element and the ring classification gives the converse. The additive order is zero for an element of infinite order. Each prime-primary identity belongs to its own component, consisting of elements killed by some power of the prime. Zero serves as the identity in a zero component. The Lean statement includes the zero semiring, where zero is a two-sided identity. Mesyan uses the term unital semiring for a nonzero semiring with a multiplicative identity. The structural classification of noncancellative semirings remains open here.
