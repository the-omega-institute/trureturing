## Abstract

Exact meaning of the smooth tail constants

**Theorem 1.1 (Floored reciprocal mass).**

$$\forall ps \in \operatorname{List}\left(\mathbb{N}\right),\; \left(\forall p \in \mathbb{N},\; \operatorname{Mem}\left(p, ps\right) \Rightarrow \operatorname{Prime}\left(p\right)\right) \Rightarrow \left(\operatorname{Nodup}\left(ps\right) \Rightarrow \left(\forall X \in \mathbb{N},\; \forall d \in \mathbb{N},\; \left(ps = \operatorname{nil}\left(\right) \Rightarrow \left(1 \le X \lor \operatorname{natDiv}\left(\operatorname{scale}\left(\right), d\right) = 0\right)\right) \Rightarrow \frac{\operatorname{smoothSum}\left(\operatorname{floorReciprocalWeight}\left(\operatorname{scale}\left(\right), d\right), ps, X, 1\right)}{\operatorname{scale}\left(\right)} \le \sum_{m \in \operatorname{factoredMultiples}\left(\operatorname{toFinset}\left(ps\right), X, 1\right)} \frac{1}{d \cdot m}\right)\right)$$

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For distinct primes and natural d, assume for an empty list that X is at least 1 or floor(scale/d)=0. the sum of floor(scale/(d m)) over the enumerated smooth integers, divided by scale, is no greater than their exact sum of 1/(d m). Flooring each term decreases the sum. The notation floorReciprocalWeight(scale,d) is the natural weight m mapped to floor(scale/(d m)). With X=0 a nonempty generator list returns zero, while the empty list retains its start 1; taking d=1 explains the remaining condition. When floor(scale/d)=0 the empty fold already has zero mass, including d=0 and d greater than scale.

**Theorem 1.2 (The finite subtraction is conservative).**

$$\forall p \in \mathbb{N},\; \frac{\operatorname{snd}\left(\operatorname{snd}\left(\operatorname{tailData}\left(p\right)\right)\right)}{\operatorname{scale}\left(\right)} \le \sum_{m \in \operatorname{factoredMultiples}\left(\operatorname{toFinset}\left(\operatorname{filterLe}\left(\operatorname{allPrimes}\left(\right), p\right)\right), \operatorname{natDiv}\left(\operatorname{bound}\left(\right), 2 \cdot p\right), 1\right)} \frac{1}{2 \cdot p \cdot m}$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Arith/Divisors/MakowskiSchinzel/SmoothPredecessor/TailConstants.tail_floor_sum_le` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

The third component of tailData(p), divided by scale, is at most the exact reciprocal mass generated by allPrimes filtered at p, through bound/(2p), weighted by 1/(2p m). The notation smoothUpTo(b,k) denotes the k-smooth integers in the interval from 1 through b. Subtracting the stored value therefore leaves at least as much Euler mass as subtracting the exact finite sum.

**Theorem 1.3 (The finite Euler product ratio).**

$$\forall p \in \mathbb{N},\; \operatorname{eulerProduct}\left(p, 1\right) = \frac{\operatorname{fst}\left(\operatorname{tailData}\left(p\right)\right)}{\operatorname{fst}\left(\operatorname{snd}\left(\operatorname{tailData}\left(p\right)\right)\right)} \Leftrightarrow p \le 100$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Arith/Divisors/MakowskiSchinzel/SmoothPredecessor/TailConstants.tail_euler_ratio` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For p at most 100, the first two components of tailData(p) are products of r and r-1 over the primes at most p. Their ratio is precisely the Euler product at exponent one exactly when p is at most 100. Above 100 the stored ratio remains fixed, whereas the Euler product acquires the strict factor 101/100. This identity and the conservative finite subtraction allow a finite certificate to bound every finite set in the unbounded tail.

**Theorem 1.4 (The remaining reciprocal mass).**

$$\forall p \in \mathbb{N},\; \forall b \in \mathbb{N},\; \forall fs \in \mathbb{N},\; 0 < p \Rightarrow \left(\forall T \in \operatorname{Finset}\left(\mathbb{N}\right),\; \left(\forall q \in \mathbb{N},\; \operatorname{Mem}\left(q, T\right) \Rightarrow \operatorname{SmoothTailSuccessor}\left(p, b, q\right)\right) \Rightarrow \left(\frac{fs}{\operatorname{scale}\left(\right)} \le \sum_{m \in \operatorname{smoothUpTo}\left(b, p + 1\right)} \frac{1}{2 \cdot p \cdot m} \Rightarrow \left(\sum_{q \in T} \frac{1}{q} \le \frac{\operatorname{eulerProduct}\left(p, 1\right)}{2 \cdot p} - \frac{fs}{\operatorname{scale}\left(\right)} \land \left(\operatorname{Nonempty}\left(T\right) \Rightarrow \sum_{q \in T} \frac{1}{q} < \frac{\operatorname{eulerProduct}\left(p, 1\right)}{2 \cdot p} - \frac{fs}{\operatorname{scale}\left(\right)}\right)\right)\right)\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Arith/Divisors/MakowskiSchinzel/SmoothPredecessor/TailConstants.reciprocal_tail_bound` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For p positive, let T be a finite set of successors 2pm+1 whose positive multipliers m exceed b and are (p+1)-smooth. If fs/scale is a lower bound for the reciprocal mass through b, the sum of 1/q over T is at most eulerProduct(p,1)/(2p)-fs/scale, and is strictly smaller when T is nonempty. The finite and tail sets enter the same Euler sum.
