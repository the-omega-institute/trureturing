# BalaA363411

## Abstract

Integer quadratic products and Bala's valuation conjectures.

**Definition 1.1 (The quadratic integer product).**

$$\forall n \in \mathbb{N},\; \operatorname{P}\left(n\right) = \prod_{k \in \operatorname{Finset}.\operatorname{range}\left(n + 1\right)} (\langle1, (k : \mathbb{Z})\rangle : \operatorname{Zsqrtd}\left(-3\right))$$

**Definition 1.5 (Coordinate recursion in a commutative ring).**

$$\forall R \in \operatorname{Type},\; [\operatorname{CommRing}\left(R\right)] (\operatorname{seq}\left(R, 0\right) = (1, 0)) \land (\forall n \in \mathbb{N},\; \operatorname{seq}\left(R, n + 1\right) = ((\operatorname{seq}\left(R, n\right)).1 - 3 \cdot \left((n : R) + 1\right) \cdot (\operatorname{seq}\left(R, n\right)).2, (\operatorname{seq}\left(R, n\right)).2 + \left((n : R) + 1\right) \cdot (\operatorname{seq}\left(R, n\right)).1))$$

*Commentary.*

For any commutative ring R in any universe, seq starts at (1,0). Multiplication by 1+(n+1)sqrt(-3) gives the two displayed successor coordinates.

**Theorem 1.6 (Real coordinates are one modulo three).**

$$\forall I \in \operatorname{Type},\; \forall s \in \operatorname{Finset}\left(I\right),\; \forall f \in I \to \mathbb{Z},\; \exists u \in \mathbb{Z},\; \operatorname{Zsqrtd}.\operatorname{re}\left(\prod_{i \in s} (\langle1, f\left(i\right)\rangle : \operatorname{Zsqrtd}\left(-3\right))\right) = 1 + 3 \cdot u$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Arith/Congruence/BalaA363411.real_one` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For any finite family of integer coefficients, the real coordinate of the product is one modulo three. The ring homomorphism to ZMod 3 sends sqrt(-3) to zero and each factor to one.

**Theorem 1.8 (A scalar block controls every later block).**

$$\forall R \in \operatorname{Type},\; [\operatorname{CommRing}\left(R\right)] \forall q \in \mathbb{N},\; \forall c \in R,\; ((q : R) = 0) \Rightarrow ((\operatorname{seq}\left(R, q\right) = (c, 0)) \Rightarrow (\forall n \in \mathbb{N},\; \operatorname{seq}\left(R, n + q\right) = (c \cdot (\operatorname{seq}\left(R, n\right)).1, c \cdot (\operatorname{seq}\left(R, n\right)).2)))$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Arith/Congruence/BalaA363411.seq_shift` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Acknowledgement.* P. Bala (2023). *OEIS A363411 and A363412 — quadratic integer product coordinates*. URL: <https://oeis.org/A363411>.

*Commentary.*

If q vanishes in R and the qth pair is (c,0), shifting an index by q multiplies both coordinates by c. This holds for every natural index.

$$\forall R \in \operatorname{Type},\; [\operatorname{CommRing}\left(R\right)] \forall q \in \mathbb{N},\; ((q : R) = 0) \Rightarrow ((\operatorname{seq}\left(R, q\right) = (1, 0)) \Rightarrow (\operatorname{Function}.\operatorname{Periodic}\left(\operatorname{seq}\left(R\right), q\right)))$$

**Theorem 1.10 (Reduction of a periodic sequence).**

$$\forall R \in \operatorname{Type},\; [\operatorname{CommRing}\left(R\right)] \forall q \in \mathbb{N},\; (\operatorname{Function}.\operatorname{Periodic}\left(\operatorname{seq}\left(R\right), q\right)) \Rightarrow (\forall n \in \mathbb{N},\; \operatorname{seq}\left(R, n\right) = \operatorname{seq}\left(R, n \bmod q\right))$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Arith/Congruence/BalaA363411.seq_mod` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Commentary.*

A periodic coordinate sequence has the same value at n and at its natural remainder modulo q. The remainder is the Lean natural-number modulo operation.
