# Complete variable-maximum numerical-semigroup realizability

## Abstract

Every numerical semigroup is the defect semigroup of a primitive finite positive jump set, with its maximum chosen existentially.

A numerical semigroup T is an additive submonoid of the natural numbers with finite complement; it includes zero and may equal all natural numbers. Finite(S) is represented by S : Finset ℕ. max(S) denotes S.sup id, and gcd(S) denotes S.gcd id. complement(T) is taken inside ℕ. The brackets closure denote the additive submonoid generated by the displayed set, including zero. Every subtraction is natural subtraction; max(S)=M and positivity make M−s the ordinary nonnegative difference for each s∈S.

**Theorem 1.1 (Every numerical semigroup is realizable).**

$$\forall (T : \operatorname{AddSubmonoid}\left(\mathbb{N}\right)), \operatorname{Finite}\left(\operatorname{complement}\left(T\right)\right) \Rightarrow \exists (M : \mathbb{N}), \exists (S : \operatorname{Finset}\left(\mathbb{N}\right)), (0 < M) \land ((\operatorname{Nonempty}\left(S\right)) \land ((\forall (s : \mathbb{N}), s \in S \Rightarrow 0 < s) \land ((\operatorname{max}\left(S\right) = M) \land ((\operatorname{gcd}\left(S\right) = 1) \land (\operatorname{closure}\left(\operatorname{singleton}\left(M\right) \cup \left\{M - s \mid s \in S\right\} \cup \left\{M + s \mid s \in S\right\}\right) = T)))))$$

*Commentary.*

The source's final discussion asks which numerical semigroups have this form and, separately, which spectral data reconstruct them. This theorem answers the first realizability clause: every numerical semigroup has the required form. The quantifier order is ∀T ∃M ∃S; the maximum is chosen for T, with no prescribed maximum or bound on the number of jumps. The source assumes a finite nonempty positive jump set with gcd one and M=max(S). All those conditions occur in the conclusion. Graph orders n>2M are outside this semigroup statement and can be chosen after M.

Mathlib's Nat.addSubmonoid_fg supplies a finite generating set H for T. Choose a natural upper bound N on the finite complement. Every a>N then lies in T. Set c=N+1, G=H∪{c,c+1}, M=max(G)+1, and S={M}∪{M−a:a∈G}. Both added consecutive generators belong to T, and H⊆G still generates T. Each a∈G is strictly below M, so every jump M−a is positive and at most M; the explicit jump M makes S nonempty with maximum M. The jumps M−c and M−(c+1) differ by one, so their common divisor gcd(S) divides one. Thus gcd(S)=1.

For the forward inclusion, M and all M+s lie above N and belong to T. The minus generators are either zero, when s=M, or an original a∈G, since M−(M−a)=a. For the reverse inclusion, each a∈H is precisely the minus generator associated to s=M−a, so the generated monoid contains T. No properness condition was used. For T=ℕ the complement is empty and the same construction applies, including the two consecutive tail elements; this boundary is part of the universal theorem.

This is an unbounded existence theorem, not finite enumeration or a certified finite instance. The necessary spectral data, reconstruction from smaller congruence families, and classification for a prescribed M remain unresolved. The literature screen is scoped; global novelty and absence of every earlier solution are not established.
