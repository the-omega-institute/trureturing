# Stabilization of exceptional offsets

## Abstract

The positive exceptional offsets stabilize at the gaps of a monoid generated by shifted powers.

**Theorem 1.6 (Offsets and exact-length representations).**

Lean statement: `D5/S3/Arith/Additive/BenfieldLippard/Stabilization.offset_iff_exists_posRep`

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Arith/Additive/BenfieldLippard/Stabilization.offset_iff_exists_posRep` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Acknowledgement.* Brennan Benfield, Oliver Lippard (2025). *Integers that are not the sum of positive powers*. URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08193v2>.

*Commentary.*

Subtract one from every positive kth power in a representation of j+s. The summands now add to the offset s and each is a generator a^k−1. Conversely, any finite sum of generators gives a positive power representation by adding one per summand.

**Theorem 1.7 (The common finite exception set).**

Lean statement: `D5/S3/Arith/Additive/BenfieldLippard/Stabilization.stabilization`

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Arith/Additive/BenfieldLippard/Stabilization.stabilization` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Acknowledgement.* Brennan Benfield, Oliver Lippard (2025). *Integers that are not the sum of positive powers*. URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08193v2>.

*Commentary.*

Padding a representation with ones preserves its offset. The fixed-length positive Waring result gives a reference length j₀ ≥ 1 with finitely many exceptions. For each exception at j₀ that belongs to the offset monoid, choose a witness length at which it has a representation. Set J to the maximum of j₀ and the supremum of these witness lengths, with empty supremum zero. For j ≥ J, pad each of the chosen witnesses with ones to length j. The bound j ≥ j₀ lets us also pad the representations already available at j₀. Thus at every j ≥ J the positive exceptions are precisely the gaps of the monoid.

The reference length matters even if no exceptions disappear from it. For squares, choose j₀ in the stable range. The witness supremum is then zero, yet 6 is an exceptional offset at length 1 because 7 is not a square. The offset 6 belongs to the monoid by the length-2 representation 8 = 2² + 2².

Benfield and Lippard's Theorem 1.1 credits Zenkin [65] with eventual fixed-length cofiniteness. For every k ≥ 2 and each fixed j ≥ G(1,k), all but finitely many positive integers have a representation of length j. Their Conjecture 1.2 gives the stable finite offset formulation, which the padding argument above derives from the formal fixed-length Waring bridge.
