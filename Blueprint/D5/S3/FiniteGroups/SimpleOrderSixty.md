# Recognition of Simple Groups of Order Sixty

## Abstract

Every finite nonabelian simple group of order sixty is multiplicatively equivalent to the alternating group on five letters.

**Definition 1.1 (The multiplicative equivalence).**

$$\forall G \in Typeu,\; Group\left(G\right) \to \left(Finite\left(G\right) \to \left(IsSimpleGroup\left(G\right) \to \left(\left(card\left(G\right) = 60\right) \to \left(Not\left(IsMulCommutative\left(G\right)\right) \to MulEquiv\left(G, alternatingGroup\left(Fin\left(5\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/FiniteGroups/SimpleOrderSixty.smallSimpleOrder60EquivAlt5` (`✓ std3`).

*Citation.* Moonshine-in-Kansas/atlas contributors (2026). *Elementary recognition of a nonabelian simple group of order sixty*. URL: <https://github.com/Moonshine-in-Kansas/atlas/blob/1bc46341457aab119f667c43b6ce7216cbb899b6/Atlas/GroupTheory/SimpleOrderSixty.lean>.

*Commentary.*

The type G and its universe are arbitrary. It carries a group structure, is finite and simple, has cardinality sixty, and is nonabelian. The conclusion is an actual multiplicative equivalence from G to the alternating group on Fin 5. No classifier or prescribed images of generators are assumed. The display gives the function type of the construction: Typeu denotes an arbitrary universe, card is Nat.card, Not is logical negation, and MulEquiv is the type of multiplicative equivalences. The chosen group structure is used throughout.

Simplicity makes every proper subgroup have index at least five: a smaller nontrivial coset action would embed G into a permutation group of order at most twenty-four. The Sylow two-subgroups have order four, and their number is five or fifteen. The Sylow five-subgroups have order five, and there are six of them.

Five Sylow two-subgroups give an index-five normalizer. If there are fifteen and their pairwise intersections are trivial, their forty-five nonidentity elements, together with twenty-four from the Sylow five-subgroups, exceed the order of G. Otherwise two distinct Sylow two-subgroups share a nonidentity element t. Both lie in its centralizer, since groups of order four are commutative. That centralizer is proper because the center of a nonabelian simple group is trivial. Its order is a multiple of four strictly larger than four, divides sixty, and is at most twelve. It therefore has order twelve and index five.

The action on the five cosets is faithful: its normal kernel is trivial or the whole simple group, and the latter would make the coset space a singleton. After choosing five coordinates, compose with the sign homomorphism. A faithful map from a group of order sixty into the two-element sign group is impossible, so the sign is trivial. The coset action thus embeds G into the alternating group on five letters. Both groups have order sixty, making this embedding bijective.

This is the classical finite-group recognition argument recorded in the cited ATLAS software source. A quotient application must supply its own cardinality, simplicity and noncommutativity proofs. The equivalence here does not identify specified generators or establish any geometric realization.

For the concrete central quotient in CFMP section 124, take G = Matrix.SpecialLinearGroup (Fin 2) (ZMod 5) and Q = Matrix.ProjectiveSpecialLinearGroup (Fin 2) (ZMod 5). The latter is definitionally G modulo Subgroup.center G; its projection is QuotientGroup.mk' (Subgroup.center G). Thus the group supplied to smallSimpleOrder60EquivAlt5 is this actual quotient Q. The field is ZMod 5, with primality of five supplying its field instance, and the matrix index type is Fin 2.

Use the original matrices u = [[0,1],[4,1]] and v = [[1,1],[0,1]], both of determinant one. Preserve the words ell = v^(-1) u^2, h = v^2 u, s = u^(-2) v, t = u^(-3) v u and k = v u^3 in this multiplication order. Matrix multiplication over ZMod 5 gives ell = [[0,1],[4,0]], h = [[3,3],[4,1]], uv = ell, ell^2 = -I, h^3 = I, h != I, u^3 = -I and v^5 = I. Since -I != I, ell has order four; h has order three. These are orders in G.

The generating subgroup contains v and ell = uv. Conjugating v^(-1) by ell gives the lower transvection [[1,0],[1,1]]. Powers of v and this conjugate give every upper and lower transvection, since every element of ZMod 5 is the image of an integer. Apply Matrix.SL2.transvection_induction with the predicate of belonging to the subgroup: its multiplication and transvection hypotheses are exactly these subgroup membership facts. Consequently u and v generate all of G, without assuming generation as input.

For K generated by s, t and k, the matrix identities k = -v, k^5 = -I and k^6 = v put v in K. The identity u = (s v^(-1)) k^5 then puts u in K, so the preceding generation argument gives K = G. The generator t is retained in the definition of K even though s and k already suffice for this conclusion.

To establish the cardinality hypothesis, identify G with the kernel of the determinant homomorphism on GL(2,ZMod 5), using Matrix.SpecialLinearGroup.toGL and its injectivity. Matrix.card_GL_field gives (25-1)(25-5) = 480 elements of GL; Matrix.GeneralLinearGroup.det_surjective identifies its quotient by the determinant kernel with the four field units. Subgroup.card_eq_card_quotient_mul_card_subgroup therefore gives |G| = 120, the same 24 times 5 count as the nonzero-first-column argument in the original construction.

Matrix.SpecialLinearGroup.mem_center_iff describes the center by scalar matrices rI with r^2 = 1. Over ZMod 5 these are exactly I and -I, which are distinct, so the center has cardinality two. The same quotient cardinality formula gives |Q| = 60. Matrix.ProjectiveSpecialLinearGroup.rank_two_simple' supplies simplicity with the field witness a = 2: a != 0 and a^2 != 1. Noncommutativity is also checked on this quotient: (uv)^(-1)(vu) equals neither I nor -I, so the images of u and v do not commute. Finiteness, cardinality sixty, simplicity and noncommutativity are thus supplied for the exact carrier Q.

Applying Atlas.GroupTheory.smallSimpleOrder60EquivAlt5 to Q now constructs Q multiplicatively equivalent to alternatingGroup (Fin 5). This is the unmarked group identification asserted in section 124. It does not choose images of u and v. An identification of this quotient pairing with the displayed section 123 pair ((345),(13524)) would require a separate simultaneous generator correspondence, preserving the reversal between function composition and right actions.

The application establishes matrix relations, generation, K = G and the central quotient group identification. Group orders and element orders alone do not construct the actual face pairings, edge first returns, link charts or normal fan quotients. Actual edge degrees, the full surface and its genus, local ramification and a twelve/nine hyperbolic metric require their corresponding geometric arguments. In particular the six/nine golden lengths are not asserted to solve the twelve/nine metric equations. This is an application of existing algebraic results, with no claim of a new group theorem or geometric completion.

**Theorem 1.2 (An index-five subgroup).**

$$\forall G \in Typeu,\; \left(\left(\left(\left(Group\left(G\right) \land Finite\left(G\right)\right) \land IsSimpleGroup\left(G\right)\right) \land card\left(G\right) = 60\right) \land Not\left(IsMulCommutative\left(G\right)\right)\right) \Rightarrow \left(\exists H \in Subgroup\left(G\right),\; index\left(H\right) = 5\right)$$
