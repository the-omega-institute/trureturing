# Weighted semilinear fixed sets

## Abstract

Relative norms determine the complete weighted fixed sets, including zero.

**Theorem 1.1 (The complete one-coordinate fixed set).**

*Commentary.*

Let F be a finite field, K a finite extension, sigma an F-automorphism, and E the native intermediate field fixed by the cyclic subgroup generated by sigma. The equation sigma(x) = a x has card(E) solutions when the actual relative norm of a is one; otherwise its only solution is zero.

The native subgroup-to-Galois equivalence identifies sigma as a generator of Gal(K/E). Native Hilbert 90 applied to the inverse of a respects its y/sigma(y) orientation and supplies a nonzero solution y. Multiplication by y is an explicit equivalence from E to the entire solution set. Conversely norm invariance forces norm(a)=1 for every nonzero solution. Failure therefore gives a singleton, not an empty fixed set.

**Theorem 1.2 (Independent coordinates and the order of the norm).**

*Commentary.*

For any finite coordinate type I and natural weights w, the vector fixed equations form a dependent product of coordinate solution sets. If u is the actual norm of c and m its multiplicative order, the norm condition at coordinate i is exactly m divides w(i). The fixed set therefore has card(E) raised to a_m, where a_m counts all original coordinates whose weights are divisible by m.

Every good coordinate may still vanish; every bad coordinate is forced to zero. This retains every partial support. An empty coordinate type gives the empty product one, and a zero weight passes every divisibility test. The identity automorphism has fixed field K and identity norm, so it is included without an exception. The coordinate universe is independent of the field universe.
