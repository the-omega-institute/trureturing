# Perfect restricted bases of size s + 1

## Abstract

Every finite abelian group with a perfect restricted basis of size s + 1 is cyclic.

**Definition 1.1 (Perfect restricted s-bases).**

$$\forall G \in Type*,\; [\operatorname{AddCommGroup}\left(G\right)] [\operatorname{DecidableEq}\left(G\right)] \forall s \in \mathbb{N},\; \forall A \in \operatorname{Finset}\left(G\right),\; (\operatorname{IsPerfectRestrictedBasis}\left(s, A\right)) \Leftrightarrow (\forall g \in G,\; \operatorname{ExistsUnique}\left((T: \operatorname{Finset}\left(G\right)), (T \subseteq A) \land ((Finset.card\left(T\right) \le s) \land (\sum_{x \in T} x = g))\right))$$

*Citation.* B. Bajnok, C. Berson and H. A. Just (2022). *On Perfect Bases in Finite Abelian Groups*. DOI: [10.2140/involve.2022.15.525](https://doi.org/10.2140/involve.2022.15.525). URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13675v1>.

*Commentary.*

The paper states (abstract, p. 1): "A is a perfect restricted s-basis of G if each element of G can be written uniquely as the sum of at most s distinct elements of A." The Lean predicate uses a finset T of distinct elements, requires T ⊆ A and T.card ≤ s, and records the sum over T. The empty finset supplies the empty sum.

**Definition 1.2 (The cyclic conclusion for Question 6).**

$$(\operatorname{claim}\left(\right)) \Leftrightarrow (\forall G \in Type,\; [\operatorname{AddCommGroup}\left(G\right)] [\operatorname{Fintype}\left(G\right)] [\operatorname{DecidableEq}\left(G\right)] \forall s \in \mathbb{N},\; \forall A \in \operatorname{Finset}\left(G\right),\; (1 \le s) \Rightarrow ((Finset.card\left(A\right) = s+1) \Rightarrow ((\operatorname{IsPerfectRestrictedBasis}\left(s, A\right)) \Rightarrow (\operatorname{IsAddCyclic}\left(G\right)))))$$

*Commentary.*

Question 6 (p. 8) asks: "Are there any noncyclic groups that have a perfect restricted s-basis of size s + 1?" The displayed proposition answers the question for finite abelian groups: positive s, a basis with cardinality s + 1, and the perfect restricted basis condition imply IsAddCyclic.

**Theorem 1.3 (Question 6 has a negative answer).**

$$\forall G \in Type,\; [\operatorname{AddCommGroup}\left(G\right)] [\operatorname{Fintype}\left(G\right)] [\operatorname{DecidableEq}\left(G\right)] \forall s \in \mathbb{N},\; \forall A \in \operatorname{Finset}\left(G\right),\; (1 \le s) \Rightarrow ((Finset.card\left(A\right) = s+1) \Rightarrow ((\operatorname{IsPerfectRestrictedBasis}\left(s, A\right)) \Rightarrow (\operatorname{IsAddCyclic}\left(G\right))))$$

*Commentary.*

The proof first identifies the admissible subsets with the proper subsets of A, giving the odd cardinality 2 raised to A.card minus 1 for G. For each a in A, the map that toggles a is a fixed-point-free involution on every selected coset of the subgroup generated by a, so each such fiber has even cardinality. Lagrange's divisibility and the proper-subset count make the subgroup cardinality odd; excluding the coset containing the full subset forces that subgroup to be all of G.
