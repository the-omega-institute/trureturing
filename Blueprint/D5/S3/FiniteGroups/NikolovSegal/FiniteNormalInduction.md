# Minimal normal subgroups and smaller quotients

## Abstract

Finite-order induction uses actual minimal nontrivial normal subgroups and coordinatewise quotient lifts.

**Lemma 1.1 (A minimal nontrivial normal subgroup exists).**

Lean statement: `D5/S3/FiniteGroups/NikolovSegal/FiniteNormalInduction.exists_minimal_normal`

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/FiniteGroups/NikolovSegal/FiniteNormalInduction.exists_minimal_normal` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Every finite nontrivial group G has a subgroup N minimal for the predicate that N is normal and nontrivial. Minimality is among normal subgroups, not among all subgroups. The finite set of such subgroups is nonempty because it contains G.

**Lemma 1.2 (The quotient has strictly smaller order).**

Lean statement: `D5/S3/FiniteGroups/NikolovSegal/FiniteNormalInduction.card_quotient_lt`

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/FiniteGroups/NikolovSegal/FiniteNormalInduction.card_quotient_lt` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For every finite group G and nontrivial normal subgroup N, Nat.card(G/N) is strictly less than Nat.card(G). The subgroup has order greater than one; quotient order is positive; their product equals the order of G.

**Lemma 1.3 (Coordinate lifts generate modulo the kernel).**

Lean statement: `D5/S3/FiniteGroups/NikolovSegal/FiniteNormalInduction.lift_tuple_generation`

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/FiniteGroups/NikolovSegal/FiniteNormalInduction.lift_tuple_generation` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For any group G, normal subgroup N and natural d, let x and y be tuples from Fin d to G and xq and yq tuples from Fin d to G/N. If the quotient map sends x(i) to xq(i) and y(i) to yq(i) for every i, and the two quotient tuple ranges generate G/N, then N joined with the subgroup generated by both lifted ranges is G. Finiteness and rank hypotheses are unnecessary for this statement.
