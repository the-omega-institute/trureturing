# Orbit Relations, Centralizers, and Cyclic Normalizers

## Abstract

On any type, the chosen-step centralizer and the cyclic normalizer are contained in the complete orbit-relation automorphism subgroup. The cyclic normalizer equals that subgroup exactly when t squared is one, or when t squared is a single nontrivial cycle and t sixth power is one.

For an arbitrary type X and chosen step t, orbitAut is the subgroup of permutations preserving and reflecting SameCycle for t. It is the complete relation stabilizer. Preserving the relation allows exchanging its orbits; fixing every invariant subset individually requires each orbit union to be fixed as a set, while normalizing the cyclic subgroup generated by t requires conjugation to preserve that subgroup. These are different definitions, but their subgroups can coincide in degenerate or involutive cases; no universal strict inequality is claimed. There is no finiteness restriction.

**Definition 1.1 (The full orbit-relation automorphism subgroup).**

$$\forall X \in Typeu,\; \forall t \in \operatorname{Perm}\left(X\right),\; \operatorname{Subgroup}\left(\operatorname{Perm}\left(X\right)\right)$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/FiniteGroups/Partitions/OrbitSymmetryDynamics.orbitAut` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

orbitAut t has type Subgroup (Perm X) and is defined as SuccessorQuotient.relationAut t.SameCycle, so both preservation and reflection of the complete orbit relation are required.

**Theorem 1.2 (The chosen-step centralizer preserves orbit structure).**

$$\forall X \in Typeu,\; \forall t \in \operatorname{Perm}\left(X\right),\; \operatorname{centralizer}\left(\left\{t\right\}\right) \subseteq \operatorname{orbitAut}\left(t\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/FiniteGroups/Partitions/OrbitSymmetryDynamics.centralizer_le_orbitAut` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Every permutation commuting with the chosen generator t preserves and reflects SameCycle. This is a centralizer of the selected step, not merely a normalizer of its cyclic subgroup.

**Theorem 1.3 (The complete orbit relation determines the step exactly for involutions).**

$$\forall X \in Typeu,\; \forall t \in \operatorname{Perm}\left(X\right),\; \left(\forall s \in \operatorname{Perm}\left(X\right),\; \left(\forall x \in X,\; \forall y \in X,\; \operatorname{SameCycle}\left(s, x, y\right) \Leftrightarrow \operatorname{SameCycle}\left(t, x, y\right)\right) \Rightarrow s = t\right) \Leftrightarrow t^{2} = 1$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/FiniteGroups/Partitions/OrbitSymmetryDynamics.orbit_relation_determines_iff` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

If every permutation with the same complete orbit relation equals t, the inverse t inverse has the same relation and forces t squared to be one. Conversely, when t is involutive, each orbit is a singleton or a two-point orbit, so relation agreement determines the image of every point. Empty and infinite X, identity permutations, singleton orbits and two-point orbits all remain in scope.

**Theorem 1.4 (Centralizer and full orbit stabilizer coincide exactly in the involutive case).**

$$\forall X \in Typeu,\; \forall t \in \operatorname{Perm}\left(X\right),\; \operatorname{centralizer}\left(\left\{t\right\}\right) = \operatorname{orbitAut}\left(t\right) \Leftrightarrow t^{2} = 1$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/FiniteGroups/Partitions/OrbitSymmetryDynamics.centralizer_eq_orbitAut_iff` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Necessity: the within-orbit swap of x and t x lies in orbitAut; equality with the centralizer makes this swap commute with t, forcing t squared x = x for every x. Sufficiency: for t squared = 1 and p in orbitAut, p*t*p inverse has the same complete orbit relation as t, so reconstruction gives p*t*p inverse = t and hence commutation. This differs from the uniqueness theorem's necessity argument using t inverse. Empty and infinite types and fixed points remain in scope. Invariant sets on arbitrary X are exactly orbit unions; fixing every invariant subset individually fixes each such subset as a set. Finite invariant subsets alone do not recover infinite orbits.

**Theorem 1.5 (The cyclic normalizer preserves the complete orbit relation).**

$$\forall X \in Typeu,\; \forall t \in \operatorname{Perm}\left(X\right),\; \operatorname{Subgroup}.\operatorname{normalizer}\left(\operatorname{Subgroup}.\operatorname{zpowers}\left(t\right)\right) \subseteq \operatorname{orbitAut}\left(t\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/FiniteGroups/Partitions/OrbitSymmetryDynamics.normalizer_le_orbitAut` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For every arbitrary type X and permutation t, the subgroup normalizer of the actual integer-power set Subgroup.zpowers t is contained in orbitAut t. Conjugating any power by a normalizer element produces another integer power, so the same global power witness transports every SameCycle relation in both directions.

**Theorem 1.6 (Sharp arbitrary-type cyclic-normalizer classification).**

$$\forall X \in Typeu,\; \forall t \in \operatorname{Perm}\left(X\right),\; \operatorname{Subgroup}.\operatorname{normalizer}\left(\operatorname{Subgroup}.\operatorname{zpowers}\left(t\right)\right) = \operatorname{orbitAut}\left(t\right) \Leftrightarrow \left(t^{2} = 1 \lor \left(\operatorname{IsCycle}\left(t^{2}\right) \land t^{6} = 1\right)\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/FiniteGroups/Partitions/OrbitSymmetryDynamics.normalizer_eq_orbitAut_iff` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

The equality holds exactly when t is involutive, or when the square t squared is a literal Mathlib IsCycle and t sixth power is one. A within-orbit transposition forces one common integer exponent; its period cases are 1, 2, or 3, and the same global exponent rules out two distinct three-orbits. The non-involutive branch therefore has one three-orbit, characterized precisely by IsCycle(t squared) together with t sixth power equal to one. For sufficiency, an orbitAut permutation preserves the three-orbit and its complement explicitly; restricting both t and the permutation to the complement gives the existing involution centralizer theorem before the two orientations are combined globally. Permutations may exchange two-point orbits, and infinitely many such orbits are allowed.

For the arithmetic step t = (finRotate n)^a with n > 0, the existing residue chart identifies the physical zero-family subgroup with the full orbit-relation stabilizer. The exact compiled application gives centralizer equality precisely when n divides 2a, including a = 0 and n = 1. The same arithmetic transport and the sharp cyclic-normalizer theorem give equality between the cyclic normalizer of that step and the zero-family subgroup exactly when n divides 2a or n equals 3. For n at least 2, the remaining branch requires 2a coprime to n and n dividing 6a, which force n = 3. This includes the non-involutive triangular case while retaining a = 0 and n = 1. Thus zero-subset observations forget the cyclic direction and retain only the orbit partition; the arithmetic transport uses ambient conjugation by residueChart rather than an abstract zeroFamilyEquiv.
