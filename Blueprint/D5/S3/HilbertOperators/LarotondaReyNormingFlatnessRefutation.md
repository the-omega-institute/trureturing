# Norming commutation does not imply local commutator-line flatness

## Abstract

Central norming matrices coexist with strict norm increase on every nonzero commutator-line step.

**Definition 1.1 (Real trace duality).**

$$\forall \left(n: \mathbb{N}\right), \left(\forall \left(N: \operatorname{Matrix}\left(\operatorname{Fin}\left(n\right), \operatorname{Fin}\left(n\right), \mathbb{C}\right)\right), \left(\forall \left(Y: \operatorname{Matrix}\left(\operatorname{Fin}\left(n\right), \operatorname{Fin}\left(n\right), \mathbb{C}\right)\right), \left(\operatorname{D5.S3.HilbertOperators.LarotondaReyNormingFlatnessRefutation.pairing}\left(N, Y\right) = -\left(\operatorname{Complex.re}\left(\operatorname{Matrix.trace}\left(\left(N\right) \cdot \left(Y\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)\right)$$

*Citation.* Gabriel Larotonda and Iván Rey (2023). *Weakly invariant norms: Geometry of spheres in the space of skew-Hermitian matrices*. DOI: [10.1016/j.laa.2023.08.023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2023.08.023). URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05957v1>.

*Commentary.*

On page 4, the trace-duality remark states: “We say that φ ∈ 𝔲ₙ′ is a unit norming functional of V ∈ 𝔲ₙ if φ(V) = ‖V‖ and ‖φ‖ = 1.” The real part of −Matrix.trace (N * Y) gives that real pairing on skew-adjoint matrices.

The polar supremum is taken over 0 ≤ q ≤ 1 of ((1−q²)|tr(Y)/2| + 2q‖Y−(tr(Y)/2)I‖)/(1+q²+q). The matrix norm in this expression is the Euclidean operator norm, supplied by Matrix.Norms.L2Operator. The proof establishes nondegeneracy, the triangle inequality, absolute real homogeneity and unitary conjugation invariance.

Take V = diag(3i/2,i/2) and X = [[0,1],[−1,0]]. The norm of V is one. For any traceless skew-adjoint W the norm at V+eW is at most 1+e²‖W‖². Consequently each norming functional at V annihilates every traceless skew direction, so its representing matrix is central and commutes with every codiagonal part.

Along V+s[X,V] the central coordinate stays i, while the squared operator norm of the traceless part is 1/4+s². Every nonzero s therefore strictly increases the polar supremum. Choosing s = δ/2 contradicts any positive local flatness radius.
