# Odd-resonance Poisson nonisomorphism

## Abstract

Nonisomorphism of the two resonant real Poisson coordinate algebras for all odd exponents.

Gahramanov and Mammadov, arXiv:2611.17457v1, Section 5.3, page 5, write: “For m and n both odd no such rescaling exists, and we leave open whether the two algebras are isomorphic by a nonlinear change of generators.” Equations (16) and (21) present the full real polynomial coordinate ring. In the presentation below u, v, x and y are H₁, H₂, X and Y, respectively.

$$
\operatorname{Paper}\left(n, m\right) = \mathrm{Real}[u,v,x,y]/\langle x^{2} + y^{2} - u^{n} \cdot v^{m} \rangle
$$

Paper(n,m) is the literal ideal quotient of the real multivariate polynomial ring in four variables by the principal ideal generated by the displayed relation. The names u, v, x, y also denote the four quotient classes. For positive exponents, paperPoisson(n,m,omega,s) is the signed bracket construction of Omega.PaisPoisson, with the positivity proofs implicit in the notation. Its bracket field is a skew biderivation whose Hamiltonians ham are real derivations in the second argument. It includes a proof of cyclic Jacobi. Use s=1 for the canonical bracket in (23) and s=-1 for the alternative bracket in (26) and (27). In the ordered pairs (u,v), (u,x), (v,x), (u,y), (v,y), (x,y) the signed generator values are as follows.

$$
\operatorname{ham}\left(\operatorname{bracket}\left(\operatorname{paperPoisson}\left(n, m, omega, s\right)\right), u, v\right) = 0
$$

$$
\operatorname{ham}\left(\operatorname{bracket}\left(\operatorname{paperPoisson}\left(n, m, omega, s\right)\right), u, x\right) = omega \cdot y
$$

$$
\operatorname{ham}\left(\operatorname{bracket}\left(\operatorname{paperPoisson}\left(n, m, omega, s\right)\right), v, x\right) = omega \cdot s \cdot y
$$

$$
\operatorname{ham}\left(\operatorname{bracket}\left(\operatorname{paperPoisson}\left(n, m, omega, s\right)\right), u, y\right) = -(omega \cdot x)
$$

$$
\operatorname{ham}\left(\operatorname{bracket}\left(\operatorname{paperPoisson}\left(n, m, omega, s\right)\right), v, y\right) = -(omega \cdot s \cdot x)
$$

$$
\operatorname{ham}\left(\operatorname{bracket}\left(\operatorname{paperPoisson}\left(n, m, omega, s\right)\right), x, y\right) = -(\frac{omega}{2}) \cdot u^{n - 1} \cdot v^{m - 1} \cdot (n \cdot v + s \cdot m \cdot u)
$$

The signed polynomial biderivation is constructed by wedging the derivative in the u and s-weighted v directions with the rotating derivative y times the x derivative minus x times the y derivative, then adding the mixed x,y wedge. The defining relation is central. Therefore the principal ideal is preserved in both arguments and the operation descends for arbitrary representatives. The cyclic Jacobiator is a derivation in each argument, so checking all generator triples proves it vanishes on the polynomial algebra and then on the quotient. Skew symmetry transfers real linearity and the Leibniz rule to the first argument. The table determines the bracket on all elements by these derivation laws.

**Theorem 1.1 (Nonisomorphism for all odd exponents).**

$$\forall n \in \mathrm{Nat},\; \forall m \in \mathrm{Nat},\; \operatorname{Odd}\left(n\right) \Rightarrow \left(\operatorname{Odd}\left(m\right) \Rightarrow \left(\forall omega \in \mathrm{Real},\; omega \ne 0 \Rightarrow \neg (\exists e \in \operatorname{AlgEquiv}\left(\mathrm{Real}, \operatorname{Paper}\left(n, m\right), \operatorname{Paper}\left(n, m\right)\right),\; \forall f \in \operatorname{Paper}\left(n, m\right),\; \forall g \in \operatorname{Paper}\left(n, m\right),\; e\left(\operatorname{ham}\left(\operatorname{bracket}\left(\operatorname{paperPoisson}\left(n, m, omega, -(1)\right)\right), f, g\right)\right) = \operatorname{ham}\left(\operatorname{bracket}\left(\operatorname{paperPoisson}\left(n, m, omega, 1\right)\right), e\left(f\right), e\left(g\right)\right))\right)\right)$$

*Commentary.*

Every odd natural n and m is positive. For every nonzero real omega, there is no unital real algebra equivalence from the alternative bracket to the canonical bracket preserving the bracket on all f,g. Here AlgEquiv(Real, A, A) denotes arbitrary real algebra equivalences of A, and ham denotes the full signed bracket from the construction. The inverse of a bracket-preserving algebra equivalence preserves the opposite brackets, giving nonisomorphism in either direction. The registered case of coprime m>n≥1 and omega>0 follows by specialization.

Put c=u-v and B=Real[c,v,x]. In the quadratic algebra over B with y²=(c+v)ⁿvᵐ-x² every element has unique form P+Qy. The coordinate-ring equivalence and its inverse send the original generators to their corresponding classes, as checked by generator extensionality. Transport the signed brackets and a hypothetical real algebra equivalence through this equivalence.

In the canonical bracket, commuting with u gives the two coefficient equations ∂P/∂x=0 and ((c+v)ⁿvᵐ-x²)∂Q/∂x-xQ=0. If Q is nonzero of degree d in x, the coefficient of x^(d+1) in the second equation is -(d+1) times its leading coefficient, a contradiction over the characteristic-zero coefficient domain. Thus Q=0 and P is a polynomial in c,v. Commuting with x forces the v derivative at fixed c to vanish. Hence every canonical central element is a polynomial in u-v.

The alternative central element u+v has one real zero character. On its zero fiber v=-u, odd exponents give x²+y²=-u^(n+m), forcing u=v=x=y=0. Every real fiber of c in the canonical ring has two distinct real characters: for nonzero c evaluate at (c,0,0,0) and (0,-c,0,0), and for c=0 evaluate at (0,0,0,0) and (1,1,1,0). These evaluations satisfy the relation. Real characters are all unital real algebra maps to Real, uniquely determined by the four generator values.

A hypothetical isomorphism e carries u+v to a polynomial in c. Precomposing the alternative origin with e inverse gives a canonical character on a fiber where that polynomial vanishes. Both distinct characters of the same fiber then vanish on the polynomial. Precomposition with e turns them into zero characters of u+v, so both equal the origin. Surjectivity of e contradicts their distinctness. The proof uses the full presented algebra, including real characters with negative sector values. Equation (30) concerns the further semialgebraic physical domain of nonnegative sector energies. Isomorphisms of the same polynomial Poisson algebra that also preserve this domain are excluded as a special case. The theorem makes no claim about Poisson structures on a separate restricted smooth space.
