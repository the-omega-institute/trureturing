# Homogeneous equations and exact compact-base correspondence.

## Abstract

Homogeneous equations and exact compact-base correspondence.

For a normalization u(b)=1 and mutually inverse kernel coordinates J and A, affine substitution is represented by an actual polynomial with total degree no greater than the original. Homogenization is the sum over k of u raised to D-k times the k-th homogeneous component evaluated on the linear projection A, where D is the affine total degree. It is homogeneous of degree D and has its exact section value on b+J(v). A nonzero affine polynomial therefore gives a nonzero homogenization of exactly the same total degree. A nonzero homogeneous polynomial has nonzero restriction: normalization covers u(x) nonzero, and polynomial continuation along x+t b covers u(x)=0. This argument includes the zero-dimensional affine chart. For a positive-dimensional compact base with nonempty boundary, every nonzero boundary equation has positive degree. Its homogenization vanishes on the entire cone boundary, including the vertex. Consequently a homogeneous minimum cone equation restricts to a nonzero minimum affine equation of exactly the same total degree. Principality is a separate assertion.

Starting instead with an arbitrary nonzero, possibly nonhomogeneous polynomial vanishing on the entire boundary of the original closed salient cone in a nonzero real carrier, one can extract a nonzero homogeneous component q of positive degree d no greater than the original total degree, and q still vanishes on the entire boundary. Positive dilations are actual cone homeomorphisms and preserve the entire frontier. For each original boundary point y, the radial polynomial with coefficient k equal to the value of the k-th homogeneous component at c(y) vanishes for every positive scalar. It has infinitely many roots and is therefore zero, so each component vanishes at y. The sum of the components is the original nonzero polynomial, hence one component is nonzero. Finally the vertex belongs to the entire frontier: if zero were interior, positive rescaling would put every vector into the cone, contradicting salience. Vanishing at this same vertex excludes degree zero for the selected component. Selecting minimum degree first and then extracting a nonzero component gives an actual homogeneous minimum equation. Such a minimum equation is squarefree: if a squared nonunit factor divided it, deleting one copy would preserve vanishing at every boundary point and strictly reduce total degree. This establishes actual homogeneous squarefree minimum equations. General principality, and the finite-degree semialgebraic boundary prerequisite used to prove it in the source, are separate obligations.
