# Uniform local solutions and global pasting

## Abstract

A uniform two-sided local solution time yields one solution on all real time.

*Commentary.*

Let E be any real normed vector space, f any map from E to E, and T a positive real number. Suppose that for every state x there is a curve C from the real numbers to E with C(0)=x and derivative f(C(s)) for every s in the open interval (-T,T). The same T must work for every state. Then for every initial state x0 there is one curve gamma on the whole real time axis with gamma(0)=x0 and derivative f(gamma(t)) at every real t.

Choose a local curve at each state and extend an existing curve at both endpoints of an expanding central interval. Preserve the old curve on that closed interval. At a joining time both one-sided derivatives equal the field evaluated at the common endpoint, so the enlarged curve remains a solution. Successive changes occur outside expanding central intervals. They form a locally finite family, yielding a locally eventual curve whose derivative and initial value agree with a sufficiently late stage.

The theorem requires neither completeness nor finite dimension of E, continuity of f, or uniqueness of local solutions. It assumes local solution witnesses with a common positive time interval. Obtaining those witnesses for a particular field, proving uniqueness, and analyzing asymptotic behavior require separate arguments.

Fix the finite ideal triangulation of a compact connected orientable three-manifold with nonempty boundary, whose components are closed surfaces of genus at least two. Write Q for its tetrahedra and use its actual face pairing p. The carrier G is p.GlobalEdge, the equivalence quotient generated by the paired face-edge slots; P(q,j)=p.edgeLabel(q,j). Occurrences are Q times Fin(6), in the order (12,13,14,34,24,23), with opposite pairs (0,3), (1,4), and (2,5). Self-gluing and repeated representatives are allowed. The finite set fiber(p,e) contains every occurrence with label e, and d(e) is its cardinality. No coloring or degree restriction is needed.

For a positive length vector l on G, use the native coordinates coordinates(p,l,q,j)=cosh(l(P(q,j))) and angle(p,l,(q,j)) from FlatComponentBudget. The latter is exactly TwoLengthAngleSum's extendedAngle: arccos(max(-1,min(rawCos(x,j),1))), where rawCos uses the original six-variable cosine and the source frame of vertex relabelings. Thus every local slot reads the same global length vector. Define K(l,e)=2*pi-sum over o in fiber(p,e) of angle(p,l,o), and B(y,e)=K(exp(y),e), with exponential applied coordinatewise to y in the entire real space R^G.

This actual B is continuous on all of R^G. Each cosh(exp(y(e))) is strictly greater than one. TwoLengthAngleSum.rad_positive makes both native square-root denominators strictly positive, so the original rational cosine is continuous. Minimum, maximum and arccos preserve continuity, including the flat clipped-angle seams. The finite occurrence sums and the finite product then give continuity of B. This uses no lower bound on the positive lengths.

Every extended angle lies in [0,pi]. Consequently 0 <= sum over fiber(p,e) of angle <= d(e)*pi and abs(B(y,e)) <= (2+d(e))*pi for every y and e. In the finite product supremum norm one may therefore take the uniform bound M=(2+card(Q times Fin(6)))*pi: norm(B(y)) <= M for all y. The bound counts actual occurrences, including multiple local representatives of the same global edge. Empty finite carriers also satisfy the same norm argument.

For every center a in R^G apply QuantitativePeano's IsPeanoODE.exists_eq_forall_mem_Icc_eq_integral to the autonomous field f(t,x)=B(x), initial time zero, interval [-1,1], radius M and speed bound M. Its cylinder inequality is M*max(1,1)<=M. Joint continuity follows from continuity of B, and the global bound holds on every such cylinder. The resulting integral equation C(s)=a+integral from 0 to s of B(C(u)) has a continuous integrand on [-1,1]. The fundamental theorem of calculus gives C'(s)=B(C(s)) at every s in (-1,1), including zero. Hence the common positive local time is T=1, independent of a. Peano's finite-dimensional integral existence is the adopted result described in Library/Geometry/rolfes2026peano.md; no Lipschitz or uniqueness hypothesis is introduced.

Apply exists_global_solution_of_uniform_local to this same B with T=1. For every positive initial vector l0 it gives one curve y on all real times, y(0)=log(l0) and y'(t)=B(y(t)) for every real t. Define l(t,e)=exp(y(t,e)) using that very curve. Then l(t,e)>0, l(0,e)=l0(e), and l'_e(t)=l(t,e)*K(l(t),e) for every real t and every e, by the scalar exponential chain rule. The initial-value identity uses positivity of l0 and exp(log(l0(e)))=l0(e). All joining times and zero are included in the global derivative statement; the local interval endpoints -1 and 1 are not required.

The analytic argument holds for every finite raw face pairing, hence for the actual pairing of the fixed manifold triangulation above. It does not assert that an arbitrary raw pairing is such a manifold or certify its links. The flow remains in the positive log chart, including arbitrarily small positive initial lengths and all flat extended-angle configurations. Zero initial lengths are outside that chart. No uniqueness, invariance of the genuine hyperideal domain, volume identity, convergence or geometric completion is a consequence of this application.
