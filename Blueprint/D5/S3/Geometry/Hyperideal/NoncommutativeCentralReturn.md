# Central low return in a generated group

## Abstract

A central low return word forces a group generated by the marked pair to be commutative.

**Theorem 1.1 (Central low return implies commutativity).**

*Commentary.*

Let u and v generate G. If every element of the generated subgroup commutes with the low return word v inverse times u squared, then u commutes with that word. The identity v = u squared times the inverse low return word then gives Commute(u,v). Since the two marked generators commute pairwise, their generated subgroup is commutative; the generation hypothesis makes every pair in G commute.

The conclusion applies to arbitrary groups and does not assume finiteness, faithfulness of an action, or a geometric realization. In a noncommutative group generated by u and v, the low return word therefore cannot be central.
