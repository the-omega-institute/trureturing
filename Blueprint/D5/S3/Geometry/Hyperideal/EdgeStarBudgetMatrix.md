# Edge-star budget matrices

## Abstract

Actual edge-star angle budgets from directed face-signature transitions.

Number the six local edges (12,13,14,34,24,23). A global edge is a class of local edge occurrences under the face pairing. Each occurrence contributes once, even when a tetrahedron or a global edge appears repeatedly around its normal circle. The Boolean face signature is false for one low edge and true for two.

Write beta2=arccos(70/99), beta3=arccos(49sqrt(6)/198), beta4=arccos(17/33). Write theta0=arccos(31/33), theta1=arccos(25/sqrt(726)), and theta2=arccos(81/88). Rows and columns are ordered by face counts one, then two. The low matrix is [beta4,beta3; beta3,beta2], and the high matrix is [theta2,theta1; theta1,theta0].

*Commentary.*

The oriented successor preserves the global edge. For a low global edge, the sum of beta angles indexed by each occurrence's four neighbouring colors equals the low matrix weighted by its actual directed transition counts. For a high edge, the corresponding theta sum equals the high matrix weighted by those same counts. In each case the local sum exceeds 2pi exactly when its matrix sum does.

The two incident faces of a low occurrence each contain its target low edge; together their low counts determine whether it has two, three, or four high neighbours. For a high occurrence they determine whether it has zero, one, or two. Face-label preservation carries the outgoing count to the next incoming count. Reindexing every actual occurrence by its ordered pair of signatures gives the matrix sum without identifying repeated occurrences.

The quantified raw face pairing defines global edges as equivalence classes generated by paired face-edge slots. Two local occurrences lie in the same class exactly when the oriented successor puts them in one cycle. Its involutive face pairing, inverse slot maps, and valid coloring supply the oriented edge-star structure through the face-paired triangulation. These matrix identities are conservative angle budgets; a geometric realization also uses a common global length vector and the covolume argument.
