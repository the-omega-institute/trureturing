# Actual cosine-integral high-pass operators

## Abstract

The positive cosine-integral kernel is the actual L2 limit of the finite cutoffs. Its ordinary convolution and original Gaussian weighted integral operator inherit the high-pass bound.

Write q(c,x)=2 Ci(c|x|), where Ci is the existing sine-tail cosine integral. The totalized origin value is immaterial in Lebesgue L2 and in the Gaussian product measure.$\operatorname{q}\left(c, x\right) = 2 \cdot \operatorname{Ci}\left(c \cdot \left|x\right|\right)$`D5/S3/Fourier/Asymptotics/CosineCutoffKernel/Highpass.highpassKernel``D5/S3/Fourier/Asymptotics/CosineIntegralLattice.cosineIntegral`

*Commentary.*

For every c>0, q(c) is measurable and belongs to real Lebesgue L2. For every N>=c and every MemLp witness for the actual kernel(c,N), the squared L2 error is 4*pi/N. Every filter of real upper cutoffs tending to infinity gives L2 convergence, without requiring a nontrivial filter.

The finite interval cancellation uses the existing positive Euler normalization. Away from x=0, kernel(c,N,x)=q(c,x)-q(N,x). The exact Gram integral at the scale N gives the squared error.
