# Support and Robust Returns of Torus Observations

## Abstract

Vanishing errors preserve bounded returns to every open window meeting a continuous torus observation image.

**Theorem 1.1 (Full image support, bounded waiting and positive lower density).**

$$\forall I \in \mathrm{FiniteTypes}, g \in \mathbb{T}^{I},\; G:=\overline{\langle g\rangle}, \mu:= \operatorname{HaarProbability}\left(G\right): \forall h \in \operatorname{C}\left(G, \mathbb{R}\right), e \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\; \nu:= \operatorname{map}\left(h, \mu\right): \lim_{n\to\infty} (e_{n}-\operatorname{h}\left(g^{n}\right))=0\implies (\operatorname{supp}\left(\nu\right)= \operatorname{h}\left(G\right)\land \forall u,v\in \mathbb{R}, (\exists z\in G: u<\operatorname{h}\left(z\right)<v)\implies \exists n_{0},M\in \mathbb{N}: n_{0}\geq1\land (\forall n\in \mathbb{N}, n\geq n_{0}\implies \exists m\in \mathbb{N}: n\leq m\leq n+M\land e_{m}\in (u,v))\land (\forall \varepsilon>0, \exists N_{0}\in \mathbb{N}, \forall N\in \mathbb{N}, N\geq N_{0}\implies \frac{1}{M+1}-\varepsilon\leq \frac{A_{(u,v)}(N)}{N}))$$

*Commentary.*

Let I be any finite type, T the complex unit circle, and g an element of T^I. Let G be the closure of the cyclic subgroup generated by g and let mu be its Haar probability measure. The observable h is any continuous real function on G, and e is any real sequence whose difference from h(g^n) tends to zero. Write nu for the pushforward of mu by h. For J=(u,v), let A_J(N) count the integers m with 0 <= m < N and e_m in J.

The support of nu is exactly h(G). For every open interval J meeting this image, the same integers n_0 >= 1 and M >= 0 give both conclusions: each interval of integers from n to n+M contains a hit whenever n >= n_0, and the lower asymptotic density of the hits is at least 1/(M+1). The last displayed clause states this density bound as an eventual epsilon inequality. The counting ratio lies in [0,1], so this is equivalent to the usual liminf inequality. Counting from 1 instead of 0 changes a ratio by at most 1/N and gives the same lower density.

Compactness makes h(G) closed. An open neighborhood of any point h(z) has a nonempty open preimage, which has positive Haar measure. The complement of h(G) has empty preimage. These two facts identify the support, including atomic pushforward measures.

Choose z with h(z) in J and a positive margin delta so that the interval from h(z)-2 delta to h(z)+2 delta lies inside J. The inverse image U of the smaller interval with radius delta is nonempty and open. Nonnegative powers are dense in the actual closed subgroup, so every translate x g^k enters U for some k >= 0. The corresponding open preimages cover G. A finite subcover supplies a maximum time M, valid for every starting point. Eventually the perturbation has absolute value less than delta, preserving each selected hit in J.

Put L=M+1 and divide the integers from n_0 onward into disjoint blocks of length L. Choose one hit per block. The chosen times increase strictly, hence the first floor((N-n_0)/L) complete blocks give that many distinct hits below N when N >= n_0. It follows that N <= n_0 + L(A_J(N)+1). Dividing by N L and letting N grow proves the claimed eventual epsilon inequalities.

The statement includes empty I, finite-order generators, proper or disconnected subgroups, constant observables, and zero or nonzero vanishing perturbations. It requires neither ambient density nor atom-free thresholds, and assumes no rate of convergence. The waiting bound depends on the generator, observable and window; no bound uniform over these parameters is asserted. The cited torus equidistribution exposition supplies background on orbit closures; the support and finite-cover argument here is a synthesis of standard compact-group and counting methods.
