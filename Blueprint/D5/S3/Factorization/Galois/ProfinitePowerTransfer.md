# ProfinitePowerTransfer

## Abstract

Continuous finite quotients transfer uniform width to profinite groups.

**Definition 1.1 (Bounded algebraic generators).**

$$\operatorname{GeneratedByAtMost}(G, d) \iff \exists (S: \operatorname{Finset}(G)), \operatorname{Card}(S) \leq d \land \operatorname{SubgroupClosure}(S) = \operatorname{TopSubgroup}(G)$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Factorization/Galois/ProfinitePowerTransfer.GeneratedByAtMost` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

A finite set of at most d elements generates the whole group algebraically. The carrier is arbitrary; the finite-group premise appears in the uniform width proposition.

**Definition 1.2 (Uniform width in finite groups).**

$$\operatorname{FinitePowerWidth}(d, m, w) \iff \forall (Q: Type), {\operatorname{Group}(Q) \land \operatorname{Finite}(Q)} \Rightarrow {{\operatorname{GeneratedByAtMost}(Q, d)} \Rightarrow {\operatorname{HasPowerWidth}(Q, m, w)}}$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Factorization/Galois/ProfinitePowerTransfer.FinitePowerWidth` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For fixed d, m and w, every finite group generated by at most d elements has ordered mth-power width at most w. This definition supplies no proof of that uniform finite-group result.

**Theorem 1.3 (Powers descend along homomorphisms).**

$$\forall (f: \operatorname{GroupHom}(G, Q)), \forall (g: G), {g \in \operatorname{powerSubgroup}(G, m)} \Rightarrow {\operatorname{f}(g) \in \operatorname{powerSubgroup}(Q, m)}$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/Galois/ProfinitePowerTransfer.map_mem_powerSubgroup` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

A group homomorphism maps the generated power subgroup into the corresponding power subgroup of its target.

**Theorem 1.4 (Ordered products commute with homomorphisms).**

$$\forall (f: \operatorname{GroupHom}(G, Q)), \forall (a: \operatorname{Functions}(\operatorname{Fin}(w), G)), \operatorname{f}(\operatorname{powerProduct}(m, w, a)) = \operatorname{powerProduct}(m, w, i \mapsto \operatorname{f}(\operatorname{a}(i)))$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/Galois/ProfinitePowerTransfer.map_powerProduct` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

A homomorphism carries an ordered product of powers to the same ordered product of the images.

**Theorem 1.5 (Dense generators in finite quotients).**

$${\operatorname{CompactTopologicalGroup}(G)} \Rightarrow {{\operatorname{TopologicalClosure}(\operatorname{SubgroupClosure}(S)) = \operatorname{TopSubgroup}(G)} \Rightarrow {\forall (N: \operatorname{OpenNormalSubgroup}(G)), \operatorname{GeneratedByAtMost}(\operatorname{Quotient}(G, N), \operatorname{Card}(S))}}$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/Galois/ProfinitePowerTransfer.finite_quotient_generated` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For a compact topological group with a finite set S generating a dense subgroup, the image of S generates each quotient by an open normal subgroup algebraically, with at most S.card generators. These quotient maps are continuous and their targets are finite and discrete.

**Theorem 1.6 (Closed-set membership detected by finite quotients).**

$${\operatorname{CompactTotallyDisconnectedTopologicalGroup}(G)} \Rightarrow {{\operatorname{IsClosed}(K)} \Rightarrow {{\forall (N: \operatorname{OpenNormalSubgroup}(G)), \exists (k: K), \operatorname{QuotientMap}(N, g) = \operatorname{QuotientMap}(N, k)} \Rightarrow {g \in K}}}$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/Galois/ProfinitePowerTransfer.mem_closed_of_finite_quotients` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

In a compact totally disconnected topological group, an element lies in a closed set K whenever every quotient by an open normal subgroup identifies it with some element of K. The representatives may depend on the quotient. Open normal neighbourhoods supply separation.

**Theorem 1.7 (Uniform finite width transfers to the group).**

$${\operatorname{CompactTotallyDisconnectedTopologicalGroup}(G)} \Rightarrow {{\operatorname{TopologicalClosure}(\operatorname{SubgroupClosure}(S)) = \operatorname{TopSubgroup}(G)} \Rightarrow {{\operatorname{FinitePowerWidth}(\operatorname{Card}(S), m, w)} \Rightarrow {\operatorname{HasPowerWidth}(G, m, w)}}}$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/Galois/ProfinitePowerTransfer.profinite_hasPowerWidth_of_finite` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For a compact totally disconnected topological group with dense generators S, a uniform width bound for all finite groups generated by at most S.card elements gives that same ordered width in the group. Compactness closes the product image; continuous finite quotients detect membership. Hausdorff separation follows from the stated group and disconnectedness assumptions.

**Theorem 1.8 (Closed power subgroup from finite width).**

$${\operatorname{CompactTotallyDisconnectedTopologicalGroup}(G)} \Rightarrow {{\operatorname{TopologicalClosure}(\operatorname{SubgroupClosure}(S)) = \operatorname{TopSubgroup}(G)} \Rightarrow {{\operatorname{FinitePowerWidth}(\operatorname{Card}(S), m, w)} \Rightarrow {\operatorname{IsClosed}(\operatorname{powerSubgroup}(G, m))}}}$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/Galois/ProfinitePowerTransfer.isClosed_powerSubgroup_of_finite_width` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Under the preceding compactness, disconnectedness and dense-generation assumptions, uniform width in finite groups implies closedness of the power subgroup.
