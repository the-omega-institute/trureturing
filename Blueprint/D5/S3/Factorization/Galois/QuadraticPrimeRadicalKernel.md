# Prime Radical Fields: Rational Square Kernel and Degree

## Abstract

The actual complex field generated by a finite set of prime square roots has the expected rational square kernel and degree, without an independence assumption.

**Definition 1.1 (The actual intermediate field).**

$$\forall S \in \operatorname{Finset}(\mathbb{N}), \operatorname{E}(S) = \operatorname{adjoin}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\{\sqrt{p} \mid p \in S\}) \subseteq \mathbb{C}$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Factorization/Galois/QuadraticPrimeRadicalKernel.primeRadicalField` (`✓ std3`).

*Citation.* J. S. Milne (2022). *Fields and Galois Theory, version 5.10*. URL: <https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/FT.pdf>.

*Commentary.*

For every finite set S of natural numbers, E(S) is IntermediateField.adjoin over the rationals inside the complex numbers, generated by the complex images of the nonnegative real square roots of the members of S. The theorems below require every member of S to be prime.

**Lemma 1.2 (The prime-product obstruction used by the induction).**

$$\forall S \in \operatorname{Finset}(\mathbb{N}), (\forall p \in S, \operatorname{Prime}(p)) \implies \forall p \in \mathbb{N}, (\operatorname{Prime}(p) \land \neg(p \in S)) \implies \forall T \in \operatorname{Finset}(\mathbb{N}), T \subseteq S \implies \neg\operatorname{IsSquare}(p \cdot \prod_{q \in T}q)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/Galois/QuadraticPrimeRadicalKernel.prime_mul_subset_not_square` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Acknowledgement.* J. S. Milne (2022). *Fields and Galois Theory, version 5.10*. URL: <https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/FT.pdf>.

*Commentary.*

If S consists of primes, p is a prime outside S and T is a subset of S, then p times the product of T is not a rational square. Rational nonsquareness is reduced to natural-number nonsquareness and prime divisibility. This obstruction is used by the simultaneous induction and the full original application.

**Theorem 1.3 (Exactly which rational numbers become squares).**

$$\forall S \in \operatorname{Finset}(\mathbb{N}), (\forall p \in S, \operatorname{Prime}(p)) \implies \forall a \in \mathbb{Q}, \operatorname{IsSquare}(\operatorname{algebraMap}(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{E}(S),a)) \iff \exists T \in \operatorname{Finset}(\mathbb{N}), T \subseteq S \land \operatorname{IsSquare}(a \cdot \prod_{p \in T}p)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/Galois/QuadraticPrimeRadicalKernel.prime_radical_square_kernel` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Acknowledgement.* J. S. Milne (2022). *Fields and Galois Theory, version 5.10*. URL: <https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/FT.pdf>.

*Commentary.*

The scalar a ranges over all rational numbers, including zero and negative values. The empty support and the prime two are included. IsSquare means existence of an element whose product with itself is the specified scalar. The statement neither assumes square-class independence nor replaces E(S) by an abstract quadratic algebra.

The simultaneous induction uses quadratic coordinate descent: for nonzero d in characteristic different from two, a base scalar is a square in the quadratic model precisely when a or a*d is a base square. Prime divisibility shows that a new prime times any product of distinct earlier primes is not a rational square. A lift into the complex numbers then proves that the model's field range is exactly the enlarged actual adjoin.

**Theorem 1.4 (Finite dimensionality and exact degree).**

$$\forall S \in \operatorname{Finset}(\mathbb{N}), (\forall p \in S, \operatorname{Prime}(p)) \implies \operatorname{FiniteDimensional}(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{E}(S)) \land [\operatorname{E}(S):\mathbb{Q}] = 2^{\lvert S \rvert}$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/Galois/QuadraticPrimeRadicalKernel.prime_radical_degree` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Acknowledgement.* J. S. Milne (2022). *Fields and Galois Theory, version 5.10*. URL: <https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/FT.pdf>.

*Commentary.*

For every finite prime support, the actual field is finite dimensional over the rationals and has degree two to the cardinality of the support. The empty support gives degree one. Each insertion uses the square-kernel induction to establish nonsquareness in the entire earlier field; the proved field-range equivalence transports the quadratic rank and the tower formula multiplies the degrees.

## Arithmetic application: the actual field and its signs

The displayed Lean declarations retain their square-kernel and degree statements. The application below composes those results with the existing normality, ring-of-integers, inertia, residue-Frobenius and ideal-factorization interfaces; it describes their mathematical consequences, rather than an additional public theorem exported by this module. No square-class independence assumption is added.

Let S be any finite set of genuine rational primes, including the empty set and sets containing 2. Use the same actual intermediate field E(S) inside C. For p in S let beta_p be its positive real square root, viewed in E(S), and let b_p be the integral representative in O(E(S)) with underlying element beta_p. Both square to p and are nonzero. The beta_p generate E(S) over Q, and prime_radical_degree gives [E(S):Q] = 2^|S|. Each singleton adjoin is quadratic and normal; their compositum is normal, characteristic zero supplies separability, and finite degree supplies the number-field structure. Thus E(S)/Q is Galois.

Every embedding phi:E(S)->C sends each beta_p to beta_p or -beta_p, since it preserves its square p. These images are real, and generation makes every phi(x) real; hence E(S) is totally real. For G = Gal(E(S)/Q), define sign_p(sigma) in the integer units {1,-1} by sigma(beta_p) = sign_p(sigma)*beta_p. The resulting homomorphism G -> (S -> Z-units) is faithful by generation. The Galois automorphism count and the degree theorem give both groups cardinality 2^|S|, so the sign homomorphism is also surjective. Consequently G is commutative and every sigma satisfies sigma^2 = 1. When S is empty these statements give Q, degree one and the trivial sign group.

## Odd primes: actual residues, inertia and arithmetic Frobenius

For every odd rational prime q outside S, write R = O(Q), A = O(E(S)) and e:R->Z for the actual ring equivalence Rat.ringOfIntegersEquiv. Define q_0 = comap(e,(q)) in R, with (q) in Z the principal ideal generated by q. This is a nonzero prime ideal, and the quotient equivalence R/q_0 = ZMod(q) gives cardinality q. For every actual prime ideal P of A lying over q_0, the induced quotient map R/q_0 -> A/P is injective. The upper residue field A/P is finite and has characteristic q; these are the actual quotients of the actual rings of integers.

An inertia element acts on beta_p by one of its two signs. If it acted negatively, its residue identity on b_p would give 2*b_p in P. Primality puts either 2 or b_p in P; squaring b_p and contracting to Z would then give q dividing 2 or p. The assumptions q odd and q outside S exclude both cases. Thus inertia fixes every radical and, by generation, is trivial. The existing inertia-cardinality and ramification-index identities give ramification index one at every P, hence Algebra.IsUnramifiedAt R P and unramifiedness over q_0.

Let F_P(q) be the actual arithmetic Frobenius at P, with residue action x -> x^q. Its residue equation on b_p, together with beta_p^2 = p and Euler's criterion, gives the residue of (p/q)*b_p, where (p/q) is the Legendre symbol. Since q does not divide p, this symbol is 1 or -1. Comparing it with the two possible signs of F_P(q)(beta_p), a mismatched sign would give 2 times the residue of b_p equal to zero. Odd characteristic and q outside S exclude this. Therefore F_P(q)(beta_p) = (p/q)*beta_p for every p in S. These values determine the automorphism on its generators, so F_P(q) is independent of the upper prime P. Write this common actual automorphism as F(q); its sign_p coordinate is the integer Legendre symbol (p/q).

## Complete splitting as an exhaustive product of actual ideals

For every such q, F(q) = 1 is equivalent to the following literal splitting statement. There exists a finite set U of ideals of A with |U| = 2^|S|, whose membership is exactly P in U iff P is prime and lies over q_0. Thus U lists all upper primes, without repetition. Every P in U has |A/P| = q. All three ideal equalities hold: map(R->A,q_0) = product(P in U,P); map(Z->A,(q)) = product(P in U,P); and the principal ideal (q) in A = product(P in U,P). Here map denotes ideal extension along the indicated algebra map, and the product is the ideal product.

To derive the forward direction, use the existing Frobenius-order/residue-degree identity underlying Chebotarev.frobenius_order_stabilizer: trivial Frobenius gives residue degree one. Unramifiedness gives ramification index one, and the Galois e*f*g formula with prime_radical_degree gives exactly 2^|S| primes above q_0. The existing primesOverFinset supplies the exhaustive finite set and its membership characterization. The ideal-factorization formula has exponent one at each member, while the residue norm formula gives each quotient cardinality q. Transport through e and preservation of principal ideals identify the extensions of q_0 and of the integer ideal (q) with the principal ideal (q) in A, proving all three equalities.

Conversely, the stipulated quotient cardinality q at any upper prime, together with the injective map from the base residue field of cardinality q, makes that map bijective. Its linear equivalence gives residue degree one; the same Frobenius-order identity then gives F(q) = 1. Upper-prime independence transfers this conclusion to every upper prime. Faithfulness of signs also identifies F(q) = 1 with (p/q) = 1 for every p in S.

## The original weighted Lucas-block Frobenius application

Fix j >= 2. Set B_i = abs(L_(3^i)^2 + 3), with L the same goldenLucas sequence, and let S be the union of the prime supports of B_i over every 1 <= i < j. This is the full earlier support used above, including primes at even depths. Let T be the finite subtype of prime factors of B_j, and take F(q) in the same Galois group Gal(E(S)/Q) to be the actual upper-prime-independent Frobenius just constructed, for each q in T. The later support T is nonempty. The existing block prime-data proof at GoldenCubicBlockNativePowerPeriods gives q > 5 and the exact equality e(q) = v_q(Fib(fibonacciRank(q))) = v_q(B_j) > 0. The pairwise block coprimality in cubic_block_native_power_periods gives q outside S. These are the genuine positive Fibonacci depths, with no depth-one substitution.

Keep the primewise condition D as an external hypothesis: for every p in S, product(q in T,(p/q)^e(q)) = 1 in the integers. For these identical S, T, F and e, the Frobenius calculation gives sign_p(F(q)) = (p/q). Substitute this integer equality separately under each power and use product congruence. Since signs form a homomorphism, D makes every sign coordinate of product(q in T,F(q)^e(q)) equal to one. Faithfulness then gives the weighted Galois equality product(q in T,F(q)^e(q)) = 1.

If Q in T has odd depth e(Q) and every other q in T has even depth, exponent two makes F(q)^e(q) = 1 for q different from Q and F(Q)^e(Q) = F(Q). The identical weighted product therefore reduces to F(Q), so D implies F(Q) = 1. The full splitting equivalence above applies to Q in this same field E(S): it gives the exhaustive set of 2^|S| distinct actual upper ideals, quotient cardinality Q at every member, and all three ideal product equalities. This implication retains its external D and its unique-odd-depth hypotheses.
