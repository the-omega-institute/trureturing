# Homomorphism Filtered Triple Census

## Abstract

Three-element sets on which an additive homomorphism is injective have exact cyclic fixed families.

Let G and A be abelian groups and let f be an additive homomorphism from G to A. All configurations below are actual unordered finite subsets of G. The codomain may be infinite, and f may have a nontrivial kernel. Injectivity is required only on each individual configuration.

**Definition 1.1 (The filtered family).**

$$\forall G,A: \operatorname{Type}\left(\right), [\operatorname{AddCommGroup}\left(G\right)] [\operatorname{AddCommGroup}\left(A\right)] {}\forall f: G \to A, \operatorname{Additive}\left(f\right) \implies \forall S: \operatorname{Finset}\left(G\right), \operatorname{IsFilteredTriple}\left(f, S\right) \iff \operatorname{card}\left(S\right) = 3 \land \operatorname{InjOn}\left(f, S\right)$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Factorization/HomomorphismFilteredTripleCensus.IsFilteredTriple` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Write V(f) for this family, represented by the subtype Triple(f). For a direction t, F(f,t) is the subset of V(f) whose point sets obey t+S=S. The additive action on Triple(f) translates precisely its underlying finite set. Translation preserves cardinality. The identity f(t+x)=f(t)+f(x) and cancellation show that it also preserves injectivity on the set. This action is defined for every abelian G and A, without any finiteness assumption.

**Definition 1.2 (The three multiples).**

$$\forall G: \operatorname{Type}\left(\right), [\operatorname{AddCommGroup}\left(G\right)] \forall t \in G, \operatorname{C}\left(t\right) = \{0,t,2t\}$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Factorization/HomomorphismFilteredTripleCensus.cycle` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

C(t) is the image of the natural indices zero, one and two under multiplication by t. This definition imposes no order assumption. Under the eligibility conditions in the next theorem, it has three distinct elements and is a subgroup. The ambient translation stabilizer supplies its additive subgroup structure.

**Theorem 1.3 (Exact fixed family and stabilizer).**

$$\begin{aligned}\forall G,A: \operatorname{Type}\left(\right), [\operatorname{AddCommGroup}\left(G\right)] [\operatorname{AddCommGroup}\left(A\right)] {}\forall f: G \to A, \operatorname{Additive}\left(f\right) \implies \forall t \in G, t \neq 0 \implies\\{}[\forall S \in \operatorname{V}\left(f\right), t+S = S \implies 3t = 0 \land \operatorname{f}\left(t\right) \neq 0] \land\\{}[\neg(3t = 0 \land \operatorname{f}\left(t\right) \neq 0) \implies \operatorname{F}\left(f, t\right) = \emptyset] \land\\{}[3t = 0 \land \operatorname{f}\left(t\right) \neq 0 \implies \exists c \in \operatorname{V}\left(f\right), c = \operatorname{C}\left(t\right) \land\\(\forall S \in \operatorname{V}\left(f\right), t+S = S \iff \exists p \in G, p+c = S) \land\\(\forall u \in G, u \in \operatorname{Stab}\left(G, c\right) \iff u \in c) \land\\(\forall p,q \in G, p+c = q+c \iff p-q \in c) \land\\\operatorname{card}\left(\operatorname{Stab}\left(G, c\right)\right) = 3]\end{aligned}$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/HomomorphismFilteredTripleCensus.fixed_family_structure` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For a fixed set S, summing its three elements before and after translation gives three times t equal to zero. If f(t)=0, translating any point of S produces a different point with the same image, since t is nonzero. This contradicts injectivity and proves both necessity and emptiness of the excluded families.

Conversely, suppose three times t is zero and f(t) is nonzero. Both t and f(t) have additive order three, by the prime-order criterion. Their first three multiples are distinct, so C(t) is a member of V(f). Translation by t cyclically permutes these multiples. If t fixes another triple S and p belongs to S, repeated invariance places every element of p+C(t) in S. Their equal cardinality three gives equality. Every translate of C(t) is conversely fixed.

A translation stabilizing C(t) belongs to C(t), because it translates zero to itself as an element of the translated set. Each multiple of t stabilizes C(t), proving the converse. Thus its stabilizer is exactly C(t), has cardinality three, and is the subgroup generated by t. Cancellation of translates gives the exact fiber criterion p+C(t)=q+C(t) precisely when p-q belongs to C(t). The fixed family is therefore the single translation orbit consisting of these cosets. All structural assertions hold even for infinite G.

**Theorem 1.4 (The finite-domain census).**

$$\forall G,A: \operatorname{Type}\left(\right), [\operatorname{AddCommGroup}\left(G\right)] [\operatorname{AddCommGroup}\left(A\right)] [\operatorname{Finite}\left(G\right)] \forall f: G \to A, \operatorname{Additive}\left(f\right) \implies \forall t \in G, t \neq 0 \implies \operatorname{card}\left(\operatorname{F}\left(f, t\right)\right) = \begin{cases}\left\lfloor\frac{\operatorname{card}\left(G\right)}{3}\right\rfloor&3t = 0 \land \operatorname{f}\left(t\right) \neq 0\\0&\neg(3t = 0 \land \operatorname{f}\left(t\right) \neq 0)\end{cases}$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/HomomorphismFilteredTripleCensus.fixed_point_census` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Only G is now assumed finite. The fixed family in the eligible case is the orbit just classified. The supplied orbit-stabilizer equivalence identifies its product with the three-element stabilizer with G, giving the quotient cardinality. In the excluded case the same actual fixed family is empty. The floor denotes natural Euclidean division; eligibility ensures divisibility by three. No finiteness of A or global injectivity of f is used. The restriction t nonzero is essential: identity translation fixes every member of V(f).

Multiplication on a residue ring.

For every natural modulus n, including zero, multiplication by a residue a is an additive homomorphism from ZMod(n) to itself. Taking f(x)=ax identifies V(f) with the slope-filtered abscissa sets. Translation preservation consequently holds for all n. For positive n, the domain has cardinality n, so each nonzero direction has count n divided by three exactly when three times t is zero and at is nonzero, and has count zero otherwise. A unit slope is unnecessary.

For n=6, a=2 and t=2, the fixed sets are exactly the even and odd cosets {0,2,4} and {1,3,5}, giving count two. For n=3, a=1 and t=1, the sole fixed set is {0,1,2}, giving count one. For modulus six, the pairs (a,t)=(3,2) and (2,1) give zero. For modulus nine, (a,t)=(2,3) gives three and (3,3) gives zero. For modulus three, (a,t)=(0,1) gives zero and (1,0) gives one; the latter uses the sole three-element subset rather than the nonidentity census. For modulus two, (a,t)=(1,1) gives zero. Modulus one has no nonzero direction.

An infinite codomain.

Take G=ZMod(3), A=ZMod(3) times the integers, and f(x)=(x,0). The codomain is infinite. The only three-element subset of G is all of G; f is injective on it and every translation fixes it. In particular t=1 has precisely this one fixed member. The census applies with the original infinite codomain.

First-coordinate filtering on the three-by-three group.

Take G=ZMod(3) times ZMod(3) and f(x,y)=x. All eight nonzero directions have order three. The six directions with nonzero first coordinate survive the kernel condition; each fixes three triples. The two nonzero kernel directions (0,1) and (0,2) fix none.

$D = \{(1,0),(1,1),(1,2),(2,0),(2,1),(2,2)\}$

Pairing each direction with its double gives the following three distinct subgroups.

$\begin{aligned}H_{0} = \{0,(1,0),(2,0)\}\\H_{1} = \{0,(1,1),(2,2)\}\\H_{2} = \{0,(1,2),(2,1)\}\end{aligned}$

The directions (1,0) and (2,0) have the cosets of H(0) as their common fixed family; (1,1) and (2,2) give H(1); (1,2) and (2,1) give H(2). For each of the three subgroups, its full coset family is exactly the following three representatives:

$\forall i \in \{0,1,2\}, \operatorname{Cosets}\left(H_{i}\right) = \{H_{i},(0,1)+H_{i},(0,2)+H_{i}\}$

Translation acts transitively on each coset family, so each is one orbit of size three. The three families are pairwise disjoint: a coset has the stabilizer of its subgroup, while H(0), H(1) and H(2) are distinct. Horizontal triples in the H(0) family are included because only the first-coordinate map is filtered.
