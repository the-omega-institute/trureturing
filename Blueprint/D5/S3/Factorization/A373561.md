# Divisor-Bucketed Quadruple Sum

## Abstract

A divisor condition sorts the summands into classes rather than restricting them, leaving a square-sum identity.

Indices run over the interval from one to n, which includes both endpoints. The summand is an integer and may be zero or negative; the divisor is taken of its absolute value. Sums are over finite index sets, so no convergence question arises.

**Definition 1.1 (The summand).**

$$\operatorname{f}\left(x, y, z\right) = {x}^{2} + {y}^{2} - {z}^{2}$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Factorization/A373561.f` (`✓ std3`).

*Citation.* OEIS Foundation Inc. (2024). *OEIS A373561*. URL: <https://oeis.org/A373561>.

*Commentary.*

Two positive squares against one negative square. The value is an integer rather than a natural number precisely so that the negative case is not silently truncated.

**Theorem 1.2 (The condition sorts rather than restricts).**

$$\forall n \in \mathbb{N}, \sum_{k \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} \sum_{z \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} \sum_{y \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} \sum_{x \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} [\operatorname{gcd}\left(\lvert\operatorname{f}\left(x, y, z\right)\rvert, n\right) = k] \operatorname{f}\left(x, y, z\right) = \sum_{z \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} \sum_{y \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} \sum_{x \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} \operatorname{f}\left(x, y, z\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/A373561.gcd_buckets_eq_sum` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

For a positive n the divisor of any summand with n lies between one and n, so each triple falls into exactly one class of the outer index. Summing over all classes therefore returns the threefold sum unchanged. A vanishing summand is not an exception: the divisor of zero with n is n, which is still a legitimate class. At n equal to zero every index set is empty and both sides vanish.

**Theorem 1.3 (The threefold sum).**

$$\forall n \in \mathbb{N}, \sum_{z \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} \sum_{y \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} \sum_{x \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} \operatorname{f}\left(x, y, z\right) = {n}^{2} \cdot \sum_{x \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} {x}^{2}$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/A373561.triple_sum_eq` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Each of the two positive squares contributes a full square sum scaled by the square of the interval length, and the negative square cancels one of them, leaving that square times a single square sum.

**Lemma 1.4 (The two layers composed).**

$$\forall n \in \mathbb{N}, \sum_{k \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} \sum_{z \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} \sum_{y \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} \sum_{x \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} [\operatorname{gcd}\left(\lvert\operatorname{f}\left(x, y, z\right)\rvert, n\right) = k] \operatorname{f}\left(x, y, z\right) = {n}^{2} \cdot \sum_{x \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} {x}^{2}$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/A373561.a373561_core` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Nothing new is proved here; it records the composition that the closed form is then read off from.

**Theorem 1.5 (The conjecture).**

$$\forall n \in \mathbb{N}, \sum_{k \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} \sum_{z \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} \sum_{y \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} \sum_{x \in \operatorname{Icc}\left(1, n\right)} [\operatorname{gcd}\left(\lvert\operatorname{f}\left(x, y, z\right)\rvert, n\right) = k] \operatorname{f}\left(x, y, z\right) = \frac{{n}^{3} \cdot {n + 1} \cdot {2 \cdot n + 1}}{6}$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/A373561.a373561` (`✓ std3`). ∎
