**Theorem 1.3 (The portrait reconstructs the principal fractional ideal).**

$$\forall R, K: Type, CommRing(R) \land Field(K) \land Algebra(R, K) \land IsFractionRing(R, K) \land IsDedekindDomain(R) \Rightarrow \forall x \in K, x \neq 0 \Rightarrow (x) = \prod_{p \in HeightOneSpectrum(R)} p^{nu(R, K, x, p)}.$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/Embeddings/FiniteValuationPortraitObjectLayer.principal_fractional_ideal_reconstruction` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Mathlib's Dedekind factorization theorem is applied directly to the nonzero principal fractional ideal generated by x.

**Theorem 1.4 (The reconstruction formula must exclude zero).**

$$(0) \neq \prod_{p \in HeightOneSpectrum(\mathbb{Z})} p^{nu(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Q}, 0, p)}.$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/Embeddings/FiniteValuationPortraitObjectLayer.reconstruction_nonzero_hypothesis_is_necessary` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Commentary.*

**Theorem 1.5 (Equal portraits are exactly base-unit ratios).**

$$\forall R, K: Type, CommRing(R) \land Field(K) \land Algebra(R, K) \land IsFractionRing(R, K) \land IsDedekindDomain(R) \Rightarrow \forall x, y \in K, x \neq 0 \land y \neq 0 \Rightarrow (nu(R, K, x) = nu(R, K, y) \iff IsBaseUnitRatio(R, K, x, y)).$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/Embeddings/FiniteValuationPortraitObjectLayer.finite_valuation_portrait_eq_iff_base_unit_ratio` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Prime-valuation faithfulness first identifies the two principal fractional ideals. Equality of singleton spans is then exactly multiplication by a unit of the base Dedekind domain.

$$(nu(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Q}, 0) = nu(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Q}, 1) \land \neg IsBaseUnitRatio(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Q}, 0, 1)) \land (nu(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Q}, 1) = nu(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Q}, 0) \land \neg IsBaseUnitRatio(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Q}, 1, 0)).$$

*Commentary.*

The totalized count assigns the zero fractional ideal the same all-zero portrait as one, but their quotient is not a base-ring unit.

**Theorem 1.10 (A composite-modulus readout is not faithful).**

$$v(4, 1) = v(4, 2) \land \neg IsBaseUnitRatio(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Q}, 1, 2).$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/Embeddings/FiniteValuationPortraitObjectLayer.composite_readout_is_not_faithful` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Commentary.*
