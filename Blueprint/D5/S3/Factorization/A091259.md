# Reduced Numerators Modulo Three

## Abstract

The reduction can consume whatever it likes: if every divisor on the denominator side is one modulo three, the reduced numerator is determined anyway.

Arguments are positive naturals. The quadratic below is the one whose roots are the primitive cube roots of unity, written out rather than taken from a cyclotomic library so the arithmetic stays elementary.

**Theorem 1.1 (Reduction without tracking).**

$$\forall a, b, A, B \in \mathbb{N}, (0 < b \land (\forall d \mid B, d \bmod 3 = 1) \land a \cdot B = b \cdot A) \Rightarrow \frac{a}{\operatorname{gcd}\left(a, b\right)} \bmod 3 = A \bmod 3$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/A091259.reduced_numerator_mod_three` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

This is the step the printed material does not supply. Cancelling a fraction could consume a factor of three or leave one behind, and following that through the exponents is delicate. It is also unnecessary: after cancelling, the two cofactors are coprime, so the denominator cofactor divides the other side's denominator, and the remaining factor divides it too. Every divisor there being one modulo three fixes the answer regardless of what was cancelled.

**Definition 1.2 (The quadratic).**

$$\forall t \in \mathbb{N}, \operatorname{cyclotomicThree}\left(t\right) = {t}^{2} + t + 1$$

*Formalization.* `D5/S3/Factorization/A091259.cyclotomicThree` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

Its values at prime powers carry the whole local behaviour.

**Theorem 1.3 (The local classification).**

$$\forall p, e \in \mathbb{N}, \operatorname{localNumerator}\left(p, e\right) \bmod 3 = (0 \mathrm{if} p \bmod 3 = 2 \land \mathrm{odd} e, 1 \mathrm{otherwise})$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/A091259.localNumerator_mod` (`✓ std3`). ∎

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Commentary.*

A prime congruent to two contributes zero at odd exponents and one at even ones; the prime three contributes one throughout; a prime congruent to one contributes a single factor of three on each side and the quotients are one. The exponent parity is what the indicator on the other side is reading.

**Theorem 1.4 (The conjecture).**

$$\forall n \in \mathbb{N}, 0 < n \Rightarrow \frac{\operatorname{sigma}\left(3, n\right)}{\operatorname{gcd}\left(\operatorname{sigma}\left(3, n\right), \operatorname{sigma}\left(1, n\right)\right)} \bmod 3 = \operatorname{indicator}\left(n\right)$$

*Proof.* Machine-checked in Lean as `D5/S3/Factorization/A091259.a091259_mod_three` (`✓ std3`). ∎
