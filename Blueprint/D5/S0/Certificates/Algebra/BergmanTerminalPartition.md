# Two-terminal binomial partition semigroups

## Abstract

Signed binomial partitions give actual two-terminal semigroups and contracted cancellation.

*Commentary.*

A positive monomial of degree below n reduces to its own lower element. A degree-n monomial reduces to terminal true precisely when its y-exponent belongs to I, and otherwise to terminal false. Empty and higher-degree monomials reduce to zero.

**Definition 1.3 (Associative commutative multiplication).**

Lean statement: `D5/S0/Certificates/Algebra/BergmanTerminalPartition.modelMul`

*Formalization.* `D5/S0/Certificates/Algebra/BergmanTerminalPartition.modelMul` (`✓ std3`).

*Source.* Repository-derived.

*Acknowledgement.* George M. Bergman (2014). *Thoughts on Eggert's Conjecture*. DOI: [10.1090/conm/609/12101](https://doi.org/10.1090/conm/609/12101). URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.0326v2>.

*Commentary.*

Multiply two lower elements by adding their exponent multisets and reducing. Every product involving a terminal or zero is zero. Reduction of positive monomials respects this product; reduction of a sum of three exponent multisets proves associativity. Thus this is an actual commutative semigroup with a two-sided absorbing zero.

*Commentary.*

For every positive n and finite I containing zero and excluding n, two free words are congruent under the specified presentation if and only if they reduce to the same actual normal form. Soundness checks each defining relation and multiplication. Completeness reduces every word to a representative: a lower monomial, x^n or y^n for the two terminals, or zero above the terminal degree. Consequently distinct lower monomials and the two terminal classes remain distinct in the presented semigroup.

*Commentary.*

For every n at least two and every finite I containing zero and excluding n, assume the integral signed sum is zero. The actual semigroup has n(n+1)/2+2 elements and is generated by x,y. Their nth powers are respectively the distinct nonzero terminals true and false. Over every field F its contracted algebra has dimension n(n+1)/2+1. The actual contracted x-y is nonzero, belongs to the span of the two generator basis images, and has nth power zero. All products of n+1 arbitrary semigroup elements vanish, and the whole subspace spanned by products of n+1 arbitrary algebra vectors is zero. In particular every such vector product vanishes. The nth power of the x basis image is nonzero, so both nilpotency indices are exactly n+1. The positivePower index n-1 means n factors. The proof establishes these claims over every field, and therefore over every characteristic-zero field in the source. No weakening of that source field domain is used. A binomial expansion in the unitization proves that the two terminal coefficients of (x-y)^n are the signed sum and its negative; it does not adjoin a basis identity to the contracted algebra. The general product-span correspondence in BergmanCyclicGrading transfers the actual semigroup degree bound to arbitrary algebra products, including products of distinct vectors.

For the application in the source, set n=35 and I={0,1,...,13,20,21}. The endpoints are separated. The alternating partial-sum identity gives the signed sum as -choose(34,13) +choose(35,20)-choose(35,21)=choose(34,15)-2choose(34,13)=0. Thus the actual semigroup has 35*36/2+2=632 elements, its contracted algebra over every characteristic-zero field has dimension 631, and both exact indices are 36. Its generator 35th powers are distinct and nonzero, whereas its nonzero contracted x-y has 35th power zero. These are consequences of the general theorem, rather than additional retained specialization declarations.

Von zur Gathen and Roche, Polynomials with two values (1997), Definition 3.2 and equation (3.2), printed pages 350-351, use b_j=a_j-a_(35-j) for odd n. Theorem 4.4(i), page 357, and the first n=35 row of Table 2, page 359, give the folded vector (1 repeated fourteen times,-1,-1,0,0); its table legend is on page 360. The selected I is one of four derived unfoldings, not an I-set printed in that paper. The authors omit the proofs of Theorem 4.4. Their binomial partition is prior; the 632-element semigroup and the application to Bergman's later Question 15 are the source manuscript's consequences. This does not claim new binomial identities, priority, minimality or further solved credit.
