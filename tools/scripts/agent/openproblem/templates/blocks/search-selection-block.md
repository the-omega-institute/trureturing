**Reasoning standard.** 深入思考与推理, 如数学推理按照正式定义定理证明推理. Check every candidate settlement against the source's formal definitions, and write its proof route as definitions, lemmas and proofs.


**Areas.** Every area of mathematics and mathematical physics counts equally: where the brief above says that mathematical physics comes first, read that only as a scanning order, never as a ranking of candidates.

## Selection priority (binding for ranking)
Rank candidates by mathematical value, not by how quickly they can be counted:
1. **Proofs** of published conjectures or answers to named open questions in any area of mathematics or mathematical physics, and research-line problems with a concrete route.
2. **Refutations with content**: the conjecture answers a question its authors pose, and the witness comes with a mechanism or a family (a whole parameter range, an explanation of why the statement fails).
3. **Single small counterexamples** to conjectures that rest on small numerical tests: allowed, but ranked below 1 and 2.
4. **Loophole refutations are not candidates on their own.** If the only witness is a degenerate case that the statement's spirit excludes (an empty or edgeless graph, n = 0 or 1, a zero parameter, a trivial group), report it, but propose the candidate only with a nondegenerate witness as well.
For each candidate, state which rank it belongs to and why.

**Prior posted solutions.** Open the problem's MathDB page (mathdb.com), not only the search listing, and read its "Solutions" count. If any solution or settlement is posted there, or on arXiv, a forum or a preprint server, the problem is not a candidate, even when the source still calls it open. If no MathDB page for the problem is found after searching its title words and author names, write "MathDB page: not located" and keep the candidate: a missing page is not a posted solution, and the citing-work and later-author screens remain the binding prior-art checks. Do not withhold an otherwise complete candidate solely for a missing MathDB page. Report the MathDB URL and its solution count for every candidate. For quantum-information and mathematical-physics candidates, also search the QIQC open-problem catalog https://github.com/Naixu-Guo/quantum-open-problems: its problem records (database/problems_json) and its issues, where independent settlements are posted for review. A settlement posted there counts as a prior posted solution. Report the search terms used and the hits.

**Prior-art screen before proposing (binding).** For every candidate: (1) open each citing paper and each later paper of the same authors, and check their examples and theorems for an implicit settlement; (2) ask whether a classical theorem (surveys, monographs and classical papers in the same area) already implies the statement as a special case, and name what you checked. A candidate whose answer is stated in published work, or is a direct special case of a published theorem, is not open; report it as excluded with the reference instead of proposing it. A candidate whose answer you can derive by a new argument that combines published results (a reduction, construction or counterexample that no screened work states) is still open: propose it, with the published ingredients, the new step, and the screens showing that no work states the answer.

**Partial screens (binding).** If the mathematics of a candidate is complete (an exact counterexample or a full proof) and only part of the citation and later-author screen remains unread, propose it anyway, with `prior_art_status: partial`, and list each unread work with its DOI or URL. The orchestrator's probe reads those works first and stops if one settles the question. Hold a candidate back only when a screened work already answers it, or when the mathematics itself is incomplete.
