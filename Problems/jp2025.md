# Johnson–Paparella Question 9.1

## Problem

Johnson and Paparella, *Perron similarities and the nonnegative inverse eigenvalue problem*, arXiv:2409.07682v4, Section 9, Question 9.1, ask:

> If $K$ is a polyhedral cone (polytope) that is closed with respect to the Hadamard product, is there an invertible matrix $S$ such that $K = \mathcal C(S)$ ($K = \mathcal P(S)$)?

Here $S\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb C)$, $\mathcal C(S)=\{z\in\mathbb C^n:SD_zS^{-1}\ge0\}$ and $\mathcal P(S)=\{z\in\mathcal C(S):SD_zS^{-1}e=e\}$. Complex nonnegativity includes zero imaginary part. A polyhedron is a finite intersection of real half-spaces in complex coordinates; a polytope is a bounded polyhedron. The proposition `claim` is the conjunction of both affirmative converses for every positive $n$ and every $K\subseteq\mathbb C^n$.

## Route

The explicit $m=10$ cone has fifteen listed generators, including an interior center and the multiplicative identity. Fourteen real inequalities define its real part, and six homogeneous half-spaces enforce its three imaginary-coordinate equations. `cone_decomposition` expresses every cone point as a nonnegative combination of three listed generators. `products_mem`, `generator_mul` and `realCone_mul` extend the generator product inequalities to arbitrary cone points.

For any proposed complex diagonalizer, `spectral_real_forms` uses three spanning cone generators to force reality of every entry form on the whole real span. `active_entry` supplies a nonzero tight form at each of ten boundary edge midpoints. `midpoint_zero_endpoints` and `distinct_edges_independent` force these forms to be distinct. `no_nine_forms` contradicts an injection from ten edges to the nine matrix entries, and `complexCone_not_spectral` rules out the representation. The public `result` applies this obstruction to the cone branch of `claim`.
