# Prefix-reversal triple count: Conjecture 1 is false

## Problem

Blanco, Golubyatnikov, Konstantinova, Maslova and Nikiforov,
*Generating the symmetric group by three prefix reversals*, Conjecture 1,
Section 4.1, page 17, propose
$f(n)=c_q n^2+O(n)$ for $n\equiv q\pmod 4$, with
$(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)=(1/13,1/9,1/10,1/7)$.
Here $f(n)$ counts the pairs $2\le k<m<n$ for which the three
prefix reversals generate the entire symmetric group.

## Route

For $4\mid n$, generation requires $m+k\ge n$, opposite parities of
$m,k$, and at least one of $m,k$ congruent to $2$ or $3$ modulo four.
For every natural modulus $3\le p\le n$, simultaneous congruences
$m\equiv k\equiv n\pmod p$ preserve the proper nonempty set
$\{i\in\mathrm{Fin}(n): i\equiv0\text{ or }i+1\equiv n\pmod p\}$.
They therefore prevent generation of the full symmetric group.

Use the moduli $4,3,5,7,11,13,17,19$. Their product is
$M=19399380$, and their joint restrictions allow
$A=115579079884800$ residue pairs. The proof uses
`ZMod.prodEquivPi` and a finite lattice estimate on each joint residue
fiber, always counting the same pairs. For $n\ge M$ and $4\mid n$,
$4M^2 f(n)\le A(n+2M)^2$ and hence
$f(n)\le (1146617856/14933966047)n^2+3Mn$.
The strict gap from $1/13$ is $2148763/14933966047>0$.
Its quadratic contribution eventually exceeds every proposed linear error.

## Falsifier

The source clause would require constants $C,N$ controlling every
sufficiently large multiple of four. The proof constructs such a multiple
above $N$, $M$, and $(C+3M)/(2148763/14933966047)$, where the lower
bound for the deficit contradicts that error estimate. No finite numerical
counterexample is substituted for the quantified asymptotic contradiction.
