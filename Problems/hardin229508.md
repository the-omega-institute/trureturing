# Hardin's recurrence for the $n\times 6$ one-defect three-colour strip

## Problem

The OEIS entry A229508, by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of defective 3-colorings of an n X 6 0..2 array connected diagonally and antidiagonally with exactly one mistake, and colors introduced in row-major 0..2 order.

and the empirical recurrence

> Empirical: a(n) = 50*a(n-1) -715*a(n-2) +324*a(n-3) +50175*a(n-4) -180630*a(n-5) -940491*a(n-6) +4556250*a(n-7) +2329155*a(n-8) -27398736*a(n-9) +13286025*a(n-10) +47829690*a(n-11) -43046721*a(n-12) for n>13.

The quotations are from the OEIS text export. The published sequence
has offset one and is column 6 of A229510.
[OEIS text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A229508&fmt=text)

The registered assertion is this order-twelve recurrence for every
$n\ge 14$, with $n$ rows, six columns and exactly $1$
monochromatic bond. OEIS writes `n>13`. This is the threshold $n\ge 14$ in #32721.
The source theorem `HardinA229508Strip.result` at `t` gives the recurrence at
$n=t+14$ for every $t\in\mathbb N$.
[Issue #32721](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/32721)

For $r$ rows, the vertices are the cells
$(i,j)\in\operatorname{Fin}(r)\times\operatorname{Fin}(6)$.
For $i+1<r$ and $j+1<6$, the bonds are
$\{(i,j),(i+1,j+1)\}$ and $\{(i,j+1),(i+1,j)\}$.
The boundaries are free. Each bond is counted once and there are ten
bonds per consecutive row pair. In Lean, `DefectTransfer.defects` uses
an empty within-row bond set and `HardinA229637RowColumn.betw` at width
six for the between-row bonds.

## Motivation

For the three-state Potts antiferromagnet on these bonds, take
$E(f)=J\sum_{\{u,v\}\in B}\mathbf 1_{f(u)=f(v)}$ with $J>0$.
Equal endpoint colours make a bond unsatisfied. A229508 counts states
with exactly $D=1$ unsatisfied bonds, up to global relabelling of the
three colours. These states have energy $J$ in this convention.

Diagonal steps preserve the parity of $i+j$. Each parity subgraph is
a finite piece of the square lattice rotated by $45^\circ$, with
three sites per array row and sawtooth side boundaries. The row-major
colour rule chooses one representative per orbit of a single colour
permutation acting on the entire array, across both subgraphs.

## Gap

The empirical recurrence calls for a proof at every registered index
from the literal array count. A finite table of values tests only the
indices it covers.

The literature check covered the OEIS entry and its links, arXiv
searches for Potts strips with unsatisfied bonds and for defective
colourings, and Chang–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 107 (2002)
1207–1253, on rectangular square-lattice strips. That check located
no proof of this recurrence.

## Route

1. **Canonical counts and boundary classes.** The literal filter in
   `HardinA229508Strip.a` reads the array in its original row-major order.
   The transfer sweeps rows. It reads the even columns from the left
   and the odd columns from the right. Each triple has one of five
   equality patterns, and the unordered pair gives fifteen classes.
   `HardinA203981Strip.scale_count` gives the labelled-to-canonical
   normalization. A constant array with $m+1$ rows has $10m$ defects,
   so it cannot occur at exact budget one. Every accepted orbit has six
   labellings.

2. **Exact-budget transfer.** The 30 states are the 15 boundary classes in each of the remaining
   budgets $1,0$. A transition of cost $c$ is allowed at remaining
   budget $b$ when $c\le b$ and moves to budget $b-c$. If $Q$ is the zero-cost block,
   $T=\begin{pmatrix}Q&R\\0&Q\end{pmatrix}$, where $R$ is
   `HardinA229508StripData.costOne`. It sums the products for defect
   allocations $(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$ across the parity subgraphs, using
   the frozen `HardinA229588Strip.finite_certificate` for the
   half-strip exact-zero and exact-one counts in the reversed target
   orientation. `HardinA203981StripCertificate.allocated_counts`
   gives the representative counts and
   `HardinA203981StripSymmetry.lift_counts` transports them to all
   labelled source rows.
   `HardinA229508StripCertificate.transition_counts` proves the block
   transition law. If $u_0$ is the single-row population of the 15
   classes, the initial row vector is $(u_0,0)$ and the terminal column
   vector has entries $(0,\mathbf 1)$ on the two sectors.

3. **Finite forward identity.** Write $W_k=uT^k$, with $u$ the
   initial row vector and $e$ the terminal column vector.
   `HardinA229508StripCertificate.forward_zeroth` projects onto the
   zero-cost system. `HardinA229508StripCertificate.forward_step`
   reuses the zero-cost updates and checks the new coordinates by
   finite kernel decisions. The degree-one table aggregates the two
   half-strip allocations, and
   `HardinA229508StripCertificate.forward_one` identifies it with the
   spent-budget sector by applying
   `HardinA203981StripCertificate.forward_table_of_steps`.
   The order-twelve coefficients come from squaring the monic
   zero-defect recurrence polynomial by convolution.
   `HardinA229508StripCertificate.coeff_values` verifies the expansion.
   On the inherited coordinates the vector relation is a linear
   combination of shifted zero-defect equations supplied by
   `HardinA229508StripCertificate.shifted_coordinate`. The new
   coordinates are checked by finite kernel decision.
   `HardinA229508StripCertificate.finite_certificate` gives
   $W_{13}=\sum_{j=0}^{11}c_jW_{1+j}$, with $c_j$ in increasing order
   of the input index.

4. **Propagation and index.** `DefectTransfer.forward_shift`
   applies further transfer steps and the terminal vector to the
   certified identity. `HardinA229508Strip.counting_bridge` applies
   `DefectTransfer.lumped_count` to give $uT^m e=6a(m+1)$ for every
   $m\ge 0$. The transfer exponent $13=1+12$ corresponds to
   $14$ rows.
   Cancelling the common factor six in $\mathbb Z$ gives the
   registered recurrence.

## Falsifier

A literal canonical count that violated the displayed recurrence at
some $n\ge 14$ would falsify the registered assertion. A mismatch
in the transition or initial counts would break the counting bridge.
A failed table update or vector equality at the specified exponents
would invalidate the forward certificate.
