# Sloane's generating functions for honeycomb cylinders of odd width

## Problem

[OEIS A329515](https://oeis.org/A329515), revision 32, by N. J. A. Sloane, has NAME

> Array read by upward antidiagonals: row n = coordination sequence for cylinder formed by rolling up a strip of width 2*n+1 hexagons cut from the hexagonal grid by cuts perpendicular to grid lines.

and FORMULA

> The g.f.s for the rows could be found using the "trunks and branches" method (see Goodman-Strauss and Sloane), as was done in A329508. This step has not yet been carried out, so the following g.f. is at present only conjectural.
>
> The g.f. G(n) for row n (n>=0) is (strongly) conjectured to be
>
> (1/(1-x))*(1 + 2*x + 3*x^2*(1-x^(2*n-1))/(1-x) - (n-2)*x^(2*n+1) - n*x^(2*n+2)).

The claim is a family of identities, one for every width index $n\ge0$.

## Motivation

The settling declaration is
`D5.S3.StatisticalMechanics.HoneycombOddCylinderCoordination.result`. The
external assertion and its conventions are preregistered in
[issue #27218](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/27218).
The source note is `Library/Geometry/sloane2019a329515.md`. The cylinders are
rolled-up honeycomb sheets, the bond graphs of carbon-nanotube-type tubes with
odd circumference, and $c_n(m)$ counts the atoms $m$ bonds away from a fixed
atom. It is the odd-width companion of `Problems/sloane329512.md`.

## Gap

The entry states that the general $G(n)$ is conjectural and that the
trunks-and-branches derivation was never carried out. The preregistration's
bounded screen found no proof for general $n$.

## Route

1. **Plane distance.** The exact honeycomb plane distance, its geodesics and
   the coordinate fibre counts are the existing declarations of
   `HoneycombCylinderCoordination`, made public at their owner.
2. **Quotient distance.** Every quotient walk lifts, with its length preserved,
   from any chosen representative, so the quotient distance is the minimum
   plane distance over the representatives of a class.
3. **Representatives.** With $s=i+j$, every $A$-class has a unique
   representative with $-n\le s\le n$, and every $B$-class one with
   $-n-1\le s\le n-1$; each minimises the distance.
4. **Shell counts.** Counting slices of constant $s$ in these bands gives
   $c_n(0)=1$, then $c_n(m)=3m$ for $0<m<2n+1$, then $c_n(2n+1)=5n+2$, then
   $c_n(m)=4n+2$ for $m>2n+1$. Second differences of these counts give the
   identity.

## Falsifier

A width index $n\ge0$ and a radius $m$ whose cylinder shell count differs from
the corresponding coefficient of $G(n)$ would contradict `result`.

## Triage

1. **Proved (kernel-checked):** Sloane's $G(n)$ for every $n\ge0$, by

2. **Proved (kernel-checked), internal:** the explicit shell counts
   $c_n(m)=3m$ for $0<m<2n+1$, $5n+2$ at $m=2n+1$ and $4n+2$ beyond.
3. **Open:** the square-grid cylinder families A329501 and A329504.

### What the settlement shows

As in the even family, each coordination sequence has a planar regime $3m$ and
a wrapped regime of constant size equal to the number of atoms in one ring,
$4n+2$. The single transitional shell at radius $2n+1$, where the ball first
wraps around the cylinder, has $5n+2$ atoms. The odd seam offset only shifts
the representative band for $B$ atoms by one, so the even and odd families
are one lift-and-minimise argument with two translation vectors; together they
settle every width of perpendicular-cut honeycomb cylinder.
