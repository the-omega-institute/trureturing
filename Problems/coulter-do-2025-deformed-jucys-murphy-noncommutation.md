# Coulter–Do Conjecture 5.4(a)

## Problem

Xavier Coulter and Norman Do, arXiv:2506.04002v1, Conjecture 5.4(a), page 34:

> The 𝒥-operators commute when restricted to 𝒳(k) — that is, 𝒥ₘ 𝒥ₙ (v) = 𝒥ₙ 𝒥ₘ (v) for 1 ⩽ m ⩽ n ⩽ k and for all v ∈ 𝒳(k).

Here k is a positive integer, the scalars are ℂ(b) with b an indeterminate,
and 𝒳(k) is the orbit of the consecutive identity pair partition under the
unital algebra generated by the operators in Definition 5.1.
The theorem `result` proves `¬ claim`, with all these quantifiers retained.

## Motivation

The source verifies the conjecture numerically for k ≤ 5. At k = 6 the
ambient vector space has 10395 basis matchings; the conjecture concerns its
cyclic orbit, rather than commutation on the whole ambient space.

## Gap

This is a tier-1 published named conjecture. Issue #11521 supplies the
preregistration and scoped literature check. The cited refined Hurwitz
operators in arXiv:2508.06188 are different operators, so their affirmative
result does not settle Definition 5.1 commutation.

## Route

Pair partitions are fixed-point-free involutive partner maps on `Fin (2*k)`,
with the paper's labels shifted down by one. The transposition action is
conjugation. The charge algorithm follows alternating identity and matching
edges and takes the parity of the first occurrence of the component maximum.
The graph-rule correspondence is an expository identification, not an
additional universally quantified Lean theorem. The weight uses the charge
of the output matching, the first argument of the paper's weight function.
Finite coefficient functions represent the paper's vector space, and
`Algebra.adjoin` represents its generated operator algebra.

Take k = 6 and v = J₆ J₆ J₅ J₄ J₃ e₆. Its generating word places v in X(6).
At T = (1 5 | 2 7 | 3 9 | 4 11 | 6 10 | 8 12), the coefficients of J₂ J₄ v
and J₄ J₂ v evaluate at b = 2 to 78 and 81. A support invariant for
increasing words and a forced reverse transposition reduce the certificate
computations. Coefficientwise ring-homomorphism transport and the injective
embedding ℤ[X] → ℂ(b) turn their integer inequality into an inequality with
b an indeterminate. Evaluation applies to polynomial coefficients, not to
arbitrary rational functions with possible poles.

## Falsifier

The refutation would fail if the word did not lie in X(6), the target were
not a pair partition, the two kernel certificates had equal values, or the
polynomial embedding were not injective. The proof discharges these
obligations. The source interpretation also requires charges on the output
matching and the stated label shift.

## Triage

### What the settlement shows

- **Proved in this module:** `result` gives a noncommuting vector inside the
  generated cyclic orbit at k = 6. Restricting the ambient space to X(k)
  therefore does not supply the missing commutativity. The failure occurs
  at the first size beyond the source's reported checks.
- **Proved in this module:** the support and forced-predecessor arguments
  occur on the live proof path to the 78 and 81 certificates. They avoid
  constructing the full 10395-by-10395 operator matrices.
- **Open in this module:** a general theorem equating the walk charge with
  the graph charge. The finite correspondence through k = 6 has independent
  computational evidence reported in #11521 and the delivery; it is not a
  proof of the unbounded identification.
- **Open in this module:** full commutation in restricted scalar cases or
  for k ≤ 5. The source's small-size checks remain evidence for those
  restricted statements; the k = 6 refutation does not contradict them.
- **Open as separately formalized consequences:** a simultaneous eigenbasis
  for these same operators would force commutation, so the noncommuting
  vector obstructs the structure proposed in Conjecture 5.4(b). This
  implication is mathematical exposition, not a second theorem here.
- **Open:** corrected weight conventions, the dimension of X(6), and the
  ordering-sensitive status of Conjectures 5.7(b) and 5.8. The refutation
  removes commutativity as a justification for order-independent products.
  Proposition 5.9 explicitly orders monomials and does not assume 5.4(a);
  this result supplies no refutation of that proposition or of 5.7(a).
