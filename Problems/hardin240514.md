# Hardin's order-twenty recurrence for the three-column binary strip

## Problem

[OEIS A240514](https://oeis.org/A240514), revision 6, by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of nX3 0..1 arrays with no element equal to exactly two horizontal and vertical neighbors, with new values 0..1 introduced in row major order.

and FORMULA

> Empirical: a(n) = 4*a(n-1) -3*a(n-2) -2*a(n-3) +2*a(n-4) +2*a(n-5) -7*a(n-6) -8*a(n-7) +15*a(n-8) +4*a(n-9) -8*a(n-10) -4*a(n-11) +15*a(n-12) +8*a(n-13) -7*a(n-14) -2*a(n-15) +2*a(n-16) +2*a(n-17) -3*a(n-18) -4*a(n-19) -a(n-20)

A board has $n$ labeled rows and three labeled columns. Entries are binary, the
boundaries are open, and adjacency is orthogonal only. For a binary alphabet,
introducing new values in row-major order means exactly that the first entry is
$0$. The recurrence is claimed for every $n\ge21$, with signed integer
coefficients.

## Gap

At revision 6 the recurrence is still marked empirical. The preregistration's
bounded screen found no proof. The only related work it located,
Dougherty-Bliss–Kauers, *Hardinian Arrays*, studies a different constraint.

## Route

States are ordered pairs of consecutive three-bit rows, 64 in all. A boundary
row compares its available neighbours and an interior row compares its three
vertical and horizontal neighbours.

1. **Counting bridge.** Splitting the finite board space at the first row gives
   a continuation recursion. Induction on its length identifies the
   continuation counts with the iterates $v_k$ of a fixed transfer step. The
   integer count $a(k+2)$ then equals the boundary-weighted total of $v_k$.
2. **Finite certificate.** A $21\times64$ integer table of $v_0,\dots,v_{20}$
   is checked step by step with `decide +kernel`. On all 64 states,
   $v_{20}$ equals the registered twenty-term combination of
   $v_{19},\dots,v_0$.
3. **Propagation and the base case.** Linearity of the transfer step
   propagates the vector identity to every shift. This gives the recurrence
   from height $22$. The checked table together with the literal value
   $a(1)=3$ settles height $21$.

## Falsifier

A board count that violates the recurrence at some $n\ge21$ would contradict
`result`.

### What the settlement shows

As in width two, the empirical recurrence comes from one annihilating
polynomial of the row-pair transfer step. Because the polynomial already
annihilates the state vector, the recurrence holds from the source's first
index, with only a single checked base height. The order grows from five in
width two to twenty in width three. Both are consistent with a transfer
operator on $4^w$ row pairs.
