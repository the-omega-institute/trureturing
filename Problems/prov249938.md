# Provvedi's prime-modulus least period for even-index Fubini numbers

## Problem

Provvedi's second conjecture in the COMMENTS of OEIS A249938, revision 76, states:

> This sequence is a periodic sequence modulo m, and if m is the k-th prime, the periods of {a(n)} over k-th prime is the sequence of the number of nonzero quadratic residues modulo k-th prime, for all k>0.

The source's finite definition is:

> a(n) = Sum_{k=0..2*n} k! * Stirling2(2*n, k) for n>=0.

Its example explicitly uses positive indices:

> Example: k=9, m = prime(9) = 23, for n>0, {a(n) mod 23} generates a period of 11 elements {3, 6, 14, 22, 5, 3, 10, 2, 4, 5, 0}, hence A130290(9) = 11

Write $F_N=\sum_{k=0}^{N}k!S(N,k)$ and $a(n)=F_{2n}$. For each natural prime $p$, let $L_p$ be the number of nonzero squares in $\mathbb F_p$. The prime clause asserts that $L_p>0$, that $a(n+L_p)\equiv a(n)\pmod p$ for every $n\ge1$, and that $L_p\le t$ for every positive period $t$ on those indices. The assertion includes $p=2$. It makes no pure-periodicity assertion starting at $a(0)$.

## Route

The consumed helper `fubini_exponential` applies the imported `StirlingTransformTotientPeriod.T_exponential_sum` and evaluates its binomial coefficients. Pairing opposite residues gives

$$a(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\bigl(2^{-(i+1)}+2^i\bigr)\bigl((i+1)^2\bigr)^n,\qquad m=(p-1)/2,$$

for odd $p$ and $n>0$, in $\mathbb F_p$. A weight vanishes exactly when $4^{i+1}=-2$. For $p\ge7$, injectivity of the first three powers of four makes three cyclic translates of the vanishing fiber disjoint. Hence at most $m/3$ weights vanish; both weights survive at $p=5$.

The consumed `spectral_period_lower_bound` works over any field with $m>0$, $m$ distinct nonzero $m$th roots, and more than half the coefficients nonzero. Vandermonde uniqueness, supplied by the imported Mathlib declaration `Matrix.eq_zero_of_forall_pow_sum_mul_pow_eq_zero`, forces every surviving frequency to be a $t$th root for any positive period $t$. The polynomial root bound limits their number by $\gcd(m,t)$. A proper divisor of $m$ is at most $m/2$, so the support inequality gives $m\le t$. The same frequencies give period $m$. The small primes are handled by `at_two` and `at_three`.
