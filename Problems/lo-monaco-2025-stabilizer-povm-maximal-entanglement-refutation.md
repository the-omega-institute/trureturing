# A stabilizer measurement exceeds every maximally entangled one

## Problem

G. Lo Monaco, S. Lorenzo, A. Ferraro, M. Paternostro, G. M. Palma and
L. Innocenti, *The non-stabilizerness cost of quantum state estimation*,
arXiv:2510.00157v2, Section IV, Conjecture 1 (PDF page 8), state:

> For any pure |ψ⟩, the maximal value of s_μ can be obtained with maximally
> entangled S.

For n data qubits and m ancillas, a stabilizer measurement is an orthonormal
common-eigenvector basis of a maximal Abelian Pauli subgroup excluding −I.
Its effective effects are μ_b = (I⊗⟨ψ|)|Φ_b⟩⟨Φ_b|(I⊗|ψ⟩), and s_μ is the
complex dimension of their linear span. The source defines
2p = n+m−dim(S_n)−dim(S_m), where S_n and S_m are the data-supported and
ancilla-supported subgroups. Maximal entanglement means p=min(n,m).
The formal claim retains all n,m and all pure normalized ancillas and
compares every stabilizer basis for the same ancilla.

## Motivation

The frozen declaration
`D5/S3/Quantum/Measurement/StabilizerPovmMaximalEntanglementRefutation.result`
refutes this universal claim. At n=m=3, one pure ancilla has only 31 nonzero
Pauli expectations. Every maximally entangled stabilizer measurement has
s_μ≤31, whereas a basis with two Bell pairs and a local Z measurement on
the third pair has s_μ≥32.

## Route

Take ψ=(1−i,1+i,−2,−2,2,2,2+2i,2−2i)/6 in computational order 000,…,111.
Exact Gaussian-integer arithmetic certifies its squared norm as one and
its Pauli support cardinality as 31. Pauli trace orthogonality and subgroup
maximality place every stabilizer-basis projector in its group span. For a
maximally entangled basis, projection onto ancilla Pauli labels is injective;
compression therefore bounds the effective span by the ancilla support.

Conjugating the computational basis by two Bell transforms gives a genuine
stabilizer basis for the group generated by X_0X_3, Z_0Z_3, X_1X_4, Z_1Z_4,
Z_2 and Z_5. For every two-qubit Pauli word P, at least one expectation of
P⊗I or P⊗Z survives. Hence all 32 independent data words P⊗I and P⊗Z belong
to its effective span. The inequality 32>31 contradicts the claimed optimum.

## Falsifier

The refutation requires a bound for every maximally entangled basis, not
only sampled Bell bases. It also requires a normalized pure ancilla, actual
Pauli subgroups with the source's maximality and exclusion conditions, an
orthonormal complete common eigenbasis, and linear independence of the 32
effective data operators. These obligations are proved on the live path of
`result`; no conjectural subgroup-size or injectivity assumption is supplied
as a hypothesis. The conclusion is `¬ claim` with no added assumptions.

### What the settlement shows

**Proved in this module:** for equal data and ancilla sizes, maximal
entanglement forces an injective ancilla Pauli-label map and thus a
support-count upper bound. The explicit basis
admits 32 independent data operators although the ancilla has support 31.
Reduced entanglement permits different data operators to share surviving
ancilla labels; counting those labels alone cannot bound every stabilizer
measurement. The equal-size support bound for maximally entangled bases
survives and is used to prove the refutation.

**Computed:** the binary group generated by these six operators has 64
elements, with two data-only and two ancilla-only elements. Thus its source
parameter is p=(6−1−1)/2=2. This parameter computation is independent evidence,
not a separately exposed kernel-certified theorem. The following command
returns `64 2 2`:

```sh
python3 -c 'from itertools import product; from functools import reduce; from operator import xor; g=[9,576,18,1152,256,2048]; G={reduce(xor,(v for b,v in zip(s,g) if b),0) for s in product(range(2),repeat=6)}; print(len(G),sum(v&3640==0 for v in G),sum(v&455==0 for v in G))'
```

Bits 0,…,5 encode X labels and bits 6,…,11 encode Z labels; qubits 0,…,2
are data and 3,…,5 are ancillas. Masks 3640 and 455 select the ancilla and
data coordinates respectively.

**Literature-attested:** Appendix D, Example 8 reports the maximally entangled
case for n=m=2. That example is not invalidated by a counterexample at n=m=3;
its separate source calculation is not proved here. The source's derived
results that do not assume Conjecture 1 retain their stated hypotheses.
Any use of Conjecture 1 to restrict a global search to maximally entangled
measurements loses that justification.

**Open:** the global optimum for this ancilla, minimal counterexample size,
robustness under perturbations, a structural criterion for when maximal
entanglement is optimal, and Conjecture 2. These require separate literature
and admission checks and are not added as declarations to this module.
