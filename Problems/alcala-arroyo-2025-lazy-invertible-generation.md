# Lazy and invertible cellular automata do not generate all cellular automata

## Problem

E. Alcalá-Arroyo and A. Castillo-Ramirez (arXiv:2510.14841, cs.FL with
nlin.CG; Theoret. Comput. Sci. 1076 (2026) 115965) study the lazy cellular
automata over `A^G`: a local map on a finite neighborhood `S ∋ e` returns the
central value `z(e)` on every pattern `z` except one, the unique active
transition `p`, where it writes a symbol different from `p(e)`. Motivated by the
generation of the full transformation monoid of a finite set by its
permutations and its idempotents of defect one, they ask:

> If $\mathcal{L}(G,A)$ is the set of all lazy cellular automata over $A^G$,
> prove or disprove the following:
> $\text{CA}(G,A) = \langle \text{ICA}(G,A) \cup \mathcal{L}(G,A) \rangle$.

Issue #11773 fixes the reading: every group `G` and every finite alphabet `A`
with at least two symbols, cellular automata and lazy automata as in the paper,
`ICA(G, A)` the units of the monoid `CA(G, A)` under composition, and `⟨C⟩` the
submonoid generated by `C`. The result disproves the equality.

## Motivation

A positive answer would reduce every cellular automaton to invertible ones and
automata that act at a single local pattern, the analogue for `A^G` of the
generation of `Tran(A)` by `Sym(A)` and the idempotents of defect one; the
paper notes that the lazy automata with neighborhood `{e}` are exactly those
idempotents. The frozen declaration
`D5/S3/StatisticalMechanics/CellularAutomata/LazyInvertibleGeneration.result`
shows that the equality fails for the Klein four-group and the binary
alphabet.

## Route

Let `G` be the Klein four-group, `A = {0, 1}`, `w(x)` the number of cells of
`x` equal to `1`, and `F(x)` the complement of `x` when `w(x) = 1`, and `x`
otherwise.
1. `F` is a cellular automaton with neighborhood `G`, since `w` is invariant
   under the shifts.
2. Every cellular automaton commutes with the shifts. In the Klein group a
   configuration has even weight exactly when a shift by some `k ≠ e` fixes
   it, so cellular automata send even weight to even weight, and they send
   the constant configurations `c₀`, `c₁` to constant configurations.
3. A lazy automaton with constant pattern `b` sends `c₀` and `c₁` to `c_{1−b}`.
   With a non-constant pattern, on a configuration of weight one (three) the
   pattern occurs at most once, located by a cell where it is `1` (`0`), so the
   automaton fixes the configuration or changes one cell and makes the weight
   even.
4. The property "if `T c₀ ≠ T c₁`, then `T` is injective on the configurations
   of odd weight whose image has odd weight" holds for invertible and for lazy
   automata and is preserved by composition, so it holds on the generated
   submonoid.
5. `F` separates `c₀` and `c₁` but sends the configuration with a single `1`
   at `e` and its complement, of weight three, to that complement.

## Falsifier

The answer would change if `ICA(G, A)` were read as a set strictly larger than
the injective automata, or if `⟨·⟩` allowed infinite compositions; the paper
defines neither. It would also change for the smaller groups: by the
computation below, the equality holds for the cyclic groups of order at most
four with the binary alphabet.

### What the settlement shows

**Proved (Lean):** over the Klein four-group with the binary alphabet, the
automaton `F` is not a finite composition of invertible and lazy cellular
automata, so `CA(G, A) ≠ ⟨ICA(G, A) ∪ L(G, A)⟩` there.

**Mechanism (proved inside `result`):** a lazy automaton either merges the two
constant configurations or moves a configuration of odd weight by at most one
cell; in the Klein group a single cell change of an odd-weight configuration
creates a nontrivial stabilizer, which no later cellular automaton can remove.
Hence every element of the generated submonoid that keeps the two constants
apart is injective on the configurations of odd weight (trivial stabilizer)
whose image also has odd weight; `F` merges two such configurations.

**Computed (Python, not formalized):** among the groups of order at most four
with the binary alphabet, the Klein four-group is the only one where the
equality fails; `⟨ICA ∪ L⟩` misses 5 824 of the 65 536 cellular automata.

**Argument (not formalized):** the equality also fails for `G = ℤ` and every
finite alphabet `A` with at least two symbols. Under the uniform Bernoulli measure the cylinder
`{x : τ(x)(0) = p(e)}` of a lazy automaton `τ` has measure
`1/|A| − |A|^{−|S|} < 1/|A|`, while surjective cellular automata on `A^ℤ`
preserve the uniform measure (Hedlund, Math. Systems Theory 3 (1969) 320–375),
so no lazy automaton is surjective. If a surjective `T = T_m ⋯ T_1` with
invertible or lazy factors, then `T_m` is surjective, hence invertible, and
induction makes `T` injective. Identifying `A` with `ℤ/|A|ℤ`, the automaton
`x ↦ (g ↦ x(g) + x(g + 1))` is surjective, since a preimage of `y` is obtained
from any value of `x(0)` by solving `x(g + 1) = y(g) − x(g)` forward and
`x(g) = y(g) − x(g + 1)` backward, and not injective, since the `|A|` choices of
`x(0)` give distinct preimages; so it is not generated.

**Open:** the follow-up of Problem 2, a description of `⟨ICA ∪ L⟩` and of
`⟨L⟩`; and which finite groups satisfy the equality.

**Source consequences:** the paper's results on the order of lazy automata do
not depend on Problem 2 and stand; Problem 1 (idempotency) is untouched.
