# Hardin's six-column trace-avoidance recurrence

## Problem

R. H. Hardin's sole FORMULA field in [OEIS A185765](https://oeis.org/A185765),
revision 9, states:

> Empirical: a(n)=a(n-1)+30*a(n-2)+24*a(n-3)-95*a(n-4)-15*a(n-5)+64*a(n-6)-116*a(n-7)+16*a(n-8)+16*a(n-9) for n>12

Its NAME field is:

> Number of (n+1)X6 binary arrays with no 2X2 subblock trace equal to any horizontal or vertical neighbor 2X2 subblock trace.

The claim concerns every natural $n\ge13$. Arrays are labeled, with binary
entries, open rectangular boundaries and no quotient by symmetries. Each
contiguous block has its ordinary main-diagonal trace in the natural numbers.
The counts are embedded in the integers before the signed recurrence is taken.

## Route

Encode each binary row by an element of $\operatorname{Fin}(64)$. Horizontally
admissible consecutive row pairs are states. Vertical comparisons constrain
transitions between overlapping pairs. `HardinA185765Transfer.counting_bridge`
identifies the literal array count $a(k+1)$ with the sum of the continuation
vector $u(k)$ for every natural $k$.

For
$Q(X)=X^9-X^8-30X^7-24X^6+95X^5+15X^4-64X^3+116X^2-16X-16$,
the residual is $Q(T)u(k)$, where $T$ is the linear continuation operator.
`finite_certificate` proves that the vector residual at five vanishes and
that its scalar totals at three and four vanish. The consumed private
`recurrence_from_certificate` propagates the vector identity by linearity,
uses the two scalar boundary identities and applies the counting bridge.
Its conclusion is `claim`; `result` supplies its certificate premise.

## Falsifier

An admissible-array count violating the displayed recurrence at any natural
$n\ge13$ would contradict `result`. A nonzero scalar residual at $n=12$
does not contradict the source; it rules out lowering that starting index
while keeping the same coefficients.

### What the settlement shows

the finite vector annihilator and its two scalar boundary equalities extend
it to every source index. These mechanisms are proved by the declarations

Lean theorem. Generalization to another width and minimality remain open.
