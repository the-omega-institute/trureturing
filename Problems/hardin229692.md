# Hardin's recurrence for the $n\times 6$ two-defect three-colour strip

## Problem

[OEIS A229692](https://oeis.org/A229692), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of defective 3-colorings of an n X 6 0..2 array connected horizontally and antidiagonally with exactly two mistakes, and colors introduced in row-major 0..2 order.

and the empirical recurrence

> Empirical: a(n) = 75*a(n-1) - 2145*a(n-2) + 29368*a(n-3) - 205200*a(n-4) + 803115*a(n-5) - 1842183*a(n-6) + 2460375*a(n-7) - 1771470*a(n-8) + 531441*a(n-9) for n > 11.

Both quotations are from the [OEIS text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A229692&fmt=text).
The assertion registered in
[issue #31329](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31329)
is this order-nine recurrence for every $n\ge 12$, with $n$ rows,
$6$ columns and exactly $2$ monochromatic bonds. The sequence offset
is one. The source's `result t`, for every `t : Nat`, uses the shifted
index $n=t+12$ and has exactly that range.

For an array $f:\operatorname{Fin}n\times\operatorname{Fin}6\to\operatorname{Fin}3$,
a defect is a bond whose endpoint colours agree. The HA bonds are
$(i,j)$--$(i,j+1)$ for $j+1<6$, and
$(i,j+1)$--$(i+1,j)$ for $i+1<n$ and $j+1<6$.
These horizontal and antidiagonal bonds make the arrays sheared
square-lattice strips with staircase ends. Vertical and periodic boundary
bonds are absent. The source sweeps six-cell rows and retains their
five-cell tails.

A229692 is the two-defect counterpart of the settled width-six
one-defect column A229584, registered in
[#30580](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/30580).
The transfer is a block extension of the frozen
`StripDefects/HardinA229584Strip*` owners.

## Motivation

The settling declaration is
`D5/S3/StatisticalMechanics/Potts/StripDefectsWide/HardinA229692Strip.result`.
The external assertion is preregistered in
[issue #31329](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31329);
the source note has bibkey `hardin2013a229692`.

The coordinate change $x=i+j$, $y=i$ sends horizontal and antidiagonal
bonds to the two unit directions of the square lattice. With
antiferromagnetic Potts energy
$E(f)=J\sum_{\{u,v\}\in B}\mathbf{1}_{f(u)=f(v)}$ and $J>0$,
these are states of the three-state Potts antiferromagnet with exactly
$D=2$ unsatisfied bonds on the sheared square strip (HA).
Row-major normalization chooses one representative from each global
colour relabelling orbit. In the nonconstant range used by the proof,
each such orbit has six labelled representatives. The theorem concerns
finite combinatorial counts under this bond and energy convention.

## Gap

The OEIS formula is labelled empirical. The registered target is its
validity for every $n\ge 12$ from the literal array definition;
agreement with a finite list of sequence terms does not supply that statement.

The literature check (the OEIS entry and its links; arXiv searches for Potts strips with unsatisfied bonds and for defective colourings; Chang–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 107 (2002) 1207–1253, which treats rectangular square-lattice strips) located no proof of this recurrence.
The literature scope includes the sources recorded in #31329.
Worldwide priority and literature completeness remain unverified.

## Route

1. **Literal count and normalization.** `a` filters every original
   `Fin n × Fin 6 → Fin 3` array by the exact HA defect count and the
   lifted row-major canonical predicate.
   `DefectTransfer.relabel_defects` supplies colour-permutation
   invariance. Inside `result`, `scale` applies
   `HardinA233177Canonical.canonical_pattern_card` to this original
   row-major domain. A constant array with $m+1$ rows has
   $5(m+1)+5m=5+10m$ HA bonds, so it cannot contribute at budget
   two. Thus the labelled count satisfies $L(m+1)=6a(m+1)$
   for every $m:\mathbb N$.
2. **Exact-budget block extension.** The source reuses `Row`, `Boundary`,
   `horizontal`, `antidiagonal`, `boundary = Fin.tail`, and `cross`
   from `HardinA229584StripData`. Appending a row $r$ to a tail $p$
   costs `cross p r + bondDefects horizontal r r`. The remaining
   budget $d\in\{0,1,2\}$ decreases by this cost. `Valid` bounds
   equal adjacent tail entries by $2-d$, in addition to $d\le2$.
   The matrix `T` has 68 states: the leading 32 use the frozen
   one-defect classifier with budget $d-1$ when $d>0$, and 36
   appended states carry zero residual budget. Its leading block is
   exactly `HardinA229584StripData.T`, and its lower-left block is zero.
   `oldClassImage` and `oldTail` transport inherited transitions;
   `zeroClassImage` uses `HardinA233260Literal.cls` to classify the
   zero-budget tails. The initial vector combines the inherited
   one-defect weights with the zero-budget extension multiplicities.
3. **Finite identities and reuse.**
   `HardinA229692StripCertificate.structural_certificate` supplies
   terminal acceptance, budget preservation, initial admissibility
   and weights. `HardinA229692StripCut.leading_counts` and
   `old_counts` reuse the frozen one-defect transition identities.
   `HardinA229692StripClassGroup0` through
   `HardinA229692StripClassGroup5` prove the remaining transition
   counts on canonical boundaries, with exhaustive finite cases using
   `decide +revert +kernel`. Colour relabelling transfers these counts
   to every labelled boundary. `HardinA229692StripInherited` reuses
   the frozen forward relation on the leading and repeated coordinates;
   the remaining coordinates are checked by finite kernel decisions.
   `HardinA229692StripForward.forward_certificate` and
   `HardinA229692Strip.finite_certificate` assemble the forward
   table, the polynomial identity, and all counting obligations.
4. **Propagation and starting index.** Write $u=\mathtt{initial}$,
   $e=\mathtt{terminal}$ and $W_j=uT^j$. The certified relation is
   $W_{11}=\sum_{j=0}^{8}c_jW_{2+j}$, with
   $(c_0,\ldots,c_8)=(531441,-1771470,2460375,-1842183,803115,-205200,29368,-2145,75)$.
   `DefectTransfer.forward_shift` propagates this shift-two,
   degree-nine relation through every later power.
   `DefectTransfer.lumped_count` identifies $uT^m e$ with $L(m+1)$,
   and the colour-orbit bridge gives $uT^m e=6a(m+1)$.
   Cancelling six in $\mathbb Z$ gives the displayed recurrence
   at every index at least $2+9+1=12$.

## Falsifier

A canonical HA count at some $n\ge 12$ that violated the displayed
recurrence would falsify the registered assertion and contradict the
source's `result`. A mismatch in the terminal, budget, transition,
initial, forward-step or polynomial identities would invalidate the
finite transfer certificate.
