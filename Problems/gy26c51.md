# Gy's coefficient log-concavity question

## Problem

René Gy asks this question in *Integers* 26 (2026), A108, §5, printed p. 16.

> More generally, one may ask whether it holds that if $(x_k)_{k\ge1}$ is a strongly $q$-log-concave sequence of polynomials in $\mathbb R[q]$, then, for each particular choice of $n$, $p$, and $j$, the sequences of coefficients of the polynomials $E_x^{n,p,j}(q)$ and $H_x^{n,p,j}(q)$ are themselves log-concave.

## Motivation

Gy considers whether the observed coefficient log-concavity of the $q$-Stirling differences extends to arbitrary strongly $q$-log-concave inputs. A common polynomial factor persists through the triple products and controls the adjacent coefficient determinant. [Source §5](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/aa108/aa108.pdf#page=15)

## Gap

The input condition controls individual coefficient signs in pairwise product differences. The output question requires inequalities between neighboring coefficients of each triple-product difference. Strong $q$-log-concavity permits an arbitrary common factor with nonnegative coefficients.

## Route

Put $g_t=1+q+tq^2$ and $x_k=(k+a)g_t$ for every $k\ge0$. The input products differ by $(r-i)g_t^2$ for every shifted pair $i\le r$, so each required coefficient is nonnegative when $t\ge0$. At $n=j+2$ and $p=j+1$, the elementary difference $E$ equals $C g_t^4$, where
$$
C=\frac{j(j+1)^2(j+2)(2a+j+2)(4a+j+3)}{48}.
$$
The first three coefficients are $C,4C,(6+4t)C$. For $j\ge1$, $a>0$, and $t>5/2$, the third coefficient is positive, so index one is interior and its determinant is $(10-4t)C^2<0$. A separate kernel-checked calculation gives $H=Cg_t^4$ for the same parameters.

## Falsifier

The common witness $x_k=(k+1)(1+q+3q^2)$ at $(n,p,j)=(3,2,1)$ gives $C=10$ and first coefficients $10,40,180$ for $E$. The determinant $-200$ contradicts the $E$ clause of the joint assertion. The supporting calculation gives the same coefficients and determinant for $H$.

## Evidence

The Lean proof of the literal negation uses Newton's identity for the elementary degree-two sum and the frozen first and second interval moments. The positive coefficient at degree two certifies the interior guard at degree one. The separate kernel-checked homogeneous calculation counts ordered pairs with the diagonal counted once and gives the same multiple of $g_t^4$. ## Triage

**Tier 1, theorem-level refutation.** Gy explicitly asks the question in the final section of the 2026 paper. The preregistered literature screen found no settlement of this arbitrary-input coefficient question. The Lean result refutes the universal conjunction by exhibiting an elementary counterexample. The elementary family and the separate homogeneous calculation give simultaneous failures for arbitrarily large $n$ along $p=n-1$ and $j=n-2$. [Original question](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/aa108/aa108.pdf#page=16) · [Literature screen](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31896)

### What the settlement shows

**Kernel-checked mechanism.** In `affine_gap` and `affine_strong`, the affine comparison leaves the common squared factor with nonnegative coefficients when $t\ge0$. The live identity in `affine_elementary_difference` gives $E$ as a scalar multiple of the fourth power. The supporting homogeneous calculation gives the same scalar for $H$. The coefficient formulas in `quadratic_four_coeffs` give the first determinant by substitution. For $a>0$ and $j\ge1$, its first interior determinant changes sign at $t=5/2$. Equality there settles that determinant alone. A coefficient log-concavity theorem covering this family would require at least $t\le5/2$, and all other interior determinants would still need to be controlled.

**Kernel-checked specialization.** The calculation at $a=1,t=3,j=1$ gives $E=10g_3^4$ and determinant $-200$, using `affine_elementary_difference` and `quadratic_four_coeffs`. The supporting check gives the same polynomial and determinant for $H$.

**Proved in prose.** Each polynomial has nonnegative coefficients. More generally, every coefficient of $Cg_t^4$ is a sum of products of nonnegative coefficients throughout the positive-scale family for $t\ge0$.

**Scope of the refutation.** Conjecture 5.1 asks for coefficient nonnegativity and is compatible with the entire counterexample family. Conjecture 5.2 and the neighboring coefficient log-concavity questions for the particular $q$-Stirling inputs have additional structure. The classical row-log-concavity question later on p. 16 concerns a different index. These source questions and the paper's reported finite checks retain their original scope. [Neighboring conjectures and finite checks](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/aa108/aa108.pdf#page=15) · [Row question](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/aa108/aa108.pdf#page=16)

**Open here.** A sufficient restriction on arbitrary inputs that recovers both coefficient log-concavity conclusions, and the full log-concavity range even within the affine family, remain outside the checked result.
