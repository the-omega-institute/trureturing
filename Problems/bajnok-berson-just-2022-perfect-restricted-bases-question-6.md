# Perfect restricted bases of size s + 1 force cyclicity

## Problem

B. Bajnok, C. Berson and H. A. Just, *On Perfect Bases in Finite Abelian Groups*, Involve 15 (2022), 525–536, arXiv:2211.13675v1, Question 6:

> Are there any noncyclic groups that have a perfect restricted s-basis of size s + 1?

The paper works with finite abelian groups and positive integers $s$. Its definition is:

> A is a perfect restricted s-basis of G if each element of G can be written uniquely as the sum of at most s distinct elements of A.

Uniqueness disregards the order of the summands. The empty subset contributes the empty sum $0$.

## Motivation

The source's Proposition 5 supplies cyclic examples of order $2^{s+1}-1$. Question 6 asks whether noncyclic groups also occur.

## Gap

Counting proper subsets gives the order $2^{s+1}-1$, but odd order alone does not force cyclicity. The extra relation is the parity of subset sums within each coset of a subgroup generated by a basis element.

## Route

Write $m=|A|=s+1$. Proper-subset sums biject onto $G$, so $|G|=2^m-1$ is odd. Fix $a\in A$ and put $H=\langle a\rangle$. Toggling $a$ pairs all subsets whose sums lie in any fixed coset of $H$. This is a fixed-point-free involution, so each such fiber has even cardinality. If that coset does not contain the full-set sum, every subset in it is admissible and its fiber bijects with $H$. Lagrange makes $|H|$ odd, a contradiction. A proper subgroup has a coset avoiding the full-set sum, so $H=G$.

The Lean proof uses the frozen `D5.S3.PrimeForms.Crossing.GlideCrossingParity.glide_crossing_count_even` for the involution parity step. The remaining constructions are proper-subset and coset-fiber equivalences, with Mathlib cardinality, subgroup and cyclicity interfaces.

## Falsifier

A finite abelian group with positive $s$, a finset $A$ of cardinality $s+1$, unique admissible subset sums, and no additive generator would contradict `result`. Changing to exactly $s$ summands, omitting uniqueness, or changing the size of $A$ gives a different problem.

## Triage

### What the settlement shows

- **Kernel-checked:** `result` proves cyclicity for every finite abelian group satisfying the source assumptions, including composite-order groups. The question's negative answer is unrestricted in the group order.
- **Proved in prose:** every $a\in A$ generates $G$. For that chosen $a$, the Route argument applies to each coset of $H=\langle a\rangle$. If $H$ were proper, select a coset other than the coset of $\sum A$; its subset fiber would have cardinality $|H|$, both odd by Lagrange and even by toggling $a$. Thus $H=G$. This strengthened statement is explanatory mathematics, not a separately published Lean theorem.
- **Proved in prose:** the sole inadmissible subset is $A$ itself; proper-subset uniqueness gives $|G|=2^{s+1}-1$. Together with the source's Proposition 5, the possible groups at size $s+1$ are exactly the cyclic groups of this order. The existence direction is literature evidence; it is not a new Lean theorem here.
- **Computed:** in the exact finite scope of the Evidence experiment, the cyclic basis counts are $1,2,2,6,6$, all basis elements are generators, and the sole noncyclic group in scope has count $0$.
- **Proved in prose:** for $m\ge2$ and $N=2^m-1$, the unit multiples of $B=\{1,2,4,\ldots,2^{m-1}\}$ give $\varphi(N)/m$ distinct perfect restricted $(m-1)$-bases. Proper binary subset sums are precisely $0,\ldots,N-1$, each once. Multiplication by $2$ cyclically permutes $B$ modulo $N$; its order is $m$, since $0<2^k-1<N$ for $0<k<m$. A unit stabilizing $B$ must send $1$ to a member of $B$, so the stabilizer is exactly these $m$ powers of $2$. Orbit-stabilizer gives the count. For $m=2,\ldots,6$, $\varphi(N)=2,6,8,30,36$, so these counts agree with the experiment.
- **Open:** whether every perfect restricted $(m-1)$-basis of size $m$ is such a unit multiple for arbitrary $m$. Finite agreement does not establish this universal basis classification.
- **Open:** the source's Questions 7, 10 and 11, concerning $s+2\le m\le2s$, $m=2s+1$ and $m\ge2s+2$, respectively. The full-set omission used here no longer describes the inadmissible subsets in those ranges. Proposition 5 remains the existence direction; the settlement closes its immediately following Question 6 without supplying the other classifications.
- **Literature status:** no published settlement was located in the inspected material recorded in #19526. Completeness is `ASSUMED-UNVERIFIED`.
