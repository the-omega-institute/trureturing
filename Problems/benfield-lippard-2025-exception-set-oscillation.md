# Oscillation of stable exception-set sizes

## Problem

Benfield and Lippard, *Integers that are not the sum of positive powers*,
arXiv:2404.08193v2, §10.1, Conjecture 10.5.

> The sequence |Bₖ| for k = 2, 3, … never becomes monotone.

For k ≥ 2 and j ≥ 1, Bₖʲ consists of the positive offsets s for which
j+s is missing from the sums of exactly j positive kth powers.
Equivalently, the raw exceptions satisfy n > j and s = n − j.
Repetition is allowed. The set Bₖ is the finite eventual value of Bₖʲ.
For every cutoff N ≥ 2 there must be an index r ≥ N with
|Bᵣ| < |Bᵣ₊₁| and an index d ≥ N with |Bd₊₁| < |Bd|.

## Motivation

Cofinite additive submonoids of the naturals admit finite presentations by
positive generators. Here the generators are explicitly prescribed by an
exponent, and the finite complement has a size that can be compared between
exponents.

## Gap

The bounded literature screen for issue 32074 found no prior full settlement
of Conjecture 10.5. The paper's Theorem 1.1 credits A. A. Zenkin [65] with
cofiniteness at each fixed length j ≥ G(1,k). Reference [65] is *Waring's
problem from the standpoint of the cognitive interactive computer graphics*.
Conjecture 1.2 gives the stable finite offset formulation. The Lean proof
derives stabilization from the positive fixed-length Waring bridge.

## Route

The eventual exceptional set is the complement among positive naturals of
the additive monoid generated by a^k − 1 for positive bases a. A finite
sum of such shifts gives an exact-length representation after restoring
one per summand. Padding with ones preserves the offset. For each k ≥ 2,
the fixed-length positive Waring bridge gives a reference length j₀ ≥ 1
with a finite exception set. Choose a witness length for each exception
at j₀ that belongs to the monoid. The threshold J is the maximum of j₀
and the supremum of those witness lengths, with empty supremum zero.
For j ≥ J, the supremum bound permits padding each chosen witness to
length j, and j ≥ j₀ permits padding the representations already
available at j₀. Positive gaps are exceptions at every length.

For a prime p ≥ 193, Fermat divisibility forces a lower bound
p^(p−1) ≤ 2|Bₚ₋₁|. For odd k with 3 ≤ k ≤ p, the shifts from the first
⌊p/3⌋ positive bases have gcd one. Representatives modulo 2^k−1 give
a bounded conductor for their additive closure, yielding
2|Bₖ| < p^(p−1). At the odd exponents p−2 and p these bounds give the
two strict inequalities. A prime p ≥ max(193,N+2) places both adjacent
changes in the specified tail.

## Falsifier

An eventually nondecreasing tail would exclude all strict adjacent falls
there. An eventually nonincreasing tail would exclude all strict adjacent
rises there. Either would contradict the two inequalities for a sufficiently
large prime.

## Evidence

`BenfieldLippard.conjecture_10_5` contains the finite stabilization and both
tail quantifiers in a single statement. A function from Fin j to the
naturals gives exactly j positive bases without any distinctness constraint.
Hilbert proved the qualitative Waring theorem. The Waring formalization
authors proved the Lean version using Linnik's method as presented in Tim
Jameson's notes. The positive-length bridge makes its bound usable with
the paper's exact summand convention.
[Hilbert](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01450405) · [Tim Jameson, p. 1](https://www.maths.lancs.ac.uk/jameson/warlin.pdf#page=1) · [Lean proof](https://github.com/yunyougu50-create/waring-lean4/blob/cd0735c253f3b640262fcc74a61306cb41e23765/WaringTheorem2.lean#L480)

## Triage

Tier 1, theorem. The positive resolution concerns exactly Conjecture 10.5.
The prime argument proves a strict local maximum at p−1 for every prime
p ≥ 193, which is stronger than the existence of two changes in every
tail. The cardinality inequalities leave the exact values and the remaining
conjectures in §10 open.

## ASSUMED-UNVERIFIED

The proof makes no worldwide priority claim. The cutoff in the prime
argument is sufficient, and its optimality is unverified.
