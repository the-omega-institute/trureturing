# Hardin's complete seven-column complementary-pair array recurrence

## Problem

[OEIS A233255](https://oeis.org/A233255), revision 9, by R. H. Hardin,
defines seven-column six-colour arrays and labels its five-step recurrence
"Empirical". The exact NAME and FORMULA transcriptions, version locator
and quotation licence are in `Library/Automata/hardin2013a233255.md`.

For each positive height $h$, let $a(h)$ be the number of all individual
functions

$$f:\{0,\ldots,h-1\}\times\{0,\ldots,6\}\longrightarrow\{0,\ldots,5\}$$

with $f(0,0)=0$, subject to the following conditions. Every horizontal
bond $(i,j)$–$(i,j+1)$ and antidiagonal bond
$(i,j+1)$–$(i+1,j)$ contained in the open rectangle has unequal endpoint
values whose natural-number sum is not $5$. Vertical, main-diagonal and
wraparound bonds are unconstrained. In row-major order, each occurrence
of colour $2$, $3$ or $4$ has a strictly earlier occurrence of colour $1$,
and each occurrence of colour $3$ has a strictly earlier occurrence of
colour $2$. A colour need not occur. Each function is counted once, with
no quotient or unproved orbit divisor.

The entire assertion is, for every natural $n\ge6$, in the integers,

$$
\begin{aligned}
a(n)={}&5504a(n-1)-6438912a(n-2)+2774532096a(n-3)\\
      &-478351982592a(n-4)+27831388078080a(n-5).
\end{aligned}
$$

The source Offset is `1,1`, supplies no $a(0)$, and gives no explicit
onset for its empirical recurrence. The domain $n\ge6$ is the disclosed,
preregistered all-positive-predecessors explication. This problem makes
no assertion at $n\le5$ and introduces no zero-height convention. A finite
height check, surrogate transfer count, changed width or generating-function
identity does not replace this assertion.

literal-count/transfer correspondence nor specifies an onset. The

The mathematical obligations are the exact scanner fibres, actual boundary

Listed terms alone discharge none of those universal obligations.

## Route

1. **Literal scanner fibres.** `HardinA233255LiteralBridge.scanner_fibres`
   pushes the original six-colour scanner sum to complementary-pair words,
   retaining its state transition and exact integer fibre weights. It holds
   for every word length, initial scanner state and integer readout.
   `PairTransfer.continuation_pair_bridge` propagates this equality through
   any number of rows. The original literal counting bridge then gives
   `PairTransfer.literal_pair_bridge` at every positive height.
2. **Actual boundary shapes and the unfinished sector.** Completed scanner
   continuations are invariant under relabelling the three pair labels.
   `ShapeSymmetry.shapeEquiv` enumerates the sixteen proper six-cell
   boundaries with first labels zero and one. The matrix $K$ counts actual
   compatible proper seven-cell pair words between these shapes. With
   $F_m=128^mK^m\mathbf1$, `literal_canonical_bridge` proves
   $$a(m+1)=16\cdot128^m+32\sum_{i=0}^{15}F_m(i)$$
   for every natural $m$. The exponential correction comes from the
   unfinished scanner sector and the anchored first-row multiplicities;
   it is retained in the actual count.
3. **A vector identity and all-height propagation.** The recurrence module
   uses $T=\operatorname{diag}(128,128K)$ with readout weights
   $(16,32,\ldots,32)$. Its finite table is checked against every entry of
   the actual $K$, and six retained vectors are checked as
   $\mathbf1,T\mathbf1,\ldots,T^5\mathbf1$. The checked relation is
   $$
   T^5\mathbf1=5504T^4\mathbf1-6438912T^3\mathbf1
   +2774532096T^2\mathbf1-478351982592T\mathbf1
   +27831388078080\mathbf1.
   $$
   The existing `TransferMatrix.scalarRelationShift` propagates it by every
   further matrix power. The exact count readout gives `shifted_recurrence`
   at heights $m+1$ through $m+6$; `result` reindexes this as every $n\ge6$.
   The table certifies an unbounded induction argument, not a finite cutoff.

## Falsifier

A natural height $n\ge6$ whose count of the individual functions defined
above violates the displayed integer recurrence would contradict `result`.
A disagreement for a quotient, a different bond convention, a different
width or a zero-height extension would concern a different statement.
