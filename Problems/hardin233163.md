# Barker's whole generating function for Hardin's three-column king strip

## Problem

[OEIS A233163](https://oeis.org/A233163), revision 8, names literal
three-column arrays with colours zero through seven, all horizontal,
vertical and both diagonal exclusions, upper-left zero and three strict
row-major first-appearance clauses. Its Barker FORMULA block conjectures

$$
\sum_{n\geq1}a(n)X^n
=X(3-64X+288X^2)((1-8X)(1-16X))^{-1}\quad\text{in }\mathbb Q[[X]].
$$

The full target E has 1354 UTF-8 bytes, no trailing newline, and SHA-256
`b6bdb7ca92fe6f1b0973750f4c6e15018daeb6eceeff0a49e6a0884061be3d90`.
The target is the entire equality, including every positive height, every
coefficient, the full numerator and the zero constant coefficient convention.

## Route

The existing Fin8 API in `HardinA233196Strip` supplies the scanner `Seen`,
`good`, `finish`, the complementary-pair map `beta`, `colour_bond_iff`,
and the arbitrary-width literal counting bridge. The new `normal_iff`
connects its lexicographic witnesses with strict rank `3*row+column`.

A proper pair boundary is ABA or ABC. At a saturated scanner, an ABA
boundary excludes its two labels from every position in the next row.
The remaining two labels must alternate, giving two pair rows and eight
independent colour lifts for each. An ABC boundary forces the next middle
label to be the fourth label; its endpoints are the previous right and
left labels. This gives one pair row with eight colour lifts. In both
cases the boundary type is preserved.

The normalized first rows are 010, 012 and 017. Their legal successors are:

| First row | Successors |
| --- | --- |
| 010 | 232, 235 |
| 012 | 230, 237, 530, 537 |
| 017 | 232, 235 |

All eight two-row seeds have seen 1, 2 and 3. Four have each pair type.
`saturated_certificate` and `finite_certificate` verify these finite
premises by kernel reduction. `continuation` proves for every natural n
that the continuation count is the nth power of the type's rate. Its
induction generalizes the whole proper boundary, and consumes the
preservation certificate at the induction step. Consequently

$$
a(1)=3,\qquad a(n+2)=4\cdot16^n+4\cdot8^n\quad(n\in\mathbb N).
$$

`recurrence` derives the source recurrence at every height at least four.
`product_identity` compares every coefficient, using the initial values
0, 3, 8, 96 and the unbounded recurrence. `result` applies the existing
power-series unit inverse theorem; the denominator constant coefficient
is one.

## Falsifier

A positive height at which the independent literal count differs from the
displayed coefficient contradicts the target. Incomplete implementation,
a timeout or an eventual recurrence without its numerator is not a refutation.
