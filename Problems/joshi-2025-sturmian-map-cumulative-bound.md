# A stronger cumulative Sturmian progression bound is false

## Problem

Gandhar Joshi, *Automatic sequences from algebraic, dynamical, and computational
perspectives*, Open University PhD thesis, §6.1, p. 118:

> “The data generated by the A(d) formulae in Corollary 4.3.13 heuristically directs
> to a conjecture that for every n ∈ N, d ≤ qₙ ⟹ A(d) ≤ A(qₙ) (Proposition 4.4.4)
> could be true for all g(d). As done for the specific case in [32, Corollary 4.17],
> this possibility for all Sturmians will be assessed in the author’s future work.”

The literal conjecture quantifies over irrational $0<\alpha<1/2$, positive
$n$, the convergent denominator $q_n$, and $1\le d\le q_n$.
Preregistration: https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/17837.

## Motivation

The source already proves the retained-hypothesis version with $g(d)\le\alpha$
(Proposition 4.4.4). Its unrestricted version would order all shorter differences
by a convergent denominator. The published Fibonacci special case does not assert
this comparison for arbitrary slopes.

## Gap

A long progression in a prefix alone cannot bound all starts. The required
counterexample needs both a concrete longer progression at $d=2$ and a uniform
upper bound for difference $3$, with the same slope, word and indexing.

## Route

For $\alpha=2-\sqrt3$, exact square-root inequalities give $0<\alpha<1/2$
and $1/\alpha=2+\sqrt3$, hence $q_1=3$. Positions $1,3,5,7,9$ all have the
first letter, so $A(2)\ge5$. At difference $3$, the fractional orbit moves
backwards by $g=3\sqrt3-5$. The proof uses the published
`FibonacciMapFirstStart.open_rotation_run` to exclude wrapping inside either
open symbol interval. Irrationality excludes both endpoints. Since
$4g>1-\alpha$ and $2g>\alpha$, every progression has length at most four.
Thus the asserted comparison would imply $5\le4$.

## Falsifier

An indexing or word-correspondence mismatch, or a five-term equal-letter run of
difference $3$ at this slope, would invalidate this refutation. The formal
statement quantifies every start, both letters and every positive length;
its upper bound is independent of a prefix cutoff or numerical approximation.
