# Complete pseudo-Pick pairs and quotient positivity

## Problem

> Let $(k, \ell)$ be a pair of reproducing kernels with $k$ non-vanishing. If $(k, \ell)$ is complete pseudo-Pick, do we have $\ell/k\succeq0$?

## Motivation

The question proposes quotient positivity as a consequence of complete pseudo-Pick interpolation. Three-way dependence among source kernel vectors can force all admissible matrix data on those points to be constant while the quotient has a negative quadratic form.

## Route

On $X=\operatorname{Fin}3$, take $x=(1/10,1,2)$ and

$$k(i,j)=1+x_ix_j,\qquad \ell(i,j)=1+\delta_{ij}x_i^2.$$

Both kernels are positive semidefinite and all source entries are nonzero. Positivity of the source Pick matrix forces the relations associated to any coefficients $t$ with $\sum t_i=\sum t_ix_i=0$. These relations imply consistency on repeated nodes and constant matrix data when all three points occur. Constant extensions are contractive for $\ell$.

If two distinct points occur, copy an observed value to the omitted point. The three scalar retraction quotient matrices have explicit rational $LDL^*$ factorizations with positive diagonal. Their Schur products with compressed source Pick matrices certify contractivity of the copied extensions. These cases cover every finite observation list and every positive matrix size.

### What the settlement shows

Proved in this module: three-point source dependence forces constant admissible matrix data when all three points are observed, while every two-point datum has a copied contractive extension. Nevertheless the quotient has a negative three-point quadratic form. Thus the interpolation property alone does not force quotient positivity. The surviving statements proved here are the repeated-node consistency, triple rigidity and explicit extension cases; no classification for other kernels is claimed.

The source's established theorems are unaffected. The proposed route from arbitrary-set complete pseudo-Pick interpolation through quotient positivity to BLH cannot use Question 9.3 as an affirmative universal premise. The separate BLH implication and the stricter source and holomorphic variants remain open here. The experiment's finite extra-label checks do not settle the infinite-rank extension family.
