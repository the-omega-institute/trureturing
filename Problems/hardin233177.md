# Hardin's recurrence for the four-row six-colour pair-exclusion ladder

## Problem

[OEIS A233177](https://oeis.org/A233177), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of 4Xn 0..5 arrays with no element x(i,j) adjacent to itself or value 5-x(i,j) horizontally, diagonally or antidiagonally, top left element zero, and 1 appearing before 2 3 and 4, and 2 appearing before 3 in row major order (unlabeled 6-colorings with no clashing color pairs).

and FORMULA

> Empirical: a(n) = 64*a(n-1) +3328*a(n-2) -77824*a(n-3) -1441792*a(n-4) +28311552*a(n-5) +33554432*a(n-6) -1073741824*a(n-7) for n>8

Each column has four cells, and consecutive columns are joined by the bonds
$(r,j)$–$(s,j+1)$ with $|r-s|\le1$: a four-row ladder with horizontal,
diagonal and antidiagonal bonds, and no bonds inside a column. A bond is
legal when its endpoints differ and do not sum to $5$.

## Route

1. **Pair patterns.** With the pairs $\{0,5\},\{1,4\},\{2,3\}$ of
   `BooleanRankThreeK4.beta` and a side bit per cell, a colouring is legal
   exactly when its pair pattern is proper (bonded cells carry different
   pair labels); the side bits are free.
2. **Canonical form.** The row-major normalization selects, in each
   relabelling orbit of a proper pattern, the unique member whose labels
   appear in first-occurrence order, and fixes the side bit at the first
   occurrence of each used pair (`HardinA233177Canonical`). For $n\ge2$ a
   proper pattern uses two or three labels, and exactly six proper patterns
   use two (constant columns alternating between two labels). Hence
   $$48\,a(n)=16^n\bigl(L(n)+6\bigr)\qquad(n\ge2),$$
   where $L(n)$ counts proper three-label pair patterns
   (`HardinA233177Ladder.counting_bridge`).
3. **Pattern transfer.** The $81$ column patterns fall into $14$ orbits
   under relabelling; the compatibility transfer commutes with relabelling,
   so the orbit transfer computes $L$ exactly with no scanner state
   (`HardinA233177PairTransfer`). A finite vector certificate $S(Q)Q\mathbf
   1=0$ with $S(t)=t^6-3t^5-16t^4+3t^3+25t^2-2t-4$ gives the order-seven
   relation for $L+6$ with polynomial $(t-1)S(t)$
   (`HardinA233177PairTransfer.recurrence`).
4. **Scaling.** Multiplying by $16^n$ scales the coefficient at lag $j$ by
   $16^j$, giving Hardin's polynomial $(x-16)\,16^6S(x/16)$; the base
   instance covers index $9$ and the shifted relation every larger index.

### What the settlement shows

The row-major normalization, which made a direct transfer carry a
$275$-state scanner with no exact quotient below $40$ classes, is only a
choice of orbit representative: the literal count is $16^n/48$ times the
number of proper pair patterns, plus the six two-label patterns. The
recurrence is therefore a property of the uncoloured pair-pattern transfer.
Its $14$ relabelling orbits reduce to a sextic $S$; the constant $6$
contributes the eigenvalue $1$ of $L+6$, which becomes Hardin's factor
$x-16$ after scaling. The recurrence starts only at index $9$ because the
bridge fails at width one: $16\cdot(81+6)/48=29$ while $a(1)=48$, so the
lag-seven relation first holds when every index it involves is at least
$2$; at index $8$ it fails with residual $31138512896$. The same bridge applies to any normalized complementary-pair
count in which the normalization is first-occurrence order, independent of
the scan order.
