# Hardin's recurrence for the diagonal one-defect king count

## Problem

[OEIS A229571](https://oeis.org/A229571) has the following verbatim NAME:

> Number of defective 4-colorings of an n X n 0..3 array connected horizontally, vertically, diagonally and antidiagonally with exactly one mistake, and colors introduced in row-major 0..3 order.

Its empirical FORMULA lines, verbatim from the
[text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A229571&fmt=text), are:

```text
Empirical (for n>=9): a(n) = 9*a(n-1) - 33*a(n-2) + 63*a(n-3) - 66*a(n-4) + 36*a(n-5) - 8*a(n-6). - _Vaclav Kotesovec_, Feb 14 2014
```

The recurrence assertion is registered in
[issue #30641](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/30641).

Let $T(n,k)$ be the literal filtered count of functions
$\operatorname{Fin}(n)\times\operatorname{Fin}(k)\to\operatorname{Fin}(4)$
with exactly one monochromatic bond and canonical first appearances of
colours in row-major order. The bonds are horizontal, vertical, diagonal
and antidiagonal, each present once when its endpoints lie in the rectangle.
The row-major convention identifies colour relabellings, while cell
positions stay distinct. The boundary is free in both directions.

Here $a(n)=T(n,n)$. The registered range is every $n\ge9$,
and all subtractions in the result are in $\mathbb Z$.

## Motivation

For the physics reading, this is the four-state Potts antiferromagnet on
the king lattice: the square lattice with both diagonals in each elementary
square. With equal positive penalties for monochromatic bonds, a proper
four-colouring has zero unsatisfied bonds, and a counted configuration has
exactly one unsatisfied bond. The count gives the degeneracy at that fixed
bond count, up to relabelling of the four states.
The theorem concerns finite canonical configuration counts.

The settling declaration is `HardinA229571.result`. The source bibkey is
`hardin2013a229571`.

## Route

1. **Literal count and difference field.** `KingGrid.T` imposes the source's
   conditions directly. For $n,k\ge2$, `KingGrid.labelled_eq_twentyFour`
   gives $L(n,k)=24T(n,k)$ for the labelled count. Identify the four colours
   with the Klein group $K=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2$. Horizontal and vertical
   differences satisfy the square cocycle equation, and
   `KingDifference.defectIntegrationEquiv` reconstructs a colouring from
   its difference field and its root colour. If $N_e$ counts fields with
   the unique zero bond at $e$, then `KingDifference.count_by_kind` gives
   $$
   N_H(N,M)+N_H(M,N)+2N_D(N,M)=6T(N+1,M+1)
   \qquad(N,M\ge1).
   $$

2. **Rainbow-block rigidity.** Every elementary block avoiding the defect
   has all four colours. `KingDifference.goodBlock_of_raw` forces opposite
   horizontal differences to agree, opposite vertical differences to agree,
   and the two directions to be distinct and nonzero. The horizontal and
   vertical slot equalities propagate this condition through all clear
   blocks.

3. **Diagonal slots.** A diagonal defect splits its exceptional horizontal
   slot into $x,y$ and its exceptional vertical slot into $y,x$, with $x,y$
   distinct and nonzero. The four ordinary words obey the cross exclusions
   in `KingDiagonal.SlotValid`. Encoding the third nonzero value by a binary
   choice gives two covering classes: either the vertical words or the
   horizontal words omit that value. Their intersection and empty sides
   are counted explicitly, yielding `KingDiagonal.N_D_closed` and the
   thin-strip diagonal count.

4. **Horizontal slots.** A boundary defect is in bijection with an ordered
   nonzero pair and compatible binary words. An interior defect is cut
   into two boundary assignments, with equality of their shared ordinary
   horizontal slots enforced by `KingHorizontal.interior_slot_equiv`.
   Binary-word supports and the six relative ordered-pair cases give
   `KingHorizontal.interior_count`; the position sums give
   `KingHorizontal.horizontal_sum` and the two- and three-row formulas.
   Transposition accounts for vertical defects and reflection for
   antidiagonal defects. These exact decompositions supply the closed
   counts used below.

5. **Diagonal closed count and recurrence.** `KingClosedForm.closed_form`
   gives the rectangular identity

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   4T(n,k)={}&(nk-n+6k+12)2^n+(nk-k+6n+12)2^k\\
            &+16nk-64n-64k-32,\qquad n,k\ge4.
   \end{aligned}
   $$

   `KingClosedForm.diagonal_closed` extends its diagonal specialization
   to $n=3$ using the width-three count:

   $$
   2T(n,n)=(n^2+5n+12)2^n+8n^2-64n-16,\qquad n\ge3.
   $$

   `HardinA229571.result` takes the indicated linear combination of this
   identity at $n,n-1,\ldots,n-6$. The smallest argument is $3$ when
   $n=9$, so the proof includes the published first instance.

## Falsifier

A natural number $n\ge9$ for which the literal canonical square count
violates the quoted six-step recurrence would contradict `HardinA229571.result`.
