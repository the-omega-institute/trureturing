# Hardin's recurrence for the $5\times n$ one-defect three-colour strip

## Problem

[OEIS A229590](https://oeis.org/A229590), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of defective 3-colorings of a 5 X n 0..2 array connected horizontally and antidiagonally with exactly one mistake, and colors introduced in row-major 0..2 order.

and the empirical recurrence

> Empirical: a(n) = 32*a(n-1) - 384*a(n-2) + 2200*a(n-3) - 6494*a(n-4) + 9016*a(n-5) - 816*a(n-6) - 14888*a(n-7) + 18879*a(n-8) - 5464*a(n-9) - 7472*a(n-10) + 8336*a(n-11) - 3648*a(n-12) + 768*a(n-13) - 64*a(n-14) for n > 18.

Both quotations are from the [OEIS text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A229590&fmt=text).
The assertion registered in
[issue #31329](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31329)
is this order-fourteen recurrence for every $n\ge 19$, with $5$ rows,
$n$ columns and exactly $1$ monochromatic bond. The sequence offset is
one. The source's `result t`, for every `t : Nat`, uses the shifted
index $n=t+19$ and has exactly that range.
This settlement completes #31329: its other sequence, A229692, is
settled in [#31527](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/pull/31527).

For an array $f:\operatorname{Fin}5\times\operatorname{Fin}n\to\operatorname{Fin}3$,
a defect is a bond whose endpoint colours agree. The HA bonds are
$(i,j)$--$(i,j+1)$ for $j+1<n$, and
$(i,j+1)$--$(i+1,j)$ for $i+1<5$ and $j+1<n$.
These horizontal and antidiagonal bonds make the $5\times n$ arrays
sheared square-lattice strips with staircase ends. Vertical and periodic
boundary bonds are absent in the original array coordinates.
The source sweeps columns with the transposed bonds, vertical plus
antidiagonal in the transfer coordinates. There are no bonds within a
swept column: the empty within-slice bond set and `between` encode exactly
the original HA graph. The pairs $(i,i)$ in `between` represent original
horizontal bonds, and $(i+1,i)$ represent antidiagonals.

A229590 is the five-row counterpart of the settled one-defect strips
A229588 and A229589, registered in
[#30838](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/30838).
Its zero-temperature block is the frozen five-row owner
`TraceAvoidingArrays/HardinA233260Literal`.

## Motivation

The settling declaration is
`D5/S3/StatisticalMechanics/Potts/StripDefectsWide/A229590/HardinA229590Strip.result`.
The external assertion is preregistered in
[issue #31329](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31329);
the source note has bibkey `hardin2013a229590`.

The coordinate change $x=i+j$, $y=i$ sends horizontal and antidiagonal
bonds to the two unit directions of the square lattice. With
antiferromagnetic Potts energy
$E(f)=J\sum_{\{u,v\}\in B}\mathbf{1}_{f(u)=f(v)}$ and $J>0$,
these are states of the three-state Potts antiferromagnet with exactly
$D=1$ unsatisfied bond on the sheared square strip (HA).
Row-major normalization chooses one representative from each global
colour relabelling orbit. In the nonconstant range used by the proof,
each such orbit has six labelled representatives. The theorem concerns
finite combinatorial counts under this bond and energy convention.

## Gap

The OEIS formula is labelled empirical. The registered target is its
validity for every $n\ge 19$ from the literal array definition;
agreement with a finite list of sequence terms does not supply that statement.

The literature check (the OEIS entry and its links; arXiv searches for Potts strips with unsatisfied bonds and for defective colourings; Chang–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 107 (2002) 1207–1253, which treats rectangular square-lattice strips) located no proof of this recurrence.
The literature scope includes the sources recorded in #31329.
Worldwide priority and literature completeness remain unverified.

## Route

1. **Literal count and normalization.** `a` filters every original
   `Fin 5 × Fin n → Fin 3` array by the exact HA defect count and the
   lifted row-major canonical predicate.
   `DefectTransfer.relabel_defects` supplies colour-permutation
   invariance. `scale_count` applies
   `HardinA233177Canonical.canonical_pattern_card` to this original
   row-major domain. `Equiv.prodComm` and `Equiv.arrowCongr` relate
   the labelled transfer domain to the original arrays; the column
   sweep does not change the canonical order.
   A constant array with $m+1$ columns has $9m$ monochromatic HA
   bonds, which cannot equal one. Thus every accepted array is
   nonconstant, and the labelled count satisfies
   $L(m+1)=6a(m+1)$ for every $m:\mathbb N$.

2. **Exact-budget block extension.** A swept column has type
   `Fin 5 → Fin 3`; `boundary` records all five entries as the frozen
   owner's tuple type `Col`. Appending column $r$ after boundary $p$
   costs `cross p r = bondDefects between (column p) r`.
   The remaining budget $d\in\{0,1\}$ decreases by this cost,
   which must be at most $d$.
   The classifier `cl` uses `Sum.inl (cls p)` for budget one and
   `Sum.inr (cls p)` for budget zero. Both sectors use the frozen
   41-class classifier of `HardinA233260Literal`, giving 82 states.
   In this order,
   $T=\begin{pmatrix}Q&R\\0&Q\end{pmatrix}$,
   where the frozen zero-temperature matrix $Q$ counts zero-defect
   transitions, and $R$ counts transitions with exactly one defect.
   The initial vector is $(\mathtt{classWeights},0)$ and the terminal
   vector is $(0,\mathbf 1)$, so accepted paths spend exactly one defect.

3. **Transition and initial identities.**
   `HardinA229590StripNew.quotient_rows` proves the $R$-block
   transition counts. Its `representative_rows` uses finite
   `decide +kernel` on the 41 frozen representatives;
   `class_rep`, `class_relabel` and colour-permutation invariance
   transport the identity to every labelled boundary.
   `HardinA229590StripCut.transition_counts` separates zero-cost
   and one-cost transitions. The zero-cost cases directly reuse
   `HardinA233260Literal.quotient_rows`; the one-cost case uses
   the new $R$ identity. `initial_counts` reuses the frozen
   `initial_class_counts`. In the result module, `count_transfer`
   applies `DefectTransfer.lumped_count` with `Valid` given by
   $d\le1$, yielding $L(m+1)=vT^m e$, where
   $v=\mathtt{initial}$ and $e=\mathtt{terminal}$.

4. **Suffix vectors and inherited powers.**
   `HardinA229590StripInjection.injection_powers` identifies the
   tabulated vectors `RU k` with $RQ^k\mathbf 1$ for $0\le k<18$.
   Its base values are finite identities by definitional reduction.
   The remaining values follow from
   `TransferMatrix.scalarRelationShift` applied to the frozen
   shift-four, degree-seven relation of `HardinA233260Literal`.
   `HardinA229590StripPowers.one_steps` checks
   $U_{k+1}=QU_k+RU_k$ by definitional reduction.
   `powers_table` then proves
   $T^k e=(U_k,Q^k\mathbf 1)$ for $0\le k<19$.
   `power_polynomial` proves
   $T^{18}e=\sum_{j=0}^{13}c_jT^{4+j}e$, with
   $(c_0,\ldots,c_{13})=(-64,768,-3648,8336,-7472,-5464,18879,-14888,-816,9016,-6494,2200,-384,32)$.
   The one-budget component is a finite identity by definitional
   reduction; the zero-budget component is a linear combination
   of shifted instances of the frozen degree-seven relation.
   `HardinA229590Strip.finite_certificate` assembles the transition,
   initial and degree-fourteen terminal-vector identities.

5. **Propagation and starting index.** Inside `result`, applying
   $w\mapsto vT^t w$ to the terminal-vector relation propagates it
   through every further power. The local `bridge` combines
   `count_transfer` and `scale_count` to identify
   $vT^m e=6a(m+1)$.
   Cancelling six in $\mathbb Z$ gives the displayed recurrence
   at every index at least $4+14+1=19$. This route uses the
   terminal-vector identity directly; it requires no recurrence
   minimality or full characteristic-polynomial assertion.

## Falsifier

A canonical HA count at some $n\ge19$ that violated the displayed
recurrence would falsify the registered assertion and contradict the
source's `result`. A mismatch in the transition sums, initial
multiplicities, suffix-vector steps or terminal-vector polynomial
would invalidate the finite transfer certificate.
