# Sun Conjecture 4.2(i) absolute-difference permanent congruences

## Problem

Zhi-Wei Sun, *Arithmetic properties of some permanents*, arXiv:2108.07723v7,
Conjecture 4.2(i), equations (4.3) and (4.4), asks whether for every odd prime
$p$ the permanents of the matrices with entries $|j-k|$ and $|j-k+1|$ are
congruent to $-1/2$ and $1/2$ modulo $p$, respectively. The integer encoding is
$A_s(j,k)=|((j.val:\mathbb Z)+1)-((k.val:\mathbb Z)+1)+s|$ for
$j,k : \operatorname{Fin} p$ and $s=0,1$.

### Statement echo

The source permanent is the finite sum over permutations of products of
row-indexed entries. The Lean claim clears the invertible denominator 2 and
states both divisibilities for the same universally quantified odd prime.

## Motivation

The result settles the two explicitly numbered congruences in the source's
open-conjecture section and gives a uniform prime-order mechanism for both
absolute-difference shifts.

## Gap

The source left both congruences conjectural. The bounded literature screen in
issue #22003 found the Brualdi--Newman prime circulant congruence as the classical
ingredient, but no prior proof of this absolute-difference pair. Literature
completeness and worldwide priority remain `ASSUMED-UNVERIFIED`.

## Route

For odd prime $p$, alternating row and column signs reduce each absolute-
difference matrix modulo $p$ to a circulant. Prime-order conjugation of
permutation weights cancels nonfixed orbits in characteristic $p$; the fixed
permutations are translations. Fermat's identity evaluates their weights, and
the alternating sum over $0,\ldots,2m$ with $p=2m+1$ is $m+s$. Clearing the
factor 2 gives the two integer divisibilities.

## Falsifier

The settled claim is
\[
\forall p\in\mathbb N,\;p\text{ prime}\to p\text{ odd}\to
((p:\mathbb Z)\mid 2\operatorname{per}(A_0)+1)\land
((p:\mathbb Z)\mid 2\operatorname{per}(A_1)-1).
\]
Its falsifier would be an odd prime and one failed divisibility.

### Neighbouring assertions proved in prose

The following arguments establish the neighbouring claims without using the
finite experiment above. They are paper proofs, not additional Lean theorems.

For $s\in\{0,1\}$ put $f_s(u)=(-1)^u(u+s)$ for the representative
$u\in\{0,\ldots,8\}$, with values in $\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z$. The odd-order
entry identity of `sign_entry`, together with `permanent_sign` and
`permanent_map`, identifies the reduction of $\operatorname{per}(A_s)$ at
order $9$ with the permanent of the circulant with entries $f_s(x-y)$.
Those three helpers require no primality assumption.

Let $t(x)=x+1$ on $\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z$ and conjugate permutations by $t$.
The weight $w(\sigma)=\prod_x f_s(\sigma(x)-x)$ is constant on each orbit.
The orbit sizes divide $9$. Orbits of size $9$ contribute zero modulo $9$;
the union of the other orbits is exactly the set of permutations commuting
with $t^3$. Hence the circulant permanent equals the sum of $w$ over this set.

Every permutation commuting with $t^3$ has a unique description
$\sigma(r+3k)=\pi(r)+3(k+a_r)$, with $r,k\in\{0,1,2\}$,
$\pi\in S_3$, and each $a_r\in\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z$. Conversely every
such choice is a commuting permutation. Its weight is
$\prod_r f_s(\pi(r)-r+3a_r)^3$, where arguments are reduced modulo $9$.
Summing independently over the three $a_r$ reduces the answer to the permanent
of the $3\times3$ matrix $B_s(r,q)=b_s(q-r)$, where indices are modulo $3$ and
$b_s(d)=\sum_{a=0}^2 f_s(d+3a)^3$.

The six entries are obtained by the explicit integer sums
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_0(0)&=0^3+(-3)^3+6^3\equiv0,&
b_0(1)&=(-1)^3+4^3+(-7)^3\equiv-1,&
b_0(2)&=2^3+(-5)^3+8^3\equiv-1,\\
b_1(0)&=1^3+(-4)^3+7^3\equiv1,&
b_1(1)&=(-2)^3+5^3+(-8)^3\equiv1,&
b_1(2)&=3^3+(-6)^3+9^3\equiv0
\pmod9.
\end{aligned}
\]
For $B_0$, only the two $3$-cycles contribute, giving
$\operatorname{per}(B_0)=2(-1)^3\equiv7\pmod9$.
For $B_1$, only the identity and one $3$-cycle contribute, each with weight
$1$, giving $\operatorname{per}(B_1)\equiv2\pmod9$.
Thus the two cleared expressions are congruent to $2\cdot7+1\equiv6$ and
$2\cdot2-1\equiv3$ modulo $9$, respectively. Both are nonzero.
This proves failure of the odd-composite extension at order $9$; it makes no
claim about other composite orders or exact integer permanent values.

For the shift-$2$ entry identity take $p=3$, $i=0$, $j=1$. Its left side is
$(-1)^{0+1}|0-1+2|=-1\equiv2\pmod3$.
The representative of $i-j$ is $2$, so the right side is
$(-1)^2(2+2)=4\equiv1\pmod3$. The entries are unequal.

### What the settlement shows

**Proved by `result`:** both absolute-difference permanent congruences hold for
every odd prime. The decisive mechanism is the alternating-sign reduction to a
prime circulant, cancellation of nonfixed permutation-weight orbits, and the
fixed-translation evaluation.

**Scope:** the entry reduction is proved for odd natural orders and shifts
$s=0,1$; the counting step requires primality. The argument does not settle
other shifts, composite moduli or prime-power strengthening.

**Effect on the source:** Conjecture 4.2(i), including (4.3) and (4.4), is
settled positively. Part (ii) is a separate assertion addressed by the screened Jiang--Sun paper;
its Lean formalization is outside this delivery.
