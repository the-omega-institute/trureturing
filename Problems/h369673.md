# Hanna's A369673 square classification and five-adic orders

## Problem

Paul D. Hanna's [OEIS A369673](https://oeis.org/A369673/internal), revision #21,
states four conjectures for
$a(N)=\prod_{k=0}^{N}(2^k+2^{N-k})$:

- (C.1) $a(N)$ is a square if and only if $4\nmid N$.
- (C.2) $5\nmid a(2n+1)$.
- (C.3) $v_5(a(4n))=2b(n)$.
- (C.4) $v_5(a(4n+2))=2b(n+1)$.

Here $b(n)=v_5((10n)!)-v_5((5n)!)$ is the signed integer difference
in [OEIS A127428](https://oeis.org/A127428/internal). All indices are natural,
including zero. The valuation agrees with [A112765](https://oeis.org/A112765/internal)
on positive arguments. The source, conventions and literature screen are in
[preregistration #21915](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/21915).

## Route

Equal boundary factors give the two-step recurrence
$a(N+2)=2^{N+1}(1+2^{N+2})^2a(N)$.
For $Q(m)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+4^j)$, induction gives
$a(2m)=2^{m^2+1}Q(m)^2$.
Positivity and oddness of $Q(m)$ identify the exact two-adic order.
At odd indices the same recurrence preserves squareness and excludes divisibility
by five. The imported `Erdos7.finset_prod_odd_nat` supplies oddness of the auxiliary
product. Mathlib's `padicValNat.pow_add_pow` supplies the odd-exponent lifting step;
`padicValNat_factorial_mul` identifies the resulting sum with $b$.

## Falsifier

A failure at any natural index of one of the four conjuncts contradicts the
claim. Index zero is included: $a(0)=2$, $b(0)=0$ and $v_5(a(0))=0$.
Finite agreement alone does not prove any universal assertion.
