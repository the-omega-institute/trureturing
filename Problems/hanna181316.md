> Conjecture: exp( Sum_{n>=1} (q-1)*((q^n-1)/(q-1))^(n-1)*x^n/n ) is an integer series for all integer q>1.

$q>1$ and every natural $N\ge0$.

## Route

For a prime $p$ and $n>0$, put $a=v_p(n)$. Prove
$b_q(pn)\equiv b_q(n)\pmod{p^{a+1}}$.

If $p\mid q-1$, geometric block factorization gives
$p^a\mid S_q(p^at)$. When $a=0$, the factor $q-1$ suffices. When $a>0$,
$n\ge2$, so $p^a$ divides each relevant positive power of the geometric sum;
the factor $q-1$ supplies one additional $p$. Both ghost terms vanish modulo
$p^{a+1}$.

If $p\nmid q-1$, the existing uniform Fermat lift gives
$q^{pn}\equiv q^n\pmod{p^{a+1}}$, including $p\mid q$. Cancellation of
$q-1$ yields $S_q(pn)\equiv S_q(n)$. If $p\nmid S_q(n)$, Euler's theorem
applies because $(p-1)n$ is a multiple of $\varphi(p^{a+1})$. Otherwise write
$n=p^at$. The case $t=1$ contradicts Frobenius $q^{p^a}=q$ modulo $p$ and
the geometric identity. Hence $t\ge2$, giving $n-1\ge a+1$ and making both
powers zero modulo $p^{a+1}$. There is no odd-prime assumption.

Apply `DoldExponentialIntegrality.integral_exp_of_dold` directly. The
geometric identity identifies its logarithmic input with the literal source
formula. Finally `Int.toNat_of_nonneg` converts every allowed integer q to
the natural parameter used in the arithmetic proof. Euler-product exponents
need not be nonnegative; dynamical realizability is not asserted.
