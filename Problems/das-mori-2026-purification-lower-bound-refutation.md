# A full-rank qutrit refutes the purification lower bound on Krylov operator complexity

## Problem

R. N. Das and T. Mori, *Krylov complexity of purification*, Phys. Rev. Lett. 136, 030201 (2026), arXiv:2408.00826v4, compare the Krylov operator complexity C_K(ρ(t)) of a mixed state ρ(t) = U_t ρ U_t† (U_t = e^{−iHt}) with complexities of its purifications.
- **The time-dependent purification.** |Ψ^{U*}_ρ(t)⟩ = (U_t ⊗ U_t^*)|Ψ_ρ⟩ (Eq. (2)), generated by H ⊗ I − I ⊗ H^*.
- **The complexities.** C_S is the spread complexity Σ_n n|ψ_n(t)|² of a state in its Lanczos basis (Eq. (S.4)), and C_K is its operator analogue for the Liouvillian L = [H, ·] with the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product (Eqs. (S.5)–(S.7)).
- **The conjecture.** "Krylov operator complexity of the mixed state is upper bounded by the operator complexity of the time-independent purification and is lower bounded by the state complexity of the time-dependent purification, C_S(|Ψ^{U*}_ρ(t)⟩) ≤ C_K(ρ(t)) ≤ C_K(|Ψ^I_ρ(t)⟩) (4)". The authors confirm it analytically for one-qubit states and numerically for Werner states.

The verbatim statements are in [the literature note](../Library/QuantumBounds/das2026krylovpurification.md).

Issue [#16717](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/16717) fixes the reading.
- The claim is the left inequality of Eq. (4), for every full-rank density matrix, Hermitian H and t ≥ 0. Full rank is a special case of the source's statement, so its failure refutes the source's.
- For full-rank ρ, |Ψ_ρ⟩ is the canonical purification Σ_{ij}(√ρ)_{ij}|i⟩|j⟩.
- The left inequality is refuted.

## Motivation

The left inequality would make the purified state's spread complexity a lower bound on the mixed state's operator complexity, which the authors use to interpret C_K(ρ(t)) through a pure state. A qutrit for which the purified state spreads further shows that the bound is not a general property of purification, although the source proves it for one-qubit states.

## Route

Take ρ = diag(1, 4, 16)/21, H = [[2, 1, 0], [1, 2, 0], [0, 0, 4]] and t = π/2.
1. **The purification.** √ρ = diag(1, 2, 4)/√21, so |Ψ_ρ⟩ = (|00⟩ + 2|11⟩ + 4|22⟩)/√21. H has eigenvalues 1, 3, 4, and U = e^{−iHπ/2} = [[0, i, 0], [i, 0, 0], [0, 0, 1]].
2. **The two Lanczos chains.** Put F = [[0, 1, 0], [−1, 0, 0], [0, 0, 0]], and for A = D = diag(1, 4, 16) or A = S = diag(1, 2, 4) take Q₀ = A/‖A‖, Q₁ = F/√2 and Q₂ = T_A/‖T_A‖ with T_A = L²(A) − β_A A. Then LQ₀ = b₁Q₁, LQ₁ = b₁Q₀ + b₂Q₂ and LQ₂ = b₂Q₁, with (b₁², b₂²) = (6/91, 358/91) for D and (2/21, 82/21) for S. Both chains stop after three vectors. Vectorization maps L to H ⊗ 1 − 1 ⊗ Hᵀ, so the S chain is the chain of the purified state.
3. **The values.** UAU† = A − L²(A)/2 at t = π/2, so the evolved vector lies on Lanczos indices 0 and 2, with index-0 amplitudes 88/91 for D and 20/21 for S. Hence C_K(ρ(π/2)) = 2[1 − (88/91)²] = 1074/8281 and C_S(|Ψ^{U*}_ρ(π/2)⟩) = 2[1 − (20/21)²] = 82/441, and C_S − C_K = 4192/74529 > 0.

## Falsifier

The refutation would not hold if:
- the module's complexities were not the source's spread and Krylov operator complexities;
- its purified state or its generator differed from the source's time-dependent purification;
- the witness were not a full-rank density matrix with a Hermitian Hamiltonian at a time t ≥ 0.

The module defines both complexities as Σ_n n|⟨K_n, e^{−iLt}v̂⟩|² with K_n the Gram–Schmidt orthonormalization of the Krylov sequence of the normalized vector v̂, the operator space vectorized row-major with the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product, L = H ⊗ 1 − 1 ⊗ Hᵀ, and the purified state the vectorization of √ρ. The witness is positive definite with trace 1, H is real symmetric, and t = π/2.

### What the settlement shows

**Proved by `result`:** the left inequality of Eq. (4) fails: for ρ = diag(1, 4, 16)/21, H = [[2, 1, 0], [1, 2, 0], [0, 0, 4]] and t = π/2, C_S(|Ψ^{U*}_ρ(t)⟩) = 82/441 > 1074/8281 = C_K(ρ(t)).

**Computed, not formalized** (orchestrator, NumPy/SciPy, a generic Lanczos implementation with full reorthogonalization, 14 October 2026):
- for this witness C_S > C_K at all 600 sampled times in (0, 6], so the violation is not isolated in time;
- 5 of 300 random qutrit triples (ρ, H, t) violate the inequality, so it is not isolated in state space;
- control: over 500 random qubit states, Hamiltonians and times, max(C_S − C_K) = −8.8 × 10⁻⁶, reproducing the source's proved qubit case.

**Argued, not formalized.**
- The mechanism of this witness: both Krylov chains have length three and the evolution at t = π/2 moves weight only between Lanczos indices 0 and 2, so each complexity is 2(1 − c²), with c the index-0 amplitude. The purified state starts from √ρ, whose spectrum diag(1, 2, 4) is less spread than ρ's diag(1, 4, 16); its index-0 amplitude 20/21 is smaller than ρ's 88/91, so its spread complexity is larger. The values are formalized; the reading of them as a spectral-spread effect is not.

**Open.**
- Whether the right inequality of Eq. (4), C_K(ρ(t)) ≤ C_K(|Ψ^I_ρ(t)⟩), holds in general; this settlement does not address it.
- The authors' conjectures 2 and 3: the approximately constant ratio C_S(|Ψ^{U*}_ρ(t)⟩)/C_K(ρ(t)), and C_K(|Ψ^{U*,I}_ρ(t)⟩) ≥ 2C_S(|Ψ^{U*,I}_ρ(t)⟩) for the same purification scheme, where C_K is the operator complexity of the purified state, not of ρ(t).
- A characterization of the states and Hamiltonians for which the left inequality holds.

**Effect on the paper.** The lower bound of Eq. (4) is not a general property of purification; it holds for one-qubit states (as the source proves) but fails already for a full-rank qutrit. The Werner-state evidence of the source concerns a family for which the inequality happens to hold.
