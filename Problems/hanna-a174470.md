## Route

For a prime $p$ and $m>0$, prove

$$
p^{v_p(m)+1}\mid c(pm)-c(m).
$$

At $p=3$, $v_3(m)+1\le m\le3m$, so the required power divides both logarithmic weights. At $p\ne3$, multiplication by $p$ preserves $v_3(m)$ and hence $b(pm)=b(m)$. Write $m=p^{v_p(m)}u$. Euler's congruence modulo $p^{v_p(m)+1}$ gives $3^{(p-1)m}\equiv1$, and

$$
c(pm)-c(m)=b(m)3^m\bigl(3^{(p-1)m}-1\bigr).
$$

The proof uses `padicValNat.mul`, `padicValNat.eq_zero_of_not_dvd`, `Nat.padicValNat_lt_self`, `Nat.ModEq.pow_totient`, and `Nat.totient_prime_pow_succ`. The private helpers combine the two prime cases into `Dold c`. `DoldExponentialIntegrality.integral_exp_of_dold` gives every integral coefficient. The formal exponential itself witnesses existence.
