# A null direct summand in a tensor power of a singular semigroup module

## Problem

David He and Daniel Tubbenhauer, *Tensor powers of representations of (diagram) monoids*, arXiv:2508.04054v1, Conjecture 4.2, page 17:

> Let V be a kS-module on which no element acts invertibly, then there is some m ≥ 1 such that Z is a summand of V^{⊗m}, and consequently a(n)=(dim V)ⁿ.

The field is algebraically closed, the semigroup is nonempty and finite with no two-sided identity, and the representation is finite dimensional and nonunital. The one-dimensional null representation has zero action. The source defines $a(n)$ as an asymptotic equivalent of the indecomposable-summand count $b(n)$, with multiplicity. Thus the final clause means $b(n)/(\dim V)^n\to1$, rather than exact equality at finite exponents. Preregistration: [#22531](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/22531).

## Route

For an operator family on $W$, write $K=\bigcap_s\ker a_s$ and $I=\sum_s\operatorname{im}a_s$. The private `split_common_null` constructs a complement $L$ of $K\cap I$ in $K$ and an ambient complement $U$ of $L$ containing $I$. Thus every action vanishes on $L$ and preserves $U$. The compiled dimension identity is $\dim L+\dim(K\cap I)=\dim K$.

The common-image and common-kernel dimension bounds yield a null line whenever $2\sum_s\operatorname{rank}a_s<\dim W$. Tensor ranks are bounded by individual-rank powers. Singularity makes these ranks strictly smaller than $d=\dim V$, so geometric decay gives a sufficient positive exponent. Strong induction on dimension constructs indecomposable decompositions. The private count bounds hold for every such decomposition and yield the universal ratio limit by squeezing.

## Falsifier

A representation satisfying every hypothesis of `claim` and violating its positive-power existence or universal ratio limit would contradict the kernel-checked settling declaration. The finite experiments test the splitting construction independently; their finite scope does not establish the universal conclusion.

4. **Computed:** the immutable experiment above verifies 1023 nonempty singular $\mathbf F_2$ matrix families in 3069 tensor cases and 25 Jordan-chain cases, with the stated command, exit code and SHA-256.
5. **Open:** universal formal sharpness of the Jordan-family formula and a general infinite-semigroup extension are not supplied. The finite Jordan computations do not establish either extension.
6. **Open:** the separate monoid group-summand conjecture for invertible actions is not settled by this argument.
7. **Proved in prose from the compiled bounds:** for any $m\ge1$ with $2|S|(d-1)^m<d^m$, `tensor_rank_sum_bound` bounds twice the tensor-rank sum by $2|S|(d-1)^m$, while `tensor_power_finrank` identifies the ambient dimension with $d^m$. The kernel-checked `exists_power_separation` supplies such an exponent and `exists_null_tensor_power` applies it. The upper bound `tensor_power_range_le` suffices without a separate rank-equality theorem.
8. **Open:** the exact maximal-null-multiplicity identity is not separately formalized; the compiled complement identity and rank/count bounds suffice for the unchanged target.
