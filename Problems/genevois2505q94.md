# Genevois Question 9.4 on intersections of parabolic subgroups

## Problem

Anthony Genevois, *Beyond graph products and cactus groups: quandle products of groups*,
arXiv:2505.09194v1, §9, printed p. 47, asks:

> In a quandle product, is the intersection between two arbitrary parabolic subgroups again a parabolic subgroup?

A standard parabolic is generated by the factor images for any subset of the index set.
A parabolic is any conjugate of one of these subgroups in the quandle product. Both input
subsets and both conjugators are arbitrary. [Versioned PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.09194v1#page=47)

## Motivation

The question asks whether subgroup intersection stays within the class of parabolics
for every system of Definition 2.4. The two-factor case of Example 2.6 isolates the
effect of the upper-factor action on the intersection of two conjugates of the same
standard parabolic. [Issue #31669](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31669) · [Prospective registration](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31669#issuecomment-7058520944)

## Route

Index the cyclic groups C16 and C4 by the lower and upper elements of a two-element
chain with empty orthogonality. The upper element of additive coordinate j acts on the
lower factor by multiplication by 13 to the power j. This defines the lower action
because 13 has order four modulo 16. The three-index law is vacuous. Example 2.6 specifies an action and then prints an empty
action slot. The presentation here uses the specified action and the relators of
Definition 2.4. The twist in the
normal-closure quotient is b⁻¹ab = b ⋆ a for lower a and upper b.

Write a and t for the images of the cyclic generators and H for the upper-factor
subgroup. The twist makes a² commute with t² since 13² · 2 ≡ 2 modulo 16. Thus the
actual intersection H ∩ a²Ha⁻² contains t². There is a homomorphism to the existing
semidirect group with forward multiplier 5 since 5⁻¹ ≡ 13 modulo 16 and the factor
inclusions satisfy the quotient relators. If an upper element equals the conjugate by
a² of another upper element, the coordinates in this group give the same upper
exponent j and force 2 − 2 · 5ʲ ≡ 0 modulo 16. This excludes j = 1 and j = 3.
The top image of the actual intersection consists of residues 0 and 2. Every
standard parabolic projects to C4 or the trivial subgroup according to whether
its defining subset contains the upper index. The projection is invariant under
conjugation because its target is abelian. This excludes every parabolic.

## Triage

`theorem`. Question 9.4 has a negative answer.

### What the settlement shows

- **Kernel-checked projection and its consequence.** The projected intersection is the
  order-two subgroup of C4. The kernel of the top projection is trivial on H because H
  is the range of the upper-factor map and the projection is a left inverse there.
  Consequently the actual intersection is exactly the subgroup generated by t².
  The last identification follows directly from the checked image and left inverse.
- **Mathematical consequence for this witness.** The paragraph following Question 9.4
  asks for an inclusion from the stabilizer of the gated projection into both coset
  stabilizers. Here that projection of a²H onto H equals H, and the predicted
  inclusion fails. To see this in the factor-generated right Cayley graph, the
  twist puts each word in the form aⁱtʲ and the factor-respecting model map
  distinguishes these forms. A lower-factor edge changes only the lower coordinate
  and can change it to any value at a fixed j. An upper-factor edge changes only the
  upper coordinate and can change it to any value at a fixed i. Thus this graph is
  the Cartesian product of the two complete factor graphs. The gate of a²tʲ in H
  is tʲ, so the projection is all of H, with its upper-factor edge labels.
  The stabilizer of H under left multiplication is H, whereas the intersection
  of the two stabilizers is the order-two subgroup already computed. This argument
  addresses the reverse stabilizer inclusion for these two gated cosets.
  [Versioned PDF, p. 47](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.09194v1#page=47)
- **Consequence of the source's Corollary 3.7.** When the input standard parabolics
  come from stable subsets R and S and are conjugated by the same g, their intersection
  is g⟨R ∩ S⟩g⁻¹. Conjugation is an automorphism and distributes over subgroup
  intersection. The upper singleton in the two-factor witness is stable, and the
  witness uses two different conjugates. The paragraph introducing Question 9.4
  cites Corollary 3.6, which is the word-problem result. The applicable standard-parabolic
  statement is Corollary 3.7. [Versioned PDF, pp. 15 and 47](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.09194v1#page=15)
