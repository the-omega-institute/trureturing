# Hardin and Barker's two-column binary ladder formulas

## Problem

[OEIS A240513](https://oeis.org/A240513), revision 11, has NAME

> Number of n X 2 0..1 arrays with no element equal to exactly two horizontal and vertical neighbors, with new values 0..1 introduced in row major order.

and its first two FORMULA fields state

> Empirical: a(n) = 2*a(n-1) + a(n-2) - a(n-3) - 2*a(n-4) + a(n-5).

> Empirical g.f.: x*(2 - x)*(1 - x^2 - 2*x^3) / ((1 - x)*(1 - x - 2*x^2 - x^3 + x^4)). - _Colin Barker_, Feb 24 2018

The sequence is R. H. Hardin's, with offset 1. Boards have $n$ labeled rows and
two labeled columns, binary entries, open boundaries and orthogonal adjacency
only. For a binary alphabet, introducing new values in row-major order means
exactly that the first entry is $0$; the value $1$ need not occur. The
recurrence is claimed for every $n\ge6$ in signed integer arithmetic. The
series $F(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}a(n)x^n$ has zero constant term.

## Motivation

The settling declaration is
`D5.S3.Combinatorics.TraceAvoidingArrays.HardinA240513Ladder.result`. The
external assertion and conventions are preregistered in
[issue #26893](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/26893).
The source note is `Library/RecurrenceComposition/hardinbarker2014a240513.md`.
Physically, a board is a two-leg ladder of Ising spins with a local exclusion
rule, and $a(n)$ counts its admissible configurations up to the global spin
flip.

## Gap

At revision 11 both formulas are still marked empirical. The preregistration's
bounded literature screen found no proof: arXiv searches for A240513 and

priority are `ASSUMED-UNVERIFIED`.

## Route

States are ordered pairs of consecutive rows, sixteen in all, with no symmetry
quotient. A boundary row compares its two available neighbors, and an interior
row compares its three. Splitting the finite board space at the first row turns
the literal count into a continuation sum. Induction on the number of remaining
rows identifies that sum with the iterates $v_k$ of a fixed linear transfer
step, so $a(k+2)$ is a weighted total of $v_k$.

A finite certificate shows that $v_5-2v_4-v_3+v_2+2v_1-v_0$ vanishes on all
sixteen states. Linearity propagates the identity to every shift, which gives
the recurrence for every $n\ge7$, since $a(k+2)$ is the total of $v_k$. The
initial values $a(1),\ldots,a(6)=2,3,6,10,21,42$ settle $n=6$. Comparing
coefficients then gives the series identity.

## Falsifier

A board count violating the recurrence at some $n\ge6$, or a coefficient
mismatch in the series identity, would contradict `result`.

### What the settlement shows

The order-five recurrence comes from a single annihilating polynomial of the
transfer step. That polynomial already kills the state vector, not just the
scalar total. Together with one initial check at $n=6$, the recurrence holds
from the first index the source states.
The denominator $(1-x)(1-x-2x^2-x^3+x^4)$ factors off the trivial root $x=1$.
This matches the constant offset in Mathar's empirical relation to A105309,
whose printed g.f. has the quartic factor as its denominator. That relation
remains open here (item 3).
