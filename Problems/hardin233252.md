# Barker's identities for the four-column antidiagonal pair-exclusion strip

## Problem

[OEIS A233252](https://oeis.org/A233252), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of n X 4 0..5 arrays with no element x(i,j) adjacent to itself or value 5-x(i,j) horizontally or antidiagonally, top left element zero, and 1 appearing before 2 3 and 4, and 2 appearing before 3 in row major order (unlabelled 6-colorings with no clashing color pairs).

with Hardin's empirical recurrence $a(n)=160a(n-1)-2304a(n-2)$ and Colin
Barker's conjectured generating function $2x(5-208x)/((1-16x)(1-144x))$
and closed form $a(n)=2^{4n-3}(4\cdot9^n+9)/9$ (verbatim in
[issue #27709](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/27709)).

## Route

1. **Width-generic bridge.** The frozen `HardinA233251TriangleStrip`
   machinery at width four reduces the literal count to three boundary
   classes: colour two unseen; colour two seen with equal boundary
   endpoints; colour two seen with unequal endpoints.
2. **Transfer.** The class transition matrix has rows $(16,32,40)$,
   $(0,80,80)$, $(0,64,64)$ and the initial vector is $(4,2,4)$; a finite
   certificate supplies these counts and an induction on the height gives
   the three identities.

## Falsifier

$2^{4n-3}(4\cdot9^n+9)/9$ would contradict `result`.

## Triage

1. **Proved (kernel-checked):** Hardin's recurrence for every $n\ge3$ and
   Barker's generating function and closed form, by
   `HardinA233252TriangleStrip.result`.
2. **Proved (from two kernel-checked closed forms):** $A233252(n) =
   A233257(2n)$ for every $n\ge1$: the $n\times4$ strip and the
   $2\times2n$ strip with the same bonds have equal counts, since
   $2^{4n-3}(4\cdot9^n+9)/9$ is the A233257 closed form
   $2^{2m-3}(4\cdot3^m+9)/9$ at $m=2n$ (`HardinA233257Strip.result`).
3. **Open:** a direct bijection between the $n\times4$ and $2\times2n$
   arrays explaining item 2, and whether $T(n,2k)=T(k,2n)$-type
   coincidences hold elsewhere in A233256.

### What the settlement shows

The transition matrix is block triangular: the unseen class has
eigenvalue $16=2^4$, and
the seen block with rows $(80,80)$, $(64,64)$ has trace $144$ and
determinant $0$, giving eigenvalues $144$ and $0$. Hence the two roots
$16$ and $144=16\cdot9$ of the recurrence (proved: item 1 and the matrix).
