# Shi–Yau–Zuo Conjecture 4.79

## Problem

Section 4.6, printed English p. 1217 and original p. 222 (PDF p. 33 in both editions).

> Conjecture 4.79. Let F be an arbitrary field, let A be a local Noetherian F-algebra with maximal ideal m, and let Q be a fixed T-map. Suppose A/m ≃ F Then, for an ideal I ◁ A, I is T-principal if and only if I is T-full and Δ(I) is T-dependent.

The Lean proposition quantifies over the field, the commutative local Noetherian algebra, the existence of an algebra equivalence from its quotient residue field to the ground field, every T-map, and every ideal. The T-map includes the finite family of linear endomorphisms and the condition Q(af) ⊆ Q(f). Definitions 4.1, 4.6, 4.8, 4.66 and 4.68, Remark 4.3 and the paragraph after Example 4.5 specify the terms. The Lean predicate existentially binds a finite generating tuple of Δ(I), and the colon test uses that tuple’s variables. Proposition 4.69 asserts tuple independence, but its change of generators uses coefficients in A and need not commute with the F-linear components. Two minimal generating tuples can have different colon tests. [Source note](../Library/Certificates/shiyauzuo2024tmaps.md)

## Motivation

The conjecture asks whether a generic polynomial criterion produces an actual generator over the residue field. The counterexample separates a nonzero polynomial over that field from a polynomial that takes a nonzero value at one of its rational tuples.

## Gap

Lemmas 4.72–4.73 select a rational projective point by using the infinitude of the residue field. Remark 4.74 identifies the failure of the intersection property over finite fields. The binary example makes that failure occur in the colon of a T-map satisfying the original conjecture's hypotheses.

## Route

Use the square-zero extension of the two-element field by a vector space with three coordinate axes e₀, e₁ and e₂. Let I be the scalar projection's kernel. The three linear component maps keep the scalar part and put u, v or u + v on their respective axes, where u and v are the first two vector coordinates. On I, multiplication by an element acts through that element's scalar part. On units the value ideal is the whole ring. This establishes the T-map multiplication law.

Every component preserves the scalar part, and each axis occurs as a component value on an element of I. Thus Δ(I) = Q(I) = I. Every ideal-generating tuple of I spans it over the binary field because the ring acts on I through its scalar part. Consequently each of u, v and u + v is nonzero on some tuple member. Their corresponding forms U, V and U + V in the generic variables are all nonzero after residue reduction. The generic ideal is generated by C(e₀)U, C(e₁)V and C(e₂)(U + V). The complementary factors of P = UV(U + V) put P times each extended generator of I in the generic ideal. The reduced P is nonzero in the polynomial domain over the binary field, so P belongs to the colon and lies outside mS. This holds for every finite generating tuple of I.

Every binary pair makes at least one of u, v and u + v zero. Ring multiplication preserves the corresponding missing coordinate for elements of I, so their value ideals are proper subideals of I. Unit inputs give the whole ring. Accordingly I is T-full and its antiderivative ideal is T-dependent, while I is never a value ideal.

## Falsifier

The example would fail if the component maps violated the T-map law, the scalar projection did not induce the claimed residue-field equivalence, either ideal equality failed, P failed either colon test, or any input had value ideal I.

## Triage

The four-dimensional binary algebra with square-zero maximal ideal refutes sufficiency under the existential, prescribed-tuple, and every-tuple readings of the arbitrary-field biconditional. For an infinite residue field, the sufficiency argument of Theorem 4.76 applies to any tuple passing the colon test. Evaluate at a ground-field tuple where the colon witness has nonzero residue. Its value is a unit, so the evaluated generic image equals I by the colon inclusion and fullness. The necessity argument uses a tuple containing a T-generator.

With the existential reading, the forward implication holds over arbitrary fields. If I = Q(f), then f is in J = Δ(I), and the definition of J gives Q(J) ⊆ I while f gives the reverse inclusion. Choose a finite generating tuple of J starting with f and write a for the column of component values on f. For each later tuple entry gᵢ, write its column of component values as Bᵢa for a matrix Bᵢ over A, since those values belong to I. Over S the column of generic component values is Ba, where B = y₀ Id + ∑ over i > 0 of yᵢ Bᵢ. The adjugate identity puts det(B) times every component of a in Q(σ). Evaluating at (1, 0, …, 0) gives det(B) = 1, so this determinant lies outside mS. If the component family is empty, I and Q(J) are zero and 1 is in the colon. This argument and the infinite-field evaluation argument are source-level deductions. The Lean result uses the binary counterexample.
