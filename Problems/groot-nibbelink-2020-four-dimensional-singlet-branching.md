# Four-dimensional determinant-one representations and cyclic singlets

## Problem

S. Groot Nibbelink, O. Loukas, A. Mütter, E. Parr and P. K. S. Vaudrevange
(arXiv:1710.09237, hep-th; Fortschr. Phys. 68 (2020) 2000044) reduce the
question of heterotic orbifolds that are supersymmetric in every twisted sector
but not globally to a statement about finite groups:

> There does not exist any finite group $\mathbf{H}$ that has a
> four-dimensional representation $D_\rep{4}$ with the following three
> properties: (i) $D_\rep{4}$ has a trivial determinant … (ii) $D_\rep{4}$ does
> not contain the trivial singlet representation of $\mathbf{H}$ … (iii) but
> the branchings of $D_\rep{4}$ to all $\Z{N} \subset \mathbf{H}$ subgroups
> always contain the trivial $\Z{N}$-singlet representation.

Issue #11489 fixes the reading: `H` is any finite group, `D` any complex
representation on `ℂ⁴`, (ii) means that no nonzero vector is fixed by all of
`D(H)`, and (iii) means that every cyclic subgroup fixes a nonzero vector. The
formal `claim` is the conjecture; the result proves it.

## Motivation

In the paper, groups violating the conjecture would give non-supersymmetric
orbifolds with local Killing spinors in every sector. The authors verified the
conjecture only by enumeration, over the 1,594 CARAT groups and all groups of
order at most 500, and asked for a proof. The frozen declaration
`D5/S3/Quantum/Algebra/FourDimensionalSingletBranching.result` proves it for
every finite group.

## Gap

Issue #11489 preregisters the reading, the route and the literature check.
INSPIRE lists 38 citing records, and full-text searches within them find no
proof or counterexample. The same authors' 2019 proceedings (arXiv:1903.03789,
§5) report only the enumeration restricted to groups whose `Λ²4` is an
integral 6×6 CARAT representation (1,594 abstract groups) and do not restate
the unrestricted conjecture. A. Mütter's 2020 TUM thesis (INSPIRE 1829126)
restates it with "we do not have a rigorous proof for this statement". Groups
in which every element has eigenvalue 1 are called unisingular. The
unisingular literature (Zalesski, arXiv:2401.16075, and the related papers
listed in #11489) treats irreducible groups; the conjecture's content lies in
reducible representations. The proof is Molien's formula for the exterior
algebra at `t = −1`, specialised to dimension 4 and determinant 1. The formula
is classical; this corollary was not found in the searched sources.

These readings are `not-found-in-searched-scope`; they do not establish an
exhaustive worldwide literature search, priority, or the absence of an
independent answer.

## Route

1. Under (iii), the cyclic subgroup generated by `h` fixes a nonzero vector,
   so `det(1 − D(h)) = 0` for every `h`.
2. For a `4 × 4` matrix with `det A = 1`, `adj A = A⁻¹` and
   `det(1 − A) = 2 − tr A − tr A⁻¹ + e₂(A)`, with
   `e₂(A) = ((tr A)² − tr A²)/2`.
3. `S = Σ_h D(h)` satisfies `D(g)S = S`, so `S = 0` by (ii). Hence the traces
   of `D(h)` and of `D(h)⁻¹ = D(h⁻¹)` sum to `0`.
4. With `T = Σ_h D(h) ⊗ D(h)` and `W` the swap, `T² = |H|T`, `W² = 1` and
   `TW = WT`. So `Q = T(1 − W)/(2|H|)` is idempotent, and
   `Σ_h e₂(D(h)) = |H| tr Q = |H| rank Q`.
5. `Σ_h det(1 − D(h)) = |H|(2 + rank Q) ≠ 0`, contradicting step 1.

## Falsifier

The statement would change in dimension 5, where the paper's `Q₈` example
violates it, or without the determinant condition, for example `ℤ₂ × ℤ₂`
acting as `a + b + c + c`. Both enter the proof: dimension 4 in the
determinant expansion of step 2, and determinant 1 in `adj A = A⁻¹`.

### What the settlement shows

**Proved (Lean):** no finite group has a representation on `ℂ⁴` with trivial
determinant, no trivial singlet and a trivial singlet in every cyclic
subgroup; the paper's enumeration over the 1,594 CARAT groups and the groups
of order at most 500 is therefore not a restriction.

**Mechanism (proved, inside `result`):** the average of `det(1 − D(h))` over
`H` equals `2 + m₂`, where `m₂` is the number of trivial singlets in `Λ²ℂ⁴`;
condition (iii) makes every `det(1 − D(h))` vanish, while the average is at
least `2`.

**Not formalized (argument):** for a representation of dimension `n` with
trivial determinant and no trivial singlet, the same average is
`Σ_k (−1)^k m_k`, with `m_k` the number of trivial singlets in `Λ^k`, and
`Λ^k ≅ (Λ^{n−k})^*` gives `m_k = m_{n−k}`. For odd `n` the terms cancel and the
average is `0`, so the argument gives no obstruction; this matches the paper's
five-dimensional `Q₈` example. For `n = 2` the average is `2`. For `n = 6` it
is `2 + 2m₂ − m₃`, which the identity alone does not keep away from `0`.

**Open:** whether the six-dimensional analogue of the conjecture holds.

**Source consequences (paper's reduction, not formalized):** in the paper,
a group violating the conjecture is what a non-supersymmetric
orbifold with a local Killing spinor in every twisted sector would require, so
no point group of this kind gives such an orbifold.
