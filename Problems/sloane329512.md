# Sloane's generating functions for honeycomb cylinders of even width

## Problem

[OEIS A329512](https://oeis.org/A329512), revision 40, by N. J. A. Sloane, has NAME

> Array read by upward antidiagonals: row n = coordination sequence for cylinder formed by rolling up a strip of width 2*n hexagons cut from the hexagonal grid by cuts perpendicular to grid lines.

and FORMULA

> The g.f.s for the rows could be found using the "trunks and branches" method (see Goodman-Strauss and Sloane), as was done in A329508. This step has not yet been carried out, so the following g.f. is at present only conjectural.
>
> The g.f. G(n) for row n (n>=1) is (strongly) conjectured to be
> (1/(1-x))*(1 + 2*x + 3*x^2*(1-x^(2*n-2))/(1-x) - (n-2)*x^(2*n) - (n-1)*x^(2*n+1)).

The claim is a family of identities, one for every width $n\ge1$. The entry
certifies only $G(1),\ldots,G(6)$, by MAGMA.

## Motivation

The settling declaration is
`D5.S3.StatisticalMechanics.HoneycombCylinderCoordination.result`. The external
assertion and its conventions are preregistered in
[issue #27075](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/27075).
The source note is `Library/StatisticalMechanics/sloane2019a329512.md`. The
cylinders are rolled-up honeycomb sheets, the bond graphs of
carbon-nanotube-type tubes, and $c_n(m)$ counts the atoms $m$ bonds away from a
fixed atom.

## Gap

The entry states that the general $G(n)$ is conjectural and that the
trunks-and-branches derivation was never carried out; only rows 1–6 are
certified. The preregistration's bounded screen found no proof for general $n$.
It covered Goodman-Strauss–Sloane arXiv:1803.08530v3, the proved
parallel-cut family A329508, Kopczyński's general rationality theorem,
formal-conjectures and MathDB.

## Route

1. **Plane distance.** Write $s=i+j$. Both potentials are instances of the existing `TeaCoordinationSequence.residual`. The distance from $A(0,0)$ to $A(i,j)$ is
   $2\max(0,i,j,s,-i,-j,-s)$. The distance to $B(i,j)$ is
   $1+2\max(0,i,j,s,-i-1,-j-1,-s-1)$.
   - Every edge changes this potential by at most one. The general potential
     bound `VertexPositionRatioRefutation.potential_dist` then gives the lower
     bound.
   - Every vertex with positive potential has an explicit neighbour of
     potential one less. Strong induction on the potential builds a walk of
     that length.
2. **Quotient distance.** Every quotient walk lifts, with its length preserved,
   from any chosen representative. The quotient distance is therefore the
   minimum plane distance over the representatives of a class.
3. **Representatives.** Every $A$-class has a unique representative with
   $-n<s\le n$, and every $B$-class one with $-n\le s<n$. The potential
   inequalities show that each of these representatives minimises the
   distance.
4. **Shell counts.** Counting slices of constant $s$ inside the strip gives
   $c_n(0)=1$, then $c_n(m)=3m$ for $0<m<2n$, then $c_n(2n)=5n-1$, then
   $c_n(m)=4n$ for $m>2n$. The second differences of these counts give the
   identity.

## Falsifier

A width $n\ge1$ and a radius $m$ whose cylinder shell count differs from the
corresponding coefficient of $G(n)$ would contradict `result`.

4. **Open:** the square-grid cylinder families A329501 and A329504.

### What the settlement shows

Each coordination sequence has two regimes. While the ball has not wrapped
around the cylinder, the counts follow the planar honeycomb sequence $3m$.
Once it has wrapped, the shells have constant size $4n$. The single
transitional shell at radius $2n$ has $5n-1$ atoms; this is the radius at which
the ball first wraps around the cylinder. The numerator terms $-(n-2)x^{2n}-(n-1)x^{2n+1}$ encode exactly this
meeting. The same lift-and-minimise argument settles item 3, and item 4 is the
neighbouring open family.
