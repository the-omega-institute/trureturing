# Normal-extension monotonicity of common transversal probability

## Problem

S. Aivazidis, M. Loukaki and T. W. Müller, *On the common transversal probability in finite groups*, arXiv:2209.00986v2, Problem 5.2, printed p. 37:

> Suppose that $H \leq G$ and $N \unlhd G$, where $G$ is finite. Is it then always true that $P_G(H) \leq P_G(HN)$ (GAP computations confirm this up to $\vert G\vert = 200$)?

Definition 3.1, printed p. 8, defines $\mathrm{DT}_G(H,K)$ as the actual subsets that are simultaneously left transversals for $H$ and right transversals for $K$. For equal-index subgroups, with $n=[G:H]=[G:K]$, it sets $P_G(H,K)=|\mathrm{DT}_G(H,K)|/|H|^n$ and $P_G(H)=P_G(H,H)$.

## Motivation

The quantity belongs to a specified subgroup; it is not the minimum invariant $\mathrm{tp}(G)$. The settlement connects literal transversal counting to double-coset components and proves the normal-extension comparison for every finite group.

## Route

The private counting equivalences separate a two-sided transversal into a matching and one actual group element per matched cell. For a double-coset component $d$ with $t_d$ left cosets, each cell has cardinality $|H|/t_d$. The private `card_dt_orbit_factorial` establishes

$$|\mathrm{DT}_G(H,H)|=\prod_d t_d!\left(\frac{|H|}{t_d}\right)^{t_d}.$$

The private `probability_eq_orbit_product` derives

$$P_G(H)=\prod_d a(t_d),\qquad a(t)=\frac{t!}{t^t}.$$

All private declaration names here belong to `D5.S3.FiniteGroups.TransversalProbabilityMonotone`. The private `normal_extension_component_surjective` proves that every component maps onto its corresponding component for $H\sqcup N$; it uses normality to move an $N$ factor across a representative. Thus upstairs sizes dominate downstairs sizes. The private `weight_product_le` compares the products by grouping factors along nonempty fibres, using that $a$ is nonnegative, at most one and antitone. These are the live ingredients of `result`.

## Falsifier

A failure of the correspondence between actual subsets and matchings, the cell count, or component surjectivity would defeat this route. The compiled result proves the exact claim; its axiom closure is contained in $\{\mathrm{propext},\mathrm{Classical.choice},\mathrm{Quot.sound}\}$. Finite computations alone do not prove its universal quantifier.
