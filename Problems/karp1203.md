# Karp Conjecture 2: general positive shifts

## Problem

D. Karp, *Positivity of Toeplitz determinants formed by rising factorial
series and properties of related polynomials*, arXiv:1203.1482v1,
Section 1, equation (3) and Conjecture 2, p. 2, states:

> If $f_k^2>f_{k-1}f_{k+1}$, $k=1,2,\ldots,n-1$, $n\geq3$, then
> $Q_n^{\alpha,\beta}(x)$ is Hurwitz stable.

> Recall that a polynomial is called Hurwitz stable if all its zeros
> have negative real part.

The standing hypotheses require positive real shifts, a nonnegative,
nonzero input, and the zero convention: if $f_N=0$ for some $N>0$,
then $f_k=0$ for every $k\geq N$. The finite assertion uses only
$f_0,\ldots,f_n$. Equation (3) is

$$
Q_n^{\alpha,\beta}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^n f_kf_{n-k}\binom nk
\big((x+\alpha)_k(x+\beta)_{n-k}-(x+\alpha+\beta)_k(x)_{n-k}\big),
\qquad (x)_r=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(x+j).
$$

Real coefficients are embedded into the complex numbers. Values of `f`
beyond `n` are unrestricted because they do not enter the finite assertion.
The constructed positive sequence also extends to all natural indices.
These carrier choices add no hypothesis. The private exponent
$(\operatorname{Nat.dist}(k,10))^2$ equals $(k-10)^2+(10-k)^2$
with truncated natural subtraction. The scaled rising factorial uses
Mathlib's `Polynomial.scaleRoots` followed by composition.

## Motivation

Strict log-concavity and positive shifts do not force Hurwitz stability.
Concentration of the convolution weights at the middle index exposes a
limiting polynomial with a negative logarithmic-derivative sign in the
right half plane. This separates a root-location assertion from positivity
of real coefficients.

## Route

Put $n=20$, $\alpha=\beta=1/t$ and
$f_k=t^{(\operatorname{Nat.dist}(k,10))^2}$, with $0<t<1$.
Every entry is positive. For $1\leq k<20$, the adjacent exponent sum is
twice the middle exponent plus two, so strict log-concavity follows from
$t^2<1$.

The scaled polynomial is $S_t(z)=t^{20}Q_{20}^{1/t,1/t}(z/t)$.
Finite-product formulas extend its value and derivative continuously to
$t=0$, where

$$
S_0(z)=184756\big((z+1)^{20}-(z(z+2))^{10}\big).
$$

At $z_0=1/50+(3/4)i$, exact rational normalization gives
$\operatorname{Re}(S_0'(z_0)\overline{S_0(z_0)})<0$.
Continuity preserves this strict sign for some $0<t<1$.
For a polynomial with all roots in the closed left half plane, the
root-sum formula for $p'/p$ gives a nonnegative real part at every
right-half-plane point. Multiplication by $|p(z_0)|^2$ preserves its sign.
Thus $S_t$ has a root with positive real part; scaling by $1/t>0$ yields
one for the original polynomial.

## Triage

### What the settlement shows

1. **Proved, kernel-checked settlement:** `result` refutes the unchanged
   general-positive-shift claim. Its construction uses $n=20$, positive
   strictly log-concave inputs, and a strictly right-half-plane root.
   `input_pos`, `input_strict`, `stable_scaledQ` and
   `positive_root_of_negative` are the consumed private statements on
   this route. The public conclusion is the negation of the universal
   claim; no public existential companion theorem is asserted.
2. **Proved, kernel-checked mechanism:** `scaledQ_zero` states the
   central-term limiting identity at $m=10$; `limit_negative` states
   its strict sign obstruction. `continuous_scaledQ_eval` and
   `continuous_scaledQ_deriv_eval` supply the continuity used by
   `result`. The root-sum sign criterion is `log_nonneg`, consumed by
   `positive_root_of_negative`. These are private statements in the
   settling module, not separate public results.
3. **Computed:** the experiment entry above gives $n=20$, $A=1000$
   and $A=10000$ examples with positive coefficients and two
   right-half-plane roots; $A=100$ is a stable control. The command,
   exit code, immutable revision and SHA-256 are in that entry. These
   finite Routh counts are not Lean proofs.
4. **Open in Lean:** the all-$m\geq10$ family, explicit finite shift
   thresholds, and increasing counts of unstable roots are not
   established by this module. Finite experimental ranges do not
   establish a uniform extension.
5. **Open:** the minimal degree, sharp shift threshold and repair
   hypotheses for arbitrary shifts are not determined here. The
   independent $n=12$ example in Gap supplies a smaller external
   instance; it does not determine minimality.
6. **Open in Lean, with an external proved neighbour:** Conjecture 1's
   coefficient-positivity assertion is not negated by this result.
   Conjecture 3 concerns positive PF-infinity inputs and unit shifts
   after translation. Zweiman, arXiv:2610.18895v1, Theorem 1.3 and
   Corollary 1.5, proves that positive unit-shift case externally;
   it is not Lean-verified here. Its concluding section says that
   the general-shift log-concave Conjectures 1 and 2 are not addressed.

The failure mechanism is loss of half-plane root location under
concentration, despite strict input log-concavity. The established real-axis
nonnegativity and unit-shift coefficient results in the source remain
compatible with this refutation. The PF-infinity unit-shift neighbour has
stronger input hypotheses and restricted shifts, so it also survives.
No unconditional theorem of the source is invalidated: Conjecture 2
cannot supply a valid general-shift premise for further conclusions.
Whether extra hypotheses repair that premise remains open.
