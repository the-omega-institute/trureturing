# CayleyPy-4 Conjecture 15 fails at k = 5, L = 4, N = 13

## Problem

CayleyPy-4 (arXiv:2603.22195v1) studies the Schreier coset graph
`S_N / (S_L × S_{N−L})` on binary words with `L` zeros and `N − L` ones,
generated by the consecutive `k`-cycles `(i, …, i + k − 1)`. Conjecture 15
treats generators without their inverses and gives the diameters as
quasipolynomials in `n` and `t = n − (l + 1)`; its `k = 5` clause reads:

> For $k=5$ with period~$4$ (again $t = n - (l+1)$, for all $L \ge 4$, starting from $t+1 \ge 9$ that is, $N \ge L + 9$): $D(L,N) = L \left\lfloor \frac{t+1}{4} \right\rfloor + \mathbf{1}_{(t+1) \equiv 0 \; (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)}$.

Issue #10253 fixes the readings: vertices and the central state as in
#10230; a directed move rotates a window of five consecutive letters one
place, to the left in one convention for the action of a cycle on a word and
to the right in the other; the diameter is read both as the largest directed
distance from the central state and as the largest directed distance between
two vertices. The formal claim is that the clause holds for some convention
and some reading.

## Motivation

A false clause makes Conjecture 15 false as printed.
`D5/S3/Combinatorics/ShrunkenGrassmannianConjectureFifteenRefutation.result`
refutes the clause under both conventions and both readings.

## Route

The inversion count of a word is the number of positions `p < q` holding a
one at `p` and a zero at `q`. By the frozen
`D5/S1/Digit/Carry/ListInversions.inv_window`, rotating five consecutive
letters changes only the inversions inside the window, and a rotation by one
place carries one letter past the other four, so one move changes the count
by at most `4`, in either direction. The central state `0^4 1^9` has no
inversion and its reversal `1^9 0^4` has `36`, so reaching the reversal takes
at least `9` moves. If the central eccentricity or the diameter were `8` at
`L = 4`, `N = 13`, the least element of its defining set would be `8`, and
the reversal would be within `8` moves of the central state. The clause gives
`4 ⌊9/4⌋ + 0 = 8`.

## Falsifier

The refutation would fail if a rotation of five consecutive letters could
change the inversion count by more than `4`, or if the clause gave more than
`8` at `L = 4`, `N = 13`.

## Evidence

Exact breadth-first search over the `715` vertices at `L = 4`, `N = 13`
gives central eccentricity `12` in both conventions; the reversal is at
distance `12` with left rotations and `9` with right rotations. With
`⌈(t + 1)/4⌉` in place of `⌊(t + 1)/4⌋` the clause matches the central
eccentricity under left rotations for `L = 4`, `13 ≤ N ≤ 18`
(`12, 12, 12, 13, 16, 16`), which points to a misprint in the source. It does
not match the other readings: under right rotations the central eccentricity
is `13` at `N = 14` against `12`, and the largest directed distance between
two vertices at `L = 4`, `N = 13` is `15` in both conventions. Independently,
the `k = 4` clause of Conjecture 15 disagrees at
`(l, n) = (2, 7)`: `5` with left rotations, `6` with right rotations, formula
`4`.
