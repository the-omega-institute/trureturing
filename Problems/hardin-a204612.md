# Hardin's OEIS A204612 recurrence

## Problem

R. H. Hardin's OEIS A204612 formula asserts that the number of $(n+1)\times5$ binary arrays whose adjacent integer $2\times2$ determinants are nondecreasing rightwards and downwards satisfies the eleven-term recurrence

$$a(n)=18a(n-1)-73a(n-2)-256a(n-3)+2060a(n-4)-1216a(n-5)-12612a(n-6)+18224a(n-7)+20992a(n-8)-40064a(n-9)-9984a(n-10)+24576a(n-11)$$

for every natural $n>15$.

### Statement echo

The Lean `minor` declaration uses the literal adjacent rows and columns and casts each `Fin 2` entry to `\mathbb Z` before applying `Matrix.det`. `OrderedMinors` is the conjunction of `Monotone` for every determinant row and every determinant column. `a n` is the integer cast of the subtype cardinality of those arrays. `claim` quantifies every natural `n`, assumes `15 < n`, and preserves all eleven coefficients, natural subtraction in the lag indices, and integer arithmetic. `result` proves this exact `claim`.

## Motivation

The OEIS entry still labels the formula empirical. The settlement supplies an all-height proof from the literal array predicate and retains the source threshold and coefficients.

## Gap

## Route

The proof identifies each valid array with a path through 68 boundary states. The transition matrix records exactly the adjacent determinant and monotonicity conditions. A degree-eleven residual is represented in an invariant span generated by paired states that differ in the first boundary bit. Every generator has total coordinate sum zero, and the transition preserves the span. The residual therefore disappears after total counting at every further height, yielding the scalar recurrence.

## Falsifier

A failure of `result : claim`, an incorrect literal array-to-path correspondence, or a residual outside the zero-sum invariant span would falsify this settlement. Finite agreement alone would not prove the universally quantified recurrence.
