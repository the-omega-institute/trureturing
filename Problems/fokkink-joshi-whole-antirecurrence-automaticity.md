# The whole positive coefficient anti-recurrence conjecture

## Problem

Robbert Fokkink and Gandhar Joshi, *Anti-Recurrence Sequences*,
arXiv:2506.13337v1, Conjecture 4; journal version, INTEGERS 26, A24,
DOI 10.5281/zenodo.18305140, Conjecture 2:

> Let a = (a₁,…,aₖ) be positive and integral of dimension k > 1.
> Let Aₙ be the anti-recurrence sequence generated by the linear form
> f(x) = a·x. Then Aₙ − κn is τ-automatic for κ = kτ+1 and τ the
> trace of the linear form.

Here τ = Σ aⱼ. The strictly increasing positive sequences A and B
partition the positive integers and satisfy
Aₙ = Σⱼ₌₁ᵏ aⱼ Bₖ₍ₙ₋₁₎₊ⱼ for every n ≥ 1.

## Motivation

The theorem constructs a source for every such vector, proves
its uniqueness, and supplies a finite DFAO with integer output computing
Aₙ − (kτ+1)n on the standard base-τ digits read most significant first.
Its equality includes every positive index and the exact initial segment.
The auxiliary zero terms and negative padding used inside the proof
are separate from the positive source indexing.

## Gap

The published source proves the conjecture for all-ones vectors and
under an additional restriction on the linear form. Those families,
including the earlier anti-Fibonacci, anti-Tribonacci and anti-Tetranacci
results, receive no separate new credit. The whole-family literature
qualification concerns the unrestricted positive coefficient vector.
Its bounded search does not certify worldwide priority.

The formal statement imposes neither an A₁ bound nor a restriction to a
finite coefficient catalogue. It constructs the source rather than
assuming the existence of sequences satisfying its equations.

## Route

1. A finite-history complement enumeration constructs successive A
   terms. Each new term lies beyond its generating B block, so
   stabilization defines the infinite complement and proves uniqueness.
2. Counting A terms below Bₘ gives Bₘ = m+t and the exact inverse
   interval Aₜ−t < m ≤ Aₜ₊₁−(t+1).
3. The weighted inverse identities yield a uniform discrepancy bound
   by strong induction. All inverse ranks used beyond a fixed initial
   segment are earlier indices.
4. Bounded inverse offsets give an exact local rule: equal bounded
   discrepancy windows at sufficiently large quotient indices give equal
   discrepancies after the same base-τ digit.
5. A finite feature stores the padded window and a capped index. The
   cap preserves every small index exactly. The local rule makes the
   feature transition well defined, producing a native finite DFAO.
   Word evaluation and the standard digit-value identity prove the
   exact output at every index.

The paired Blueprint gives the identities, bounds and window radii.

## Falsifier

A mismatch between the source's complement convention, coefficient
ordering, block indices, slope, digit order or positive-index output
would invalidate the claimed correspondence. An unproved boundedness
or finite-window premise would leave the whole conjecture unresolved.

## Triage

Proved: the whole positive coefficient conjecture, with the actual
inhabited unique complementary source, unshifted integer discrepancy
and every positive index, follows from
`D5/S1/Recurrence/AntiRecurrenceAutomaticity.result`.
The two publication numbers designate this one question.
The source's all-ones and other restricted automaticity theorems remain
known special cases; no coefficient or A₁ restriction is required by
the whole result.

Proved mechanism: complement stabilization supplies the source, and
counting below each complementary term determines its inverse rank.
The weighted rank identity gives a uniform discrepancy bound for each
fixed vector. Bounded rank offsets then make equal finite discrepancy
windows have equal digit successors. A capped index retains the exact
small-index behavior, allowing one finite DFAO to handle the entire
sequence.

Proved weaker consequence: the source's bounded-discrepancy question
holds for every positive coefficient vector. This bound is an
intermediate result of the proof, rather than a replacement for
automaticity or a separate independent problem resolution. The whole
result removes the restriction on applying the source's automaticity
conjecture; it does not alter the hypotheses or conclusions of the
source's other theorems.

Unresolved here: a uniform machine construction across coefficient
vectors, minimal state counts, an executable machine generator and
runtime bounds. The constants and finite state space in the theorem
may depend on the vector. No optimal discrepancy bound or extension
to zero or negative coefficients, or to dimension one, is established.
No additional independent neighbouring theorem is asserted.
