# Hanna's OEIS A181084 integrality conjecture

## Problem

Paul D. Hanna, [OEIS A181084](https://oeis.org/A181084), NAME and complete
COMMENTS field, official revision #9, Sep 22 2025;
[immutable primary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oeis/oeisdata/de48538e52019dc132e250666b0d677b40045ceb/seq/A181/A181084.seq);
[preregistration #30115](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/30115).

> Expansion of g.f.: exp( Sum_{n>=1} [Sum_{k=0..n} binomial(n,k)^(n+k+1) * x^k] * x^n/n ).

> Conjecture: this sequence consists entirely of integers.

In $\mathbb Q[[x]]$, let
$H(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^{n+k+1}x^{n+k}/n$
and $A(x)=\exp(H(x))$. For every natural $m\ge0$, including zero, there is
an integer $z$ with $[x^m]A(x)=z$ in $\mathbb Q$. These are ordinary
coefficients, with no factorial scaling. There is no finite cutoff, positivity
condition, divisibility addendum or analytic convergence hypothesis.

## Route

Set $B(n,k)=\binom nk$ and $F(n,k)=B(n,k)^{n+k+1}$, extending the array by zero
when $k>n$. The existing `HannaA181070Integrality.binomDiv` gives
$n\mid kB(n,k)$; `divPower` with exponent parameter $n+k$ gives
$n\mid kF(n,k)$. Also
$n\mid(n+k)B(n,k)$ by addition of $n\mid nB(n,k)$ and $n\mid kB(n,k)$.

For every prime $p$, `binomCong` gives
$B(pn,pk)\equiv B(n,k)\pmod{p^{v_p(n)+1}}$.
Raise it to $p(n+k)+1$ and compose with `variableExponent`, with its exponent
parameter set to $n+k$ and its base set to $B(n,k)$. Since
$pn+pk+1=p(n+k)+1$, this yields
$F(pn,pk)\equiv F(n,k)\pmod{p^{v_p(n)+1}}$.
There is no coprimality or odd-prime restriction.

The array vanishes for $k>n$ because its binomial base is zero and its exponent
is positive. `literalBounded` therefore identifies the literal logarithm with
`HannaA181070Integrality.L F` in every degree. The artificial $n=0$ term of
that library series contributes zero by rational division by zero. Finally
`integralGeneric` gives the exact all-degree integrality claim. The finite
diagonal for each arbitrary degree expresses formal local finiteness, not a
finite truncation of the assertion.

## Falsifier

A noninteger ordinary coefficient in any degree would contradict the claim.
A finite prefix cannot prove the universal assertion. Changing the exponent,
omitting either inner endpoint, or scaling coefficients by a factorial gives
a different problem.
