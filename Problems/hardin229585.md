# Hardin's width-seven one-defect strip recurrence

## Problem

[OEIS A229585](https://oeis.org/A229585), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of defective 3-colorings of an n X 7 0..2 array connected horizontally and antidiagonally with exactly one mistake, and colors introduced in row-major 0..2 order.

and FORMULA

> Empirical: a(n) = 84*a(n-1) - 2466*a(n-2) + 31428*a(n-3) - 206469*a(n-4) + 750384*a(n-5) - 1513404*a(n-6) + 1574640*a(n-7) - 656100*a(n-8).

Both quotations are from the
[original source export](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oeis/oeisdata/main/seq/A229/A229585.seq).
The source's offset is 1. The reading registered in
[issue #30580](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/30580)
is the recurrence for every $n\ge9$. The starting inequality is separate
from the verbatim FORMULA, which supplies no starting index.

For an array $A:\operatorname{Fin}n\times\operatorname{Fin}7\to\operatorname{Fin}3$,
horizontal bonds join $(i,j)$ to $(i,j+1)$ for $j<6$.
Antidiagonal bonds join $(i,j+1)$ to $(i+1,j)$ for $i+1<n$ and $j<6$.
Exactly one bond has equal endpoint colours. Normalization requires each
occurrence of a colour to have earlier occurrences of all smaller colours,
with cell rank $7i+j$.

## Boundary

The external assertion is the registered universal recurrence, rather
than extrapolation from sequence terms. The OEIS entry calls the formula
empirical; this note does not assert an exhaustive literature or priority
check. The claim excludes recurrence minimality, other widths, additional
OEIS generating functions and infinite-volume physics.
