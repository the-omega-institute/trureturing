# Hardin's recurrence for the width-7 one-defect three-colour strip

## Problem

[OEIS A229585](https://oeis.org/A229585), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of defective 3-colorings of an n X 7 0..2 array connected horizontally and antidiagonally with exactly one mistake, and colors introduced in row-major 0..2 order.

and the empirical recurrence

> Empirical: a(n) = 84*a(n-1) - 2466*a(n-2) + 31428*a(n-3) - 206469*a(n-4) + 750384*a(n-5) - 1513404*a(n-6) + 1574640*a(n-7) - 656100*a(n-8).

Both quotations are from the [OEIS text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A229585&fmt=text).
The assertion registered in
[issue #30580](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/30580)
is this recurrence for every $n\ge 9$, with $n$ the number of rows
and the fixed width $k=7$ the number of columns. The sequence offset is one.

The OEIS recurrence line has no starting inequality. The registration fixes
$n\ge 9$, the first index at which all eight predecessor indices are positive.

For an array $f:\operatorname{Fin}n\times\operatorname{Fin}7
\to\operatorname{Fin}3$, the bonds are
$(i,j)$--$(i,j+1)$ for $i<n$ and $j+1<7$, and
$(i,j+1)$--$(i+1,j)$ for $i+1<n$ and $j+1<7$.
A defect is a bond whose two endpoint colours agree. There are no periodic
boundary bonds or same-column vertical bonds.

## Motivation

The settling declaration is
`D5/S3/StatisticalMechanics/Potts/StripDefectsWidth7/HardinA229585Strip.result`.
The external assertion is preregistered in
[issue #30580](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/30580);
the source note has bibkey `hardin2013a229585`.
A229585 is column 7 of the defect-counting table
[A229586](https://oeis.org/A229586).

For the physics reading, the coordinate change $x=i+j$, $y=i$ sends
horizontal and antidiagonal bonds to the two unit directions of the square
lattice. The finite graph is a sheared square-lattice strip of width 7.
With antiferromagnetic Potts energy
$E(f)=J\sum_{\{u,v\}\in B}\mathbf{1}_{f(u)=f(v)}$ and $J>0$,
a three-colour array with exactly one defect is a state with exactly one
unsatisfied bond of the three-state Potts antiferromagnet.
Row-major normalization counts these states up to a global permutation of
the colours. The theorem concerns finite combinatorial counts under this
bond and energy convention.

## Gap

The OEIS formula is labelled empirical. The registered target is its
validity for every $n\ge 9$ from the literal array definition;
agreement with a finite list of sequence terms does not supply that statement.

The literature check (the OEIS entry and its links; arXiv searches for Potts strips with unsatisfied bonds and for defective colourings; Chang–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 107 (2002) 1207–1253, which treats rectangular square-lattice strips) located no proof of this recurrence.

The cited partition-function paper is
[Exact Potts Model Partition Function for Strips of the Square Lattice](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0108144).
The literature finding is limited to the checked sources.

## Route

1. **Literal count and normalization.** `a` filters every array by the
   exact defect count and row-major `Canonical` condition.
   `DefectTransfer.relabel_defects` and
   `HardinA233177Canonical.canonical_pattern_card` give the source's
   private `scale_count`:
   $L(n)=6a(n)$ for $n\ge 1$, where $L$ is `literalCount`
   with defect budget one. `DefectTransfer.constant_defects` excludes
   constant arrays in this range. At least two colours occur, so each
   colour orbit has six labelled members.
2. **Exact-budget transfer.** `DefectTransfer.continuation` retains
   the six-cell outgoing row tail `boundary = Fin.tail` and the remaining
   budget $d\in\{0,1\}$. Appending a row $r$ costs
   `cross p r + bondDefects horizontal r r`; it is allowed only when
   that cost is at most $d$, and subtracts that cost from $d$.
   The invariant `Valid` bounds equal adjacent boundary entries by
   $1-d$. `cl` records boundary equality patterns and residual budget
   in 72 classes. `DefectTransfer.lumped_count` proves the source's
   private `count_transfer`:
   $L(m+1)=vT^m e$ for every $m:\mathbb{N}$, with
   $v=\mathtt{initial}$ and $e=\mathtt{terminal}$.
   Terminal acceptance requires remaining budget zero, so precisely one
   defect has been spent.
3. **Block structure.** In source order there are 16 classes with
   remaining budget one, followed by 56 with budget zero. The latter
   include proper boundaries and boundaries with one adjacent equality.
   Nonnegative costs cannot restore spent budget. With row-vector
   evolution the matrix has block form
   $T=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\0&C\end{pmatrix}$.
   Transposing to column-vector evolution gives the lower-triangular
   convention used in the registration. The quotient blocks have
   different sizes; the proof uses the certified vector relation below.
4. **Finite certificates and colour orbits.**
   `HardinA229585StripData` supplies the classes, transfer and weights.
   The nine `OrbitChecks` shards certify `nextClasses` on canonical
   boundaries, as the corresponding successor multiset `images`.
   The nine `TableChecks` shards certify the multiplicities of these
   multisets as entries of `T`.
   `HardinA229585StripOrbitCertificate.transition_counts` combines
   those identities. The settlement's private `transition_counts`
   extends them to every labelled boundary: `canonical_exists_unique`
   chooses a colour permutation, and reindexing all next rows by that
   permutation preserves `cl`, horizontal defects and cross defects.
   `HardinA229585StripCertificate.structural_certificate` supplies
   terminal acceptance, budget preservation, the initial histogram,
   ten forward steps and the vector relation. Its initial and forward
   identities use the `Initial` and `ForwardChecks` shards.
   `finite_certificate` combines these with the unrestricted transition
   counts.
5. **Propagation and starting index.** The source's `powers_table`
   identifies $W_j=vT^j$. Its `polynomial_relation` is
   $W_{10}=\sum_{j=0}^{7}c_jW_{2+j}$ with
   $(c_0,\ldots,c_7)=(-656100,1574640,-1513404,750384,-206469,31428,-2466,84)$.
   `DefectTransfer.forward_shift` propagates this shift-two vector
   relation to scalar recurrence indices at least eleven.
   Inside `result`, a separate `decide +kernel` check verifies the two
   scalar relations
   $W_{8+i}e=\sum_{j=0}^{7}c_jW_{i+j}e$ for $i\in\{0,1\}$,
   giving row counts nine and ten. These two cases and the propagated
   cases prove every registered index. Cancelling the common factor
   six in the resulting integer equalities gives `result`.

## Falsifier

A row count $n\ge 9$ at which the literal normalized array count
violates the quoted recurrence would falsify the registered assertion and
contradict the source's `result`. A mismatch in the certified transition
sums or in the exact-budget counting identity would invalidate the proposed
transfer bridge.
