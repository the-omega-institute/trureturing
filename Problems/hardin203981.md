# Hardin's recurrence for the $(n+1)\times 6$ zero-defect three-colour strip

## Problem

The OEIS entry A203981, by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of (n+1) X 6 0..2 arrays with no 2 X 2 subblock having equal diagonal elements or equal antidiagonal elements, and new values 0..2 introduced in row major order.

and the empirical recurrence

> Empirical: a(n) = 25*a(n-1) - 45*a(n-2) - 963*a(n-3) + 2025*a(n-4) + 3645*a(n-5) - 6561*a(n-6).

The quotations are from the OEIS text export. The published sequence
has offset one and is column 5 of A203984.
[OEIS text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A203981&fmt=text)

The registered assertion is this order-six recurrence for every
$n\ge 7$, with $n+1$ rows, six columns and exactly $0$
monochromatic bonds. The OEIS formula line gives no starting index. With offset one, the first index with six published predecessors is $n=7$, the start specified in #32721.
The source theorem `HardinA203981Strip.result` at `t` gives the recurrence at
$n=t+7$ for every $t\in\mathbb N$.
[Issue #32721](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/32721)

For $r$ rows, the vertices are the cells
$(i,j)\in\operatorname{Fin}(r)\times\operatorname{Fin}(6)$.
For $i+1<r$ and $j+1<6$, the bonds are
$\{(i,j),(i+1,j+1)\}$ and $\{(i,j+1),(i+1,j)\}$.
The boundaries are free. Each bond is counted once and there are ten
bonds per consecutive row pair. In Lean, `DefectTransfer.defects` uses
an empty within-row bond set and `HardinA229637RowColumn.betw` at width
six for the between-row bonds.

## Motivation

For the three-state Potts antiferromagnet on these bonds, take
$E(f)=J\sum_{\{u,v\}\in B}\mathbf 1_{f(u)=f(v)}$ with $J>0$.
Equal endpoint colours make a bond unsatisfied. A203981 counts states
with exactly $D=0$ unsatisfied bonds, up to global relabelling of the
three colours. At zero defects these are the proper three-colourings and the zero-energy states.

Diagonal steps preserve the parity of $i+j$. Each parity subgraph is
a finite piece of the square lattice rotated by $45^\circ$, with
three sites per array row and sawtooth side boundaries. The row-major
colour rule chooses one representative per orbit of a single colour
permutation acting on the entire array, across both subgraphs.

## Gap

The empirical recurrence calls for a proof at every registered index
from the literal array count. A finite table of values tests only the
indices it covers.

The literature check covered the OEIS entry and its links, arXiv
searches for Potts strips with unsatisfied bonds and for defective
colourings, and Chang–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 107 (2002)
1207–1253, on rectangular square-lattice strips. That check located
no proof of this recurrence.
[Chang–Salas–Shrock](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0108144)

## Route

1. **Canonical counts and boundary classes.** The literal filter in
   `HardinA203981Strip.a` reads the array in its original row-major order.
   The transfer sweeps rows. It reads the even columns from the left
   and the odd columns from the right. Each triple has one of five
   equality patterns, and the unordered pair gives fifteen classes.
   `HardinA203981Strip.scale_count` gives
   $L_D(m+1)+3\mathbf 1_{D=10m}=6N_D(m+1)$, where $L_D(r)$
   counts labelled arrays. A constant array makes all $10m$ bonds
   unsatisfied and contributes three labellings to one canonical orbit.
   Every other orbit has six labellings. At $D=0$ the correction is
   confined to $m=0$, the single-row case.

2. **Exact-budget transfer.** The transfer uses the 15 values of `HardinA203981StripData.boundary`.
   `HardinA203981StripCertificate.allocated_counts` splits an exact
   transition cost across the parity subgraphs and multiplies the
   corresponding half-strip counts. At cost zero,
   `HardinA203981StripCertificate.zero_exact` reuses the frozen
   `HardinA229588Strip.finite_certificate` with the target triple
   reversed for the half-strip orientation. The product counts both
   orders of distinct target classes and one order for a repeated class.
   `HardinA203981StripSymmetry.lift_counts` transports the
   representative counts to all labelled source rows by independent
   colour permutations and column reflection.
   `HardinA203981StripCertificate.counts` certifies the entries of $T$
   and `HardinA203981StripCertificate.initial_counts` gives the labelled
   single-row multiplicities $u$. The terminal vector is $\mathbf 1$.

3. **Finite forward identity.** Write $W_k=uT^k$, with $u$ the
   initial row vector and $e$ the terminal column vector.
   `HardinA203981StripCertificate.forward_step` checks the successive
   updates of the table built from the two zero-cost half-strip vectors.
   `HardinA203981StripCertificate.forward_table_of_steps` turns the
   initial entry and the checked steps into the forward table.
   `HardinA203981StripCertificate.finite_certificate` verifies
   $W_7=\sum_{j=0}^5 c_j W_{1+j}$ for
   $(c_0,\ldots,c_5)=(-6561,3645,2025,-963,-45,25)$.

4. **Propagation and index.** `DefectTransfer.forward_shift`
   applies further transfer steps and the terminal vector to the
   certified identity. `HardinA203981Strip.counting_bridge` and `DefectTransfer.lumped_count`
   identify $uT^m\mathbf 1$ with $L_0(m+1)$. Every exponent in the
   propagated relation is at least one, so the correction vanishes.
   Since $a(m)=N_0(m+1)$, the left index starts at $1+6=7$.
   Cancelling the common factor six in $\mathbb Z$ gives the
   registered recurrence.

## Falsifier

A literal canonical count that violated the displayed recurrence at
some $n\ge 7$ would falsify the registered assertion. A mismatch
in the transition or initial counts would break the counting bridge.
A failed table update or vector equality at the specified exponents
would invalidate the forward certificate.
