# Hardin's recurrence for the $n\times 5$ two-defect three-colour strip

## Problem

[OEIS A229634](https://oeis.org/A229634), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of defective 3-colorings of an n X 5 0..2 array connected horizontally, diagonally and antidiagonally with exactly two mistakes, and colors introduced in row-major 0..2 order.

and the empirical recurrence

> Empirical: a(n) = 42*a(n-1) - 723*a(n-2) + 6569*a(n-3) - 33687*a(n-4) + 94839*a(n-5) - 116010*a(n-6) - 55215*a(n-7) + 305043*a(n-8) - 230994*a(n-9) - 118011*a(n-10) + 243513*a(n-11) - 84933*a(n-12) - 40719*a(n-13) + 42804*a(n-14) - 14887*a(n-15) + 2598*a(n-16) - 228*a(n-17) + 8*a(n-18) for n > 20.

Both quotations are from the [OEIS text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A229634&fmt=text).
The assertion registered in
[issue #31328](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31328)
is this order-eighteen recurrence for every $n\ge 21$, with $n$ rows,
$5$ columns and exactly $2$ monochromatic bonds. The sequence offset
is one. The source's `result t`, for every `t : Nat`, uses the shifted
index $n=t+21$ and has exactly that range.

For an array $f:\operatorname{Fin}n\times\operatorname{Fin}5\to\operatorname{Fin}3$,
a defect is a bond whose endpoint colours agree. The HDA bonds are
$(i,j)$--$(i,j+1)$, $(i,j)$--$(i+1,j+1)$, and
$(i,j+1)$--$(i+1,j)$ whenever both endpoints lie in the array. Vertical
and periodic boundary bonds are absent. Every bond changes the column
index by one, so the graph is bipartite by column parity and has six
neighbours at an interior site. The source sweeps rows.

A229634 is the two-defect counterpart of the settled width-five one-defect
A229531, registered in [#31121](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31121)
and merged in [#31278](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/pull/31278).
A229532 is the width-six one-defect member of the same HDA family.

## Motivation

The settling declaration is
`D5/S3/StatisticalMechanics/Potts/CrossedStripDefectsWide/HardinA229634Strip.result`.
The external assertion is preregistered in
[issue #31328](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31328);
the source note has bibkey `hardin2013a229634`.

With antiferromagnetic Potts energy
$E(f)=J\sum_{\{u,v\}\in B}\mathbf{1}_{f(u)=f(v)}$ and $J>0$,
each monochromatic bond contributes one positive unsatisfied-bond cost.
These canonical colourings represent states of the three-state Potts
antiferromagnet with exactly $D=2$ unsatisfied bonds on the
horizontal–diagonal–antidiagonal strip without vertical bonds (HDA).
The row-major condition chooses one representative from each global colour
relabelling orbit in the nonconstant range used by the proof. The theorem
is a finite combinatorial statement under this bond and energy convention.

## Gap

The OEIS formula is labelled empirical. The registered target is its
validity for every $n\ge 21$ from the literal array definition;
agreement with finitely many sequence terms does not supply that statement.

The literature check (the OEIS entry and its links; arXiv searches for Potts strips with unsatisfied bonds and for defective colourings; Chang–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 107 (2002) 1207–1253, which treats rectangular square-lattice strips) located no proof of this recurrence.
The literature scope is the one recorded in #31328 and the linked family registration.
Worldwide priority and literature completeness remain unverified.

## Route

1. **Literal count and normalization.** `a` filters every `Fin n × Fin 5 → Fin 3` array by exactly two HDA defects and the lifted row-major canonical predicate. `DefectTransfer.relabel_defects` supplies colour-permutation invariance. `HardinA233177Canonical.canonical_pattern_card` gives the six-to-one labelled-to-canonical bridge in the nonconstant range. A constant array with $m+1$ rows has $4(m+1)+8m$ HDA bonds, so it cannot contribute to the two-defect sector.
2. **Exact-budget block extension.** The source uses `HardinA229531StripData.Row`, `horizontal`, and `between`. A state records a row and residual budget $d\in\{0,1,2\}$, subject to `Valid`. The 33-class matrix `T` is a block extension of the frozen `HardinA229531Strip*` transfer: the leading 20 classes use the inherited one-defect table with budget $d-1$ when $d>0$, and 13 appended classes carry zero residual budget. `oldClassImage` and `oldTail` transport the inherited zero-budget counts into the appended sector. Proper-row entries retain the inherited `HardinA233171DiagonalStrip.transfer/32` blocks. The structural cut and class identities supply `HardinA229634StripClasses.transition_counts`. The finite certificate checks terminal acceptance, budget preservation, every transition sum, initialization, the cached forward steps, and the degree-eighteen relation.
3. **All-index propagation.** Write $u=\mathrm{initial}$, $v=\mathrm{terminal}$, and $W_m=uT^m$. The certificate gives $W_{20}=\sum_{j=0}^{17}c_jW_{2+j}$, where the $c_j$ are the displayed recurrence coefficients in reverse order. `DefectTransfer.forward_shift` propagates this identity through every later power. `DefectTransfer.lumped_count` identifies $uT^{n-1}v$ with the labelled exact-budget count, and the colour-orbit bridge gives $uT^{n-1}v=6a(n)$ in the range used. Cancelling six gives the registered recurrence for every $n\ge21$.

## Falsifier

A canonical HDA count at some $n\ge 21$ that violated the displayed
recurrence, or a failed terminal, budget, transition, initialization, or
forward identity in the finite transfer certificate, would contradict
the result theorem.
