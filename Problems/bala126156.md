# The A126156 eventual totient-period conjecture is false

## Problem

OEIS A126156, revision 110, NAME:

> Expansion of e.g.f. sqrt(sec(sqrt(2)*x)), showing coefficients of only the even powers of x.

The third COMMENTS entry states:

> Conjecture: the sequence {a(n)} reduced modulo a positive integer k is eventually periodic with the period dividing phi(k), where phi(n) = A000010(n). An example is given below. - _Peter Bala_, Mar 01 2026

The source has offset zero. For a rational formal power series $A$ with
$A(0)=1$ and $A(x)^2\cos(\sqrt2x)=1$, put
$a_A(n)=(2n)![x^{2n}]A$. The assertion includes existence of such a series
and says that, for every such $A$ and every positive natural modulus $k$,
there are natural numbers $N,p$ with $p>0$, $p\mid\varphi(k)$ and
$a_A(n+p)-a_A(n)=kz$ for some integer $z$ at every $n\ge N$.
The delivered `claim` is this positive universal assertion; `result` negates it.

## Route

Write $D$ for formal differentiation and $C(x)=\cos(\sqrt2x)$.
The private `cos_second` proves $D^2C=-2C$. Differentiating $A^2C=1$
yields the system $DA=AU$, $DU=1+2U^2$, with $U(0)=0$
(`source_system`). For the integer-polynomial operator
$L(P)=XP+(1+2X^2)P'$, iterated differentiation gives
$D^rA=A(L^r1)(U)$ (`deriv_iter`). Consequently
$a_A(n)=[X^0]L^{2n}1$ (`source_intA`), which proves integrality.

The coefficient calculation `step_four` gives
$[X^0]L^4P+[X^0]P=4(2[X^0]P+11[X^2]P+6[X^4]P)$.
The parity invariant `intA_odd` and this identity imply
$a_A(n+2)-a_A(n)=4z+2$ for some integer $z$, for every natural $n$
(`persistent_obstruction`). This is an all-index identity.

## Falsifier

At $k=4$, $\varphi(4)=2$ and the permitted positive periods are $1,2$.
Period $2$ contradicts `persistent_obstruction` at any proposed onset.
Period $1$ implies period $2$ by adding consecutive differences, so it is
also excluded. A finite failed shift alone would not exclude arbitrary
onsets. The proof uses the all-index obstruction instead.

### What the settlement shows

The failure is the factor-of-two obstruction at modulus $4$: the scaled
coefficients remain odd and a shift by two always changes the residue by
two. The finite four-cycle is compatible with allowing period four at this
modulus; a universal twice-totient bound remains open. The displayed
modulus-55 example survives on its tested interval. The literature screen
in #22077 identified no result in the inspected moment papers that relies
on Bala's conjecture; this is a bounded literature reading, not a theorem
about every source citing the sequence.
