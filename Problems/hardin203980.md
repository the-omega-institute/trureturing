# Hardin's recurrence for the $(n+1)\times5$ zero-defect three-colour strip

## Problem

[OEIS A203980](https://oeis.org/A203980), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of (n+1) X 5 0..2 arrays with no 2 X 2 subblock having equal diagonal elements or equal antidiagonal elements, and new values 0..2 introduced in row major order.

and the empirical recurrence

> Empirical: a(n) = 15*a(n-1) - 270*a(n-3) + 324*a(n-4).

Both quotations are from the [OEIS text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A203980&fmt=text).
The assertion registered in
[issue #31593](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31593)
is this order-four recurrence for every $n\ge5$.
Let $N_D(r)$ be the number of canonical $r\times5$ arrays with
exactly $D$ monochromatic diagonal or antidiagonal bonds.
Here $a(n)=N_0(n+1)$, so $a(n)$ counts $(n+1)\times5$ arrays, with exactly $D=0$ unsatisfied bonds.
The OEIS sequence offset is one.
The source's `result t` quantifies over every `t : Nat` and
uses index $t+5$, exactly the registered range.

For an array $f:\operatorname{Fin}r\times\operatorname{Fin}5\to\operatorname{Fin}3$,
the DA bonds are $(i,j)$--$(i+1,j+1)$ and
$(i,j+1)$--$(i+1,j)$, for $i+1<r$ and $j+1<5$.
Each bond is counted once. Horizontal, vertical and periodic boundary
bonds are absent. A defect means equality of the endpoint colours.
The canonical predicate requires every occurrence of a colour to
have an earlier occurrence of every smaller colour, in row-major
order with rank $5i+j$. It forces the first cell to be zero on a
nonempty array; it does not require all three colours to occur.

## Motivation

The settling declaration is
`D5/S3/StatisticalMechanics/Potts/DiagonalStripDefects/HardinA203980Strip.result`.
The external assertion is preregistered in
[issue #31593](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31593);
the source note has bibkey `hardin2012a203980`.

With antiferromagnetic Potts energy
$E(f)=J\sum_{\{u,v\}\in B}\mathbf1_{f(u)=f(v)}$ and $J>0$,
these counts describe three-state Potts antiferromagnet states on
five-column strips whose bonds are the diagonals and antidiagonals
of the square grid. The family has exactly $D=0,1,2$ unsatisfied
bonds; this sequence selects $D=0$ and thus energy $E=JD$.
The $D=0$ sector consists of proper colourings, the zero-temperature states.

The bonds preserve $i+j$ modulo two. On parity class $p\in\{0,1\}$,
the coordinates $((i+j-p)/2,(i-j-p)/2)$ send the two DA bond
directions to the two unit square-lattice directions. Each parity
class is a square lattice rotated by $45^\circ$, restricted by the
strip's sawtooth boundaries. The global colour normalization couples
the two parity classes in their orbit count. It uses a single
permutation of the three colours for the whole array.
The theorem concerns finite combinatorial counts with this graph
and energy convention.

## Gap

The OEIS recurrence is labelled empirical. The registered target is
its validity for every $n\ge5$ from the literal canonical
array count. Agreement with finitely many terms does not supply that
universal assertion.

The literature check (the OEIS entry and its links; arXiv searches for Potts strips with unsatisfied bonds, defective colourings and diagonal strips; Chang–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 107 (2002) 1207–1253, and Chang–Jacobsen–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 114 (2004) 763–823, which treat rectangular square- and triangular-lattice strips) located no proof of this recurrence.
The literature scope is the check recorded in #31593.

## Route

1. **Literal count and global normalization.** `a n` filters all
   `Fin (n + 1) × Fin 5 → Fin 3` arrays by defect count zero and
   `Canonical` in the original row-major order. With $Q=\mathtt{T}$,
   $u=\mathtt{initial}$ and $e=\mathbf 1$, `counting_bridge m hm`
   uses `DefectTransfer.lumped_count` with boundary projection `id`,
   validity $d=0$, and classifier `boundary` to give
   $L_0(m+1)=uQ^m e$. Here $L_0$ counts labelled arrays.
   `DefectTransfer.relabel_defects` supplies permutation invariance.
   A constant array on $m+1$ rows has $8m$ monochromatic bonds, so
   $m\ge1$ and zero defects force nonconstancy.
   `HardinA233177Canonical.canonical_pattern_card` then gives
   $uQ^m e=6a(m)$ for every $m\ge1$.
   The restriction is essential: a single row has no bonds.

2. **Eight boundary classes and parity symmetry.** A row is
   `Fin 5 → Fin 3`. `HardinA203980StripData.boundary` records
   the equality pattern of entries $0,2,4$ up to reflection and
   whether entries $1,3$ agree. These give eight classes, with
   representatives and initial multiplicities
   $u=(9,18,18,36,36,72,18,36)$.
   In `DiagonalSymmetry`, independent colour permutations $e,f$
   on the even and odd column positions of a source row are
   paired with $f,e$ on its successor row. Every diagonal bond
   connects opposite column parities, so `cost_recolor` preserves
   the defect count. `cost_reverse` handles simultaneous reversal;
   `boundary_recolor` and `boundary_reverse` preserve the class.
   `class_rep` reaches a representative, and `lift_counts`
   transports the representative sums bijectively to every labelled
   source row. These are symmetries of the labelled transfer;
   the final canonical predicate still uses one global colour order.

3. **Finite transition and terminal-vector identities.**
   `HardinA203980StripCertificate.representative_counts` checks
   the zero-cost successor sums on the eight representatives with
   `decide +kernel`. `counts` lifts them through
   `DiagonalSymmetry.lift_counts`, and `initial_counts` checks the
   distribution of all $3^5$ labelled rows.
   `powers` uses `TransferMatrix.tabulatedPowers` to identify
   the tabulated vectors $W_j=Q^j e$ for $0\le j<14$.
   `finite_certificate` proves
   $$
   Q^5e=324Qe-270Q^2e+15Q^4e
       =\sum_{j=0}^{3}c_jQ^{1+j}e,
   \qquad (c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)=(324,-270,0,15).
   $$
   This is a shift-one, degree-four relation on the terminal vector.

4. **Propagation and registered index.** `result` applies
   `TransferMatrix.scalarRelationShift` to that relation.
   For each $t:\mathbb N$, the resulting identity involves
   $uQ^{t+5}e$ and its prescribed predecessors.
   `counting_bridge` replaces them by six times the literal
   canonical counts. Cancelling six in $\mathbb Z$ gives the
   recurrence at index $t+5$, hence at every $n\ge5$.
   No characteristic-polynomial or recurrence-minimality assertion
   is needed.

## Falsifier

A literal canonical DA count at some $n\ge5$ that violates
the quoted recurrence would falsify the registered assertion and
contradict the source's `result`. A mismatch in a representative
transition sum, initial multiplicity, symmetry bijection, terminal-power
step or vector relation would invalidate the corresponding counting
certificate.
