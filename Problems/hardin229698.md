# Hardin's recurrence for the $5\times n$ two-defect three-colour strip

## Problem

[OEIS A229698](https://oeis.org/A229698), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of defective 3-colorings of a 5 X n 0..2 array connected horizontally and antidiagonally with exactly two mistakes, and colors introduced in row-major 0..2 order.

and the empirical recurrence

> Empirical: a(n) = 48*a(n-1) - 960*a(n-2) + 10468*a(n-3) - 68685*a(n-4) + 282276*a(n-5) - 718624*a(n-6) + 997536*a(n-7) - 166467*a(n-8) - 2005784*a(n-9) + 3520224*a(n-10) - 1956780*a(n-11) - 1660207*a(n-12) + 3547332*a(n-13) - 2236608*a(n-14) - 28632*a(n-15) + 1014720*a(n-16) - 774528*a(n-17) + 307392*a(n-18) - 71424*a(n-19) + 9216*a(n-20) - 512*a(n-21) for n > 27.

Both quotations are from the [OEIS text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A229698&fmt=text).
The assertion registered in
[issue #31594](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31594)
is this order-twenty-one recurrence for every $n\ge28$, with $5$ rows,
$n$ columns and exactly $2$ monochromatic bonds. The sequence offset
is one. The source's `result t`, for every `t : Nat`, uses the shifted
index $n=t+28$ and has exactly that range.

For an array $f:\operatorname{Fin}5\times\operatorname{Fin}n\to\operatorname{Fin}3$,
a defect is a bond whose endpoint colours agree. The HA bonds are
$(i,j)$--$(i,j+1)$ for $j+1<n$, and
$(i,j+1)$--$(i+1,j)$ for $i+1<5$ and $j+1<n$.
These horizontal and antidiagonal bonds make the $5\times n$ arrays
sheared square-lattice strips with staircase ends. Vertical and periodic
boundary bonds are absent in the original array coordinates.
The source sweeps columns with the transposed bonds, vertical plus
antidiagonal in the transfer coordinates. There are no bonds within a
swept column: the empty within-slice bond set and `between` encode
exactly the original HA graph. The pairs $(i,i)$ in `between`
represent original horizontal bonds, and $(i+1,i)$ represent antidiagonals.

A229698 is the two-defect counterpart of the settled A229590 on the
same strip ([#31591](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/pull/31591);
[one-defect dossier](hardin229590.md)).
Its transfer is a block extension of the frozen A229590 delivery, and
its zero-temperature block is the frozen five-row owner
`TraceAvoidingArrays/HardinA233260Literal`.

## Motivation

The settling declaration is
`D5/S3/StatisticalMechanics/Potts/StripDefectsWide/A229698/HardinA229698Strip.result`.
The external assertion is preregistered in
[issue #31594](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31594);
the source note has bibkey `hardin2013a229698`.

The coordinate change $x=i+j$, $y=i$ sends horizontal and antidiagonal
bonds to the two unit directions of the square lattice. With
antiferromagnetic Potts energy
$E(f)=J\sum_{\{u,v\}\in B}\mathbf{1}_{f(u)=f(v)}$ and $J>0$,
these are states of the three-state Potts antiferromagnet with exactly
$D=2$ unsatisfied bonds on the sheared square strip (HA).
Row-major normalization chooses one representative from each global
colour relabelling orbit. In the nonconstant range used by the proof,
each such orbit has six labelled representatives. The theorem concerns
finite combinatorial counts under this bond and energy convention.

## Gap

The OEIS formula is labelled empirical. The registered target is its
validity for every $n\ge28$ from the literal array definition;
agreement with a finite list of sequence terms does not supply that statement.

The literature check (the OEIS entry and its links; arXiv searches for Potts strips with unsatisfied bonds and for defective colourings; Chang–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 107 (2002) 1207–1253, which treats rectangular square-lattice strips) located no proof of this recurrence.
The literature scope includes the sources recorded in #31594.
Worldwide priority and literature completeness remain unverified.

## Route

1. **Literal count and normalization.** `a` filters every original
   `Fin 5 × Fin n → Fin 3` array by the exact HA defect count and the
   lifted row-major canonical predicate.
   `DefectTransfer.relabel_defects` supplies colour-permutation
   invariance. `scale_count` applies
   `HardinA233177Canonical.canonical_pattern_card` to this original
   row-major domain. `Equiv.prodComm` and `Equiv.arrowCongr` relate
   the labelled transfer domain to the original arrays; the column
   sweep does not change the canonical order.
   A constant array with $m+1$ columns has $9m$ monochromatic HA
   bonds, which cannot equal two. Thus every accepted array is
   nonconstant, and the labelled count satisfies
   $L(m+1)=6a(m+1)$ for every $m:\mathbb N$.

2. **Exact-budget block extension.** A swept column has type
   `Fin 5 → Fin 3`; the inherited `boundary` records all five
   entries as the frozen owner's tuple type `Col`. Appending column
   $r$ after boundary $p$ costs `cross p r`, using the inherited
   nine-element `between` bond set. The remaining budget
   $d\in\{0,1,2\}$ decreases by this cost, which must be at most $d$.
   The classifier `cl` uses `Sum.inl (cls p)` when $d=2$ and
   otherwise embeds `HardinA229590StripData.cl p d` in `Sum.inr`.
   Each sector uses the frozen 41-class classifier of
   `HardinA233260Literal`, giving 123 states in budget order $2,1,0$.
   In this order,
   $$
   T=\begin{pmatrix}Q&R&S\\0&Q&R\\0&0&Q\end{pmatrix}.
   $$
   The zero-cost matrix $Q$ is frozen, $R$ is the frozen one-defect
   transition matrix, and $S$ counts transitions with exactly two
   defects. The lower-right 82-state block is exactly the frozen
   `HardinA229590StripData.T`; only the leading 41 states extend
   the budget. The initial vector is
   $(\mathtt{classWeights},0,0)$ and the terminal vector is
   $(0,0,\mathbf1)$, so accepted paths spend exactly two defects.

3. **Transition and initial identities.**
   `HardinA229698StripClassGroup0` through
   `HardinA229698StripClassGroup6` prove the $S$-block transition
   counts on the 41 frozen representatives, using finite
   `decide +kernel` after case splitting.
   `HardinA229698StripNew.quotient_rows` uses `class_rep`,
   `class_relabel` and the inherited `cross_relabel` to transfer
   those counts to every labelled boundary.
   `HardinA229698StripCut.transition_counts` separates the possible
   costs. For budget two, zero-cost transitions reuse the frozen
   A229590 transition identity, one-cost transitions reuse
   `HardinA229590StripNew.quotient_rows`, and two-cost transitions
   use the new $S$ identity. Budgets zero and one reuse the frozen
   A229590 cut. `initial_counts` reuses its frozen initial counts.
   In the result module, `count_transfer` applies
   `DefectTransfer.lumped_count` with validity condition $d\le2$,
   yielding $L(m+1)=uT^m e$, where $u=\mathtt{initial}$ and
   $e=\mathtt{terminal}$.

4. **Suffix vectors and inherited powers.** Write $T_1$ and $e_1$
   for the frozen one-defect matrix and terminal vector, and
   $B=(R\ S)$ for the new injection block.
   `HardinA229698StripInjection.two_injection_powers` identifies
   `SQ k` with $SQ^k\mathbf1$ for $0\le k<19$.
   Its initial identities are supplied by the
   `HardinA229698StripTwoInjectionGroup*` modules; the remaining
   identities use the frozen shift-four, degree-seven relation of
   `HardinA233260Literal` through
   `TransferMatrix.scalarRelationShift`.
   `injection_powers` identifies `J k` with $BT_1^k e_1$ for
   $0\le k<27$, using the
   `HardinA229698StripOneInjectionGroup*` base identities and
   the frozen shift-four, degree-fourteen relation of A229590.
   The injection base identities use definitional reduction.
   `HardinA229698StripStepGroup0` through
   `HardinA229698StripStepGroup5` check
   $V_{k+1}=QV_k+J_k$ by definitional reduction.
   `HardinA229698StripPowers.powers_table` then proves
   $T^k e=(V_k,T_1^k e_1)$ for $0\le k<28$.
   Its `power_polynomial` proves the shift-six, degree-twenty-one
   terminal-vector identity
   $$
   T^{27}e=\sum_{j=0}^{20}c_jT^{6+j}e,
   $$
   where `coefficients j` lists the quoted recurrence's lag
   coefficients in ascending power order. The new component is a
   finite identity by definitional reduction. The inherited
   component is a linear combination of shifts $2$ through $9$
   of the frozen one-defect vector identity.
   `HardinA229698Strip.finite_certificate` assembles the
   transition, initial and terminal-vector identities.

5. **Propagation and starting index.** Inside `result`,
   `forward_eq` converts the terminal-vector relation to a
   forward relation for $T^{\mathsf T}$ with initial row $e$.
   `DefectTransfer.forward_shift` propagates that relation
   through every later power. The local `pairing` identifies
   $e^{\mathsf T}(T^{\mathsf T})^m u^{\mathsf T}=uT^m e$,
   and the local `bridge` combines `count_transfer` and
   `scale_count` to give $uT^m e=6a(m+1)$.
   Cancelling six in $\mathbb Z$ gives the displayed recurrence
   at every index at least $6+21+1=28$. This route requires
   neither recurrence minimality nor a full characteristic polynomial.

## Falsifier

A canonical HA count at some $n\ge28$ that violated the displayed
recurrence would falsify the registered assertion and contradict the
source's `result`. A mismatch in the transition sums, initial
multiplicities, injection identities, suffix-vector steps or
terminal-vector polynomial would invalidate the finite transfer certificate.
