# Gorshkov–Shpectorov Problem 3.20, forward implication

## Problem

> Is it true that the simplicity of an axial algebra $A$ implies the simplicity of the inner Lie algebra $DA_X$ for any generating set of axes $X$? Vice versa, does simplicity of $DA_X$ imply simplicity of $A$?

The first sentence is the forward implication in Problem 3.20, §3.3, p. 13 of arXiv:2606.30048v1, and Problem 2.20, §2.3, p. 17 of the journal edition. The Lie algebra $DA_X$ is the Lie subalgebra of the linear endomorphisms generated by $L_aL_b-L_bL_a$ for $a,b\in X$. [Source and definition locators](../Library/LieTheory/gorshkovshpectorov2026axialquestions.md)

## Motivation

Simplicity of an algebra requires its multiplication ideals to be trivial. The question asks whether simplicity forces the corresponding selected-axis inner Lie algebra to have trivial Lie ideals for every generating set.

## Route

Use the rational spin factor $J_4=\mathbb Q\oplus\mathbb Q^4$ with product $(a,u)(b,v)=(ab+u\cdot v,av+bu)$ and the eight axes $(1/2,\pm e_i/2)$. Their actual eigenspaces satisfy the primitive Jordan-half fusion law and the axes linearly span the carrier. A nonzero multiplication ideal contains the unit: a pure nonzero scalar can be rescaled, and a nonzero vector coordinate gives a nonzero pure vector via a coordinate commutator whose square is a nonzero scalar multiple of the unit. The unit is a nonzero idempotent, so the witness satisfies simplicity under the usual nonzero-product convention.

The actual selected-axis commutators are skew vector blocks, and the elementary skew blocks arise from positive-coordinate axes up to a factor of $1/4$. Both inclusions identify their Lie span with $\mathfrak{so}_4(\mathbb Q)$ for the standard form. The frozen `OrthogonalLieStructure.soFourEquiv` gives its splitting as $\mathfrak{so}_3(\mathbb Q)\times\mathfrak{so}_3(\mathbb Q)$. [Orthogonal Lie structure](../Blueprint/D5/S3/QuadraticForms/OrthogonalLieStructure.md)

## Falsifier

The kernel of the second projection contains the nonzero preimage of $(r,0)$ and excludes the preimage of $(0,r)$ for the elementary skew matrix $r$ with entry 1 in row 0, column 1. The selected inner Lie algebra therefore has a nonzero proper Lie ideal, even though the five-dimensional axial algebra is simple.

## Triage

`theorem`. The rational case supplies a counterexample to the first universal implication as printed.

### What the settlement shows

- **Refuted in Lean.** A simple finite-dimensional rational primitive Jordan-half axial algebra has a nonsimple inner Lie algebra generated by the commutators for its chosen axes. The explicit witness is $J_4$ and the settling declaration is `AxialInnerLieForwardRefutation.result`.
- **Mechanism.** The exact selected Lie span is the standard skew-block algebra, whose dimension-four split gives a proper ideal. The frozen orthogonal simplicity theorem applies at dimensions at least three other than four for this positive-definite rational form. Applicability to other axial algebras requires a Lie-span identification for those algebras.
- The second sentence of the question has its own counterexample and separate refutation. [Converse dossier](gorshkov-shpectorov-2026-axial-simplicity-converse.md)
