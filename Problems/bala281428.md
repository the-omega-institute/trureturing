## Problem

Hanna's [OEIS A281428](https://oeis.org/A281428), revision 49, COMMENTS, states verbatim: “1) If N is not divisble by either 2 or 3 then there exists n_0 such that N divides a(n) for all n >= n_0.” The source NAME is “E.g.f. C(x) satisfies C(x) = cosh( Integral C(x)^4 dx ).” The preregistration is [#22065](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/22065).

Work in $\mathbb Q[[x]]$, with the integral's constant coefficient zero and $C(0)=1$. The source EXAMPLE fixes $a(n)=(2n)![x^{2n}]C(x)$. The assertion covers every positive natural modulus $N$ with $\gcd(N,6)=1$ and every sufficiently large natural index; divisibility means equality to $N$ times an integer.
