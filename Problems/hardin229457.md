# Hardin's recurrence for the $n\times 6$ one-defect three-colour square strip

## Problem

[OEIS A229457](https://oeis.org/A229457), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of defective 3-colorings of an nX6 0..2 array connected horizontally and vertically with exactly one mistake and colors introduced in row-major 0..2 order.

and the empirical recurrence

> Empirical: a(n) = 60*a(n-1) -1482*a(n-2) +20016*a(n-3) -167163*a(n-4) +924894*a(n-5) -3537110*a(n-6) +9593166*a(n-7) -18693789*a(n-8) +26218062*a(n-9) -26188161*a(n-10) +18139608*a(n-11) -8262712*a(n-12) +2220864*a(n-13) -266256*a(n-14) for n>15

Both quotations are from the [OEIS text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A229457&fmt=text).
The assertion registered in
[issue #31660](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31660)
is this order-14 recurrence for every $n\ge 16$, with $n$ rows,
$6$ columns and exactly $1$ monochromatic bond. The sequence offset
is one. The source's `result t`, for every `t : Nat`, uses the shifted
index $n=t+16$ and has exactly that range.

For an array $f:\operatorname{Fin}n\times\operatorname{Fin}6\to\operatorname{Fin}3$,
a defect is a bond whose endpoint colours agree. The bonds are
$(i,j)$--$(i,j+1)$ for $j+1<6$, and
$(i,j)$--$(i+1,j)$ for $i+1<n$.
These are rectangular square-lattice strips with free boundaries;
no antidiagonal, wraparound or periodic boundary bonds are present.
The transfer sweeps six-cell rows. The within-row set `horizontal`
has five bonds, and `ProperTransfer.between` consists of the six
pairs $(j,j)$ joining corresponding columns in consecutive rows.

## Motivation

The settling declaration is
`D5/S3/StatisticalMechanics/Potts/SquareStripDefects/HardinA229457Strip.result`.
The external assertion is preregistered in
[issue #31660](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31660);
the source note has bibkey `hardin2013a229457`.

With antiferromagnetic Potts energy
$E(f)=J\sum_{\{u,v\}\in B}\mathbf{1}_{f(u)=f(v)}$ and $J>0$,
these are three-state Potts antiferromagnet states with exactly
$D=1$ unsatisfied bond on $n\times6$ strips of the square lattice
with free boundaries, counted up to global relabelling of the colours.
Row-major normalization chooses one representative from each such orbit.
For the nonconstant arrays used in the proof, every orbit has six
labelled representatives. The theorem concerns finite combinatorial
counts under this bond and energy convention.

Widths 2–5 of both defect columns follow from the published
full-temperature square-strip transfer matrices of Chang–Salas–Shrock.
Width 6 is the first open width identified by the literature check
registered in #31660. The chromatic-polynomial treatment of Salas–Sokal
concerns the zero-defect sector and does not supply either positive-defect
recurrence.

## Gap

The OEIS formula is labelled empirical. The registered target is its
validity for every $n\ge 16$ from the literal canonical array definition;
agreement with finitely many terms does not supply that statement.

The literature check (the OEIS entry and its links; arXiv searches for Potts strips with unsatisfied bonds and defective colourings; Chang–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 107 (2002) 1207–1253, which gives free square strips of widths 2–5 at full temperature; Salas–Sokal, arXiv:1002.3761, which treats the chromatic polynomial only) located no proof of this recurrence.
This is the literature scope recorded in #31660. Literature completeness
and worldwide priority remain unverified.

## Route

1. **Literal count, residual budget and normalization.**
   `a` filters the original `Fin n × Fin 6 → Fin 3` arrays by
   exactly $1$ defect and the lifted row-major `Canonical` predicate.
   A boundary is a full row `Row = Fin 6 → Fin 3`. Appending $r$ after
   $p$ costs
   $c(p,r)=\operatorname{bondDefects}(\mathtt{between},p,r)+\operatorname{bondDefects}(\mathtt{horizontal},r,r)$.
   This cost must be at most the remaining budget $d$, which becomes
   $d-c(p,r)$. The predicate `Valid p d` is
   $d\le 1$ together with the bound that the horizontal defects
   of $p$ are at most $1-d$.
   `finite_certificate` supplies terminal acceptance at $d=0$,
   successor admissibility and sums, and first-row admissibility and counts.
   `DefectTransfer.lumped_count` then gives
   $L(m+1)=uT^m e$, where $u=\mathtt{initial}$,
   $e=\mathtt{terminal}$, and $L$ counts labelled arrays at budget $1$.
   `DefectTransfer.relabel_defects` supplies colour-permutation invariance,
   and `HardinA233177Canonical.canonical_pattern_card` gives
   $L(m+1)=6a(m+1)$. A constant array has $5(m+1)+6m=11m+5$
   monochromatic bonds, which cannot equal $1$; this discharges
   the nonconstancy hypothesis for every $m:\mathbb N$.

2. **Delegated proper-row transfer.**
   `ProperTransfer.cl` selects the ten saturated classes of
   `HardinA233127SquareStrip.boundaryClass (true,true)`, dropping its
   unused class zero. `ProperTransfer.T` is the corresponding submatrix
   of `HardinA233127SquareStrip.transfer` divided by $64$.
   `ProperTransfer.raw_successors` delegates to `row_counts` from
   `HardinA233127Certificate`. The scanner-fibre identity
   `HardinA233255LiteralBridge.scanner_fibres` and the saturated-row
   identities `HardinA233255ShapeSymmetry.full_row` identify the
   multiplicity as $2^6=64$. Thus dividing the scanner transfer by 64
   gives the actual number of proper successor rows that differ from
   the boundary in every column. This exposes the existing certificate
   as the ordinary zero-defect row transfer.

3. **The 42-state one-defect extension.**
   The first ten classes have one unit of budget left and a proper
   boundary. The remaining 32 have budget zero: ten proper-row classes
   and 22 classes of rows with one horizontal defect.
   The ten-class proper transfer appears on the two proper-row
   diagonal blocks. This retained partition uses 42 states;
   the registration's coarsest partition has 40.
   `HardinA229457StripCut.class_cut` identifies the relevant zero-cost
   transitions structurally, and `proper_counts` divides
   `ProperTransfer.raw_successors` by 64. `active_counts` supplies
   the leading ten successor columns.
   `HardinA229457StripClass0` through `HardinA229457StripClass7` supply
   the other target columns, using the proper-row result on its live
   cases and finite kernel decisions for the remaining canonical
   boundaries. `HardinA229457StripClasses.transition_counts` uses
   `canonical_exists_unique` and colour-permutation invariance to
   transport these identities to every labelled boundary.

4. **Forward identities and starting index.**
   Write $W_j=uT^j$. `HardinA229457StripPowers.steps` identifies the
   26 tabulated vectors with these forward powers; its step modules
   supply the finite identities. `HardinA229457StripPowers.relation`
   gives the vector relation
   $W_{15}=\sum_{j=0}^{13}c_jW_{1+j}$, with
   $(c_0,\ldots,c_{13})=(-266256,2220864,-8262712,18139608,-26188161,26218062,-18693789,9593166,-3537110,924894,-167163,20016,-1482,60)$.
   `DefectTransfer.forward_shift` propagates this shift-one,
   degree-fourteen relation through every later matrix power.
   The bridge $uT^m e=6a(m+1)$ and cancellation of six in
   $\mathbb Z$ yield the recurrence at every index at least
   $1+14+1=16$. The argument needs no minimality or full
   characteristic-polynomial assertion.

## Falsifier

A canonical square-strip count at some $n\ge16$ that violated the
displayed recurrence would falsify the registered assertion and contradict
the source's `result`. A mismatch in terminal acceptance, budget preservation,
successor sums, first-row counts, forward steps or the polynomial identities
would invalidate the transfer certificate. The scanner-to-row bridge must
preserve its factor 64, and the labelled-to-canonical bridge its factor six.
