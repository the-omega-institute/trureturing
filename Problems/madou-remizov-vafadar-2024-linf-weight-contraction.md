# L∞-contraction for a weight

## Problem

F. Madou, I. Remizov, M. Vafadar (eds.), *Open problems in one-parameter operator semigroups theory*, arXiv:2410.00416v2, §6 “L∞-bounds”, equation (1), state verbatim:

> The semigroup (T(t))t≥0 is called L∞-bounded, if there exists M > 0 such that ‖T(t)f‖∞ ≤ M‖f‖∞, for all f ∈ L²(Ω) ∩ L∞(Ω). (1)

Equation (3) states verbatim:

> (3) ‖gT(t)f‖∞ ≤ ‖gf‖∞ for all f ∈ L∞(Ω) ∩ L²(Ω)

Problem 22 and its comment state verbatim:

> Problem 22 (L∞-contraction for a weight problem). In the setting of Problem 21 assume that the semigroup (T(t))t≥0 is L∞-bounded, i.e., satisfies (1) for some M ≥ 1. Does there exist a function g ∈ L∞(Ω) satisfying g > 0 µ-a.e., 1/g ∈ L∞(Ω), and (3)? Comments. In general, this might be more than one can hope for.

The setting of Problem 21 is a σ-finite measure space $(\Omega,\mu)$ and a closed sectorial sesquilinear form $a$ on a dense $V\subseteq L^2(\Omega)$, with associated operator $A$ and bounded analytic semigroup $T(t)$ generated by $-A$. The locator binds to the edited problem list, without assigning individual authorship to this question.

## Motivation

A bounded positive weight with bounded inverse defines an equivalent weighted sup norm. The question asks whether the uniform bound $M$ can always be replaced by contractivity in a norm of this specific form.

## Gap

The problem list presents Problem 22 as open. Its comment anticipates a negative answer but gives no counterexample. That source status does not establish the observation's novelty outside the list.

## Route

Use the two-point space with counting measure, $V=\mathbb C^2$, and $a(u,v)=\langle Au,v\rangle$, where

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\-2&1\end{pmatrix}=I+2J,\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad J^2=-I.
$$

The real part of $\langle Au,u\rangle$ is $\|u\|_2^2$, and its imaginary part has absolute value at most $2\|u\|_2^2$. Thus the matrix satisfies `IsSectorialMatrix` with constant 2. The algebra map $\varphi(a+bi)=aI+bJ$ transports the scalar exponential to the matrix exponential, giving

$$
T(t)=\exp(-tA)=e^{-t}
\begin{pmatrix}\cos(2t)&-\sin(2t)\\\sin(2t)&\cos(2t)\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Each entry has modulus at most 1 for $t\ge0$, so $\|T(t)u\|_\infty\le2\|u\|_\infty$. The finite-dimensional form is closed on the full, dense space, and its associated operator is $A$. For the $L^2$ step, $J^*=-J$, hence $A^*=I-2J$ and $AA^*=A^*A=5I$. Thus $A$ is normal and unitarily diagonalizable, with eigenvalues $1\pm2i$. For $z$ in a sector $|\arg z|\le\theta<\arctan(1/2)$,

$$
\operatorname{Re}(z(1\pm2i))\ge|z|(\cos\theta-2\sin\theta)\ge0,
\qquad |e^{-z(1\pm2i)}|\le1.
$$

Unitary diagonalization therefore gives $\|e^{-zA}\|_{2\to2}\le1$ throughout that sector. The matrix exponential is entire, so this proves sector contractivity and bounded analyticity on $L^2$. These form and complex-time identifications explain the source correspondence; the Lean predicates encode sectoriality and the real-time matrix bound.

## Falsifier

Let $g_0,g_1>0$ and $r=g_0/g_1$. The real vectors $(1/g_0,-1/g_1)$ and $(1/g_0,1/g_1)$ both have weighted sup norm 1. Testing the zeroth coordinate of the first image and the first coordinate of the second image forces

$$
e^{-t}(\cos(2t)+r\sin(2t))\le1,\qquad
e^{-t}(\cos(2t)+r^{-1}\sin(2t))\le1
\quad(t\ge0).
$$

For $f_c(t)=e^{-t}(\cos(2t)+c\sin(2t))$, one has $f_c(0)=1$ and $f_c'(0)=2c-1$. If $c>1/2$, then $f_c(t)>1$ at some positive time. Either $r>1/2$, or $r\le1/2$ and $r^{-1}>1/2$. One of the two required inequalities therefore fails. Equivalently, their right derivatives would require both $2r\le1$ and $2/r\le1$.

## Triage

### What the settlement shows

1. **Proved (Lean) — the counterexample.** The finite-dimensional matrix instance is sectorial, L∞-bounded, and admits no contractive positive weight. Via its finite-dimensional sectorial-form interpretation, the answer to Problem 22 is negative.

2. **Computed (exact mathematical reasoning) — the mechanism.** A diagonal weight conjugates the rotation generator but cannot eliminate rotation. The real slice of the ℓ∞ unit ball is a square whose corners rotation pushes outward. Diagonal rescaling turns it into a rectangle; with angular speed 2 and damping rate 1, at least one corner still moves outward at small positive times, since contractivity would require both $2r\le1$ and $2/r\le1$. A scalar diagonal weight commutes with rotation, but does not remove this obstruction; a nonscalar diagonal weight does not commute with it.

3. **Open — Problem 21.** Characterization of L∞-boundedness via the form is not settled by this result.

4. **Open — Problem 23.** Characterization of weighted L∞-contractive semigroups is not settled by this result.

5. **Open — Problem 24.** Construction of weights is not settled by this result.

6. **Open — positive semigroups.** Problem 22 under the positivity assumption discussed in the comments is not settled by this result. The counterexample's real-time matrix has a negative off-diagonal entry for small positive times, so it does not preserve the positive cone.
