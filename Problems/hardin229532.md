# Hardin's recurrence for the $n\times 6$ one-defect three-colour strip

## Problem

[OEIS A229532](https://oeis.org/A229532), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of defective 3-colorings of an n X 6 0..2 array connected horizontally, diagonally and antidiagonally with exactly one mistake, and colors introduced in row-major 0..2 order.

and the empirical recurrence

> Empirical: a(n) = 36*a(n-1) - 436*a(n-2) + 1854*a(n-3) + 340*a(n-4) - 19128*a(n-5) + 23849*a(n-6) + 56184*a(n-7) - 108224*a(n-8) - 40816*a(n-9) + 160400*a(n-10) - 34136*a(n-11) - 83203*a(n-12) + 42628*a(n-13) + 6468*a(n-14) - 6334*a(n-15) + 204*a(n-16) + 240*a(n-17) - 25*a(n-18) for n > 19.

Both quotations are from the [OEIS text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A229532&fmt=text).
The assertion registered in
[issue #31328](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31328)
is this order-eighteen recurrence for every $n\ge 20$, with $n$ rows,
$6$ columns and exactly $1$ monochromatic bond. The sequence offset
is one. The source's `result t`, for every `t : Nat`, uses the shifted
index $n=t+20$ and has exactly that range.

For an array $f:\operatorname{Fin}n\times\operatorname{Fin}6\to\operatorname{Fin}3$,
a defect is a bond whose endpoint colours agree. The HDA bonds are
$(i,j)$--$(i,j+1)$, $(i,j)$--$(i+1,j+1)$, and
$(i,j+1)$--$(i+1,j)$ whenever both endpoints lie in the array. Vertical
and periodic boundary bonds are absent. Every bond changes the column
index by one, so the graph is bipartite by column parity and has six
neighbours at an interior site. The source sweeps rows.

A229532 is the width-six one-defect member of the HDA family containing
the settled width-five one-defect A229531, registered in
[#31121](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31121)
and merged in [#31278](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/pull/31278).
A229634 is the two-defect counterpart of that width-five strip.

## Motivation

The settling declaration is
`D5/S3/StatisticalMechanics/Potts/CrossedStripDefectsWide/HardinA229532Strip.result`.
The external assertion is preregistered in
[issue #31328](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31328);
the source note has bibkey `hardin2013a229532`.

With antiferromagnetic Potts energy
$E(f)=J\sum_{\{u,v\}\in B}\mathbf{1}_{f(u)=f(v)}$ and $J>0$,
each monochromatic bond contributes one positive unsatisfied-bond cost.
These canonical colourings represent states of the three-state Potts
antiferromagnet with exactly $D=1$ unsatisfied bond on the
horizontal–diagonal–antidiagonal strip without vertical bonds (HDA).
The row-major condition chooses one representative from each global colour
relabelling orbit in the nonconstant range used by the proof. The theorem
is a finite combinatorial statement under this bond and energy convention.

## Gap

The OEIS formula is labelled empirical. The registered target is its
validity for every $n\ge 20$ from the literal array definition;
agreement with finitely many sequence terms does not supply that statement.

The literature check (the OEIS entry and its links; arXiv searches for Potts strips with unsatisfied bonds and for defective colourings; Chang–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 107 (2002) 1207–1253, which treats rectangular square-lattice strips) located no proof of this recurrence.
The literature scope is the one recorded in #31328 and the linked family registration.
Worldwide priority and literature completeness remain unverified.

## Route

1. **Literal count and normalization.** `a` filters every `Fin n × Fin 6 → Fin 3` array by exactly one HDA defect and the lifted row-major canonical predicate. `DefectTransfer.relabel_defects` supplies colour-permutation invariance. `HardinA233177Canonical.canonical_pattern_card` gives the six-to-one labelled-to-canonical bridge in the nonconstant range. A constant array with $m+1$ rows has $5(m+1)+10m$ HDA bonds, so it cannot contribute to the one-defect sector.
2. **Width-six exact-budget transfer.** `HardinA229584StripData` supplies the six-site row type and horizontal bonds; `HardinA229532StripData.between` is the antidiagonal bond set united with its endpoint-swapped image. The 31 classes comprise ten proper-row classes with one residual defect, ten proper-row classes with zero residual defects, and eleven defective-row classes with zero residual defects. Both ten-by-ten proper diagonal blocks reuse the published `HardinA233172DiagonalStrip.transfer/64` entries. `raw_successors` supplies the factor-64 bridge to actual proper successor counts; the structural cut and class modules establish the remaining defect entries and `HardinA229532StripClasses.transition_counts`. The finite certificate checks terminal acceptance, budget preservation, every transition sum, initialization, the cached forward steps, and a degree-eighteen vector relation.
3. **Propagation and the first registered index.** Write $u=\mathrm{initial}$, $v=\mathrm{terminal}$, and $W_m=uT^m$. The vector certificate gives $W_{20}=\sum_{j=0}^{17}c_jW_{2+j}$, with the displayed recurrence coefficients in reverse order. `DefectTransfer.forward_shift` propagates it to recurrence indices at least $21$. The separate `base_certificate` gives $W_{19}v=\sum_{j=0}^{17}c_jW_{1+j}v$, which supplies index $20$ as a terminal scalar identity. The source's vector identity starts at power $20$. `DefectTransfer.lumped_count` identifies $uT^{n-1}v$ with the labelled exact-budget count, and the colour-orbit bridge gives $uT^{n-1}v=6a(n)$ in the range used. Cancelling six gives the registered recurrence for every $n\ge20$.

## Falsifier

A canonical HDA count at some $n\ge 20$ that violated the displayed
recurrence, or a failed terminal, budget, transition, initialization, or
forward identity in the finite transfer certificate, would contradict
the result theorem.
