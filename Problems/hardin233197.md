# Barker's generating function for the three-column eight-colour pair-exclusion strip

## Problem

[OEIS A233197](https://oeis.org/A233197), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of n X 3 0..7 arrays with no element x(i,j) adjacent to itself or value 7-x(i,j) horizontally, antidiagonally or vertically, top left element zero, and 1 appearing before 2 3 4 5 and 6, 2 appearing before 3 4 and 5, and 3 appearing before 4 in row major order (unlabelled 8-colorings with no clashing color pairs).

and FORMULA

> Empirical: a(n) = 64*a(n-1) - 960*a(n-2) + 4096*a(n-3) for n>4.
>
> Empirical g.f.: x*(3 - 156*x + 2016*x^2 - 7168*x^3) / ((1 - 8*x)*(1 - 56*x + 512*x^2)). - _Colin Barker_, Oct 10 2018

Each row is a path of three cells; consecutive rows are joined by the three
vertical bonds and the two antidiagonal bonds $(i,j+1)$–$(i+1,j)$: a
three-leg strip of the triangular lattice. A bond is legal when its
endpoints differ and do not sum to $7$.

## Route

1. **Scanner and pairs.** The eight-colour row-major scanner, the
   width-generic strip transfer and the bond equivalence of
   `HardinA233196Strip` are applied directly; the map $c\mapsto\min(c,7-c)$
   onto `Fin 4` turns each bond condition into inequality of pair labels.
2. **Classes.** Boundaries are grouped into five classes: colour $2$ not yet
   seen (the row uses only the pairs of colours $0$ and $1$); colour $2$ seen
   but not colour $3$, with the outer pair labels equal ($ABA$) or unequal
   ($ABC$); and colour $3$ seen, again split into $ABA$ and $ABC$. The first
   rows $(0,1,0)$, $(0,1,7)$ and $(0,1,2)$ give the initial vector
   $(2,0,1,0,0)$.
3. **Transfer.** Kernel decisions over single rows give the class matrix
   $$T=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&2&2\\0&0&0&8&8\\0&0&8&4&16\\0&0&0&16&16\\0&0&0&8&40\end{pmatrix}.$$

   $a(1..4)=3,36,1440,62720$ and $a(0)=0$ gives the series identity.

### What the settlement shows

The class matrix is block upper triangular. The first two classes have zero
rows on the diagonal block: a row before colour $2$ or of type $ABA$ before
colour $3$ cannot be followed by a row of the same kind, so these classes
are transient and account for the extra numerator degree and the start
$n>4$ of Hardin's recurrence. The class $ABC$ before colour $3$ is a
one-dimensional sector with eigenvalue $8$, Barker's factor $1-8x$. Once
colour $3$ is seen, the two classes form the block
$\begin{pmatrix}16&16\\8&40\end{pmatrix}$ with characteristic polynomial
$t^2-56t+512$, Barker's quadratic factor. Compared with width two, where the
sectors had eigenvalues $4$ and $16$, the width-three strip needs the
outer-label split $ABA$/$ABC$, which is what makes the second sector
quadratic.
