# Irwin's hook-sum conjecture for OEIS A138099

## Problem

Paul Curtz's OEIS A138099, revision 26, NAME and FORMULA blocks,
contains Benedict W. J. Irwin's unnumbered conjecture:

> Conjecture: a(n) = HookSum(T(n))-HookSum(T(n-1)), where HookSum(A) is the sum of all hooks across a tableau A, and T(n) are the spiraling sequence of tableaux:

The complete displayed diagrams and arithmetic rows are reproduced in
`Library/FiniteGeometry/irwin2016a138099.md`. The arithmetic row is
$R(k,j)=j+\lfloor k/2\rfloor$, for $k\geq1$ and
$1\leq j\leq\lceil k/2\rceil$. Put
$S(k)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lceil i/2\rceil$. A source sequence satisfies
$a(S(k-1)+j)=R(k,j)$ on that domain, with no restriction on $a(0)$.

The literal square spiral begins at $(0,0)$, travels west, south, east,
north, and repeats these directions with leg lengths $1,1,2,2,3,3,\ldots$.
$T_0=\varnothing$ and $T_{n+1}$ inserts walk cell $n$ into $T_n$.
For occupied integer cells, an ordinary hook contains its own cell and the
occupied cells strictly right in its row and strictly below in its column.
$H(D)$ sums these hook lengths; $H(\varnothing)=0$.

## Route

The private helper `hook_sum_insert` proves that inserting a fresh cell
$p$ into an arbitrary finite diagram $D$ gives
$H(D\cup\{p\})=H(D)+1+r+c$, where $r$ and $c$ are the old row and column
sizes. The proof partitions each line about $p$ and sums hook changes.

The private `square_invariant` and `layer_diagrams` identify the actual
walk with a complete $m$ by $m$ square followed by its two boundary strips.
At the $j$th insertion in the first strip the old line sizes are
$(m,j-1)$; in the second they are $(j-1,m)$. Thus both increments are
$m+j$. The private `first_block` and `second_block` compare these with
rows $2m$ and $2m+1$, using
$S(2m-1)=m^2$ for $m\geq1$ and $S(2m)=m^2+m$ for $m\geq0$.
The singleton at $m=0$ is handled separately. Row addresses construct
a source arithmetic sequence, while insertion constructs and uniquely
determines the spiral sequence. These arguments are used by `result`.

For cardinality and injectivity, the geometric lemmas give freshness of
each inserted cell: every positive index lies in exactly one of the two
row-address blocks, whose `column_counts` or `row_counts` excludes the
next cell from the old diagram. The first cell is outside the empty
set. By induction, inserting a fresh cell increases cardinality by one,
so $|T_n|=n$. If walk cells $i<j$ were equal, the cell at $i$ would
already belong to $T_j$, contradicting freshness at $j$. This is a
proof in prose from the indicated formal geometry; no additional
public Lean theorem for cardinality or injectivity is claimed.

For any finite diagram, each horizontal or vertical unordered pair of
cells contributes exactly once to the hook sum. Hence
$H(D)=|D|+\sum_y\binom{r_y}{2}+\sum_x\binom{c_x}{2}$.
This is a proof in prose by assigning each pair to its left or upper
cell. It explains orientation invariance and why only old line sizes
matter at insertion. At the complete square $T_{m^2}$, its $m$ rows and
$m$ columns all have size $m$, so the same argument gives
$H(T_{m^2})=m^2+2m\binom m2=m^3$ (proved in prose).
These explanations are not separately claimed as kernel-checked
statements. Other growth paths remain open. No source downstream
conclusion needs adjustment because none is stated in the read entry.
