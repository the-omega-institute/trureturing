# Hardin and Barker's corner-occupied king configurations on four-column strips

## Problem

[OEIS A228502](https://oeis.org/A228502), revision 12, has NAME

> Number of n X 4 binary arrays with top left value 1 and no two ones adjacent horizontally, vertically, diagonally or antidiagonally.

and FORMULA

> Empirical: a(n) = 2*a(n-1) + 7*a(n-2) - 2*a(n-3) - 3*a(n-4).

> Empirical g.f.: x*(1 - x)*(3 + 2*x) / (1 - 2*x - 7*x^2 + 2*x^3 + 3*x^4). - _Colin Barker_, Sep 12 2018

The sequence is R. H. Hardin's, with offset 1. A board has $n$ labeled rows and
four labeled columns, open boundaries, and its top-left entry is $1$. Two cells
are adjacent when their row and column indices each differ by at most one and
the cells are distinct, the king-move adjacency. The recurrence is claimed for
every $n\ge5$ in signed integer arithmetic. The series $F(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}a(n)x^n$
has zero constant term.

## Motivation

The settling declaration is
`D5.S3.Combinatorics.TraceAvoidingArrays.HardinA228502Kings.result`. The
external assertion and its conventions are preregistered in
[issue #26982](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/26982).
The source note is
`Library/RecurrenceComposition/hardinbarker2013a228502.md`. Physically, a board
is a configuration of a hard-core lattice gas on the king graph of an $n\times4$
strip, with nearest- and next-nearest-neighbour exclusion and a pinned occupied
corner. In combinatorial terms it is a placement of non-attacking kings.

## Gap

At revision 12 both formulas are still marked empirical. The preregistration's
bounded screen found no proof of the corner-occupied formula. Calkin et al.
(2006) treat the corner-free width-4 count with the same denominator but
without the corner condition.

## Route

States are single rows, that is, functions from four columns to `Bool`. A row is
valid when no two horizontally adjacent cells are both occupied. Two consecutive
rows are compatible when no occupied cell of the first lies within one column of
an occupied cell of the second.

The transfer vectors are $v_0(r)=[r\text{ valid}]$ and
$v_{k+1}(r)=[r\text{ valid}]\sum_s[r,s\text{ compatible}]\,v_k(s)$.
Splitting the finite board space at the first row turns the literal count into
a continuation count, and induction on its length gives
$a(k+1)=\sum_{r:\,r_0=1}v_k(r)$ for every $k$.

A finite certificate shows that $v_4=2v_3+7v_2-2v_1-3v_0$ on all sixteen rows.
Linearity of the transfer step propagates this identity to every shift. That
gives the recurrence for every $n\ge5$ with no exceptional initial index. The
initial values $a(1),\ldots,a(4)=3,5,29,87$ then fix the numerator in the
coefficient comparison for the series.

## Falsifier

Either of the following would contradict `result`:
- a board count violating the recurrence at some $n\ge5$;
- a coefficient mismatch in the series identity.

## Triage

1. **Proved (kernel-checked):** the exact recurrence for every $n\ge5$ and
   Barker's rational generating function, by `HardinA228502Kings.result`.
2. **Proved (kernel-checked):** the order-four annihilator identity on the
   sixteen-row transfer vectors, by `HardinA228502Kings.finite_certificate`.
3. **Open:** the other columns of Hardin's table A228506, and a
   width-uniform transfer description.
4. **Open:** the relation between the corner-occupied count and the
   corner-free count of Calkin et al. The two share the denominator. A proof of
   the numerator relation is not given here.

### What the settlement shows

The recurrence comes from a single annihilating polynomial of the row transfer
step. Because the polynomial annihilates the state vector itself, the recurrence
holds from the first index the source states, with no special case. The
corner condition affects only the boundary weights. This is consistent with the
shared denominator with the corner-free strip (item 4).
