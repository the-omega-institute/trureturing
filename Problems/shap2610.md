# Shapiro Conjecture 1(iii): identity words on finite trees

## Problem

Boris Shapiro, *Transposition Cayley Spectra and Subgraph Counts*,
arXiv:2610.14373v1, Section 4.1, page 14, Conjecture 1(iii), states:

> For a tree T, a word of length 2|E(T)| in the edges of T using every edge has product 1 if and only if every edge occurs exactly twice and, for every vertex x of T, the subword formed by the edges incident to x is a non-crossing double occurrence word.

Each edge acts by the transposition of its endpoints. Multiplication uses
$(\sigma\rho)(v)=\sigma(\rho(v))$. Alternating occurrences of distinct letters
are crossings; nested pairs are allowed. The one-vertex tree and empty word
are included.

| Product is identity | `(w.map edgeSwap).prod = 1` | Existing endpoint transposition; literal list product |
| Every edge occurs twice | `w.count a = 2` for each tree edge | Exact multiplicity |
| Every vertex's incident subword is noncrossing | `NoncrossingDoubleOccurrence` of the order-preserving membership filter | Existing `Crossing` predicate on the position pairs |
| If and only if | Both directions of `result : claim` | No dropped clause |

`NoncrossingDoubleOccurrence` displays the source convention literally:
every occurring letter has two copies and distinct letters have no crossing
position pairs. `claim` retains the preregistered body. The proofs reuse
`SupportAndEdgeWordReflection.edgeSwap` and
`NoncrossingNonnestingGraphRecurrence.Crossing`.

## Motivation

The theorem replaces the bounded verification of the local criterion by a
uniform proof for all finite trees. It identifies the local obstruction to an
identity word of minimal onto-support length.

## Route

The following helper names are private declarations in
`D5.S3.Combinatorics.Graph.SupportForestMoments.ShapiroTreeIdentityWords`.

`ShapiroProbe.multiplicity_two` uses acyclicity: an edge used exactly once
would transport a vertex across the cut left by deleting that edge. Positivity
of every edge count and the total length then force all counts to be two.

`ShapiroList.interval_cancellation` applies to any monoid with involutive
labels and commuting labels at alternating pairs. It replaces a contiguous
interval by its letters occurring exactly once, in their original order.
The proof selects a repeated pair with a duplicate-free interior, commutes its
label across that interior, cancels the pair, and uses strong induction.

`ShapiroProbe.distinct_forest_fixed` says that a product of distinct forest
edges fixes a vertex exactly when no used edge is incident to that vertex.
`ShapiroLeaf.leaf_interval` extracts a fixed-neighbor constraint from the two
copies of a leaf edge. In `ShapiroMain.identity_separated`, cancellation and
the distinct-edge criterion rule out crossings with that leaf edge; deleting
the leaf edge gives the induction step. Conversely, vertex-disjoint edge
transpositions commute, and interval cancellation proves sufficiency.

## Falsifier

A finite tree and onto edge word of length twice its edge count for which
identity product differs from the stated multiplicity-and-local condition
would contradict the target. A cyclic graph is outside its hypotheses.
Source-fidelity checks must retain alternating crossings while allowing
nested pairs and the empty word.

## Triage

`theorem`: the named Conjecture 1(iii) is proved.

### What the settlement shows

1. **Proved, kernel-checked:** `result` establishes the equivalence for every
   finite tree. The necessity mechanism is the leaf interval and distinct
   forest-edge support criterion; sufficiency uses commuting crossing labels.
2. **Proved in prose using kernel-checked helpers:**
   `ShapiroMain.identity_separated`, `ShapiroProbe.multiplicity_two`, and
   `ShapiroProbe.distinct_forest_fixed` assume only acyclicity, allowing
   isolated vertices. Together with `ShapiroPattern.local_iff` and
   `ShapiroList.interval_cancellation`, the same implications give the finite
   forest analogue: multiplicity two follows from onto support and length,
   identity implies separated alternating edges, the local condition is
   equivalent to separation, and commuting disjoint labels give identity.
   This assembled consequence is proved in prose using those helpers;
   the only exported settling theorem is for trees.
3. **Proved, kernel-checked:** `ShapiroList.interval_cancellation` is generic
   over a monoid. It requires involutive labels and commuting labels at every
   alternating pair in a double word, and applies to each contiguous interval.
4. **Computed:** acyclicity cannot be removed wholesale. The triangle instance
   in the experiment entry has identity product and fails the local condition.
   It is neither a tree counterexample nor a Lean-certified graph extension.
5. **Open as a delivery boundary:** no Lean Catalan enumeration is delivered.
   Shapiro states that (iii) implies the star formula (i), by counting
   noncrossing pairings and their edge labelings. That enumeration has
   classical antecedents; no novelty or open-status claim about (i) is made.
6. **Open as a delivery boundary:** the cycle formula (ii), inversion, and
   broader reconstruction are outside this settlement. Their mathematical
   status is not determined by this proof; separate repository results are
   not included in this resolution claim.

The settlement supplies the local criterion used in the source's implication
to the star formula. It makes no further claim about the source's remaining
problems.
