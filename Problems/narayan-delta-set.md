# Narayan's expanded-generator delta-set conjecture

## Problem

Ashwin Narayan, *The Delta Set of a Numerical Monoid Generated by a Generalized Arithmetic Sequence*, [arXiv:2610.02495v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02495v1), Conjecture 18.3, p. 22, states:

> For $S_h=\langle a,ah,ah+d,ah+2d,\ldots,ah+kd\rangle$, $\Delta(S_h)=E(e,d)$.

Section 3 assumes $\gcd(a,d)=1$, $1<k<a$, $h>1$, and puts $e=h-1$. All four parameters are positive integers. Every displayed generator, including the redundant $ah$, is retained as a separate nonnegative factorization coordinate. The delta set is the union over represented natural numbers of positive differences between consecutive distinct lengths. The subtractive Euclidean algorithm starts at $(e,d)$; its final equal pair may be repeated without adding visited positive coordinates. These interpretations resolve the unbound $s$, omitted endpoint order and malformed initialization in Definitions 3.1–3.2, as fixed by [#23185](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/23185).

## Gap

Adding $ah$ changes factorization lengths despite leaving the generated monoid unchanged. In particular, $2a$ admits both a single $2a$ coordinate and two $a$ coordinates. A finite calculation cannot exclude larger gaps at larger represented values. The proof therefore establishes complete normalization and intermediate-length realization for the whole family, without a cutoff in the represented value.

## Route

For $a>0$ coprime to $2$, put $b_i=2a+2i$ for $0\le i<a$ and use the tuple $(a,b_0,\ldots,b_{a-1})$. A configuration consists of $x$ copies of $a$ and a list of occurrences $b_i$. The rules replace $b_0$ by two copies of $a$, replace $b_i+b_j$ by $a+a+b_{i+j}$ when $i+j<a$, and replace it by $b_0+b_0+b_{i+j-a}$ otherwise. Each rule preserves value and raises length by one (`rewrite_value`, `rewrite_size`). Positive generators bound length by value (`size_le_value`). Strong induction on value minus length produces a terminal configuration (`normalizes`).

A terminal list is empty or consists of one positive index. Its residue modulo $a$ determines that index because multiplication by $2$ is cancellable modulo $a$ under coprimality (`cancel_residue`, using `Nat.ModEq.cancel_left_of_coprime`; `terminal_unique`). Induction over the actual rewrite trace supplies every intervening length (`trace_interval`). Coordinate packing and expansion preserve value and length (`pack_value`, `pack_size`, `pack_expand`, `expand_value`, `expand_size`, `lengths_iff`). Thus any length between two represented lengths is represented (`family_intermediate`). At value $2a$, lengths $1$ and $2$ occur, giving global delta set $\{1\}$ (`family_delta`). These helper declarations are private in the settling module.

At $a=3$, `generators_three` identifies $(3,6,8,10)$ with this family. The proof of `result` instantiates the universal assertion at $(a,h,d,k)=(3,2,2,2)$ and contradicts its initial Euclidean coordinate $2\in E(1,2)$.

## Falsifier

The source equality predicts a gap $2$ for $(3,6,8,10)$. Complete interval realization excludes that gap for every represented value; finite-window agreement alone would not suffice. The conclusion concerns the displayed expanded tuple, not the invariant obtained by first deleting redundant generators.

### What the settlement shows

The failure mechanism is the extra factorization coordinate $ah$: for $h=2$ the family-specific replacements fill all intermediate lengths, while the Euclidean prediction still includes $2$. The positive coprime family above survives as the precise interval and singleton-delta statements proved by the private helpers. The computational assertion about the tuple omitting $ah$ has only the stated finite scope. The refutation supplies no conclusion about the catenary conjectures or the main theorem for unexpanded generators; an application of the expanded equality requires an independent valid replacement. No classification outside this family or sharpness of coprimality is asserted.
