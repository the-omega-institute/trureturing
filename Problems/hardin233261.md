# Hardin's complete six-row antidiagonal array recurrence

## Problem

R. H. Hardin's [OEIS A233261](https://oeis.org/A233261), revision 8,
defines six-row six-colour arrays and labels the ten-lag recurrence
"Empirical", explicitly for `n>16`. The verbatim NAME and FORMULA,
pinned export and quotation licence are in
`Library/Automata/hardin2013a233261.md`.

The complete event in the
[immutable preregistration](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/28857#issuecomment-6768941004)
is the following paragraph, whose UTF-8 SHA256 without a trailing newline is
`932336f06d425b3988e1c636ab6db5275e017dd94af2eccba1f0f52fb052d93f`:

For every integer n >= 1, c(n) counts exactly the 6-by-n arrays A:{0,...,5} x {0,...,n-1}->{0,...,5} described by the original NAME. For 0<=r<6 and 0<=j<n-1, the values at (r,j),(r,j+1) must differ and their natural values must not sum to 5. The same restriction applies at (r,j+1),(r+1,j) for 0<=r<5 and 0<=j<n-1: the antidiagonal bonds. A(0,0)=0. In strict row-major order (r*n+j), every occurrence of 2, 3 or 4 has an earlier 1, and every occurrence of 3 has an earlier 2. Colours may be absent and no occurrence of every colour is required. Counts are natural numbers and are cast into the integers in the following equality. ## Gap

The source states the recurrence empirically. Listed sequence values do
not prove the literal normalization/counting correspondence or an
unbounded recurrence. A presumed common orbit divisor would lose the
different lift weights of patterns using two and three complementary
pairs. Both the exact weighted count and all-width propagation are needed.

## Route

1. The literal predicate is identified with the existing finite-array
   normalization infrastructure. Pair labels represent `{0,5}`, `{1,4}`
   and `{2,3}`. Canonical first occurrences fix label names and the first
   side of each used pair; a pattern using `k` pairs has `2^(6*n-k)` lifts.
2. At width at least two a proper pair pattern uses two or three labels.
   Label permutations act in orbits of size six. Two-label patterns have
   constant columns alternating with parity, giving one canonical pattern.
   Summing the different lift weights proves `counting_bridge`:
   $$48(a(n):\mathbb Z)=64^n((L(n):\mathbb Z)+6).$$
   The structural classification is supplied, up to finite row transport,
   by the existing private `HardinA233258Strip.columns_constant` and public
   `HardinA233177Ladder.two_label_classification`. The retained proof
   repeats the structural arguments; no new compiled supplier adapter is
   asserted here.
3. Six pair labels form a column, giving 729 raw states. `row_certificate`
   checks the sparse transitions against the exact bond predicate.
   `count_transfer` identifies their scalar readout with `L`.
   Seventeen vectors, represented through 122 classes, are verified as
   successive powers of the actual transfer on the all-ones vector using
   `TransferMatrix.tabulatedPowers`. `power_polynomial` checks a degree-ten
   relation after six transitions, and `TransferMatrix.scalarRelationShift`
   propagates it through arbitrarily many further powers.
4. The polynomial vanishes at one, retaining the correction six.
   `scale_recurrence` consumes `counting_bridge`, scales lag `j` by `64^j`
   and cancels 48. `shifted` and `result` reindex the equality as every
   integer `n>16`, with no lagged count below width seven.
