# Bala's even-step Fibonacci-polynomial integrality conjecture

## Problem

Peter Bala's COMMENT 2 in OEIS A349272 states:

> More generally, if F(n,x) denotes the n-th Fibonacci polynomial we conjecture that, for each n, the rational function Product_{k = 1..2*n+1} F(m*k,x) / Sum_{k = 1..2*n+1} F(m*k,x) is an integral polynomial.

The preceding COMMENT begins “Let m be an even positive integer.” The offset starts at zero. Thus $m,n\in\mathbb N$, $m\ge2$, and $m$ is even. The polynomial convention is $F_0=0$, $F_1=1$, $F_{j+2}=XF_{j+1}+F_j$. Both inclusive ranges are $1\le k\le2n+1$. Write $P=\prod F_{mk}$ and $D=\sum F_{mk}$. The conclusion is $D\ne0$ and $P/D=H$ in $\mathbb Q(X)$ for some $H\in\mathbb Z[X]$. It asserts no pointwise division at roots of $D$.

## Route

The proof reuses `LucasEvenDescent.lucas_recurrence` and its published `doubling`, `LucasCompanion.companion_power_det`, `MatrixTracePowerSum.pow_add_two`, and `FibonacciRecurrencePolynomialCoefficients.fibonacci_recurrence_polynomial_coefficients`.

For the Fibonacci companion matrix $A$, evenness gives $\det(A^m)=1$. Setting $c=F_m$ and $t=\operatorname{tr}(A^m)$ identifies $F_{mk}=cU_k(t)$, where $U_0=0$, $U_1=1$ and $U_{k+2}=tU_{k+1}-U_k$. The odd partial sum is
$\sum_{k=1}^{2n+1}U_k=U_{n+1}(U_{n+1}+U_n)$.
For $n>0$, put
$W=\prod_{1\le k\le2n,\ k\ne n+1}U_k$ and
$H=c^{2n}(U_{n+1}-U_n)W$.
The doubling identity $U_{2n+1}=U_{n+1}^2-U_n^2$ gives $DH=P$ without cancellation. The factors at $n+1$ and $2n+1$ occupy distinct numerator positions. For $n=0$, take $H=1$. The final denominator summand has unique greatest degree $m(2n+1)-1$ and coefficient one, so $D\ne0$.

## Falsifier

A positive even $m$ and natural $n$ contradicting the literal polynomial claim would contradict `result`. A zero of $D$ at a numerical value of $X$ is outside the pointwise quotient assertion and is not such a falsifier.
