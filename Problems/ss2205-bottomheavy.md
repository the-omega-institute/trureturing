# Bottom heaviness of the four q-Stirling coefficient families

## Problem

Bruce E. Sagan and Joshua P. Swanson, *q-Stirling numbers in type B*,
arXiv:2205.14078v1, Section 7.5, Conjecture 7.6, printed page 40:

> For each n, k, the coefficients of S[n, k], c[n, k], S_B[n, k], and c_B[n, k] are all bottom heavy.

The preceding definition, printed page 39, is:

> A sequence is bottom heavy if aₖ ≥ aₙ₋ₖ for k < n/2.

The published version, *European Journal of Combinatorics* 118 (2024), 103899,
DOI 10.1016/j.ejc.2023.103899, repeats the conjecture on page 31.

### Statement echo

For every $n\in\mathbb N$, $k\in\mathbb Z$, and
$P\in\{S[n,k],c[n,k],S_B[n,k],c_B[n,k]\}$, the conclusion is

$$
2r<\operatorname{natDegree}(P)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
[q^{\operatorname{natDegree}(P)-r}]P\leq[q^r]P
\qquad(r\in\mathbb N).
$$

The Lean definitions `S`, `c`, `SB`, and `cB` use the source's common delta
initial row and its recurrences (arXiv equations (2), (5), (8), and (9)).
The names `SB` and `cB` denote $S_B$ and $c_B$. The existing
`D5.S0.Certificates.Polynomials.QProductNecessityRefutation.qInteger` supplies
$[a]_q=\sum_{j<a}q^j$ for natural $a$, including $[0]_q=0$.
The second-kind definitions explicitly give zero for negative $k$; this is
the zero branch of the source recurrence, so no negative q-integer is
represented by an incorrect polynomial. All integer $k$ remain quantified.
The first-kind multipliers have natural lengths $n$ and $2n+1$ in successor
rows. Coefficients start at exponent zero. The zero polynomial has
$\operatorname{natDegree}(0)=0$; it and the constant polynomials have no
strict-half index. There is no additional positivity or valid-index hypothesis.
The separately shifted $\overline S[n,k]=q^{\binom{k}{2}}S[n,k]$ is excluded.

## Route

Write $U_a=1+q+\cdots+q^{a-1}$ for $a>0$. The recursive identity

$$
U_{a+1}U_{b+1}=U_{a+b+1}+qU_aU_b
$$

constructs concentric interval layers. The private predicate `Centered d p`
is generated by the interval $U_{d+1}$, addition at a common center, and the
shift $(d,p)\mapsto(d+2,qp)$. Structural induction proves support at most $d$,
reflection symmetry about $d/2$, and coefficient increase toward the center.
The private theorem `centered_mul_interval` proves closure under multiplication
by $U_{a+1}$.

For a centered layer with positive constant coefficient, symmetry makes its
coefficient at $d$ positive. Any ambient support bound $D$ therefore satisfies
$d\leq D$. If $D-r>d$, the reflected coefficient is zero. Otherwise put
$t=d-(D-r)$. For $2r<D$ one has $t\leq r$ and $t+r\leq d$;
symmetry followed by increase toward the center proves
$[q^{D-r}]p\leq[q^r]p$.

The private predicate `Cone p` includes zero, positive-constant centered atoms,
and their sums. In natural coefficients, a support bound on a sum bounds each
summand separately. Consequently `cone_ambient` proves the comparison at the
ambient degree of the entire sum. `cone_mul` and `families_cone` show that all
four recurrences preserve this class. Applying the support bound at
$D=\operatorname{natDegree}(P)$ gives `result`.

## Falsifier

A counterexample would specify one of the four unshifted source polynomials,
an integer $k$, a natural $n$, and a natural strict-half index $r$ whose
reflected coefficient is larger. The theorem rules out such an instance
for the displayed definitions. A differently shifted polynomial or a
comparison below the polynomial's support bound is outside the conclusion.

## Triage

### What the settlement shows

- **Mechanism — proved, kernel-checked:** `interval_mul` and
  `centered_mul_interval` establish the interval-layer multiplication
  invariant; `cone_mul`, `families_cone`, and `cone_ambient` preserve it through
  the four recurrences and compare at the actual degree of the whole sum.
  These are private declarations of the settling module, used by `result`.
- **Formal extension — proved, kernel-checked:** the private `cone_ambient`
  proves the comparison for every `Cone p` and every natural ambient $D$
  above all nonzero coefficients, under $2r<D$. It is not restricted to the
  four source recurrences. The coefficients remain natural.
- **Finite validation — computed:** the experiment in Evidence reproduces
  $S_B[6,4]$ and $c_B[7,5]$ as nonunimodal and bottom heavy; every finite and
  structural check in the stated ranges exits 0. No larger numerical range
  is claimed.
- **Ambient-degree condition — computed:** the checker's convolution of
  $(1,1)$ with itself gives $(1,2,1)$, of actual degree $2$.
  At $D=1$, $r=0$, the reflected coefficient is $2>1$.
  Thus removing $D\geq d$ invalidates the generic comparison, although the
  original claim always uses the actual degree. This product occurs in the
  experiment's length-two product range; the failure at $D=1$ is direct
  arithmetic on its displayed coefficients.
- **Exponent-zero anchor — computed:** the checker's table gives
  $S[2,2]=1$. The source's shifted family gives
  $\overline S[2,2]=q$, coefficient list $(0,1)$.
  At actual degree $1$ and $r=0$, its reflected coefficient is $1>0$.
  Exponent shifts therefore need not preserve bottom heaviness. The table
  entry lies in the experiment's stated range; the shifted comparison is
  direct arithmetic using the source's definition.
- **Surviving neighbours — open:** coefficient log-concavity, parity
  log-concavity, general unimodality, asymptotic normality, Conjectures 7.4
  and 7.5, and a formal version for arbitrary real nonnegative weights are
  not settled by this module. The source's other established results remain
  statements of that source; the bounded screen establishes no consequence
  for another result depending on Conjecture 7.6.
