# Khetan's six-cell given-orbit-closure periodicity question

## Problem

Abhishek Khetan, *A Counterexample to Nivat's Conjecture for a Non-Convex
Window of Full Affine Span*, arXiv:2607.09830v2, Conjecture 2:

> Let F be an exact cluster with full affine span. Then for every F-tiling T,
> the orbit closure $\overline{\mathbb Z^2\cdot T}$ contains a 1-periodic F-tiling.

Here “full affine span” has the integer meaning of Definition 1.4:
the subgroup generated by the differences F−F is all of Z². An exact
F-tiling means F⊕T=Z², or equivalently

$$
\sum_{f\in F}1_T(x-f)=1\qquad(x\in\mathbb Z^2).
$$

The question here is its six-element restriction. Section 3.5 says:
“Only the case |F|=6 is left. We cannot decide it, and we leave it open.”
The quantifier is over every given T; existence of an unrelated periodic
tiling complement would not answer this question.

## Motivation

The pinned paper gives an eight-cell counterexample with no periodic point
in the given orbit closure. Its prime-squared theorem covers four cells,
and the cited prime-cardinality theorem covers two, three, five and seven.
The six-cell question determines which of six and eight is the first
cardinality at which the stated orbit-closure property can fail.

## Gap

Tan–Zhang, arXiv:2609.26576v1, Theorem 1.1, already proves that every
tileable set of cardinality twice an odd prime, or nine, in any dimension
has some fully periodic complement. Taking the prime three covers six
cells. That existence theorem does not locate its complement in the orbit
closure of each given T. Herva–Kari, arXiv:2301.05232v1, §3 Theorem 2,
attributes its general finitary integral periodizer result to Kari–Szabados:
if all genuine Laurent line factors of a periodizer have one direction,
the original configuration is periodic. This supplies the one-direction
branch, without a six-cell or low-complexity hypothesis.

The bounded comparisons are recorded in the corresponding three
`Library/Dynamics/` notes. They establish neither exhaustive novelty nor
priority. No pre-probe full-result preregistration issue was found in the
bounded GitHub searches: the Khetan-title search found audit issue #29287
only; the exact arXiv identifier and Nivat-title searches found none.
This absence is not retrospective preregistration and supplies no
open-problem-resolution admission exception. The substantive geometry and

## Route

Use the actual negative tile mask acting by positive shifts on the given
integral indicator. Exact coverage makes its action the constant one.
The proof divides according to the geometric directions of its genuine
nonzero nonunit Laurent line divisors.

With at most one line direction, the integral product-difference
annihilator is split into longitudinal and transverse factors. Primitive
binomial irreducibility and descent from the coefficient fraction field
give the required transverse coprimality in the original coordinates.
A Laurent Bézout identity is cleared before it acts on configurations.
The resulting one-direction recurrence has a uniform nonzero period
because the alphabet is finite. The period belongs to the original T.

With two nonparallel directions, normalize both actual line divisors to
primitive integer vectors without changing their images. Nonzero complex
roots of the divisors force cancellation on every actual occupied fiber.
Determinant keys are jointly injective on the original six points. Every
occupied fiber has at least two points, so each key set has size two or
three. If both had size three, every degree would be two. Summing a
nonzero complex character by columns and by rows forces an omitted
column weight to vanish, a contradiction. This uses the general private
missing-column lemma on arbitrary finite objects, arbitrary key types,
arbitrary complex weights and arbitrary positive common column degree.

Thus the incidence is a complete two-by-three grid, in either orientation,
with every intersection realized by an original point. It follows that
F=p+{0,a}+{0,bu,cu}. Full integer difference span forces the frame (a,u)
to be unimodular and gcd(b,c)=1. These are derived conclusions. The given
T is transported through this affine lattice equivalence, the existing
product theorem gives a period of that same coordinate complement, and
the period is transported back to T. No fixed incidence catalog is used.

For either branch, the fixed-point set of translation by the resulting
nonzero period is closed in the product topology. It contains every shift
of the indicator and hence the entire original orbit closure.

## Falsifier

The bridge would fail if the line-factor hypothesis already encoded
coprimality, if cancellation were assumed instead of derived, if virtual
line intersections replaced actual lattice points, if real span replaced
integer span, or if an alternative complement replaced the given T.
The definitions and final telescope exclude these substitutions. The
orbit closure uses natural-valued configurations with their product
topology; the original zero-one indicator belongs to it via the zero shift.

## Evidence

The scoped-compiled theorem is
`D5/S1/Dynamics/Symbolic/KhetanSixCellPeriodicity.six_cell_exact_cover_periodic`:

$$
\begin{gathered}
F\subset\mathbb Z^2,\quad |F|=6,\quad
\langle F-F\rangle_{\mathbb Z}=\mathbb Z^2,\quad F\oplus T=\mathbb Z^2
\\[2pt]
\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists t\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\},\quad
(\forall x,\ T(x+t)\leftrightarrow T(x))
\ \land\
\left(\forall c\in\overline{\{x\mapsto1_T(x+s):s\in\mathbb Z^2\}},
\ \forall x,\ c(x+t)=c(x)\right).
\end{gathered}
$$

periodic exact tiling and lies in its own orbit closure. The final

## Triage

- [proved: KhetanSixCellPeriodicity.six_cell_exact_cover_periodic]
  Every given exact tiling by an arbitrary six-point full-integer-span
  tile has a nonzero period common to its entire original orbit closure.
- [proved: KhetanLatticeGeometry.two_direction_cancellation_grid]
  Two actual nonparallel primitive cancellation directions force an
  affine two-by-three grid, with both orientations covered.
- [proved: KhetanDirectionalCoprimality.integer_one_direction_periodizer_periodic]
  A nonzero integral periodizer with at most one geometric line-factor
  direction gives a nonzero period of the original finitary integral
  configuration.
- [open] Removing full integer difference span, or replacing it by real
  affine span, is outside the final theorem. No extension is asserted.
- [open] The proof does not assert two independent periods of every given
  T, or a uniform period vector shared by all complements of a fixed F.
- [open] Other cardinalities and higher-dimensional given-orbit-closure
  questions require separate arguments. The eight-cell counterexample in
  the pinned source prevents a blanket extension to arbitrary finite F.
