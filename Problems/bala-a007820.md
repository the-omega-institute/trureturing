# Square divisibility of odd central Stirling numbers

## Problem

Peter Bala's unnumbered COMMENTS conjecture in [OEIS A007820](https://oeis.org/A007820), revision 89, states:

> Conjecture: a(2*n+1) is divisible by (2*n + 1)^2.

The NAME is “Stirling numbers of second kind S(2n,n).” Thus the assertion is
$(2n+1)^2\mid S(4n+2,2n+1)$ for every $n\in\mathbb N$, including zero.
Here $S(r,k)$ counts partitions of an $r$-element labelled set into exactly
$k$ nonempty, unlabelled blocks. The conjecture is OEIS-only; it has no
identified source DOI or arXiv identifier. Preregistration: [#22943](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/22943).

## Route

The module directly applies
`D5.S1.Recurrence.Parity.StirlingPowerFactorialPrimePeriod.stirling2_inclusion_exclusion`.
Writing $E_r(m)=m!S(r,m)$, inclusion-exclusion gives
$E_r(m)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-i}\binom mi i^r$.
Reflecting $i$ to $m-i$, expanding the binomial, interchanging the two finite
sums and cancelling the nonzero integer $m!$ gives

$$
S(2m,m)=\sum_{j=0}^{2m}(-1)^{m+j}\binom{2m}{j}m^jS(2m-j,m).
$$

The private helper `stirling_reflection` proves this for every natural $m$,
including zero. The full range is retained; no truncation at $j=m$ is used.
For odd $m$, the $j=0$ term is $-S(2m,m)$, while the $j=1$ term is
$2m^2S(2m-1,m)$. For $j\geq2$, $m^2$ divides $m^j$. Hence
$m^2\mid2S(2m,m)$. Since $\gcd(m^2,2)=1$, cancellation proves
$m^2\mid S(2m,m)$. The private helper `odd_square_dvd` proves this implication;
`result` substitutes $m=2n+1$.

## Falsifier

An $n\in\mathbb N$ for which $(2n+1)^2$ does not divide
$S(4n+2,2n+1)$ would refute the source assertion. The kernel-checked universal
result excludes such a counterexample within the formal system.
