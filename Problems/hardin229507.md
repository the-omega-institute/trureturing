# Hardin's recurrence for the $n\times5$ one-defect three-colour strip

## Problem

[OEIS A229507](https://oeis.org/A229507), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of defective 3-colorings of an n X 5 0..2 array connected diagonally and antidiagonally with exactly one mistake, and colors introduced in row-major 0..2 order.

and the empirical recurrence

> Empirical: a(n) = 30*a(n-1) - 225*a(n-2) - 540*a(n-3) + 8748*a(n-4) - 9720*a(n-5) - 72900*a(n-6) + 174960*a(n-7) - 104976*a(n-8) for n > 9.

Both quotations are from the [OEIS text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A229507&fmt=text).
The assertion registered in
[issue #31593](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31593)
is this order-eight recurrence for every $n\ge10$.
Let $N_D(r)$ be the number of canonical $r\times5$ arrays with
exactly $D$ monochromatic diagonal or antidiagonal bonds.
Here $a(n)=N_1(n)$, so $a(n)$ counts $n\times5$ arrays, with exactly $D=1$ unsatisfied bond.
The OEIS sequence offset is one.
The source's `result t` quantifies over every `t : Nat` and
uses index $t+10$, exactly the registered range.

For an array $f:\operatorname{Fin}r\times\operatorname{Fin}5\to\operatorname{Fin}3$,
the DA bonds are $(i,j)$--$(i+1,j+1)$ and
$(i,j+1)$--$(i+1,j)$, for $i+1<r$ and $j+1<5$.
Each bond is counted once. Horizontal, vertical and periodic boundary
bonds are absent. A defect means equality of the endpoint colours.
The canonical predicate requires every occurrence of a colour to
have an earlier occurrence of every smaller colour, in row-major
order with rank $5i+j$. It forces the first cell to be zero on a
nonempty array; it does not require all three colours to occur.

## Motivation

The settling declaration is
`D5/S3/StatisticalMechanics/Potts/DiagonalStripDefects/HardinA229507Strip.result`.
The external assertion is preregistered in
[issue #31593](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31593);
the source note has bibkey `hardin2013a229507`.

With antiferromagnetic Potts energy
$E(f)=J\sum_{\{u,v\}\in B}\mathbf1_{f(u)=f(v)}$ and $J>0$,
these counts describe three-state Potts antiferromagnet states on
five-column strips whose bonds are the diagonals and antidiagonals
of the square grid. The family has exactly $D=0,1,2$ unsatisfied
bonds; this sequence selects $D=1$ and thus energy $E=JD$.
The $D=0$ sector consists of proper colourings, the zero-temperature states.

The bonds preserve $i+j$ modulo two. On parity class $p\in\{0,1\}$,
the coordinates $((i+j-p)/2,(i-j-p)/2)$ send the two DA bond
directions to the two unit square-lattice directions. Each parity
class is a square lattice rotated by $45^\circ$, restricted by the
strip's sawtooth boundaries. The global colour normalization couples
the two parity classes in their orbit count. It uses a single
permutation of the three colours for the whole array.
The theorem concerns finite combinatorial counts with this graph
and energy convention.

## Gap

The OEIS recurrence is labelled empirical. The registered target is
its validity for every $n\ge10$ from the literal canonical
array count. Agreement with finitely many terms does not supply that
universal assertion.

The literature check (the OEIS entry and its links; arXiv searches for Potts strips with unsatisfied bonds, defective colourings and diagonal strips; Chang–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 107 (2002) 1207–1253, and Chang–Jacobsen–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 114 (2004) 763–823, which treat rectangular square- and triangular-lattice strips) located no proof of this recurrence.
The literature scope is the check recorded in #31593.
Literature completeness and worldwide priority remain unverified.

## Route

1. **Literal count and global normalization.** `a n` filters all
   `Fin n × Fin 5 → Fin 3` arrays by exactly one monochromatic
   DA bond and `Canonical` in the original row-major order.
   `counting_bridge` applies `DefectTransfer.lumped_count` with
   boundary projection `id`, cost `bondDefects between`, and
   validity $d\le1$. With $u=\mathtt{initial}$ and
   $e=\mathtt{terminal}$, this identifies the labelled count as
   $L_1(m+1)=uT^m e$ for every $m:\mathbb N$.
   Colour-permutation invariance comes from
   `DefectTransfer.relabel_defects`.
   A constant array on $m+1$ rows has $8m$ monochromatic bonds,
   which cannot equal one. Every accepted array is nonconstant,
   and `HardinA233177Canonical.canonical_pattern_card` gives
   $uT^m e=6a(m+1)$.

2. **Eight classes in each residual-budget sector.** The source
   reuses `HardinA203980StripData.Row`, `horizontal`, `between`
   and `boundary`. The classifier `cl p d` places `boundary p`
   in the first eight states for residual budget one and the last
   eight for residual budget zero. Write
   $Q=\mathtt{HardinA203980StripData.T}$,
   $R=\mathtt{costOne}$ and
   $u_0=\mathtt{HardinA203980StripData.initial}$.
   In this sector order the sixteen-state transfer is
   $$
   T=\begin{pmatrix}Q&R\\0&Q\end{pmatrix},
   \qquad u=(u_0,0),\qquad e=(0,\mathbf1).
   $$
   A transition spends its exact bond cost; terminal acceptance
   requires budget zero, so the paths spend exactly one defect.

3. **Symmetry transport and inherited transitions.**
   `HardinA229507StripCertificate.representative_counts` checks
   the cost-one sums on the eight zero-defect representatives with
   `decide +kernel`. `one_counts` applies
   `DiagonalSymmetry.lift_counts` at defect cost one.
   The symmetry uses independent permutations on the two column
   parities, interchanged on the successor row, and simultaneous
   reflection. It preserves the cost and boundary class and supplies
   a bijection of successor rows. It does not replace the global
   row-major canonical normalization by separate parity normalizations.
   `transition_counts` splits targets structurally by residual
   budget, reuses `HardinA203980StripCertificate.counts` for
   zero-cost transitions, and uses `one_counts` for the cost-one
   block. Initial counts reuse the zero-defect `initial_counts`;
   the exhausted sector starts with weight zero.

4. **Terminal powers and the degree-eight relation.**
   `HardinA229507StripCertificate.W` appends the inherited
   zero-defect `W` as its last eight coordinates.
   `tail_step` obtains these steps from
   `HardinA203980StripCertificate.powers` and the lower block
   of $T$. `powers` checks only the new leading eight-coordinate
   steps by finite kernel decisions and proves $W_j=T^j e$
   for $0\le j<14$. `tail_polynomial` combines five shifted
   instances of the zero-defect degree-four relation; the leading
   polynomial identity is checked by `decide +kernel`.
   `finite_certificate` assembles
   $$
   T^9e=\sum_{j=0}^{7}c_jT^{1+j}e,
   $$
   with
   $(c_0,\ldots,c_7)=(-104976,174960,-72900,-9720,8748,-540,-225,30)$.

5. **Propagation and registered index.** In `result`, applying
   $w\mapsto uT^t w$ to `finite_certificate` propagates the
   terminal-vector relation to every $t:\mathbb N$.
   `counting_bridge` gives $uT^m e=6a(m+1)$, so cancellation
   in $\mathbb Z$ yields the recurrence at index $t+10$.
   The shift-one, degree-eight vector identity therefore gives
   exactly the registered range $n\ge10$.

## Falsifier

A literal canonical DA count at some $n\ge10$ that violates
the quoted recurrence would falsify the registered assertion and
contradict the source's `result`. A mismatch in a representative
transition sum, initial multiplicity, symmetry bijection, terminal-power
step or vector relation would invalidate the corresponding counting
certificate.
