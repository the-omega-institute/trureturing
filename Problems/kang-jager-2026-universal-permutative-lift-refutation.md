# Question 1.10: regular Toeplitz and strictly ergodic strengthening

## Problem

The cited v1 asks whether a given minimal/strictly ergodic (Toeplitz)
subshift admits a permutative sliding block code of length ℓ≥2 whose entire
inverse image is minimal/strictly ergodic. This dossier includes the delivered
general minimal refutation and its strengthening to a regular Toeplitz,
strictly ergodic base. Both concern the same numbered external question.

The original `UniversalRefutation.claim` quantifies over all finite discrete
alphabets A with at least two letters and all nonempty minimal two-sided
subshifts. Its conclusion asks for some k≥0 and h:A^(k+2)→A, bijective in
both endpoints with all remaining letters fixed, whose whole inverse image
is minimal. `UniversalRefutation.result : Not claim` is the general-branch
refutation retained as a preliminary result.

The strengthened `BalancedRegularRefutation.claim` already restricts the
input to binary subshifts that are nonempty and minimal, have exactly one
invariant probability measure, and are generated by one aperiodic Toeplitz
point with a genuine essential period structure. The structure has positive
nonempty essential periods, strict growth, divisibility into the next period,
and coverage of every signed coordinate; its actual normalized periodic
skeleton densities tend to one. The conclusion still allows every length
k+2 and every bipermutative local rule, and asks for minimality of the whole
inverse image. `BalancedRegularRefutation.result : ¬ claim` is its literal
negation. Since strict ergodicity implies minimality, it also rules out a
strictly ergodic whole lift. A binary counterexample suffices for the original
finite-alphabet question.

## Motivation

The original finite extension has the actual fibre A^(ℓ−1). Group holonomy
over a regular group action cannot silently replace it. The construction
trivializes every finite letter-permutation cocycle on one fixed infinite
minimal binary base. The balanced version of this construction supplies
unique ergodicity and actual regular Toeplitzness on the same base.

## Route

The original factorial-word base and `UniversalRefutation.result` remain
valid. The strengthened counterexample uses the balanced-word subshift,
constructed as the intersection of compact two-sided tiling subshifts.
Recognizable marked boundaries give unique continuous tilings, uniform
recurrence and minimality, and exclude every positive global period.

Sparse disagreements between the two balanced words give a vanishing error
for every finite-window event. Existence of an invariant probability and
uniqueness on measurable cylinders establish unique ergodicity on actual
subshift points. An aligned compact-limit point has periodic coordinates,
is aperiodic and generates exactly this same base. Its actual periodic
skeleton densities tend to one; extracting least skeleton periods gives
strictly increasing essential periods with divisibility and coverage of all
signed coordinates. Density is preserved under this extraction.

For any finite Q and two permutations, a high word level has identity
holonomy on both block types. Prefix holonomy gives a continuous transfer
β(Sy)=π(y₀)β(y). Independently, `BipermutativeLift` reconstructs every
two-sided preimage from its coded point and actual length-(k+1) prefix.
Together these homeomorphisms split the whole lift as Y×Bool^(k+1), with
shift acting on Y and fixing the transformed fibre label. The invariant label
is β(y)⁻¹(sourcePrefix x), not the raw prefix. Distinct labels give nonempty
clopen invariant minimal fibres, exactly 2^(k+1) distinct orbit closures.

The same regular Toeplitz, strictly ergodic binary base works for every k and
every permitted code. No length is fixed in advance and no component is
substituted for the entire lift.

## Falsifier

The construction would fail if the fixed base were empty or nonminimal; if
unique ergodicity concerned different points or a different base; if the
period structure lacked actual essentiality, divisibility, strict growth,
coverage or normalized density convergence; if the transfer identity failed
for some finite permutation action; or if reconstruction missed a point of
the whole inverse image. An exact prior resolution would defeat a novelty
claim without changing the formal statement.
