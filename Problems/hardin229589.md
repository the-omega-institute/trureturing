# Hardin's recurrence for the $4\times n$ one-defect three-colour strip

## Problem

[OEIS A229589](https://oeis.org/A229589), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of defective 3-colorings of a 4 X n 0..2 array connected horizontally and antidiagonally with exactly one mistake, and colors introduced in row-major 0..2 order.

and the empirical recurrence

> Empirical: a(n) = 18*a(n-1) - 111*a(n-2) + 282*a(n-3) - 333*a(n-4) + 180*a(n-5) - 36*a(n-6) for n > 12.

Both quotations are from the [OEIS text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A229589&fmt=text).
The assertion registered in
[issue #30838](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/30838)
is this recurrence for every $n\ge 13$, with $n$ the number of
columns and the fixed number of rows $k=4$. The sequence offset is one.

For an array $f:\operatorname{Fin}4\times\operatorname{Fin}n
\to\operatorname{Fin}3$, a defect is a bond whose endpoint colours agree.
The bonds are $(i,j)$--$(i,j+1)$ for $j+1<n$, and
$(i,j+1)$--$(i+1,j)$ for $i+1<4$ and $j+1<n$.
These are the horizontal and antidiagonal (HA) bonds; vertical bonds are absent.
The $4\times n$ arrays are sheared square-lattice strips with
staircase ends, swept column by column in the source. Its empty
`horizontal` is the absence of bonds within a swept column; original
horizontal bonds are included in `between` as the pairs $(i,i)$,
alongside $(i+1,i)$ for the antidiagonals.
There are no periodic boundary bonds.

## Motivation

The settling declaration is
`D5/S3/StatisticalMechanics/Potts/StripDefects/HardinA229589Strip.result`.
The external assertion is preregistered in
[issue #30838](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/30838);
the source note has bibkey `hardin2013a229589`.

The coordinate change $x=i+j$, $y=i$ sends horizontal and
antidiagonal bonds to the two unit directions of the square lattice. The finite $4\times n$ graph has staircase ends.
The source sweeps its columns without changing the row-major
normalization order.
With antiferromagnetic Potts energy
$E(f)=J\sum_{\{u,v\}\in B}\mathbf{1}_{f(u)=f(v)}$ and $J>0$,
three-colour arrays with exactly $D=1$ monochromatic bonds are states of
the three-state Potts antiferromagnet with exactly one unsatisfied
bond on the sheared square strip (HA).
Row-major normalization counts these states up to a global permutation of
the colours. The theorem concerns finite combinatorial counts under this
bond and energy convention.

## Gap

The OEIS formula is labelled empirical. The registered target is its
validity for every $n\ge 13$ from the literal array definition;
agreement with a finite list of sequence terms does not supply that statement.

The literature check (the OEIS entry and its links; arXiv searches for Potts strips with unsatisfied bonds and for defective colourings; Chang–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 107 (2002) 1207–1253, which treats rectangular square-lattice strips) located no proof of this recurrence.
The search scope is the one recorded in #30838; worldwide priority and
literature completeness remain unverified.

## Route

1. **Literal count and normalization.** `a` filters every original
   array by the exact defect count and row-major canonical condition.
   `DefectTransfer.relabel_defects` gives colour-permutation
   invariance. Inside `result`, `scale` applies
   `HardinA233177Canonical.canonical_pattern_card` to the original
   row-major array domain. `Equiv.prodComm` and `Equiv.arrowCongr` relate
   the labelled column-transfer domain to those original arrays;
   they do not replace row-major canonical order by column-major order.
   `DefectTransfer.constant_defects` excludes constant arrays at the
   prescribed budget: at length $m+1$ they have $7m$ defects,
   which cannot equal $1$. Thus every accepted array uses at least
   two colours. The six-to-one orbit identity is
   $L(m+1)=6a(m+1)$ for all $m:\mathbb{N}$, where $L$ is the source's
   labelled `literalCount` at budget $1$.
2. **Exact-budget transfer.** The source's `Row = Fin 4 → Fin 3` is a swept
   column. `horizontal = ∅`, `between` is the displayed cross-column
   bond set, and the retained boundary is the whole column (`id`).
   Appending column $r$ to $p$ costs `bondDefects between p r`;
   `bondDefects horizontal r r` is zero. The remaining budget
   $d\in\{0,\ldots,1\}$ decreases by that cost, which must be at most
   $d$. `Valid` is just $d\le 1$.
   `cl` records equality patterns and remaining budget in 28
   classes. Inside `result`, `labelled` applies
   `DefectTransfer.lumped_count` to give
   $L(m+1)=vT^m e$, with $v=\mathtt{initial}$ and
   $e=\mathtt{terminal}$.
3. **Budget blocks.** In source order the classes with remaining
   budgets $1, 0$ have sizes $14, 14$, respectively.
   Nonnegative costs cannot restore spent budget, so the row-vector
   transfer is block upper triangular in that ordering. The recurrence
   uses the certified forward-vector relation; no minimality or full
   characteristic-polynomial assertion is needed.
4. **Finite certificates.** `HardinA229589StripData` supplies the class map, matrix and
   vectors. `HardinA229589StripCertificate.structural_certificate` proves
   terminal acceptance, budget preservation, initial admissibility and
   weights, forward-table steps and the vector relation.
   `HardinA229589StripClasses0` through `HardinA229589StripClasses13` prove
   transition counts for every labelled boundary, partitioned by
   destination class. Their finite decisions use `decide +kernel`;
   `HardinA229589Strip.finite_certificate` assembles them with the structural
   certificate.
5. **Propagation and starting index.** Inside `result`, `tab`
   identifies $W_j=vT^j$. The relation `poly` is
   $W_{12}=\sum_{j=0}^{5}c_j W_{6+j}$, with
   $(c_0,\ldots,c_{5})=(-36, 180, -333, 282, -111, 18)$.
   `DefectTransfer.forward_shift` propagates this shift-6,
   degree-6 relation to every further length. The scalar products
   therefore give the recurrence for all indices at least
   $6+6+1=13$. Inside `result`, `bridge` identifies each
   scalar product with six times the corresponding canonical count;
   cancellation in $\mathbb{Z}$ gives `result` at the registered
   starting index.

## Falsifier

A column count $n\ge 13$ at which the literal normalized array count
violates the quoted recurrence would falsify the registered assertion and
contradict the source's `result`. A mismatch in the transition sums or in
the exact-budget counting identity would invalidate the transfer bridge.
