# Leap groups of prisms and hypercubes

## Problem

Mark Schachner and Gabe Udell, *The group generated by a graph’s derangements*, arXiv:2610.18463v1, ask in Question 5.4 on printed and PDF page 21:

> What are the leap groups of the prism graphs $P_1\square C_n$? What are the leap groups of the hypercube graphs $P_1^{\square n}$?

On page 2, Question 1.1 defines a hop as a vertex bijection $h$ with $v\sim h(v)$ for every vertex, and Question 1.3 asks for the subgroup of $\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ generated by all such bijections. Page 9 identifies $P_1$ as the path of length one. The prism carrier is $\mathbb Z/2\times\mathbb Z/n$ for every $n\ge3$. In a fixed layer its neighbors are at indices $i\pm1$ modulo $n$, and at a fixed index its rung connects the two layers. The cube carrier is $\{0,1\}^{n}$ with adjacency at Hamming distance one for every $n\ge1$. [Question 5.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18463v1#page=21)

The odd prism leap group equals the full group of vertex permutations for every odd $n\ge3$. For every even $n\ge4$, the prism leap group equals the setwise stabilizer of the unordered pair of fibers of $(b,i)\mapsto b+i\pmod 2$. For every $n\ge1$, the cube leap group equals the setwise stabilizer of the unordered pair of Hamming-weight parity fibers. Both fibers are inhabited in the stated bipartite ranges. In dimension zero the cube has one vertex, no hops, and the trivial subgroup.

## Motivation

A leap group records which arrangements can be attained by composing adjacent bijections. The two families compare a cyclic factor with repeated products of a single edge. Their subgroup inclusions take place in the original vertex permutation groups, so the classification also specifies which arrangements are attainable. The wider graph-product question appears as Question 5.5 on PDF page 21. [Definitions and motivation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18463v1#page=2)

## Route

If $h$ is a hop and the edges $h^{-1}(a)\sim c$ and $h^{-1}(c)\sim a$ exist, then $\tau h$ is a hop for $\tau=(a\ c)$. All images outside those two preimages agree with $h$. Thus $(\tau h)h^{-1}=\tau$ belongs to the leap group.

For the prism, take the rung hop and the diagonal move $(b,i)\mapsto(b+1,i+1)$. Repeated diagonal moves reach the desired index, including across the wraparound. When $n$ is odd, another $n$ moves switch the layer at that index. When $n$ is even, the parity and index determine the layer. Transpositions along a chain generate the endpoint swap, so these moves give all vertex swaps in the odd case and all same-class swaps in the even case.

For the cube, a one-coordinate flip is a hop. With that hop the cross-edge construction yields the transposition between a vertex and its two-coordinate flip. Correcting differing coordinates in pairs preserves parity and decreases the Hamming distance to the target. This gives every same-class transposition.

Every edge in the bipartite cases switches parity. The upper inclusion follows because each hop exchanges the two classes. Same-class transpositions generate all permutations preserving both classes. Composing with the fixed rung or coordinate-flip hop supplies the exchanging coset, giving the other inclusion.

## Falsifier

A graph satisfying a stated parameter condition would disprove its clause if a vertex permutation belonged to exactly one side of the asserted subgroup equality. At the proof-step level, a bad adjacency after switching the two images or a failure to connect the claimed class by diagonal moves would invalidate the construction.

## Triage

### What the settlement shows

The same cross-edge construction works on arbitrary simple graphs with the stated hop and two cross-edges. For the stated bipartite families, the class-preserving subgroup consists of independent permutations of the two fibers, each extended by the identity on the other fiber. The rung or coordinate flip is an involution. Its conjugation interchanges the two symmetric factors via the induced bijection of the fibers, yielding the split extension.

The triangular prism and the one-dimensional cube have full symmetric leap groups. The square prism and cubes of dimension at least two have proper stabilizers. A swap across the two classes that fixes a third vertex gives an obstruction to membership. The zero-dimensional cube contributes the trivial boundary above. The argument for these families does not settle Question 5.5 for general products.
