# Hardin's recurrence for the $n\times5$ two-defect three-colour strip

## Problem

[OEIS A229682](https://oeis.org/A229682), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of defective 3-colorings of an n X 5 0..2 array connected diagonally and antidiagonally with exactly two mistakes, and colors introduced in row-major 0..2 order.

and the empirical recurrence

> Empirical: a(n) = 45*a(n-1) - 675*a(n-2) + 2565*a(n-3) + 25272*a(n-4) - 211410*a(n-5) + 3805380*a(n-7) - 8188128*a(n-8) - 14959080*a(n-9) + 70858800*a(n-10) - 85030560*a(n-11) + 34012224*a(n-12) for n > 13.

Both quotations are from the [OEIS text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A229682&fmt=text).
The assertion registered in
[issue #31593](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31593)
is this order-twelve recurrence for every $n\ge14$.
Let $N_D(r)$ be the number of canonical $r\times5$ arrays with
exactly $D$ monochromatic diagonal or antidiagonal bonds.
Here $a(n)=N_2(n)$, so $a(n)$ counts $n\times5$ arrays, with exactly $D=2$ unsatisfied bonds.
The OEIS sequence offset is one.
The source's `result t` quantifies over every `t : Nat` and
uses index $t+14$, exactly the registered range.

For an array $f:\operatorname{Fin}r\times\operatorname{Fin}5\to\operatorname{Fin}3$,
the DA bonds are $(i,j)$--$(i+1,j+1)$ and
$(i,j+1)$--$(i+1,j)$, for $i+1<r$ and $j+1<5$.
Each bond is counted once. Horizontal, vertical and periodic boundary
bonds are absent. A defect means equality of the endpoint colours.
The canonical predicate requires every occurrence of a colour to
have an earlier occurrence of every smaller colour, in row-major
order with rank $5i+j$. It forces the first cell to be zero on a
nonempty array; it does not require all three colours to occur.

## Motivation

The settling declaration is
`D5/S3/StatisticalMechanics/Potts/DiagonalStripDefects/HardinA229682Strip.result`.
The external assertion is preregistered in
[issue #31593](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/31593);
the source note has bibkey `hardin2013a229682`.

With antiferromagnetic Potts energy
$E(f)=J\sum_{\{u,v\}\in B}\mathbf1_{f(u)=f(v)}$ and $J>0$,
these counts describe three-state Potts antiferromagnet states on
five-column strips whose bonds are the diagonals and antidiagonals
of the square grid. The family has exactly $D=0,1,2$ unsatisfied
bonds; this sequence selects $D=2$ and thus energy $E=JD$.
The $D=0$ sector consists of proper colourings, the zero-temperature states.

The bonds preserve $i+j$ modulo two. On parity class $p\in\{0,1\}$,
the coordinates $((i+j-p)/2,(i-j-p)/2)$ send the two DA bond
directions to the two unit square-lattice directions. Each parity
class is a square lattice rotated by $45^\circ$, restricted by the
strip's sawtooth boundaries. The global colour normalization couples
the two parity classes in their orbit count. It uses a single
permutation of the three colours for the whole array.
The theorem concerns finite combinatorial counts with this graph
and energy convention.

## Gap

The OEIS recurrence is labelled empirical. The registered target is
its validity for every $n\ge14$ from the literal canonical
array count. Agreement with finitely many terms does not supply that
universal assertion.

The literature check (the OEIS entry and its links; arXiv searches for Potts strips with unsatisfied bonds, defective colourings and diagonal strips; Chang–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 107 (2002) 1207–1253, and Chang–Jacobsen–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 114 (2004) 763–823, which treat rectangular square- and triangular-lattice strips) located no proof of this recurrence.
The literature scope is the check recorded in #31593.
Literature completeness and worldwide priority remain unverified.

## Route

1. **Literal count and global normalization.** `a n` filters all
   `Fin n × Fin 5 → Fin 3` arrays by exactly two monochromatic
   DA bonds and `Canonical` in the original row-major order.
   `counting_bridge` applies `DefectTransfer.lumped_count` with
   boundary projection `id`, cost `bondDefects between`, and
   validity $d\le2$. With $u=\mathtt{initial}$ and
   $e=\mathtt{terminal}$, the labelled count is
   $L_2(m+1)=uT^m e$ for every $m:\mathbb N$.
   `DefectTransfer.relabel_defects` gives invariance under global
   colour permutations. A constant array on $m+1$ rows has $8m$
   monochromatic bonds, which cannot equal two. Thus
   `HardinA233177Canonical.canonical_pattern_card` supplies
   $uT^m e=6a(m+1)$.

2. **Block extension with the old sixteen-state tail.** The source
   reuses the A203980 row type, empty within-row bond set and
   eight-class `boundary`. Its classifier `cl` puts residual
   budget two in eight leading states and embeds
   `HardinA229507StripData.cl` for residual budgets one and zero
   in the last sixteen states.
   Write $Q=\mathtt{HardinA203980StripData.T}$,
   $R=\mathtt{HardinA229507StripData.costOne}$,
   $S=\mathtt{costTwo}$ and
   $u_0=\mathtt{HardinA203980StripData.initial}$.
   In residual-budget order two, one, zero, the transfer is
   $$
   T=\begin{pmatrix}Q&R&S\\0&Q&R\\0&0&Q\end{pmatrix},
   \qquad u=(u_0,0,0),\qquad e=(0,0,\mathbf1).
   $$
   The lower-right sixteen-state block is exactly the A229507
   transfer. Terminal acceptance requires residual budget zero,
   hence exactly two defects in the entire array.

3. **New cost-two counts and structural reuse.**
   `HardinA229682StripCertificate.representative_counts` checks
   the cost-two sums on the eight A203980 representatives with
   `decide +kernel`. `two_counts` lifts them through
   `DiagonalSymmetry.lift_counts` at cost two.
   Independent permutations on even and odd column positions are
   interchanged on the successor row; simultaneous reflection is
   also allowed. These give cost-preserving successor bijections
   and preserve the boundary class. The final canonical count
   retains one global row-major colour normalization.
   `transition_counts` proves the cuts structurally: targets in
   the new sector require residual budget two and cost zero;
   old-sector transitions at budgets below two reuse
   `HardinA229507StripCertificate.transition_counts`.
   From budget two, costs one and two use the inherited
   `one_counts` and the new `two_counts` respectively.
   Initial weights reuse `HardinA203980StripCertificate.initial_counts`.

4. **Inherited terminal powers and the degree-twelve relation.**
   `HardinA229682StripCertificate.W` appends the full sixteen
   A229507 terminal-power coordinates after the eight new ones.
   `tail_step` reuses `HardinA229507StripCertificate.powers`
   through the lower-right block. `powers` checks only the new
   eight-coordinate steps by finite kernel decisions and proves
   $W_j=T^j e$ for $0\le j<14$.
   `tail_polynomial` combines five shifted copies of the A229507
   degree-eight vector relation. The new coordinates of the
   polynomial identity are checked with `decide +kernel`.
   `finite_certificate` assembles
   $$
   T^{13}e=\sum_{j=0}^{11}c_jT^{1+j}e,
   $$
   with
   $$
   (c_0,\ldots,c_{11})=
   (34012224,-85030560,70858800,-14959080,-8188128,3805380,
   0,-211410,25272,2565,-675,45).
   $$
   The zero coefficient is the absent lag-six term of the recurrence.

5. **Propagation and registered index.** In `result`, applying
   $w\mapsto uT^t w$ to `finite_certificate` propagates this
   terminal-vector identity to every $t:\mathbb N$.
   Substitution of `counting_bridge` and cancellation of six
   in $\mathbb Z$ give the recurrence at index $t+14$.
   Thus the shift-one, degree-twelve relation gives exactly the
   registered range $n\ge14$.

## Falsifier

A literal canonical DA count at some $n\ge14$ that violates
the quoted recurrence would falsify the registered assertion and
contradict the source's `result`. A mismatch in a representative
transition sum, initial multiplicity, symmetry bijection, terminal-power
step or vector relation would invalidate the corresponding counting
certificate.
