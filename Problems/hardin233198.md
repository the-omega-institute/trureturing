# Barker's full generating function for Hardin's width-four eight-colour arrays

## Problem

For every positive height $n$, let $a(n)$ count the functions
$\operatorname{Fin}(n)\times\operatorname{Fin}(4)\to\operatorname{Fin}(8)$
with colour zero at $(0,0)$. The contained horizontal bonds
$(i,j)$–$(i,j+1)$, vertical bonds $(i,j)$–$(i+1,j)$ and antidiagonal
bonds $(i,j+1)$–$(i+1,j)$ join unequal colours whose natural-number sum
differs from seven. The other pairs of cells are unconstrained by the
bond rule. In the strict order of $4i+j$, each occurrence of colours
$2,3,4,5,6$ requires an earlier $1$, each occurrence of $3,4,5$ requires
an earlier $2$, and each occurrence of $4$ requires an earlier $3$.
Colours may be absent.

With OEIS offset `1,1`, let $A$ have constant coefficient zero and
$[X^n]A=a(n)$ for every $n\ge 1$. Barker's empirical formula is

$$
A=\frac{X(11-1760X+99328X^2-3080192X^3+55050240X^4-369098752X^5)}
        {(1-16X)(1-192X+10240X^2-278528X^3+4194304X^4)}
\qquad\text{in }\mathbb Q[[X]].
$$

The denominator has constant coefficient one, so its inverse is formal.

## Motivation

The width-two sequence A233196 uses the same bond and colour rules. At
width four, the complementary-pair map connects the normalized count
to ordinary colourings of the same open triangular strip.
[Continuation #34546](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/34546)

## Gap

The live and pinned OEIS entries label the formula empirical.
Jacobsen–Salas–Sokal give a four-dimensional transfer for ordinary
colourings of the width-four free strip. Roček–Shrock–Tsai give an
ordinary chromatic-polynomial generating function with longitudinal
index $L_x-2$. The literal count requires the complementary-pair
weights, the strict row-major prerequisites, and the height-one term.
[Jacobsen–Salas–Sokal §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0204587v3#S3.SS3)
· [Roček–Shrock–Tsai §VIII.2](https://arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/9712148v1#S8.SS2)
· [Source search #28431](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/28431)

## Route

Map every colour $c$ to the pair $\{c,7-c\}$. A legal pair map with
$k$ used pairs forces the first representative of each pair and admits
$2^{4n-k}$ choices at the other cells. The same normalization of a
proper colouring on $q\le4$ labels has $q(q-1)\cdots(q-k+1)$ injective
relabellings, including the case $k>q$ where there are none.
Writing $C_q(n)$ for the ordinary count, the weights for $k=2,3,4$ give

$$
a(n)=16^{n-1}\left(\frac{C_4(n)}{24}+\frac{C_3(n)}6+
                          \frac{5C_2(n)}4\right),\qquad n\ge1.
$$

At height one the graph is a path, so $C_2(1)=2$, $C_3(1)=24$ and
$C_4(1)=108$. At larger heights a triangle excludes two colours and the
three-colour row transition gives $C_3(n)=6$. The four-colour row
classification uses five equality patterns and a fourth-order relation
for its continuation vectors. Rescaling its series by $16$ and adding
the two- and three-colour contributions gives the full numerator and
denominator, with the height-one correction included.

## Falsifier

A legal-array cardinality at any positive height that differs from the
corresponding coefficient of the displayed series would refute the formula.
