# Barker's closed form and generating function for the two-row antidiagonal pair-exclusion strip

## Problem

[OEIS A233257](https://oeis.org/A233257), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of 2 X n 0..5 arrays with no element x(i,j) adjacent to itself or value 5-x(i,j) horizontally or antidiagonally, top left element zero, and 1 appearing before 2 3 and 4, and 2 appearing before 3 in row major order (unlabelled 6-colorings with no clashing color pairs).

with Hardin's empirical recurrence $a(n)=16a(n-1)-48a(n-2)$ for $n>3$ and
Colin Barker's conjectured generating function
$x(3-38x+88x^2)/((1-4x)(1-12x))$ and closed form
$a(n)=2^{2n-3}(4\cdot3^n+9)/9$ for $n>1$ (verbatim in
[issue #28792](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/28792)).

A bond joins horizontally adjacent cells $(r,j),(r,j+1)$ and antidiagonal
cells $(0,j+1),(1,j)$; it is legal when its endpoints differ and do not sum
to $5$.

## Route

1. **Labels.** The complementary-pair labels are the fibres
   $\{0,5\},\{1,4\},\{2,3\}$ of `BooleanRankThreeK4.beta`; a bond is legal
   exactly when its endpoints carry different labels.
2. **Canonical form.** The frozen `HardinA233177Canonical.normalized_card`
   and `canonical_pattern_card` reduce the normalized count to the proper
   three-label patterns of the bond graph, weighted by $2$ to the number of
   cells minus the number of labels used.
3. **Proper patterns.** A transfer over the last column, with matrix rows
   $(2,2)$ and $(1,1)$ and initial weights $(12,12)$, gives
   $24\cdot3^{n}$ proper patterns at width $n+2$ (the matrix power is an
   eigenvector power, `Module.End.HasUnifEigenvector.pow_apply`); exactly six of them use
   two labels, the alternating constant columns (by the frozen
   `HardinA233177Ladder.two_label_classification` applied to the upper row).
4. **Result.** The weights give the closed form; the recurrence and the
   generating function follow by expansion.

### What the settlement shows

Each statement below follows from the closed form and item 2 (proved).

1. Exactly one canonical label class uses two labels; it contributes
   $2^{2n-2}=4^n/4$, a pure root-$4$ term.
2. The canonical classes with three labels number $4\cdot3^{n-2}-1$ and each
   contributes $2^{2n-3}$, so this sector contributes
   $2^{2n-3}\cdot4\cdot3^{n-2}-2^{2n-3}$: both the root $12$ and the root $4$.
3. The root $12=4\cdot3$ is the factor $2^2$ of the two free side bits of
   each new column times the nonzero eigenvalue $3$ of the last-column
   transfer with rows $(2,2)$ and $(1,1)$ (trace $3$, determinant $0$).
