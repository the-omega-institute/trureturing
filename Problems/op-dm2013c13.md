# Diaconis–Miclo absolute spectral-gap unimodality

## Problem

The source is [the versioned HAL submission](https://hal.science/hal-00719047v1), DOI `10.5802/afst.1383`; it has no arXiv identifier. The [Library note](../Library/Fourier/diaconismiclo2013secondorder.md) quotes the full statement. Preregistration: [#26482](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/26482).

## Motivation

An absolute spectral gap uses eigenvalue modulus. A negative real eigenvalue can reduce this gap even when a real-part or continuous-time comparison behaves differently. The witness has $G=\mathbb Z/11\mathbb Z$ and $S=\{1,2,9,10\}=\{\pm1,\pm2\}$, hence 44 position–speed states.

## Route

Fourier characters split the 44-state eigenvalue equation into four-dimensional blocks. The proof uses `FullBispectrumStability.dft_translation` for translation and `RestrictedSpineConstantPotential.uniformWeight` for the uniform-weight specialization. The trivial character contributes the eigenvalue $a$ on the mean-zero part. The nontrivial traces are roots of

$$t^5+t^4-4t^3-3t^2+3t+1.$$

Rational root windows and Schur inequalities bound all endpoint eigenvalue moduli by $239/500$. At $a=1/4$, a quartic sign change yields a real root below $-239/500$, which lifts to a nonzero mean-zero eigenfunction. Supremum inequalities produce a strict valley in the absolute gap. A function that increases to a single turning point and then decreases cannot have this valley.

## Falsifier

For any candidate turning point $a_0$, either $1/4\le a_0$, contradicting monotonicity between $1/5$ and $1/4$, or $a_0<1/4$, contradicting antitonicity between $1/4$ and $3/10$. The strict gap inequalities are the falsifier. A change to a singular-value gap, a real-part gap, or a different interpolation is outside this result.
