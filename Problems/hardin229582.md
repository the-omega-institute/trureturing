# Hardin's recurrence for the width-4 one-defect three-colour strip

## Problem

[OEIS A229582](https://oeis.org/A229582), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of defective 3-colorings of an n X 4 0..2 array connected horizontally and antidiagonally with exactly one mistake, and colors introduced in row-major 0..2 order.

and the empirical recurrence

> Empirical: a(n) = 18*a(n-1) - 81*a(n-2) for n>3.

Both quotations are from the [OEIS text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A229582&fmt=text).
The assertion registered in
[issue #30580](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/30580)
is this recurrence for every $n\ge 4$, with $n$ the number of rows
and the fixed width $k=4$ the number of columns. The sequence offset is one.

For an array $f:\operatorname{Fin}n\times\operatorname{Fin}4
\to\operatorname{Fin}3$, the bonds are
$(i,j)$--$(i,j+1)$ for $j+1<4$, and
$(i,j+1)$--$(i+1,j)$ for $i+1<n$ and $j+1<4$.
A defect is a bond whose two endpoint colours agree. There are no periodic
boundary bonds.

## Motivation

The settling declaration is
`D5/S3/StatisticalMechanics/Potts/StripDefects/HardinA229582Strip.result`.
The external assertion is preregistered in
[issue #30580](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/30580);
the source note is `Library/SpinModels/hardin2013a229582.md`.
A229582 is column 4 of the defect-counting table
[A229586](https://oeis.org/A229586).

For the physics reading, the coordinate change $x=i+j$, $y=i$ sends
horizontal and antidiagonal bonds to the two unit directions of the square
lattice. The finite graph is a sheared square-lattice strip. With
antiferromagnetic Potts energy
$E(f)=J\sum_{\{u,v\}\in B}\mathbf{1}_{f(u)=f(v)}$ and $J>0$,
a three-colour array with exactly one defect is a state with exactly one
unsatisfied bond of the three-state Potts antiferromagnet.
Row-major normalization counts these states up to a global permutation of
the colours. The theorem concerns finite combinatorial counts under this
bond and energy convention.

## Gap

The OEIS formula is labelled empirical. The registered target is its
validity for every $n\ge 4$ from the literal array definition;
agreement with a finite list of sequence terms does not supply that statement.

The literature check (the OEIS entry and its links; arXiv searches for Potts
strips with unsatisfied bonds and for defective colourings;
Chang–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 107 (2002) 1207–1253, which treats
rectangular square-lattice strips) located no proof of this recurrence. The
bond graph of an $n\times 4$ array is a chain of $2n-2$ four-cycles in which
consecutive cycles share one edge, with one pendant edge at each end; no
publication of the resulting count was located.

## Route

1. **Literal count and normalization.** `a` filters every array by the
   exact defect count and row-major `Canonical` condition.
   `DefectTransfer.relabel_defects` and
   `HardinA233177Canonical.canonical_pattern_card` give the source's
   private `scale_count`:
   $L(n)=6a(n)$ for $n\ge 1$, where $L$ is `literalCount`
   with defect budget one. The source excludes constant arrays in this
   range using `DefectTransfer.constant_defects`; hence at least two
   colours occur and each colour orbit has six members.
2. **Exact-budget transfer.** `DefectTransfer.continuation` retains
   the outgoing row tail `boundary = Fin.tail` and the remaining budget
   $d\in\{0,1\}$. Appending a row $r$ costs
   `cross p r + bondDefects horizontal r r`; it is allowed only when
   that cost is at most $d$, and subtracts that cost from $d$.
   The invariant `Valid` also bounds equal adjacent boundary entries by
   $1-d$. `cl` records boundary equality patterns and residual budget in
   6 classes. `DefectTransfer.lumped_count` proves the source's
   private `count_transfer`:
   $L(m+1)=vT^m e$ for every $m:\mathbb{N}$, with
   $v=\mathtt{initial}$ and $e=\mathtt{terminal}$.
3. **Block structure.** In source order there are 2 classes with
   remaining budget one, followed by 4 with budget zero. Nonnegative costs cannot restore spent budget.
   With row-vector evolution the displayed matrix therefore has block
   form $T=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\0&C\end{pmatrix}$.
   Transposing to column-vector evolution gives the lower-triangular
   convention used in the registration. The quotient blocks can have
   different sizes; the proof uses the certified vector relation below,
   not an assertion that these two quotient blocks are identical.
4. **Finite certificates.** `HardinA229582Strip.finite_certificate` is in the settlement module itself:
   `decide +kernel` proves terminal acceptance, budget preservation, all
   class-transition counts, initial weights, the forward-table steps and
   their integer linear relation. There is no separate companion
   certificate module for this width.
5. **Propagation and starting index.** The source's `powers_table`
   identifies $W_j=vT^j$. Its `polynomial_relation` is
   $W_{3}=\sum_{j=0}^{1}c_jW_{1+j}$ with
   $(c_0,\ldots,c_{1})=(-81, 18)$.
   The vector relation starts at shift one; `forward_shift`
   and the six-to-one bridge give the recurrence at every index at least four.
   Dividing the resulting integer equality by the common factor six
   gives `result`.

## Falsifier

A row count $n\ge 4$ at which the literal normalized array count
violates the quoted recurrence would falsify the registered assertion and
contradict the source's `result`. A mismatch in the certified transition
sums or in the exact-budget counting identity would invalidate the proposed
transfer bridge.
