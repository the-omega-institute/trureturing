# Hardin's recurrence and Barker's closed form for the width-3 two-defect king strip

## Problem

[OEIS A229750](https://oeis.org/A229750) has the following verbatim NAME:

> Number of defective 4-colorings of an n X 3 0..3 array connected horizontally, vertically, diagonally and antidiagonally with exactly two mistakes, and colors introduced in row-major 0..3 order.

Its FORMULA lines, verbatim from the
[text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A229750&fmt=text), are:

```text
Empirical: a(n) = 9*a(n-1) - 33*a(n-2) + 63*a(n-3) - 66*a(n-4) + 36*a(n-5) - 8*a(n-6) for n>8.
Conjectures from _Colin Barker_, Sep 21 2018: (Start)
G.f.: x*(1 + 51*x + 91*x^2 - 1118*x^3 + 1706*x^4 - 176*x^5 - 612*x^6 - 64*x^7) / ((1 - x)^3*(1 - 2*x)^3).
a(n) = (2*(1732+117*2^n) + (-4084+279*2^n)*n + (484+9*2^n)*n^2) / 8 for n>2.
(End)
```

The assertions are registered in
[issue #30655](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/30655).

Let $T_2(n,k)$ denote `KingTwoGrid.T n k`, the literal count of functions
$\operatorname{Fin}(n)\times\operatorname{Fin}(k)\to\operatorname{Fin}(4)$
with exactly two monochromatic bonds and canonical first appearances of
colours in row-major order. Horizontal, vertical, diagonal and antidiagonal
bonds are each counted once when both endpoints lie in the rectangle.
The boundaries are free. Canonical first appearances identify colour
relabellings while keeping cell positions distinct.

Here $a(n)=T_2(n,3)$, column $3$ of A229755. Claim C3 is the
quoted recurrence for every natural $n>8$, equivalently $n\ge9$,
with counts cast to $\mathbb Z$.
This dossier also settles B3, Barker's closed form for every $n>2$:

$$
8T_2(n,3)=2(1732+117\cdot2^n)+(279\cdot2^n-4084)n
                    +(9\cdot2^n+484)n^2,\qquad n\ge3.
$$

The quoted generating function is not a separate registered claim.

## Motivation

For the physics reading, these are states of the four-state Potts
antiferromagnet on king-lattice strips: the square lattice with both
diagonals in each elementary square. With equal positive penalties for
monochromatic bonds, the counted states have exactly two unsatisfied
bonds. The count is the degeneracy at that fixed bond count up to
relabelling of the four states. The statements concern finite canonical
configuration counts.

The settling declarations are `D5/S3/StatisticalMechanics/Potts/KingDefects/HardinA229750.result`, `D5/S3/StatisticalMechanics/Potts/KingDefects/HardinA229750.closed_form`.
The source bibkey is `hardin2013a229750`.

## Route

1. **Exact defect budget.** Use `DefectTransfer` with $q=4$ and
   total budget $D=2$. The boundary is a row $p$ and a remaining budget
   $d\in\{0,1,2\}$. The in-row set $H$ consists of horizontal bonds;
   the between-row set $V$ consists of vertical, diagonal and antidiagonal
   bonds. Appending $r$ costs
   $\operatorname{bondDefects}(V,p,r)+\operatorname{bondDefects}(H,r,r)$.
   A transition is allowed only when that cost is at most $d$, and it
   subtracts the cost from $d$. The initial weights charge the first row's
   horizontal defects; the terminal indicator accepts exactly $d=0$.
   `Width3.Certificate.transition_counts` proves the successor multiplicities
   for all valid boundaries, including their colour relabellings. Together
   with `Width3.Forward.structural_certificate`, these discharge
   `DefectTransfer.lumped_count` for the 9-class integer transfer.

2. **Reuse of zero-temperature structure.** `Width3.ZeroTemperature.transition_counts` delegates the proper
   zero-budget block to the frozen trace-avoiding king-strip owner
   `HardinA233163KingStrip.saturated_certificate`. The signed-to-four-colour
   fibre identity in `SignedFibres.sum_beta` removes the factor $2^3$;
   `HardinA233196Strip` supplies the signed colour compatibility interface.

3. **Literal grid and canonical colours.** `GridBridge.defects_rows`
   splits the row-list cost into the four literal king-bond sums, and
   `GridBridge.literal_bridge` identifies the labelled transfer count with
   `KingTwoGrid.L`. `KingTwoGrid.labelled_eq_twentyFour` gives
   $L(n,3)=24T_2(n,3)$ for $n\ge2$. Its three-colour condition uses the
   two-colour $2\times3$ obstruction and an injective grid embedding;
   `KingGrid.canonical_count_three` supplies the canonical normalization.
   In general this normalization excludes the single $2\times2$ rectangle;
   that exception does not occur at width 3.

4. **Forward certificate and propagation.** Write $M$ for `Data.T` and
   $W_i=\operatorname{forward}(M,\mathrm{initial},i)$.
   `Width3.Forward.structural_certificate` pins the initial vector,
   successive row-vector multiplications, and

   $$
   W_{8}=-8W_{2}+36W_{3}-66W_{4}
       +63W_{5}-33W_{6}+9W_{7}.
   $$

   `DefectTransfer.forward_shift` propagates this relation after every
   further transfer multiplication. Pairing with the terminal vector,
   using the literal bridge and cancelling $24$ gives the stated recurrence
   for every $n\ge9$.

5. **Barker's closed form.** `HardinA229750.closed_step` verifies that the
   displayed polynomial-exponential expression satisfies the same
   order-six recurrence. `HardinA229750.initial_closed` evaluates the
   transfer at heights three through eight; the canonical values are
   $598,2347,6809,17404,41872,97565$. Mathlib's
   `LinearRecurrence.eq_iff_eqOn_range_order` identifies the two solutions
   from these six initial values, giving `HardinA229750.closed_form`
   for every $n\ge3$.

## Falsifier

A natural height $n>8$ for which the literal $T_2(n,3)$ violates
the quoted recurrence would contradict `HardinA229750.result`.
A natural height $n\ge3$ violating the displayed closed count would
contradict `HardinA229750.closed_form`.
