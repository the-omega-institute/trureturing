# Barker's generating function for the two-row six-colour pair-exclusion strip

## Problem

[OEIS A233175](https://oeis.org/A233175), revision 7, by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of 2 X n 0..5 arrays with no element x(i,j) adjacent to itself or value 5-x(i,j) horizontally, diagonally or antidiagonally, top left element zero, and 1 appearing before 2 3 and 4, and 2 appearing before 3 in row major order (unlabelled 6-colorings with no clashing color pairs).

and FORMULA

> Conjectures from Colin Barker, Oct 10 2018: (Start)
> G.f.: x*(3 - 28*x - 16*x^2 + 224*x^3) / ((1 - 4*x)*(1 - 8*x - 32*x^2)).

A board has two labelled rows and $n$ labelled columns. Bonds join each cell to
the two cells of the next column (horizontal and both diagonal neighbours);
cells in the same column are not bonded. A bond is legal when its endpoints
differ and do not sum to $5$.

## Gap

The entry labels the generating function a conjecture. The preregistration's
bounded screen found no proof connecting it to the normalized arrays.

## Route

1. **Transducer.** Reading cells in row-major order, a finite state records
   the previous column's colours and the two flags "colour $1$ seen" and
   "colour $2$ seen". The literal count equals the total of a transfer
   recursion over $576$ continuation states.
2. **Finite certificate.** The transfer vectors for continuation lengths
   $0$ to $7$ are checked entry by entry with `decide +kernel`. On all $576$
   states they satisfy the seven-step identity
   $v_7=16v_6-32v_5-320v_4+512v_3+2048v_2$, whose characteristic polynomial
   is $z^2(z^3-12z^2+128)(z^2-4z-16)$.
3. **Propagation.** Linearity of the transfer step propagates the identity
   to every length. The scalar initial values $a(1),\ldots,a(7)$ eliminate the
   extra factor for the total count, giving
   $a(n)=12a(n-1)-128a(n-3)$ for $n\ge5$, and comparing coefficients gives
   the power-series identity.

## Falsifier

A width $n$ whose literal count differs from the coefficient of $x^n$ in
Barker's generating function would contradict `result`.

## Triage

1. **Proved (kernel-checked):** Barker's generating function for every
   width, by `HardinA233175PairStrip.result`. Hardin's recurrence
   $a(n)=12a(n-1)-128a(n-3)$ for $n>4$ is the private step `recurrence` on
   the path to `result`.
2. **Proved (kernel-checked):** the seven-step identity
   $v_7=16v_6-32v_5-320v_4+512v_3+2048v_2$ on the $576$-state transfer
   vectors, together with the initial values $a(1),\ldots,a(7)$, by
   `HardinA233175PairStrip.finite_certificate`. The vectors themselves do not
   satisfy the order-three relation; only the total count does.
3. **Open (not formalized here):** the structural explanation of the
   denominator. With colour pairs $\{0,5\},\{1,4\},\{2,3\}$ (the fibres of
   the reused frozen map `BooleanRankThreeK4.beta`), a bond is legal exactly
   when its endpoints lie in different pairs, so the count reduces to
   proper $3$-colourings of pair labels on the connected bipartite constraint
   graph. The normalization then gives $2^{2n}(P_n+6)/48$ for $n\ge2$, where
   $P_n$ satisfies $P_{n+2}=2P_{n+1}+2P_n$. This would account for the factor
   $1-8x-32x^2$ (as $1-2y-2y^2$ with $y=4x$) and for the factor $1-4x$ from the
   two-pair correction. The derivation is the search seat's sketch and is not
   kernel-checked; the orchestrator computed that the formula matches $a(n)$ for
   $n=2,\ldots,7$ (with $P_1=9$, $P_2=18$).
4. **Proved (kernel-checked):** the sibling strip A233251, by
   `HardinA233251TriangleStrip.result` (`Problems/hardin233251.md`).
5. **Open:** the other rows of A233174.

### What the settlement shows

Barker's conjecture is exact for every width. The denominator factors as a
pure pair-label transfer, $1-8x-32x^2$, times $1-4x$. The candidate structure
in item 3 identifies $1-4x$ with arrays that use only two of the three colour
pairs; proving that identification needs the pair-normalization lemma. In the
triangular-strip sibling A233251 the same split is exact and proved: its
factor $1-8x$ is the sector in which colour $2$ (hence colour $3$) never occurs.
