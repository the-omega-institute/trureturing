# Higher exterior traces of Spezia's perimeter matrix

## Problem

Stefano Spezia's OEIS A330700, COMMENTS, revision 23, gives this second line in its Conjectures block:

> For k > 2, the trace of the k-th exterior power of M(n) is equal to zero.

The same block and OEIS A330287, COMMENTS, revision 31, specify:

> The matrix M(n) is defined as M[i,j,n] = i*j if i < 3 or j < 3 and M[i,j,n] = 2*(i + j) - 4 otherwise.

Indices are one-based. For every positive natural $n$, interpret the integer entries in $\mathbb C$ and take every natural $k>2$, including $k>n$. The exterior power is represented by the existing `compoundMatrix`; its trace is `Matrix.trace`. ## Route

For source indices $1\le i,j\le n$, put $v_i=i$ and $w_i=\max(i-2,0)$. Then

$$M(n)=vv^{\mathsf T}-ww^{\mathsf T}=AB,\qquad A=[v\;w],\qquad B=\begin{bmatrix}v^{\mathsf T}\\-w^{\mathsf T}\end{bmatrix}.$$

If $i<3$ or $j<3$, at least one of $w_i,w_j$ is zero, so this entry equals $ij$. Otherwise

$$ij-(i-2)(j-2)=2(i+j)-4.$$

These are precisely the two branches of the public matrix definition. For any $k$-element row and column subsets $S,T$, restriction gives $M[S,T]=A[S,:]B[:,T]$. The imported `cauchyBinet` writes its determinant as a sum indexed by $k$-element subsets of a two-element set. For $k>2$ there are no such subsets, hence every such determinant is zero. Thus the entire higher compound matrix is zero. The live local facts `factor`, `hempty` and `hz` implement this derivation inside `result`; they are not separately published theorems. Taking the trace gives the claimed zero. For $k>n$, no $k$-element row or column subset exists and the trace is an empty sum.

## Falsifier

A positive natural $n$ and natural $k>2$ with a nonzero exterior trace of the literal matrix would refute the claim. Finite agreement, the vanishing of only the full determinant, or a rank hypothesis assumed rather than proved would not settle it.
