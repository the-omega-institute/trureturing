# Hardin's recurrence for the 3-row three-state sum-exclusion strip

## Problem

[OEIS A233156](https://oeis.org/A233156), by R. H. Hardin, has NAME
(verbatim from the [text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A233156&fmt=text)):

> Number of 3 X n 0..2 arrays with no element x(i,j) adjacent to value 2-x(i,j) horizontally or antidiagonally.

The empirical recurrence in that export is, verbatim:

```text
Empirical: a(n) = 5*a(n-1) - 2*a(n-2) for n>4.
```

This is row 3 of the table A233155: the height is fixed and the width grows.
A bond joins horizontal neighbours $(i,j),(i,j+1)$ or antidiagonal neighbours
$(i,j+1),(i+1,j)$; its values must have natural-number sum different from
$2$. Every labelled function counts, without normalization. No vertical or
main-diagonal bond condition is imposed.

## Route

1. **Colour-permutation equivalence.** Put $c(x)=2-x$ and replace the
   label in column $j$ by $c^j(x)$. Every horizontal or antidiagonal bond
   joins consecutive columns, so sum exclusion becomes inequality of the
   transformed labels. `HardinA233155ColumnTransfer.properEquiv 3 n` is the
   bijection on the legal subtypes, including at zero width. The private
   `HardinA233156Row.counting_bridge` applies `Fintype.card_congr` to prove
   `U3 n = HardinA233258PairTransfer.L n` for every natural `n`.
2. **Frozen labelled proper-colouring count.**
   `HardinA233258PairTransfer.L` counts every labelled three-colouring of
   precisely these horizontal and antidiagonal bonds. Its frozen
   `count_transfer` expresses `L (k + 2)` as the weighted scalar of a power
   of its five-class matrix `Q`. At `n = 0`, the proof applies the frozen `initial_values` at
   `Fin 6` indices 1, 2 and 3: `L 3 = 432`, `L 4 = 1944` and
   `L 5 = 8856`. The scalar identity `8856 = 5*1944 - 2*432` gives
   the recurrence at width 5.
3. **Continuation relation.** For the successor case, the original
   `HardinA233258PairTransfer.powers_table` identifies the powers at
   indices 2, 3 and 4 with its original `W` vectors. Their entrywise
   relation `W 4 = -2 • W 2 + 5 • W 3` proves
   $Q^4\mathbf1=-2Q^2\mathbf1+5Q^3\mathbf1$.
   `TransferMatrix.scalarRelationShift` multiplies the relation by every
   further matrix power and takes its weighted scalar. Reusing the frozen
   count formula yields every width at least 6; together with the base
   case this is exactly the source range $m\ge5$.

## Falsifier

A width $m\ge5$ at which the literal 3-row count violates the quoted
recurrence would contradict `result`, with its natural parameter set to
$m-5$. A mismatch in a model with additional bonds or label
normalization would test a different count.
