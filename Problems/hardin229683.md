# Hardin's recurrence for the $n\times 6$ two-defect three-colour strip

## Problem

The OEIS entry A229683, by R. H. Hardin, has NAME

> Number of defective 3-colorings of an n X 6 0..2 array connected diagonally and antidiagonally with exactly two mistakes, and colors introduced in row-major 0..2 order.

and the empirical recurrence

> Empirical: a(n) = 75*a(n-1) - 2010*a(n-2) + 19486*a(n-3) + 60075*a(n-4) - 2206575*a(n-5) + 7404210*a(n-6) + 70536825*a(n-7) - 435322350*a(n-8) - 601275555*a(n-9) + 8082823950*a(n-10) - 6257717775*a(n-11) - 54520532190*a(n-12) + 92909172825*a(n-13) + 132368667075*a(n-14) - 366499782594*a(n-15) + 470715894135*a(n-17) - 282429536481*a(n-18) for n > 20.

The quotations are from the OEIS text export. The published sequence
has offset one and is column 6 of A229685.
[OEIS text export](https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A229683&fmt=text)

The registered assertion is this order-eighteen recurrence for every
$n\ge 21$, with $n$ rows, six columns and exactly $2$
monochromatic bonds. OEIS writes `n > 20`, giving the threshold $n\ge 21$ in #32721. The omitted lag-sixteen term has coefficient zero in `HardinA229683StripCertificate.coeff_values`.
The source theorem `HardinA229683Strip.result` at `t` gives the recurrence at
$n=t+21$ for every $t\in\mathbb N$.
[Issue #32721](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/32721)

For $r$ rows, the vertices are the cells
$(i,j)\in\operatorname{Fin}(r)\times\operatorname{Fin}(6)$.
For $i+1<r$ and $j+1<6$, the bonds are
$\{(i,j),(i+1,j+1)\}$ and $\{(i,j+1),(i+1,j)\}$.
The boundaries are free. Each bond is counted once and there are ten
bonds per consecutive row pair. In Lean, `DefectTransfer.defects` uses
an empty within-row bond set and `HardinA229637RowColumn.betw` at width
six for the between-row bonds.

## Motivation

For the three-state Potts antiferromagnet on these bonds, take
$E(f)=J\sum_{\{u,v\}\in B}\mathbf 1_{f(u)=f(v)}$ with $J>0$.
Equal endpoint colours make a bond unsatisfied. A229683 counts states
with exactly $D=2$ unsatisfied bonds, up to global relabelling of the
three colours. These states have energy $2J$ in this convention.

Diagonal steps preserve the parity of $i+j$. Each parity subgraph is
a finite piece of the square lattice rotated by $45^\circ$, with
three sites per array row and sawtooth side boundaries. The row-major
colour rule chooses one representative per orbit of a single colour
permutation acting on the entire array, across both subgraphs.

## Gap

The empirical recurrence calls for a proof at every registered index
from the literal array count. A finite table of values tests only the
indices it covers.

The literature check covered the OEIS entry and its links, arXiv
searches for Potts strips with unsatisfied bonds and for defective
colourings, and Chang–Salas–Shrock, J. Stat. Phys. 107 (2002)

## Route

1. **Canonical counts and boundary classes.** The literal filter in
   `HardinA229683Strip.a` reads the array in its original row-major order.
   The transfer sweeps rows. It reads the even columns from the left
   and the odd columns from the right. Each triple has one of five
   equality patterns, and the unordered pair gives fifteen classes.
   `HardinA203981Strip.scale_count` gives the labelled-to-canonical
   normalization. A constant array with $m+1$ rows has $10m$ defects,
   so it cannot occur at exact budget two. Every accepted orbit has six
   labellings.

2. **Exact-budget transfer.** The 45 states are the 15 boundary classes in each of the remaining
   budgets $2,1,0$. A transition of cost $c$ is allowed at remaining
   budget $b$ when $c\le b$ and moves to budget $b-c$. With $Q$ the zero-cost block, $R$ the exact-one
   block and $S=$ `HardinA229683StripData.costTwo`, the matrix is
   $T=\begin{pmatrix}Q&R&S\\0&Q&R\\0&0&Q\end{pmatrix}$.
   `HardinA229696Strip.finite_certificate` supplies the half-strip
   exact-two count with target reversal. Together with the half-strip
   exact-zero and exact-one counts and
   `HardinA203981StripCertificate.allocated_counts`, it gives the
   representative counts for allocations $(0,2)$, $(1,1)$ and $(2,0)$.
   `HardinA203981StripSymmetry.lift_counts` transports the identities
   to all labelled source rows.
   `HardinA229683StripCertificate.transition_counts` reuses
   `HardinA229508StripCertificate.transition_counts` on the inherited
   block and certifies transitions into the spent-budget sector.
   With $u_0$ the single-row population, the initial row vector is
   $(u_0,0,0)$ and the terminal column vector has entries
   $(0,0,\mathbf 1)$ on the three sectors.

3. **Finite forward identity.** Write $W_k=uT^k$, with $u$ the
   initial row vector and $e$ the terminal column vector.
   `HardinA229683StripCertificate.forward_inherited` projects onto the
   one-budget system. `HardinA229683StripCertificate.forward_two`
   verifies the terminal-sector updates by finite kernel decisions,
   reuses the inherited steps, and applies
   `HardinA203981StripCertificate.forward_table_of_steps` to identify
   the total-degree-two table with the spent-budget coordinates.
   The coefficients are obtained by cubing the monic zero-defect
   recurrence polynomial by convolution, with expansion certified by
   `HardinA229683StripCertificate.coeff_values`.
   On the inherited coordinates the relation is a linear combination
   of shifted one-defect equations from
   `HardinA229508StripCertificate.shifted_coordinate`. The terminal
   coordinates are checked by finite kernel decision.
   `HardinA229683StripCertificate.finite_certificate` gives
   $W_{20}=\sum_{j=0}^{17}c_jW_{2+j}$, with $c_j$ in increasing order
   of the input index.

4. **Propagation and index.** `DefectTransfer.forward_shift`
   applies further transfer steps and the terminal vector to the
   certified identity. `HardinA229683Strip.counting_bridge` applies
   `DefectTransfer.lumped_count` to give $uT^m e=6a(m+1)$ for every
   $m\ge 0$. The transfer exponent $20=2+18$ corresponds to
   $21$ rows.
   Cancelling the common factor six in $\mathbb Z$ gives the
   registered recurrence.

## Falsifier

A literal canonical count that violated the displayed recurrence at
some $n\ge 21$ would falsify the registered assertion. A mismatch
in the transition or initial counts would break the counting bridge.
A failed table update or vector equality at the specified exponents
would invalidate the forward certificate.
