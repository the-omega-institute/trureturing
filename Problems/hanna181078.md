## Route

Write $s=n+k$, $b=\binom{s-1}{k}$ and $F(n,k)=b^{s-1}$. The existing negative-binomial identities `HannaA181074Integrality.negbinomDiv` and `negbinomCong` supply $n\mid kb$ and the prime-scaling congruence for $b$. For $s=1$, $n=1$ handles the zero exponent. Otherwise multiplication by $b^{s-2}$ yields $n\mid kF(n,k)$.

For each prime $p$, put $v=v_p(n)$. If $p\nmid b$, cancellation yields $p^v\mid k$ and hence $p^v\mid s$. Euler's theorem modulo $p^{v+1}$ then equates the exponents $ps-1$ and $s-1$. If $p\mid b$, the identities $b(1,k)=b(n,0)=1$ force $n\ge2$ and $k\ge1$; $v_p(n)<n$ makes both exponents at least $v+1$, so both powers vanish modulo $p^{v+1}$. Combining with the binomial congruence gives

$$F(pn,pk)\equiv F(n,k)\pmod{p^{v_p(n)+1}}.$$

`HannaA181070Integrality.literalUnbounded` identifies the literal logarithm with the generic unbounded diagonal series. `HannaA181070Integrality.integralGeneric` applies the existing Dold exponential criterion to the two established hypotheses and gives every coefficient's integer witness.
