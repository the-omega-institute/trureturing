# Effect probabilities and finite-dimensional trace representation

The theorem and its proof treat all positive contractions, rather than only projections. The probability is a function of the effect itself, with normalization and additivity for admissible sums. The proof obtains monotonicity, rational and real homogeneity, a positive-cone extension and a positive linear functional. The result includes two-dimensional Hilbert spaces.

In finite dimension, binary additivity suffices for these steps. A positive matrix can be scaled into the effect interval. Independence of scale follows from homogeneity; additivity follows by choosing a common scale. Differences of positive matrices give the Hermitian extension, and complexification gives a functional on the whole matrix algebra. The nondegenerate trace pairing represents it uniquely. Rank-one positivity tests and evaluation at the identity give positivity and trace one of the representative. Countable additivity in the paper supplies normality in infinite dimension; finite-dimensional trace duality requires no additional countable-additivity hypothesis.

The matrix representation is an additional quantum probability assumption. It does not follow from a static event structure, a history grading, or a choice of clock. For pure density and rank-one projection the trace pairing is the squared modulus of the corresponding vector inner product.
