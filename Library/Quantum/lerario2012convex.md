# Topology from a pencil of two quadratic forms

The cited version is arXiv:1106.4678v3, updated 6 September 2012; the first
version was posted 23 June 2011. Theorem 18 in the appendix concerns
Y = {x in S^n : q(x) in K}, where q has two homogeneous quadratic
components and K is a closed convex cone. It uses homology over Z/2.
For k < n−2, it expresses the reduced kth Betti number of Y as the sum
of a relative zeroth and a relative first Betti number of the positive
inertia sets on the polar circle. For K = {0}, the whole circle is used.

Quantum-volume section 219 applies the sphere formula with n = 4 and
k = 0. Reduced b0 = 3 means four sphere components. The sign of the
nonzero kernel-coordinate separates them into two equal gauge copies.
The theorem permits singular intersections and does not itself establish
regularity; section 219 proves regularity from simple pencil roots.

The source provides the topological counting tool, not the particular
rational pencil certificate, its perturbation neighborhood, the
channel-specific rank-one-defect processor, or the analytic sharp
preparation with exact two-jets. The real-fiber counterexample in section
219 also does not establish an obstruction to complex preparations.
