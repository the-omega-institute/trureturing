# Null-phase positivity, anchor gauge, and real coordinates

Chaturvedi et al., arXiv:1302.0206v1 (1 February 2013), equation (2.10),
define a null-phase curve using strictly positive three-state Bargmann
invariants for all triples along it. Equations (2.11) and (3.6) relate
this to vanishing geometric phase and globally in-phase lifts.

The discussion before equation (3.6) constructs the lift from an anchor
by normalizing the vector obtained by applying each rank-one projection
to that anchor. Positivity guarantees nonzero overlaps and makes all
pairwise inner products in this gauge positive. Equations (4.11)-(4.13)
then describe a fixed orthonormal basis in which the lifted vectors have
real coefficients, using their real Gram relations.

This is a prior source for the anchor-gauge and real-Gram ingredients.
Its positivity condition is stronger than simultaneous realification:
real vectors may have a negative three-state cyclic product, with phase
pi instead of zero. It also excludes orthogonal pairs. A curve argument
allowing real products of either sign, or isolated anchor-orthogonality
points, must handle those cases separately. In the quantum volume,
analyticity supplies density away from such zeros and continuation from
open parameter rectangles; the channel-specific support lower bound
supplies the obstruction to a common real structure.
