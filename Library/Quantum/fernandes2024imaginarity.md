# Set imaginarity and finite-ensemble Gram data

Fernandes et al., arXiv:2403.15066v1 (22 March 2024; manuscript dated
25 March 2024), define set imaginarity by the absence of a single unitary
that makes every density matrix in an ensemble real. This differs from
imaginarity of one state relative to a specified basis.

The discussion preceding equation (1) explains that a non-real trace of
a product of states witnesses this property, using the unitary and gauge
invariance of Bargmann invariants. For three pure states with nonzero
pairwise overlaps, equation (1) displays a Gram gauge in which two
entries are positive and the remaining phase is the three-state cyclic
phase. Theorem 1 recalls the positive-semidefinite, unit-diagonal
criterion for a candidate Gram matrix.

Theorem 4 states that three squared pairwise overlaps attainable by
complex pure states have a real realization in some Hilbert space; it
does not require preserving the original dimension. Theorem 5 supplies
four-state pairwise-overlap data without a real realization. Thus the
fact that each second-order invariant is real does not preclude using
joint inequalities among several such invariants to witness imaginarity.

These are finite-ensemble results. The quantum volume's analytic-curve
argument additionally uses the identity theorem and the density of the
nonorthogonality set of a fixed anchor, to infer local three-state
witnesses from the absence of a common real structure. Its exact channel
and prescribed-jet requirements need their own continuation proof. The
underlying Bargmann and real-Gram principles are prior results.
