# Spectral strong symmetry and the redundant sharpness premise

[Barnum–Hilgert, arXiv:1904.03753v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.03753v1#S1.Thmtheorem1),
Theorem 1.1, classifies finite-dimensional spectral strongly symmetric
compact convex sets. Its alternatives are a simplex and the normalized
state space of a simple Euclidean Jordan algebra. Definition 3.4 requires
each state to lie in the convex hull of one frame. Definition 3.5 requires
transitivity on ordered frames of every fixed size. These are the source
conditions, not consequences of an arbitrary symmetric comparison table.

## Exact application to the quantum-origin carrier

Use the real ordered vector space $A$, cone $C$, normalization $u$, compact
state base $K$, and full effects $[0,u]$ from
[Definition 26.1 of the quantum-origin volume](../../docs/develop/theory/QUANTUM_ORIGIN_ENDOGENOUS_CUT_ATLAS_AND_DISCRETE_RECONSTRUCTION_BOUNDARIES.md).
A frame $(w_1,\ldots,w_r)$ has one common family of positive effects
$f_i$ with $\sum_i f_i\le u$ and $f_i(w_j)=\delta_{ij}$.
This joint condition is stronger than asking every pair to have a
possibly different distinguishing measurement.

Suppose signed Sp holds: every $x\in A$ can be written
$x=\sum_i\lambda_iw_i$ in such a frame. If $x\in C$, then directly

$$
\lambda_i=f_i(x)\ge0.
$$

If also $u(x)=1$, applying $u$ gives $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$.
Thus signed Sp implies the source's normalized-state spectrality
**without** using least effects, reciprocity or an inner product.
The effects $f_i$ that already witness the frame extract its coefficients;
no newly constructed state–effect map is needed for this step.

The source's measurement convention and the project's convention agree.
The residual effect $h=u-\sum_i f_i$ is nonnegative and vanishes on every
frame member. Adding $h$ to $f_1$ gives effects summing to $u$ with the
same frame evaluations. Conversely a normalized frame measurement already
satisfies the project's inequality. This includes a one-element frame.

Assume now the project's all-frame strong symmetry from Definition 28.1.
Every supplied reversible map induces an affine automorphism of $K$;
transitivity of the supplied subgroup therefore implies the source's
transitivity under the full affine automorphism group. The source theorem
applies directly. Its affine isomorphism homogenizes to a linear cone
isomorphism preserving $u$, with Jordan trace normalization, because $C$
is generated by the normalized base. Neither half of Principle S has
entered this classification.

In the resulting Jordan representation, pure states are primitive
idempotents. The usual trace pairing identifies the least proper effect
saturating $p$ with $x\mapsto\tau(p\circ x)$. The proof already supplied
inside Theorem 28.2 applies at this point: for $0\le a\le1$ saturating
$p$, the unit-eigenvalue spectral projection $r$ of $a$ satisfies
$p\in J_1(r)$, hence $p\le r\le a$. That argument uses Jordan spectral
and neutral-compression properties after classification, rather than
assuming the project's physical slit operations exist. Symmetry of the
Jordan trace pairing then gives reciprocity. The Jordan spectral theorem
also gives signed Sp for arbitrary $x\in A$.

Consequently, on this fixed carrier the following packages have the same
models, with the same supplied reversible subgroup required to be strongly
symmetric in every row:

| Package | Conditions stated before classification |
| --- | --- |
| Original single-system package | S, signed Sp, all-frame strong symmetry |
| Package without sharpness input | signed Sp, all-frame strong symmetry |
| Source spectral package | normalized-state spectrality, all-frame strong symmetry |

The first implies the second by forgetting S; the coefficient calculation
gives the third from the second; the classification and Jordan properties
give the first from the third. The last implication recovers signed Sp
and S on the original ordered carrier through the isomorphism. It does
not enlarge the original physical reversible group.

This is a direct application and premise comparison of the published
classification and the project's existing Jordan calculation. It is not
a new classification theorem or a reduction in the number of logically
independent assumptions of the original full package: S was already
redundant there. This note does not formalize the classification in Lean.

## Complete conditional reconstruction and its remaining contracts

A short single-system candidate can therefore be stated as follows:
every state is a probabilistic mixture of one jointly distinguishable
pure frame, and any two ordered frames of the same size are related by
an available reversible transformation. Full effects and the finite
ordered-state framework remain explicit model premises.

Its geometry is the normalized slice of a classical positive cone or a
simple Jordan cone. Pure states and their least confirming effects share
one trace geometry, so their transition comparison is reciprocal. In rank
two, the nonclassical state spaces are Euclidean balls of different
dimensions, as in the source's Theorem 6.1; the complex qubit is the
three-dimensional Bloch-ball case. These conclusions do not establish
all-frame symmetry from bit symmetry or from endpoint exchange.

For the complex-field selection, retain the **entire** self-composition
contract of the quantum-origin volume's Hypothesis 29.1: local maximum
frame size $n\ge2$; connected reversible pure-state transitivity; a
self-composite with the same spectral strong-symmetry properties; its
connected pure-state transitivity or explicit simplicity; rank $n^2$;
and the stated bilinear state/effect products, normalization, spanning
and separation conditions of local tomography. The same classification
first recovers S for both systems, so Theorem 29.2 applies unchanged and
selects $A\simeq\operatorname{Herm}_n(\mathbb C)$ and
$C\simeq\operatorname{Herm}_{n^2}(\mathbb C)$. Its explicit dimension
comparison excludes real and quaternionic types, all other spin-factor
dimensions and the Albert algebra under these conditions. Connected
pure-state transitivity excludes the nontrivial classical branch.

| Quantum ingredient | Consequence and condition |
| --- | --- |
| Pure states and mixed states | Under the full complex-selection contract, rank-one projectors and positive trace-one matrices on $\mathbb C^n$ |
| Binary effects and probabilities | $0\le E\le I$ and $p(E\mid\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}(E\rho)$, from the full original effect interval and its dual identification |
| Pure-state comparison | $p(P,Q)=\operatorname{Tr}(PQ)=|\langle\psi,\phi\rangle|^2$; endpoint exchange is a consequence |
| Composite type | The stated rank and tomography contract selects the complex matrix type; this is not uniqueness of every product embedding |
| Coherent slit response | Theorem 28.2 still requires its full physical compression/filter contract; the Jordan cone alone supplies no such operation menu |
| General instruments and state updates | Require the specified process, reference and instrument contracts; arbitrary positive maps are not automatically completely positive |
| Time evolution | Requires an admitted time parameter and the corresponding continuity and dynamical contracts; state geometry does not choose a clock or Hamiltonian |

Real, complex and quaternionic matrix systems, simple spin factors, the
Albert algebra and classical simplices all pass the single-system
classification's corresponding branch. A general nonclassical Jordan
direct sum is not another alternative in that theorem. Removing the
explicit composite conditions therefore removes the complex-selection
conclusion, rather than deriving those conditions from reciprocal
comparison.

Endpoint exchange and sequential order are distinct even for a qubit.
Let $P=|0\rangle\langle0|$, $Q=|+\rangle\langle+|$, and use
Lüders yes-branch maps $K_P(\rho)=P\rho P$ and
$K_Q(\rho)=Q\rho Q$. Although
$\operatorname{Tr}(PQ)=\operatorname{Tr}(QP)=1/2$, on input $\rho=P$
the probabilities for two yes outcomes are

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(K_Q(K_P(P)))=1/2,\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(K_P(K_Q(P)))=1/4.
$$

The two laws compare different physical histories. Reciprocal transition
probabilities do not require their equality.

The unresolved reduction is precise: derive the joint-frame spectral and
all-frame reversible conditions, and the relevant composition and process
contracts, from fewer relational conditions. A symmetric function
$p(a,b)=p(b,a)$ by itself specifies none of these. The source theorem
settles their conditional geometry, not that remaining derivation.
