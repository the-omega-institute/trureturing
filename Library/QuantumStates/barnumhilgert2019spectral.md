# Spectral strong symmetry and the redundant sharpness premise

[Barnum–Hilgert, arXiv:1904.03753v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.03753v1#S1.Thmtheorem1),
Theorem 1.1, classifies finite-dimensional spectral strongly symmetric
compact convex sets. Its alternatives are a simplex and the normalized
state space of a simple Euclidean Jordan algebra. Definition 3.4 requires
each state to lie in the convex hull of one frame. Definition 3.5 requires
transitivity on ordered frames of every fixed size. These are the source
conditions, not consequences of an arbitrary symmetric comparison table.

## Exact application to the quantum-origin carrier

Use the real ordered vector space $A$, cone $C$, normalization $u$, compact
state base $K$, and full effects $[0,u]$ from
[Definition 26.1 of the quantum-origin volume](../../docs/develop/theory/QUANTUM_ORIGIN_ENDOGENOUS_CUT_ATLAS_AND_DISCRETE_RECONSTRUCTION_BOUNDARIES.md).
A frame $(w_1,\ldots,w_r)$ has one common family of positive effects
$f_i$ with $\sum_i f_i\le u$ and $f_i(w_j)=\delta_{ij}$.
This joint condition is stronger than asking every pair to have a
possibly different distinguishing measurement.

Suppose signed Sp holds: every $x\in A$ can be written
$x=\sum_i\lambda_iw_i$ in such a frame. If $x\in C$, then directly

$$
\lambda_i=f_i(x)\ge0.
$$

If also $u(x)=1$, applying $u$ gives $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$.
Thus signed Sp implies the source's normalized-state spectrality
**without** using least effects, reciprocity or an inner product.
The effects $f_i$ that already witness the frame extract its coefficients;
no newly constructed state–effect map is needed for this step.

The source's measurement convention and the project's convention agree.
The residual effect $h=u-\sum_i f_i$ is nonnegative and vanishes on every
frame member. Adding $h$ to $f_1$ gives effects summing to $u$ with the
same frame evaluations. Conversely a normalized frame measurement already
satisfies the project's inequality. This includes a one-element frame.

Assume now the project's all-frame strong symmetry from Definition 28.1.
Every supplied reversible map induces an affine automorphism of $K$;
transitivity of the supplied subgroup therefore implies the source's
transitivity under the full affine automorphism group. The source theorem
applies directly. Its affine isomorphism homogenizes to a linear cone
isomorphism preserving $u$, with Jordan trace normalization, because $C$
is generated by the normalized base. Neither half of Principle S has
entered this classification.

In the resulting Jordan representation, pure states are primitive
idempotents. The usual trace pairing identifies the least proper effect
saturating $p$ with $x\mapsto\tau(p\circ x)$. The proof already supplied
inside Theorem 28.2 applies at this point: for $0\le a\le1$ saturating
$p$, the unit-eigenvalue spectral projection $r$ of $a$ satisfies
$p\in J_1(r)$, hence $p\le r\le a$. That argument uses Jordan spectral
and neutral-compression properties after classification, rather than
assuming the project's physical slit operations exist. Symmetry of the
Jordan trace pairing then gives reciprocity. The Jordan spectral theorem
also gives signed Sp for arbitrary $x\in A$.

Consequently, on this fixed carrier the following packages have the same
models, with the same supplied reversible subgroup required to be strongly
symmetric in every row:

| Package | Conditions stated before classification |
| --- | --- |
| Original single-system package | S, signed Sp, all-frame strong symmetry |
| Package without sharpness input | signed Sp, all-frame strong symmetry |
| Source spectral package | normalized-state spectrality, all-frame strong symmetry |

The first implies the second by forgetting S; the coefficient calculation
gives the third from the second; the classification and Jordan properties
give the first from the third. The last implication recovers signed Sp
and S on the original ordered carrier through the isomorphism. It does
not enlarge the original physical reversible group.

This is a direct application and premise comparison of the published
classification and the project's existing Jordan calculation. It is not
a new classification theorem or a reduction in the number of logically
independent assumptions of the original full package: S was already
redundant there. This note does not formalize the classification in Lean.

There is also a [comparison-based route](mullerududec2012selfduality.md#reciprocal-least-confirmation-and-the-spectrality-bridge):
on this same full-effect carrier, S implies signed Sp by positive-dual
peeling and a shift in one completed frame. Thus S together with all-frame
strong symmetry gives the same classified package. The S-to-Sp bridge is
a paper derivation whose positive-dual generation step has a separate
formal theorem; it does not derive the required strong symmetry from
endpoint reciprocity.

## Complete conditional reconstruction and its remaining contracts

A short single-system candidate can therefore be stated as follows:
every state is a probabilistic mixture of one jointly distinguishable
pure frame, and any two ordered frames of the same size are related by
an available reversible transformation. Full effects and the finite
ordered-state framework remain explicit model premises.

Its geometry is the normalized slice of a classical positive cone or a
simple Jordan cone. Pure states and their least confirming effects share
one trace geometry, so their transition comparison is reciprocal. In rank
two, the nonclassical state spaces are Euclidean balls of different
dimensions, as in the source's Theorem 6.1; the complex qubit is the
three-dimensional Bloch-ball case. These conclusions do not establish
all-frame symmetry from bit symmetry or from endpoint exchange.

For the complex-field selection, retain the **entire** self-composition
contract of the quantum-origin volume's Hypothesis 29.1: local maximum
frame size $n\ge2$; connected reversible pure-state transitivity; a
self-composite with the same spectral strong-symmetry properties; its
connected pure-state transitivity or explicit simplicity; rank $n^2$;
and the stated bilinear state/effect products, normalization, spanning
and separation conditions of local tomography. The same classification
first recovers S for both systems, so Theorem 29.2 applies unchanged and
selects $A\simeq\operatorname{Herm}_n(\mathbb C)$ and
$C\simeq\operatorname{Herm}_{n^2}(\mathbb C)$. Its explicit dimension
comparison excludes real and quaternionic types, all other spin-factor
dimensions and the Albert algebra under these conditions. Connected
pure-state transitivity excludes the nontrivial classical branch.

| Quantum ingredient | Consequence and condition |
| --- | --- |
| Pure states and mixed states | Under the full complex-selection contract, rank-one projectors and positive trace-one matrices on $\mathbb C^n$ |
| Binary effects and probabilities | $0\le E\le I$ and $p(E\mid\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}(E\rho)$, from the full original effect interval and its dual identification |
| Pure-state comparison | $p(P,Q)=\operatorname{Tr}(PQ)=|\langle\psi,\phi\rangle|^2$; endpoint exchange is a consequence |
| Composite type | The stated rank and tomography contract selects the complex matrix type; this is not uniqueness of every product embedding |
| Coherent slit response | Theorem 28.2 still requires its full physical compression/filter contract; the Jordan cone alone supplies no such operation menu |
| General instruments and state updates | Require the specified process, reference and instrument contracts; arbitrary positive maps are not automatically completely positive |
| Time evolution | Requires an admitted time parameter and the corresponding continuity and dynamical contracts; state geometry does not choose a clock or Hamiltonian |

Real, complex and quaternionic matrix systems, simple spin factors, the
Albert algebra and classical simplices all pass the single-system
classification's corresponding branch. A general nonclassical Jordan
direct sum is not another alternative in that theorem. Removing the
explicit composite conditions therefore removes the complex-selection
conclusion, rather than deriving those conditions from reciprocal
comparison.

Endpoint exchange and sequential order are distinct even for a qubit.
Let $P=|0\rangle\langle0|$, $Q=|+\rangle\langle+|$, and use
Lüders yes-branch maps $K_P(\rho)=P\rho P$ and
$K_Q(\rho)=Q\rho Q$. Although
$\operatorname{Tr}(PQ)=\operatorname{Tr}(QP)=1/2$, on input $\rho=P$
the probabilities for two yes outcomes are

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(K_Q(K_P(P)))=1/2,\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(K_P(K_Q(P)))=1/4.
$$

The two laws compare different physical histories. Reciprocal transition
probabilities do not require their equality.

The unresolved reduction is precise: derive the joint-frame spectral and
all-frame reversible conditions, and the relevant composition and process
contracts, from fewer relational conditions. A symmetric function
$p(a,b)=p(b,a)$ by itself specifies none of these. The source theorem
settles their conditional geometry, not that remaining derivation.

### Ordered carriers and the shared composite contract

For each of the three systems $A,B,D$, use the nonzero finite-dimensional
real ordered carrier of Definition 26.1 of the
[quantum-origin volume](../../docs/develop/theory/QUANTUM_ORIGIN_ENDOGENOUS_CUT_ATLAS_AND_DISCRETE_RECONSTRUCTION_BOUNDARIES.md).
Write $C_X$ for its closed pointed generating cone, $u_X$ for its strictly
positive normalization, $K_X$ for its compact normalized base and $P_X$
for its pure states. All effects in $[0,u_X]$ are allowed. Assume S on
each system: every pure $p$ has a least proper confirming effect $e_p$,
and $e_p(q)=e_q(p)$ for pure $p,q$.

The earlier paper bridge supplies a positive definite self-dualizing
form $B_X$ with $e_p=B_X(p,\cdot)$ and
$u_X=B_X(U_X,\cdot)$. A complete frame is a finite pure family satisfying

$$
B_X(p_i,p_j)=\delta_{ij},\qquad \sum_i p_i=U_X.
$$

Every orthogonal pure family extends to a complete frame. In particular,
every pure state belongs to one. Every complete frame has cardinality
$r_X=u_X(U_X)$, because all its members are normalized. Pure states span
$X$. Any orthogonal pure family satisfies $\sum_i p_i\le U_X$ by
self-duality and the corresponding proper-effect bound.

Supply bilinear products of states and of functionals, both denoted by
$\otimes$ with their domains understood:

$$
A\times B\longrightarrow D,\qquad
A^*\times B^*\longrightarrow D^*.
$$

Both products are positive on their positive cones. They obey, for all
vectors and functionals,

$$
(f\otimes g)(a\otimes b)=f(a)g(b),\qquad
u_D=u_A\otimes u_B.
\tag{1}
$$

Finally require local tomography: product functionals separate $D$.
These products describe one joint realization, including its marginals
and conditional states. No assumption on reversible physical processes
or on an instrument menu is made.

The pairing in (1) makes the induced map $A\otimes_{\mathbb R}B\to D$
injective: dual bases on the two local vector spaces separate every
tensor. Product functionals span a space of dimension at most
$\dim A\dim B$; local tomography says that this span is all of $D^*$.
Consequently

$$
\dim D=\dim A\dim B,
\qquad
D=\operatorname{span}\{p\otimes q:p\in P_A,q\in P_B\}.
\tag{2}
$$

This establishes the state-spanning clause as well as the dimension
identity. It does not identify the positive cone of $D$ with the cone
generated by product states.

### A pure marginal fixes every conditional state

For a normalized joint state $\omega$, define its marginals by
$f(\omega_A)=(f\otimes u_B)(\omega)$ and the analogous formula for
$\omega_B$. Positivity, (1) and finite-dimensional duality put these
marginals in $K_A$ and $K_B$.

Suppose $\omega_A=p$ is pure. For every proper effect $g$ of $B$, define
the unnormalized conditional state $a_g$ by

$$
f(a_g)=(f\otimes g)(\omega).
$$

Positive products imply $0\le a_g\le p$. The ray through a pure state
is extreme, so $a_g=\lambda_g p$. Applying $u_A$ identifies
$\lambda_g=g(\omega_B)$. Thus

$$
(f\otimes g)(\omega)=f(p)g(\omega_B).
$$

Proper effects span the local dual spaces; hence local tomography and
(1) give $\omega=p\otimes\omega_B$.

In particular $p\otimes q$ is pure when both $p$ and $q$ are pure.
Indeed, in any nontrivial convex decomposition of $p\otimes q$, purity
of both marginals forces every component to have marginals $p,q$.
The preceding result forces every component to equal $p\otimes q$.
This part uses the composite contract and local convex geometry, not S.

### Product frames have no remaining direction

Choose complete local frames $(p_i)$ and $(q_j)$ and write

$$
P_{ij}=p_i\otimes q_j,\qquad h_{ij}=e_{p_i}\otimes e_{q_j}.
$$

Each $P_{ij}$ is pure. Positive bilinearity and (1) give
$h_{ij}\ge0$, $\sum_{ij}h_{ij}=u_D$ and
$h_{ij}(P_{kl})=\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}$. Thus $h_{ij}$ is a proper
effect confirming $P_{ij}$, and global leastness gives

$$
e_{P_{ij}}\le h_{ij}.
\tag{3}
$$

Evaluation at the other product-frame states shows that the $P_{ij}$
are mutually $B_D$-orthogonal. Set

$$
V=U_A\otimes U_B=\sum_{ij}P_{ij},\qquad W=U_D-V.
$$

The orthogonal-family bound gives $W\in C_D$. Bilinearity makes $V$
independent of the chosen complete local frames. Given any pure $p,q$,
choose complete local frames containing them. In that product frame,
orthogonality gives

$$
B_D(W,p\otimes q)
=u_D(p\otimes q)-B_D(V,p\otimes q)=1-1=0.
$$

By the spanning statement (2) and nondegeneracy of $B_D$, $W=0$.
Thus every product of complete local frames is a complete global frame:

$$
U_D=U_A\otimes U_B,\qquad r_D=r_A r_B.
\tag{4}
$$

For the cardinality equality, either count its pure members or apply
$u_D$ to the vector identity and use (1). The missing-complement argument
is essential: a single product frame only gives $r_D\ge r_A r_B$.
It is the same residual $W$ for every choice of local frames, and its
orthogonality to all product pure states, that gives equality.

### Least comparisons factor without an extra product axiom

For a fixed pair of local frames, the positive differences in (3) sum to

$$
\sum_{ij}(h_{ij}-e_{P_{ij}})
=u_D-B_D(V,\cdot)=0.
$$

Pointedness of the positive dual cone forces each difference to be zero.
Choosing frames through arbitrary pure $p,q$ therefore gives

$$
e_{p\otimes q}=e_p\otimes e_q.
\tag{5}
$$

Consequently, for pure $p,q,r,s$,

$$
T_{p\otimes q}(r\otimes s)=T_p(r)T_q(s).
\tag{6}
$$

Here $T$ is the full-effect least-confirmation infimum, with its first
argument pure. This equality is derived within the composite contract;
it has not been added as an independent comparison law. Pure-state
spanning and bilinearity extend it to

$$
B_D(a\otimes b,c\otimes d)=B_A(a,c)B_B(b,d)
\quad(a,c\in A,\ b,d\in B).
\tag{7}
$$

Equations (4)–(7) recover the product-frame and factorization properties
needed to connect to the cited Jordan-model result, without presupposing
primitive product effects. The exact implication from S and the stated
full-effect contract is the premise bridge supplied here; it is not
attributed verbatim to the source's Lemma 8.

### Connected comparisons and composite simplicity

Give the pure states of a system the undirected graph with an edge
between distinct $p,q$ when $B_X(p,q)>0$. “Connected” means that any two
vertices can be joined by a finite edge path; orthogonal states may still
have zero direct comparison.

The graph components span mutually orthogonal nonzero subspaces, so
there are finitely many components. Pure-state decomposition of every
positive vector shows that $C_X$ is the direct sum of the cones generated
by those components. Each summand equals the intersection of $C_X$ with
its component subspace and hence is closed. Conversely, a nontrivial
direct-sum decomposition of $C_X$ separates its pure rays into different
graph components: a least confirming effect for a state in one summand
can be chosen to vanish on every other summand, so its least effect
vanishes there. Thus graph connectedness is equivalent to
indecomposability of the ordered cone. For a Euclidean Jordan cone this
is equivalently simplicity of the Jordan algebra.

If the two local graphs are connected, (6) connects any two product
pure states: first follow a path in the first factor with the second
fixed, then a path in the second with the first fixed. A fixed pure
state has self-comparison one, so every step remains an edge. If a pure
state of $D$ lay outside this component, it would be orthogonal to every
product pure state. Equation (2) and nondegeneracy would force it to be
zero, contradicting normalization. The composite graph is therefore
connected as well. In the Jordan representation, local simplicity thus
implies composite simplicity under this contract; it need not be assumed
separately.

### Conditional complex selection and its remaining inputs

If the single-system S-to-Jordan paper bridge is used, suppose further
that the local comparison graph of $A$ is connected and $r_A=n\ge2$.
The state/effect product contract and local tomography for its
self-composite $D$ remain explicit inputs, and S holds on $D$ as well.
The preceding argument makes both Jordan systems simple.
Equations (2) and (4) provide the rank and
dimension equalities used in Theorem 29.2 of the quantum-origin volume.
The classification step is also supplied directly by
[van de Wetering, arXiv:1803.11139v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.11139v3),
Proposition 40: a simple Euclidean Jordan algebra whose rank and dimension
both square in a simple composite is of complex matrix type. That
proposition's rank/dimension hypotheses are the inputs used here; its
Theorem 3 has an additional sequential-product compatibility contract,
which is not assumed as a physical process contract in this note.
The Jordan dimension comparison in that theorem therefore selects

$$
A\simeq\operatorname{Herm}_n(\mathbb C),\qquad
D\simeq\operatorname{Herm}_{n^2}(\mathbb C).
$$

For clarity, real matrices have dimension $n(n+1)/2$, whose square is
less than the minimum simple rank-$n^2$ dimension for $n\ge2$.
Quaternionic matrices have dimension $n(2n-1)$, whose square exceeds the
maximum simple rank-$n^2$ dimension. For a rank-two spin factor the local
dimension $m$ must satisfy $m^2\in\{10,16,28\}$, so $m=4$, the complex
qubit. The Albert case would require a simple rank-nine algebra of
dimension $27^2$, whereas the available dimensions are $45,81,153$.
For complex matrices, the dimension $n^4$ selects the complex composite
among the three rank-$n^2$ matrix types. These are applications of the
known finite-dimensional Jordan classification and the volume's existing
dimension argument.

For the single-sector conclusion in this section, connectedness of the
local comparison graph remains an input. Neither that condition,
existence of the compatible composite nor local tomography has been
derived from endpoint symmetry. The comparison argument removes the
separate rank-multiplication and composite-simplicity assumptions; the
S-to-Jordan route also avoids supplying an independent strongly symmetric
reversible group for this state-space argument. These consequences
supply neither physical instruments, complete positivity, a clock nor a
Hamiltonian, and do not establish uniqueness of the product embedding.
The whole reconstruction remains a paper route pending its missing
formal bridges.

In ordinary words: independently chosen local reference states give
mutually distinguishable joint reference states. Their unused positive
complement of the unit is the same for every choice of complete local
reference lists. That complement would escape every possible choice of
local reference, so local tomography forces it to vanish. The product
lists therefore form complete joint frames, and their comparisons
multiply without adding another axiom. A complete frame supplies every
outcome of one reference measurement; it need not linearly span the
whole state space.

### Sector composites without a connectedness input

Use the same carrier, S, positive state/effect products, pairing and unit
identities, and local tomography as above. In this section also use the
conditional S-to-Jordan paper bridge, with its trace normalization. No
connectedness or simplicity is supplied for either factor or for $D$.

Distribution of Jordan composites over direct sums is an established
result: [Barnum–Graydon–Wilce, arXiv:1606.09331v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09331v3),
Propositions 4.8–4.9 and Corollary 4.10, treat it for their dynamical EJA
composites. Their Definition 4.1 also requires a dynamical composite and
adjoint compatibility, so it cannot be applied to the present static
contract without a bridge. The following frame argument supplies the
algebraic identities and sector restrictions needed here. It does not
claim the source's physical process contract or historical priority.

Write $x\circ y$ for the derived Jordan product, and $L_x(y)=x\circ y$.
Define the local embeddings

$$
j_A(a)=a\otimes U_B,\qquad j_B(b)=U_A\otimes b.
$$

For local spectral expansions $a=\sum_i\lambda_i p_i$ and
$b=\sum_j\mu_jq_j$ in complete frames, the already derived complete
product frame gives

$$
j_A(a)=\sum_{ij}\lambda_iP_{ij},\qquad
j_B(b)=\sum_{ij}\mu_jP_{ij}.
$$

Squaring in this Jordan frame gives $j_A(a)^2=j_A(a^2)$; polarization
proves that $j_A$ is a unital Jordan homomorphism. The same holds for
$j_B$. Two elements diagonal in one Jordan frame have commuting
multiplication operators: on each Peirce subspace both act by scalars.
Multiplying the displayed expansions therefore gives

$$
[L_{j_A(a)},L_{j_B(b)}]=0,\qquad
j_A(a)\circ j_B(b)=a\otimes b.
\tag{8}
$$

For arbitrary $a,c\in A$ and $d\in B$, use that operator commutation and
the homomorphism property to obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
(a\otimes U_B)\circ(c\otimes d)
&=j_A(a)\circ\bigl(j_A(c)\circ j_B(d)\bigr)\\
&=j_B(d)\circ\bigl(j_A(a)\circ j_A(c)\bigr)\\
&=(a\circ c)\otimes d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$

There is an analogous identity for the other factor. Thus the mixed
Jordan identities come from complete product frames; no local physical
filter or reversible-process extension has been assumed.

Decompose the local Jordan systems into their simple ideals, equivalently
their comparison-graph components:

$$
A=\bigoplus_i A_i,\qquad B=\bigoplus_j B_j.
$$

Let $c_i$ and $d_j$ be their respective central unit projections.
Equation (9), together with product-state spanning, identifies

$$
L_{j_A(c_i)}=L_{c_i}\otimes\mathrm{id}_B.
$$

The right side is an idempotent linear projection with eigenvalues zero
and one. Since $j_A(c_i)$ is a Jordan idempotent, its Peirce
$1/2$-subspace is therefore zero. The Peirce multiplication rules then
make its zero and one spaces complementary ideals: $j_A(c_i)$ is central
in $D$. Likewise each $j_B(d_j)$ is central.

The products $z_{ij}=j_A(c_i)\circ j_B(d_j)=c_i\otimes d_j$ are
nonzero mutually orthogonal central projections summing to $U_D$.
Their multiplication operators act on product vectors as

$$
L_{z_{ij}}(a\otimes b)=(c_i\circ a)\otimes(d_j\circ b).
$$

The vector-space tensor isomorphism (2) consequently gives the exact
ideal decomposition

$$
D=\bigoplus_{i,j}D_{ij},\qquad
D_{ij}=\operatorname{span}(A_i\otimes B_j),\qquad
\dim D_{ij}=\dim A_i\dim B_j.
\tag{10}
$$

The units are $z_{ij}$. This is a direct sum of ordered cones as well as
vector spaces, not merely a list of mutually orthogonal product states.

Each restricted pair $A_i,B_j\to D_{ij}$ inherits the entire static
contract. To see the effect side explicitly, extend a functional on a
local ideal by zero on all other local ideals, take its given product,
and restrict the resulting functional to $D_{ij}$. Positivity survives
extension and restriction. The pairing identity survives as well; the
product of the two restricted normalizations equals $u_D|_{D_{ij}}$,
as is checked on the spanning product vectors. Restrictions of all
original product effects are products of the corresponding local
restrictions, so they separate $D_{ij}$. Thus local tomography is
inherited, rather than separately postulated on the smaller systems.

All three restricted systems are Jordan ideals with their original
trace normalization and full effect intervals; hence they satisfy the
same least-confirmation condition S. Their pure states and comparison
forms agree with the restrictions of the original ones. The graphs of
$A_i$ and $B_j$ are connected. Equation (6) connects all their product
pure states inside $D_{ij}$, and these states span that ideal by (10).
The preceding connectedness argument therefore makes $D_{ij}$ simple.
Applying the already proved rank rule to this restricted composite gives

$$
r(D_{ij})=r(A_i)r(B_j).
\tag{11}
$$

### Complex sectors from a single self-composite

Now take $B=A$, with the same one self-composite as in the stated
contract. For each $i$, the inherited pair
$A_i,A_i\to D_{ii}$ is a locally tomographic self-composite of simple
Jordan systems. If $r(A_i)=n_i\ge2$, the preceding single-sector
dimension argument applies to this actual restricted realization and
gives $A_i\simeq\operatorname{Herm}_{n_i}(\mathbb C)$. If $n_i=1$,
every pure state is its one-member complete frame, so the sector is the
single positive ray $\operatorname{Herm}_1(\mathbb C)$.

Consequently, without a connectedness assumption,

$$
\boxed{
A\simeq\bigoplus_i\operatorname{Herm}_{n_i}(\mathbb C),\qquad
D\simeq\bigoplus_{i,j}\operatorname{Herm}_{n_i n_j}(\mathbb C).
}
\tag{12}
$$

For the second assertion, each $D_{ij}$ is simple, has rank
$k=n_i n_j$ by (11) and dimension $k^2$ by (10). For $k\ge3$ the known
simple Jordan dimensions select the complex matrix type, including
exclusion of the rank-three Albert case. For $k=2$ the dimension-four
spin factor is the complex qubit; $k=1$ is the positive ray. No new
classification theorem is claimed.

In these ordered representations a normalized state is a tuple of
positive semidefinite matrices $(\rho_i)$ with
$\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}\rho_i=1$. A binary effect is a tuple
$0\le E_i\le I_{n_i}$, with probability

$$
\Pr(E\mid\rho)=\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}(E_i\rho_i).
\tag{13}
$$

Pure states are rank-one projectors in one sector; their comparison
vanishes across sectors and is $\operatorname{Tr}(pq)$ within a sector.
Thus one-dimensional sectors give classical probabilities, while each
irreducible rank-two sector has the three-dimensional Bloch-ball
geometry. Equation (12) describes the algebra and cone types; it does
not classify every product embedding in fixed local coordinates.

Connectedness has been removed from the premises for complex sector
type. The earlier connected case remains exactly the one-sector case;
absence of superselection sectors has not been derived. For an explicit
nonclassical example with more than one sector, take
$A=\operatorname{Herm}_2(\mathbb C)\oplus\operatorname{Herm}_2(\mathbb C)$
and let $D$ be four copies of $\operatorname{Herm}_4(\mathbb C)$, with
the usual blockwise product and full effects. This has positive paired
state/effect products, the unit identity, local tomography and S on both
systems; its local graph has two components. Its dimensions are $8,64$
and its ranks are $4,16$. Thus the static contract permits distinct
classical sector labels even when each sector is quantum.

The complete candidate for this classification keeps the finite ordered
full-effect carrier and defines
$T_p(x)=\inf\{f(x):0\le f\le u,\ f(p)=1\}$ with pure first endpoint.
On both $A$ and its actual self-composite $D$, require

$$
T_p\!\left(\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell q_\ell\right)
=\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell T_{q_\ell}(p)
\quad\left(p,q_\ell\text{ pure},\quad
\alpha_\ell\ge0,\quad\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell=1\right)
\tag{14}
$$

for every finite preparation. The state and effect products must both
be positive and bilinear, obey (1), and have product effects separating
$D$. These remain independent model inputs. With the conditional
single-system bridges, (12)–(13) are the paper consequences; no
connectedness, specified qubit, physical reversible group, independent
rank rule or independent comparison-factorization rule is added.
The entire route remains unformalized beyond the previously identified
positive-dual-generation theorem, and supplies no physical instrument
menu or chosen dynamics.

In ordinary words: comparisons can first separate the system into blocks
that never overlap. Applying the same comparison rule to an independent

alternatives. Requiring only one block is a further question. The
comparison rule, positive compatible products and local tomography remain
visible inputs throughout.

### The matrix carrier and exact product contract

Assume complex matrix type and its dimensions have already been obtained:
$A=H_n$, $B=H_m$, $D=H_{nm}$, where $H_k=\operatorname{Herm}_k(\mathbb C)$
and $n,m\ge2$. Each uses its PSD cone, trace normalization and all effects
$0\le f\le I$. These are explicit inputs to this application.
Let $M:A\times B\to D$ be real bilinear and positive on positive product
inputs. The retained product contract supplies a real bilinear
$N:A\times B\to D$, positive on positive effect inputs, with exact pairing
and effect unit

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(M(x,y)N(f,g))
 =\operatorname{Tr}(xf)\operatorname{Tr}(yg),
\qquad N(I,I)=I.
$$

The displayed pairing is required for all $x,f\in A$ and $y,g\in B$.
For bilinear $M,N$, normalized states and proper effects span the
respective real spaces, so pairing on those inputs suffices to check
the full identity. If effect data are supplied only on proper-effect
pairs, the trace-dual formula below gives their unique bilinear extension
under the restricted pairing. The restriction alone does not determine
preassigned values of a nonbilinear map outside that domain.
No positivity of the linearization of $M$ on the entire entangled PSD
cone is assumed.

### Conditional normal-form theorem and proof

There are one fixed unitary $U$ and fixed $\varepsilon,\eta\in\{0,1\}$ such that

$$
M(x,y)=U(x^{T^\varepsilon}\otimes y^{T^\eta})U^*,
\qquad N(f,g)=U(f^{T^\varepsilon}\otimes g^{T^\eta})U^*.
$$

Identify the real tensor product $H_n\otimes_{\mathbb R}H_m$ with $H_{nm}$
using Kronecker products only as vector-space coordinates. Write $\Phi$
for the linearization of the given $M$. If $\Phi(z)=0$, the pairing implies
$\operatorname{Tr}(z(f\otimes g))=0$ for all local effects. They span the
whole tensor space, so $z=0$. Equal dimensions make $\Phi$ invertible.
The same pairing therefore determines

$$
N(f,g)=(\Phi^\dagger)^{-1}(f\otimes g).
$$

For data on proper-effect pairs alone, the same injectivity argument
uses only proper effects, and the formula agrees with the supplied
values by testing against the spanning normalized product states.
Thus it gives the claimed extension. With full-domain pairing the
formula forces bilinearity even without a separate hypothesis.
The unit identity also gives
$\operatorname{Tr}M(x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}(x)\operatorname{Tr}(y)$.

Choose complete local rank-one projection frames $(p_i)_{i=1}^n$ and
$(q_j)_{j=1}^m$. The $nm$ matrices $\rho_{ij}=M(p_i,q_j)$ are density
matrices. The effects $E_{ij}=N(p_i,q_j)$ form a POVM and satisfy
$\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{ij}E_{k\ell})=\delta_{ik}\delta_{j\ell}$.
For PSD matrices $R,S$, zero trace pairing implies $RS=0$.
Consequently $E_{ij}$ acts as identity on the support of $\rho_{ij}$
and as zero on every other support. The supports are orthogonal. There
are $nm$ nonzero supports in Hilbert dimension $nm$, so every support is
one-dimensional and the supports exhaust the space. Thus $\rho_{ij}$ and
$E_{ij}$ are the same complete rank-one projection frame. Every local pure
pair can be extended to such local frames; hence every $M(p,q)$ is a
normalized rank-one projection.

Every nonzero rank-one Hermitian input is a nonzero real multiple of a
rank-one projection. The Main Theorem now applies to $\Phi$, yielding

$$
M(x,y)=\lambda S(x^{T^\varepsilon}\otimes y^{T^\eta})S^*,
\qquad \lambda\in\{-1,1\},\quad S\text{ invertible}.
$$

Positivity on pure products gives $\lambda=1$. Their trace normalization
implies $\operatorname{Tr}((S^*S-I)(p^{T^\varepsilon}\otimes q^{T^\eta}))=0$
for every pure pair. Product pure projections span $H_{nm}$, so
$S^*S=I$. Put $U=S$. This proves the asserted form for $M$.
This form is an isometry for the real trace inner product; hence
$(\Phi^\dagger)^{-1}=\Phi$, proving the form for $N$ as well.

Product-frame completeness, product purity, trace-form factorization,
$M(I,I)=I$, product-state spanning and product-effect separation therefore
need no separate assumptions in this already specified matrix setting.
This does not remove the earlier operational bridges which were used to
obtain complex matrix type and the dimension $nm$ in the first place.

### Scalar-factor boundary

If $n=1$ and $m\ge2$, write $M(\lambda,y)=\lambda L(y)$.
Pairing makes $L$ invertible and fixes the effect map as
$(L^\dagger)^{-1}$. Both maps are positive on the entire single-factor
PSD cone. Self-duality then makes $L^{-1}$ positive too, so $L$ is an
order isomorphism and carries extreme rays to extreme rays. Thus it
preserves rank one, and pairing with the effect unit preserves trace.
Proposition 2.1 of the same paper, pages 410–411, or its equal-size
one-factor case, gives $L(y)=Uy^{T^\eta}U^*$ after trace normalization.
The case $m=1$ is symmetric. If both sizes are one, bilinearity, pairing
and the unit identity give ordinary scalar multiplication.
A scalar factor has no intrinsic transpose bit. In this boundary case
positivity on the whole single-factor cone was proved from the product
contract; it was not assumed for a genuinely bipartite linearization.

### The remaining three-copy condition is independent

The allowed form $M(x,y)=x\otimes y^T$, $N=M$, satisfies this entire
contract, although its linearization is partial transpose and is not
positive on all entangled states. A Bell projection acquires eigenvalue
$-1/2$ after partial transpose.

Use this same rule for every pair of matrix sizes. A positive label swap
is $\sigma(Z)=FZ^TF^*$, with $F$ the factor-flip unitary. Nevertheless,
left and right three-factor products are respectively
$x\otimes y^T\otimes z^T$ and $x\otimes y^T\otimes z$.
The unique linear reassociation agreeing on all product preparations is
$\operatorname{id}\otimes\operatorname{id}\otimes T$, which fails positivity
on an entangled first/third-factor Bell state with a pure middle spectator.
Positive two-copy label exchange therefore does not imply positive
three-copy reassociation. An actual common three-system record set needs
its own explicit compatibility condition. No instrument, complete
positivity or time-evolution conclusion follows from this application.

## One spectator swap and positive reassociation

Fix one actual self-composite $(D,m,e)$ of $A$ and two actual composites
$(E_L,\mu_L,e_L)$ of $(D,A)$ and $(E_R,\mu_R,e_R)$ of $(A,D)$.
Both brackets reuse the same actual $D,m,e$ with fixed copy labels.
For each $X\in\{A,D,E_L,E_R\}$, use a nonzero finite-dimensional real
ordered carrier with closed pointed generating cone $C_X$, strictly
positive normalization $u_X$, compact normalized convex base
$K_X=\{x\in C_X:u_X(x)=1\}$, and all proper effects $[0,u_X]$ in its dual
order. Impose S, defined below, on each of these four systems.

For each actual composite $Z$ of $X,Y$, write $m_Z$ and $e_Z$ for its
state and effect products, using the state spaces and their full real
duals as the respective domains. Both products are bilinear and positive
on pairs of positive inputs. Require exact pairing and the unit identity

$$
e_Z(f,g)(m_Z(x,y))=f(x)g(y),\qquad e_Z(u_X,u_Y)=u_Z
$$

for all state vectors and dual functionals, and local tomography:
product effects separate the joint space. Equivalently their readings
distinguish normalized joint states, since their span contains the
joint unit. No positivity of a state-product linearization on
arbitrary entangled PSD inputs in product coordinates is assumed.

Use the existing single-system S-to-Jordan and product-frame/factorization
bridges and the sector/rank/dimension bridge as conditional paper inputs.
In particular these supply the sector labels and the complex type and
sizes of the actual blocks:

$$
A=\bigoplus_iH_{n_i},\qquad D=\bigoplus_{i,j}H_{n_i n_j},
\qquad E_L,E_R\simeq\bigoplus_{i,j,k}H_{n_i n_j n_k}.
$$

These identifications are ordered, trace-normalized representations.
For each of the three actual products, the sector bridge places
products of inputs from specified sectors in their corresponding
joint sector.
The matrix-product theorem then gives the fixed form of the actual
product on a block with $n_i,n_j\ge2$:

$$
m_{ij}(a,b)=W_{ij}(a^{T^{\varepsilon_{ij}}}
 \otimes b^{T^{\delta_{ij}}})W_{ij}^*,
\qquad \kappa_{ij}=\varepsilon_{ij}\oplus\delta_{ij}.
$$

The relative bit is invariant under an output unitary or global transpose.
An input of dimension one has no intrinsic transpose bit. Scalar blocks
are treated separately below; they are not silently discarded.

### The common-record condition

Let $q_L(a,b,c)=\mu_L(m(a,b),c)$ and
$q_R(a,b,c)=\mu_R(a,m(b,c))$. Pairing makes each two-factor
linearization injective and local tomography gives the matching
dimension. Thus the linearizations $J_L,J_R$ from
$A^{\otimes_{\mathbb R}3}$ are isomorphisms.
Define the unique maps

$$
\alpha=J_RJ_L^{-1},\qquad P_{12}=J_L\tau_{12}J_L^{-1},
\quad \tau_{12}(a\otimes b\otimes c)=b\otimes a\otimes c.
$$

Require $\alpha$ to be an order isomorphism and $P_{12}$ to be positive.
Since $P_{12}^2=\mathrm{id}$, its inverse is positive automatically.
Product units and spanning imply both maps preserve normalization.
For a normalized joint state, define its whole local record, indexed
by all triples of proper local effects $f,g,h$, by

$$
\mathcal R_L(x)(f,g,h)=e_L(e(f,g),h)(x),\qquad
\mathcal R_R(y)(f,g,h)=e_R(f,e(g,h))(y).
$$

Local tomography makes these record maps injective. Exact pairing and
product spanning make the new condition equivalently

$$
\mathcal R_L(K_{E_L})=\mathcal R_R(K_{E_R}),\qquad
\tau_{12}\mathcal R_L(K_{E_L})=\mathcal R_L(K_{E_L}),
$$

where $\tau_{12}$ interchanges the first two effect arguments. The two
brackets give the same legal record set, and relabeling sends each legal
record to a legal record that can have different entries. The one fixed
map $P_{12}$ must act positively on the whole triple cone, including
entangled states and every sector, also those with a scalar middle
factor. This requirement concerns copy labels; it implies no commutation
of measurements performed in time.

### Partial-transpose lemma

On $\bigotimes_{r=1}^\ell\mathbb C^{d_r}$ let $\Gamma_t$ transpose exactly
those tensor factors with $t_r=1$. Then $\Gamma_t$ preserves the PSD cone
if and only if $t_r$ is constant on all positions with $d_r\ge2$.
If the bits are constant the map is identity or global transpose, with
scalar factors irrelevant. Otherwise select two active positions with
different bits, use their two-dimensional Bell projection, and put real
rank-one projections in the remaining positions. Partial transpose has
expectation $-1/2$ in the two-position antisymmetric vector. Thus it is
not positive. This proof also covers unequal dimensions by restriction
to two-dimensional subspaces.

Consequently a product-label correspondence between two fixed twisted
matrix-product representations is an order isomorphism precisely when
their mask difference is constant on the active positions. In particular
all active pairwise relative bits are unchanged. Unitary output changes
and the usual factor-flip unitary do not change that criterion.

### Three-copy orientation theorem

Let $Q=\{i:n_i\ge2\}$. Under the stated common-record condition there are
fixed bits $s_i$, $i\in Q$, such that

$$
\kappa_{ij}=s_i\oplus s_j\qquad(i,j\in Q).
$$

Moreover one fixed choice of ordered coordinates on each of $A,D,E_L,E_R$
simultaneously makes the actual two- and three-factor products standard.

**Proof.** Apply the matrix-product theorem to the two products of a
triple block. The left product, after one output unitary, has a mask
$(t_1,t_2,t_3)$. Its inner actual product forces
$t_1\oplus t_2=\kappa_{ij}$. Positive reassociation and the lemma force
$t_2\oplus t_3=\kappa_{jk}$. The same positive $P_{12}$ maps block
$(i,j,k)$ to $(j,i,k)$. After reassociation there, its last two positions
use the actual $D_{ik}$. The lemma therefore forces
$t_1\oplus t_3=\kappa_{ik}$. For $i,j,k\in Q$ this proves

$$
\kappa_{ik}=\kappa_{ij}\oplus\kappa_{jk}.
$$

Repeated indices are allowed because the copies are independent.
They give $\kappa_{ii}=0$ and $\kappa_{ij}=\kappa_{ji}$. Fix $r\in Q$
and set $s_i=\kappa_{ri}$; the triangle identity gives the claimed form.
If $Q$ is empty there is no orientation condition.

For a general triple, adjacent active positions are controlled by the
actual inner pair on the appropriate bracket. If only positions one and
three are active, the positive swap followed by reassociation makes them
adjacent, so their relative bit is controlled too. With zero or one active
position there is no relative restriction. Thus every triple mask differs
from $(s_i,s_j,s_k)$ by one common bit on all active positions.
Choose arbitrary bits on scalar sectors.

Put $\theta_A=\bigoplus_iT^{s_i}$. On each actual two-system block,
remove $W_{ij}$ and, if needed, transpose the entire output block.
The relative-bit identity gives one fixed order isomorphism $\theta_D$
such that

$$
\theta_Dm(a,b)=\theta_A(a)\otimes\theta_A(b).
$$

Do the same with each triple block to choose one fixed $\theta_L$ for
$E_L$, and set $\theta_R=\theta_L\alpha^{-1}$ for $E_R$. Then both
triple products equal
$\theta_A(a)\otimes\theta_A(b)\otimes\theta_A(c)$ in those coordinates.
Spanning extends these identities to the outer products on arbitrary
$d\in D$, including entangled states. Exact pairing determines the
effect products as well. All choices are fixed on whole spaces, not
selected separately for individual inputs. This proves the assertion.

Every other label permutation now acts by the ordinary tensor-factor
permutation and is positive. Its group identities and compatibility
with reassociation also follow directly from its specified product
action and product spanning. No separate full positive $S_3$ premise is
needed. The $s_i$ are unique up to one common flip on $Q$; bits assigned
to scalar sectors have no meaning. Usual changes of bases remain free.

Conversely, if an actual two-product has this relative-bit form, use its
fixed $\theta_A,\theta_D$ and standard blockwise threefold matrix cones
to construct both outer products by pullback. Their effect products,
pairing, units, positivity and local tomography hold, and common records
satisfy reassociation and the swap. Thus existence of such compatible
threefold extensions is equivalent to the relative-bit condition under
the stated matrix/sector bridges. This does not identify an independently
given fourfold or higher realization without its own compatibility
contract.

### Three boundary models for the added condition

All blocks below use standard PSD cones, trace, full effects and S.
Each product $(X,Y)\mapsto X^{T^a}\otimes Y^{T^b}$ uses the same mask
on its effect product. Positivity on products, pairing, unit identities,
normalization and local tomography therefore hold.

First take $A=H_2$, $D=H_4$, $m(a,b)=a\otimes b^T$,
$\mu_L(d,c)=d\otimes c$ and $\mu_R(a,d)=a\otimes d^T$.
Both brackets give $a\otimes b^T\otimes c$, so reassociation is the
identity order isomorphism. The two-system label swap is the positive
map $d\mapsto Fd^TF^*$. Its uniquely specified spectator lift is
$P_{12}(x)=F_{12}x^{T_{\{1,2\}}}F_{12}^*$.
For $|G\rangle=(|000\rangle+|111\rangle)/\sqrt2$ and
$|w\rangle=(|001\rangle-|110\rangle)/\sqrt2$, its expectation is
$\langle w|P_{12}(|G\rangle\langle G|)|w\rangle=-1/2$.
Thus positive reassociation and a positive two-system swap do not supply
the positive spectator lift.

Second take $A=H_2\oplus H_2\oplus H_2$, put $\kappa_{ij}=1$ for
$i\ne j$ and $\kappa_{ii}=0$, and use
$m_{ij}(a,b)=a\otimes b^{T^{\kappa_{ij}}}$.
Let $\lambda_{ij}=1$ when $i>j$ and zero otherwise, and set

$$
\mu_{L,ij,k}(d,c)=d^{T^{\lambda_{ij}}}\otimes c,
\qquad \mu_{R,i,jk}(a,d)=a\otimes d.
$$

Because $\lambda_{ji}=\lambda_{ij}\oplus\kappa_{ij}$, the global
$P_{12}$ is just block exchange and the ordinary first-factor flip on
each triple block; it is positive even on all entangled inputs.
But reassociation has mask
$(\lambda_{ij},\lambda_{ij}\oplus\kappa_{ij},\kappa_{jk})$.
On block $123$ it is $(0,1,1)$, which sends the projection of
$(|000\rangle+|110\rangle)/\sqrt2$ to a matrix with eigenvalue $-1/2$.
Here $\kappa_{12}\oplus\kappa_{23}\oplus\kappa_{31}=1$.
Thus one positive global spectator swap does not supply positive
reassociation or eliminate odd sector triangles by itself.

Finally let $A=H_{2,r}\oplus H_{2,k}\oplus\mathbb R_c$, with $r\ne k$,
and let every two-system product be standard, so $\kappa=0$.
Use standard threefold products except on block $(r,c,k)$, where the
product is $q(a,\lambda,b)=\lambda a\otimes b^T$.
On that block take $\mu_L(d,b)=d\otimes b^T$ and
$\mu_R(a,d)=a\otimes d^T$; the brackets agree. Every block has positive
reassociation. The swap is positive on all blocks whose third label is
$r$, since neither those blocks nor their swapped targets are $(r,c,k)$.
However the swap from $(r,c,k)$ to $(c,r,k)$ is a one-factor partial
transpose and is not positive. Thus testing only one fixed nonclassical
spectator sector does not standardize the entire actual triple,
including quantum factors separated by a classical factor. The theorem
requires the one fixed swap on the whole legal triple record set.

In each failed correspondence, the trace adjoint in the displayed
block coordinates transposes local factors and permutes labels as
needed. It sends proper local product tests to proper local product
tests, so every local product-test entry stays in $[0,1]$. Local
tomography nevertheless makes the image matrix the unique candidate
for the transported record.
When it has a negative eigenvalue, the projection onto a corresponding
eigenvector is a proper full joint effect whose evaluation on that
matrix is negative. The record then has no legal joint state.

### A complete finite comparison candidate

On each of the four actual carriers $X=A,D,E_L,E_R$ above, let $P_X$
be the pure states, the extreme points of $K_X$. For $p\in P_X$ and
$x\in K_X$, use the full-effect least-confirmation comparison

$$
T_p(x)=\inf\{f(x):0\le f\le u_X,\ f(p)=1\}.
$$

Condition S requires, for every pure reference $p$, every finite
family of pure states $q_1,\ldots,q_\ell$ with $\ell\ge1$, and every
choice of preparation weights,

$$
T_p\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell} a_iq_i\right)
=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell} a_iT_{q_i}(p),
\qquad a_i\ge0,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_i=1.
$$

Together with these carriers and S, the candidate keeps the same actual
$D,m,e$ in both brackets, the positive bilinear state and effect
products, exact pairing, product unit identities and local tomography.
It adds order-isomorphic reassociation and one positive full-domain
spectator swap, equivalently the common-record condition above. With
these inputs, the conditional single-system, product-frame/factorization
and sector/rank/dimension paper bridges, and the cited matrix-preserver
theorem, the finite state/effect geometry is a direct sum of complex
PSD cones. In these coordinates, normalized states $(\rho_i)$ satisfy
$\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}\rho_i=1$, and proper effects $(F_i)$ with
$0\le F_i\le I$ have Born probabilities $\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}(F_i\rho_i)$.
The actual two- and three-system products are simultaneously standard
in fixed coordinates. The pure states in sector $i$ form the complex
projective space $\mathbb{CP}^{n_i-1}$; comparison within a sector is
$\operatorname{Tr}(pq)$ and across different sectors is zero. If the
whole local pure-state graph with edges of strictly positive comparison
is connected and
nonclassical (rank at least two), this is the ordinary one-sector finite
complex quantum case. The general classification allows superselection
sectors.

A singleton preparation gives endpoint reciprocity; the full finite
condition also gives preparation consistency. Existence of the actual
composites, the product contract and the common-record condition remain
model inputs. This closes a product representation gap, without reducing
the primitive physical assumptions. Independently supplied higher
composites, infinite dimension, physical instruments and state updates,
CP extensions of operations, a physical clock and Hamiltonian all
remain outside the result. This is a conditional paper reconstruction;
the existing positive-dual Lean theorem does not formalize these bridges,
and no new kernel evidence or historical-priority claim is made.

In ordinary words, fix a pure reference and compare another preparation
by the lowest acceptance probability among all tests that accept the
reference with certainty. For any finite random preparation of pure
states, require that comparison to equal the weighted average obtained
by reversing each pure comparison. Use the same rule for the local
system and each actual composite. The two triple brackets must allow
the same records, and swapping the first two labels must send each
legal record to a legal record. With the product contract, both record
conditions and the stated paper bridges, one choice of coordinates per
sector fixes the relative mirror choices.
An inconsistent triangle would send a legal entangled record to one
with no legal joint state: its only candidate matrix gives a negative
value on a full joint effect, even though local product-test entries
remain valid probabilities. These are conditions on statistical
descriptions and say nothing about exchanging measurements in time.

## Positive marginals recover the joint-effect contract

The following application starts at the trace-normalized EJA stage of
the [single-system S-to-Jordan paper bridge](mullerududec2012selfduality.md#complete-conditional-candidate-and-its-scope).
It replaces the independently supplied binary effect product by two
globally positive marginals and preparation spanning. The implication
below is a paper composition of existing Jordan facts, not a theorem
quoted verbatim from a source, a novelty claim or a Lean result.

The spectral facts used are [Barnum–Hilgert, arXiv:1904.03753v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.03753v1#S4),
Theorem 4.2, Definition 4.3 and Proposition 4.4: spectral expansions in
primitive frames, the canonical trace base, and its pure states.
For ambient operator commutation, [van de Wetering, *Commutativity in
Jordan Operator Algebras*, arXiv:1912.01903v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.01903v1#S2.counter12),
Proposition 2.12, states that an idempotent and another element of a
Jordan algebra operator commute exactly when they generate an associative
subalgebra. Theorem 3.13 gives the corresponding equivalence for arbitrary
elements of a JB-algebra. These facts include exceptional EJAs; they are
not restricted to Hermitian matrix subalgebras. Source use is citation-only.
[Barnum–Graydon–Wilce, arXiv:1606.09331v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09331v3),
Definition 4.1 and Proposition 4.3, pages 22–23, give related composite
identities with dynamical extension and adjoint compatibility among their
hypotheses. Those extra hypotheses are not imported here. Likewise the
product-test requirement in Barnum–Wilce's Lemma 8 is not assumed before
the joint effects have been constructed.

### Exact marginal input and conclusion

Let $A,B,D$ be nonzero finite-dimensional Euclidean Jordan algebras,
with units $1_X$, cones of squares $X_+$ and canonical traces $\tau_X$.
Each primitive idempotent has trace one, including in every direct-sum
sector. Use the associative inner products

$$
\langle x,y\rangle_X=\tau_X(x\circ y).
$$

The normalized states are **all** $x\ge0$ with $\tau_X(x)=1$; the effects
are the **full** intervals $[0,1_X]$, identified with functionals by these
pairings. Arbitrarily rescaled sector traces are not substituted for
this normalization. Supply real-bilinear $M:A\times B\to D$, positive
on every pair $x\in A_+,y\in B_+$, and real-linear maps
$R_A:D\to A$, $R_B:D\to B$ such that

$$
\begin{gathered}
R_A(D_+)\subseteq A_+,\qquad R_B(D_+)\subseteq B_+,\\
\tau_A R_A=\tau_D=\tau_B R_B,\\
R_A M(x,y)=\tau_B(y)x,\qquad
R_B M(x,y)=\tau_A(x)y\quad(x\in A,\ y\in B),\\
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{M(x,y):x\in A,y\in B\}=D.
\end{gathered}
\tag{15}
$$

The marginal maps are positive on the **whole joint cone**, not just on
prepared product states; their trace identities hold on all vectors.
The two identities for $R_A M$ and $R_B M$ quantify over all local
vectors. By bilinearity they can equivalently be checked on all normalized product
states. Real spanning does not say that every joint state is a convex
mixture of products.

Set $a=R_A^*$, $b=R_B^*$ for the displayed trace inner products. Then
$a,b$ are unital injective Jordan homomorphisms with

$$
[L_{a(x)},L_{b(y)}]=0\quad(x\in A,y\in B),\qquad
N(f,g):=a(f)\circ b(g),\qquad L_z(w)=z\circ w.
\tag{16}
$$

The map $N$ is positive bilinear, $N(1_A,1_B)=1_D$, and

$$
\langle N(f,g),M(x,y)\rangle_D
=\langle f,x\rangle_A\langle g,y\rangle_B
\quad(f,x\in A,\ g,y\in B).
\tag{17}
$$

Thus $e(f,g)(z)=\langle N(f,g),z\rangle_D$ supplies the entire earlier
joint-effect contract, including separation. Moreover $M=N$ under the
trace identifications, $M(1_A,1_B)=1_D$, the tensor linearization is an
isometric vector-space isomorphism, pure products are pure, and

$$
\dim D=\dim A\dim B,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}D=\operatorname{rank}A\operatorname{rank}B.
\tag{18}
$$

No effect product, product purity, factorized trace, rank-minimality or
complex matrix type is an assumption in (15).

### Faithfulness and the exceptional-inclusive support bridge

First, trace preservation and (15) give
$\tau_D M(x,y)=\tau_A(x)\tau_B(y)$. Self-duality of the cones makes
$a,b$ positive; the trace identities make them unital. Put
$V=M(1_A,1_B)$. For positive $x\le s1_A$, $y\le t1_B$, positive
bilinearity gives $0\le M(x,y)\le stV$. If $h\ge0$ satisfies
$\langle h,V\rangle_D=0$, it therefore annihilates every positive
product. Positive vectors span each factor, so it annihilates every
product and, by (15), all of $D$. Hence $h=0$. By finite-dimensional
self-duality, $V$ is interior to $D_+$: it has support $1_D$. This does
not yet say $V=1_D$ or that it has minimum possible rank.

Here is the support calculation without associative matrix multiplication.
For an idempotent $r$, the orthogonal Peirce decomposition is
$D=D_1(r)\oplus D_{1/2}(r)\oplus D_0(r)$, with $L_r$ having the indicated
eigenvalues. The zero and one spaces are Jordan subalgebras, with zero
product between them. For $z=t^2\ge0$, associativity of the trace form
gives

$$
\langle r,z\rangle_D=\langle L_rt,t\rangle_D
=\|t_1\|^2+\tfrac12\|t_{1/2}\|^2.
\tag{19}
$$

Consequently zero pairing puts $t$, and then $z$, in $D_0(r)$.
Its support $s(z)$, the sum of its positive spectral projections, is
therefore below $1_D-r$. Spectrally expanding a positive $c$ and using
self-duality shows that $\langle c,z\rangle=0$ puts $s(z)$ in the zero
spectral space of $c$. In particular, for an effect $d$ and nonzero
$z\ge0$, probability zero confines $s(z)$ to the zero-eigenvalue
projection of $d$, while
$\langle d,z\rangle=\tau_D(z)$ confines it to the unit-eigenvalue
projection, by applying the zero statement to $1_D-d$. In these two
cases $d\circ s(z)$ equals zero and $s(z)$, respectively. These Peirce
and spectral statements apply to every EJA summand, including an Albert
summand; no special-Jordan embedding is used.

Choose complete primitive frames $(p_i)$ in $A$ and $(q_j)$ in $B$ and
write

$$
\rho_{ij}=M(p_i,q_j),\quad e_i=a(p_i),\quad f_j=b(q_j),
\quad r_{ij}=s(\rho_{ij}).
$$

The $\rho_{ij}$ are nonzero normalized positive vectors summing to $V$.
The $e_i$ and $f_j$ are effects summing to $1_D$. The marginal identities
give $\langle e_i,\rho_{kl}\rangle=\delta_{ik}$ and
$\langle f_j,\rho_{kl}\rangle=\delta_{jl}$. The support calculation
therefore yields

$$
e_i\circ r_{kl}=\delta_{ik}r_{kl},\qquad
f_j\circ r_{kl}=\delta_{jl}r_{kl}.
\tag{20}
$$

Two distinct $r_{ij}$ are orthogonal: a differing first index places
them in the orthogonal unit and zero spectral projections of one $e_i$;
a differing second index uses one $f_j$. Their sum $r$ is a projection
and every $\rho_{ij}$ belongs to $D_1(r)$. Thus $V$ belongs to $D_1(r)$.
Faithfulness of $V$ forces $r=1_D$. Multiplying this unit decomposition
by $e_i$ or $f_j$ gives

$$
\sum_{ij}r_{ij}=1_D,\qquad e_i=\sum_j r_{ij},\qquad
f_j=\sum_i r_{ij},\qquad e_i\circ f_j=r_{ij}.
\tag{21}
$$

In particular the local images are sharp projections. The $r_{ij}$
are not asserted to be primitive at this step.

For arbitrary spectral expansions $x=\sum_i\lambda_i p_i$ and
$y=\sum_j\mu_jq_j$, (21) shows that $a(x)^2=a(x^2)$ and similarly
for $b$. Polarization gives Jordan homomorphisms. If $a(x)=0$, testing
against $M(z,q)$ for one primitive $q$ gives
$\langle x,z\rangle_A=0$ for every $z$, so $x=0$; likewise $b$ is
injective. Both $a(x)$ and $b(y)$ are diagonal in the same orthogonal
projection decomposition (21). Refining it to a primitive Jordan frame,
their multiplication operators act by scalars on each common Peirce
space: on an off-diagonal space the scalar is the average of the two
corresponding diagonal coefficients. Thus they commute on **all** of
$D$, proving the ambient assertion in (16). This also follows from
the cited idempotent commutation criterion and linearity.

Equation (21) now gives
$N(f,g)=\sum_{ij}\lambda_i\mu_jr_{ij}\ge0$ when $f,g\ge0$ have
nonnegative spectral coefficients. Bilinearity and unitality follow
from its definition. It sends proper-effect pairs to proper effects:
$1_D-N(f,g)=N(1_A-f,g)+N(1_A,1_B-g)\ge0$.

### Construct pairing and separation before using product purity

For primitive $p,q$, let $\rho=M(p,q)$. Given a proper effect $g$ of
$B$, define $c_g\in A$ by the trace-dual identity

$$
\langle f,c_g\rangle_A=\langle N(f,g),\rho\rangle_D
\quad(f\in A).
$$

Positivity of $N$ gives $0\le c_g\le p$, since
$N(f,1_B)=a(f)$ and $R_A\rho=p$. The ray through $p$ is extreme,
so $c_g=\lambda_g p$. Evaluation at $1_A$ gives
$\lambda_g=\langle b(g),\rho\rangle_D=\langle g,q\rangle_B$.
This proves (17) for pure product states. Proper effects span the local
spaces, primitive states span them by spectrality, and all the maps are
bilinear. Hence (17) holds for all four vector arguments.

Testing (17) with dual local bases makes the linearization
$\widetilde M:A\otimes_{\mathbb R}B\to D$ injective; preparation spanning
makes it surjective. The same pairings show that product $N$ effects
separate $D$. At this point the whole old binary contract has been
established. Its marginals agree with $R_A,R_B$, because
$N(f,1_B)=a(f)$ and $N(1_A,g)=b(g)$.

Now directly apply [A pure marginal fixes every conditional
state](#a-pure-marginal-fixes-every-conditional-state), whose positivity,
pairing and separation hypotheses have just been derived. It makes
every $M(p,q)$ pure. Proposition 4.4 of Barnum–Hilgert identifies a
normalized pure vector with a primitive idempotent. Consequently
$\rho_{ij}=r_{ij}$ in (21), and summing gives $V=1_D$. Choosing frames
through any $p,q$ gives $M(p,q)=a(p)\circ b(q)=N(p,q)$; spanning and
bilinearity give $M=N$ on all inputs. Equation (17) is now trace-form
factorization and proves the tensor isometry. Equation (21) is a
complete **primitive** product frame, proving the rank formula in (18).
The earlier least-comparison factorization applies with no new premise.

to construct $N$.

### Exact reduced Jordan contract

Let $A,D,E$ be actual nonzero finite-dimensional EJAs, with square cones,
units $1_X$, canonical traces $\tau_X$ and trace pairings
$\langle x,y\rangle_X=\tau_X(x\circ y)$. Every primitive idempotent in
every sector has trace one. All positive trace-one vectors are states,
and all of $[0,1_X]$ are effects. Keep real-bilinear preparations

$$
M:A\times A\to D,\qquad L:D\times A\to E,
\qquad M(A_+,A_+)\subseteq D_+,\quad L(D_+,A_+)\subseteq E_+,
$$

whose real product spans are the whole $D$ and $E$, respectively.
In particular $L$ is positive for every $d\in D_+$, including entangled
$d$, not just $M$-prepared positive vectors. The only primitive
real-linear maps are $r:D\to A$ and $p_{13},p_{23}:E\to D$.
All three are globally positive; only $r,p_{13}$ are required to be TP.
For every real $a,b,c\in A$ require

$$
\begin{aligned}
rM(a,b)&=\tau_A(b)a,\\
p_{13}Q(a,b,c)&=\tau_A(b)M(a,c),\\
p_{23}Q(a,b,c)&=\tau_A(a)M(b,c),
\qquad Q(a,b,c):=L(M(a,b),c).
\end{aligned}
\tag{39}
$$

Both crossing views land in the same actual $D$ with the same $M$.
These are all-vector identities. There is no primitive second singleton,
joint effect, pure-product rule, right triple, reassociator, spectator
permutation, CP extension or temporal-exchange condition.

**Unique-extension theorem.** This contract forces $p_{23}$ to be TP and
admits exactly one real-linear extension to the four-map contract (24):

$$
p_{12}=H^*,\qquad H(d)=L(d,1_A),\qquad
p_{12}L(d,c)=\tau_A(c)d\quad(d\in D,\ c\in A).
\tag{40}
$$

The adjoint is for the displayed canonical trace pairings. The unit in
$H$ is the **unnormalized** $1_A$, with no extra rank factor. The derived
$p_{12}$ is positive on the entire $E_+$ and TP. The proof below obtains
this before using any matrix representation, and includes scalar,
exceptional and direct-sum EJAs in its ordered argument. The theorem is
a unique-extension equivalence for these fixed data, not an independently
measured minimum number of axioms.

### Trace redundancy and the derived singleton views

The products $Q(a,b,c)$ span $E$: expand $d$ in $M$-products inside every
$L(d,c)$ and use both preparation spans. TP of $r$ gives
$\tau_D M(a,b)=\tau_A(a)\tau_A(b)$. Therefore, for all real inputs,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_E Q(a,b,c)
 &=\tau_D p_{13}Q(a,b,c)
  =\tau_A(b)\tau_D M(a,c)
  =\tau_A(a)\tau_A(b)\tau_A(c),\\
\tau_D p_{23}Q(a,b,c)
 &=\tau_A(a)\tau_D M(b,c)
  =\tau_A(a)\tau_A(b)\tau_A(c).
\end{aligned}
\tag{41}
$$

Spanning proves $\tau_Dp_{23}=\tau_E$ on all of $E$. Thus its TP
condition is redundant. Also, $M$-spanning in the first line proves
$\tau_E L(d,c)=\tau_D(d)\tau_A(c)$ for every real $d,c$.
This calculation needs neither $p_{12}$ nor a Jordan classification;
it also holds on ordered carriers with $u_X$ in place of $\tau_X$.

Choose any $c_0\ge0$ with $\tau_A(c_0)=1$. Then
$K_{c_0}(d)=L(d,c_0)$ is globally positive TP. Set

$$
r_2=r\,p_{23}K_{c_0},\qquad
r_2M(a,b)=\tau_A(a)b.
\tag{42}
$$

The law follows directly from (39). It and $M$-spanning make $r_2$
unique and independent of $c_0$; a spectator confined to a proper sector
is allowed. Global positivity and TP hold by composition. Apply the
[binary marginal lemma](#positive-marginals-recover-the-joint-effect-contract)
only to $(M,r,r_2)$ at this point. In particular its linearization
$J_M:A\otimes_{\mathbb R}A\to D$ is an onto trace-inner-product isometry
and $M(1_A,1_A)=1_D$.

Define three maps on the one actual $E$ by

$$
R_1=rp_{13},\qquad R_2=rp_{23},\qquad R_3=r_2p_{13}.
$$

They are globally positive TP, and (39), (42) give

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_1Q(a,b,c)&=\tau_A(b)\tau_A(c)a,\\
R_2Q(a,b,c)&=\tau_A(a)\tau_A(c)b,\\
R_3Q(a,b,c)&=\tau_A(a)\tau_A(b)c.
\end{aligned}
\tag{43}
$$

It remains to prove the ternary inference from these laws. Applying a

### Ternary support and the common frame

Take trace adjoints $\alpha_\ell=R_\ell^*:A\to E$. Self-duality makes
them positive, and TP makes them unital. Put $V=Q(1_A,1_A,1_A)$.
For positive $a\le s1_A,b\le t1_A,c\le u1_A$, positivity and
trilinearity give $0\le Q(a,b,c)\le stuV$. If $z\ge0$ pairs to zero
with $V$, it pairs to zero with all such products. Positive vectors
span $A$, so real spanning of $Q$ makes it orthogonal to all of $E$,
and hence zero. Finite-dimensional self-duality implies that $V$ is
interior and has support $1_E$. No assertion $V=1_E$ is used yet.

Choose three complete primitive frames $(p_i),(q_j),(s_k)$ of $A$, and
write $\rho_{ijk}=Q(p_i,q_j,s_k)$ and $h_{ijk}=s(\rho_{ijk})$.
The $\rho_{ijk}$ are nonzero trace-one positive vectors by (41), and
sum to $V$.
The singleton laws give, for example,
$\langle\alpha_1(p_i),\rho_{i'j'k'}\rangle=\delta_{ii'}$.
The corresponding equalities hold for the other two positions.

Use the [exceptional-inclusive Peirce support rule](#faithfulness-and-the-exceptional-inclusive-support-bridge),
proved in (19): an effect with zero pairing with a positive vector
annihilates its support; pairing equal to the vector's trace makes
the effect act as the unit on that support. The latter case is the
zero case for the complementary effect. Thus

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_1(p_i)\circ h_{i'j'k'}&=\delta_{ii'}h_{i'j'k'},\\
\alpha_2(q_j)\circ h_{i'j'k'}&=\delta_{jj'}h_{i'j'k'},\\
\alpha_3(s_k)\circ h_{i'j'k'}&=\delta_{kk'}h_{i'j'k'}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{44}
$$

Two different $h$'s are orthogonal: some index differs, placing them
below the orthogonal unit- and zero-eigenvalue projections of one local
effect. Their sum is an idempotent whose Peirce-one space contains every
$\rho_{ijk}$ and hence $V$. Full support of $V$ forces that sum to be
$1_E$. Multiplying this unit decomposition by the local effects gives

$$
\sum_{i,j,k}h_{ijk}=1_E,\quad
\alpha_1(p_i)=\sum_{j,k}h_{ijk},\quad
\alpha_2(q_j)=\sum_{i,k}h_{ijk},\quad
\alpha_3(s_k)=\sum_{i,j}h_{ijk}.
\tag{45}
$$

The $h$'s have not yet been declared primitive. The three local frame
images are nevertheless projections. Varying the local spectral frames
gives $\alpha_\ell(x^2)=\alpha_\ell(x)^2$ for every $x$; polarization
makes each $\alpha_\ell$ a unital Jordan homomorphism. It is injective:
if $\alpha_1(f)=0$, test against $Q(a,q,s)$ with normalized primitive
$q,s$ in (43) to get $\langle f,a\rangle_A=0$ for every $a$.
The other two cases use their respective singleton law.

For arbitrary three spectral expansions, (45) places all their images
in one common orthogonal projection decomposition. Refine it to a
primitive Jordan frame of $E$. On each common Peirce space, multiplication
by a diagonal element acts as a scalar, with the usual average of the
two diagonal coefficients on an off-diagonal space. The three local
multiplication operators therefore commute on **all** of $E$. This is
an ambient Jordan statement, not associative matrix multiplication or
homogeneity of an ordered carrier.

### Conditional states, exact pairing and product purity

Define, with the indicated parentheses,

$$
N(f,g,h)=(\alpha_1(f)\circ\alpha_2(g))\circ\alpha_3(h).
\tag{46}
$$

In the common frame (45) this is
$\sum_{ijk}\lambda_i\mu_j\nu_kh_{ijk}$ for the three local spectral
coefficients. Consequently $N$ is positive trilinear and unital, with
$N(f,1_A,1_A)=\alpha_1(f)$ and the other two analogous identities.
For proper effects $g,h$ and positive $f$ the same coefficients give
$0\le N(f,g,h)\le\alpha_1(f)$; with $f=1_A$ they also give
$0\le N(1_A,g,h)\le\alpha_2(g)$ for positive $g$ and proper $h$.
In particular triples of proper effects give proper effects in $E$.

Here is the conditional-state argument for any normalized $\sigma\in E_+$
whose three singleton states are specified primitive $p,q,s$. For proper
$g,h$, define $x_{g,h}\in A$ by

$$
\langle f,x_{g,h}\rangle_A=\langle N(f,g,h),\sigma\rangle_E.
$$

The preceding inequalities and $R_1\sigma=p$ imply $0\le x_{g,h}\le p$.
The primitive ray is extreme, so $x_{g,h}=\lambda_{g,h}p$.
Its trace, obtained by setting $f=1_A$, is
$\lambda_{g,h}=\langle N(1_A,g,h),\sigma\rangle_E$.
For fixed proper $h$, define $y_h\in A$ for all real $g$ by
$\langle g,y_h\rangle_A=\langle N(1_A,g,h),\sigma\rangle_E$.
Positivity and $R_2\sigma=q$ similarly give $0\le y_h\le q$,
so $y_h=\mu_hq$. Finally

$$
\mu_h=\langle N(1_A,1_A,h),\sigma\rangle_E
     =\langle h,R_3\sigma\rangle_A=\langle h,s\rangle_A.
$$

Therefore the pairing with $\sigma$ is
$\langle f,p\rangle\langle g,q\rangle\langle h,s\rangle$.
This proves, in particular for $\sigma=Q(p,q,s)$, the product identity.
Proper effects span $A$, and primitive vectors span $A$ by spectrality.
Multilinearity extends it to all six real arguments:

$$
\langle N(f,g,h),Q(a,b,c)\rangle_E
 =\langle f,a\rangle_A\langle g,b\rangle_A\langle h,c\rangle_A.
\tag{47}
$$

Testing (47) with tensor products of dual local bases makes
$J_Q:A^{\otimes_{\mathbb R}3}\to E$ injective. Preparation spanning
makes it onto. The same dual-basis pairings show that the $N$ effects
separate $E$: write any vector as a linear combination of $Q$-products
and extract its tensor coefficients. Since the argument for $\sigma$
used only its three pure singleton marginals, those marginals now
uniquely determine $\sigma=Q(p,q,s)$.

If $Q(p,q,s)$ has a nontrivial convex decomposition into normalized
positive states, positivity and TP of each $R_\ell$ express its pure
singleton marginal as the same convex decomposition. Extremality
forces every component to have those same three marginals. Uniqueness
just proved forces every component to equal $Q(p,q,s)$. Thus this
product is pure; canonical trace spectrality identifies it with a
primitive idempotent. In the frames above, $h_{ijk}=\rho_{ijk}$.
Equation (45) consequently gives $Q(1_A,1_A,1_A)=1_E$.
Moreover (45), (46) give $N(p_i,q_j,s_k)=h_{ijk}=Q(p_i,q_j,s_k)$.
Choose frames through any three primitives, then extend spectrally:
$N=Q$ on all real vectors. Thus (47) becomes trace-inner-product
factorization, and $J_Q$ is an onto isometry. The $Q$-products of three
primitive frames form a complete primitive frame of $E$.
Neither product purity nor a joint-effect contract was supplied as input.

### The positive unital adjoint on the actual carriers

Let $J_L:D\otimes_{\mathbb R}A\to E$ linearize $L$. Algebraically
$J_Q=J_L(J_M\otimes\mathrm{id}_A)$. Since $J_M$ and $J_Q$ are onto

$$
H(1_D)=L(M(1_A,1_A),1_A)=Q(1_A,1_A,1_A)=1_E.
$$

$\tau_D(H^*z)=\langle H(1_D),z\rangle_E=\tau_E(z)$.
For arbitrary real $e,d\in D,c\in A$, the isometry gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle e,p_{12}L(d,c)\rangle_D
 &=\langle L(e,1_A),L(d,c)\rangle_E\\
 &=\langle e,d\rangle_D\langle1_A,c\rangle_A
  =\langle e,\tau_A(c)d\rangle_D.
\end{aligned}
\tag{48}
$$

Nondegeneracy proves (40), and $L$-spanning proves uniqueness even among
all real-linear maps with that law. There is no division by
$\tau_A(1_A)=\operatorname{rank}A$ in this adjoint: inserting the
normalized unit would instead divide the desired marginal and its trace
by that rank. This is the decisive stopping point for deleting $p_{12}$;
no complex type, rank-one-preserver theorem or right carrier has entered.

The derived map supplies exactly the missing part of (24), while (41)
supplies the omitted TP condition for $p_{23}$. Conversely every model

trace condition. This identifies a redundant supplied condition without
