# Constrained states and the separate operation correspondence

Chandran, Burnell and Sondhi, arXiv:1909.09652v1, study the nearest-neighbor
Rydberg blockade constraint $n_i n_{i+1}=0$, equation (1). Section III,
equation (19), identifies occupation basis states with the Fibonacci
fusion-tree basis in each fixed boundary-condition sector by
$Z_i=1\leftrightarrow n_i=1$ and $Z_i=\tau\leftrightarrow n_i=0$.
The journal publication is Physical Review B **101**, 075104 (2020),
with the DOI given above; the cited arXiv version is the 2019 source.

This vector-space correspondence does not preserve generic operator
locality. Section III, equations (20)–(21), explains how a local Rydberg
occupation or flip can involve net fusion outcomes of extended anyon
subsets. The paper also gives the other direction: local anyonic
operators map to local Rydberg operators. These are directional
statements about the specified correspondence, not an assertion that
every operator in either model is nonlocal.

Section IV.B, equation (30), discusses the additional topological
symmetry obstruction in the $(\tau,\tau)$ boundary sector. Generic local
Rydberg operators need not commute with the total-charge projector;
appropriately tuned combinations can preserve it. Sections V–VI explain
why the Hilbert-space correspondence alone supplies neither generic
anyon dynamics nor topological protection of a Rydberg qubit.

For a length-three binary window with the two outer neighbors fixed
empty, the same nearest-neighbor exclusion has five allowed occupancy
strings. That elementary combinatorial match does not identify an
ordered FIB source tree, its high-to-low reader, or its quantities and
seams with an anyon chain. In particular, it supplies no calibrated
source-controlled probe, guard-testing instrument, phase reference,
reset, copy, or acquisition-cost correspondence. Those require a
separate mathematical or physical operation contract.
