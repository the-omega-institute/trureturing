# Pure-state ECQC

H. Iqbal, *On the CQC Conjecture: A sufficient condition and an extension*,
arXiv:2509.08286v2; Quantum Information Processing 25, 250 (2026).

The arXiv PDF, p. 6, Conjecture 3.1, §3.1, states:

> Let $\rho_{AB}$ be a bipartite state where each subsystem has prime dimension $d$.
> If both parties make $d + 1$ measurements on their individual subsystems,
> then the mutual information of the resulting random variables will have the following upper bound:

$$
I(A : B) \ge \underset{\mathcal{S} \in [d + 1]^d}{\min}
\sum_{i \in \mathcal{S}} I(M^A_i : M^B_i).
$$

The immediately following sentence fixes the counting convention:

> The elements of the set $\mathcal{S}$ are collections of $d$ mutual information
> resulting from $d + 1$ bipartite MUB measurements.

The Closing Remarks and Future Works, p. 24, ask:

> For example, the original CQC conjecture holds for all pure states, but the technique that was used
> to prove this turned out to be inapplicable for the ECQC conjecture. However, as our simulations
> for dimensions $3$ and $5$ show, there are no observed contradictions of the ECQC for pure states.
> So it would be interesting to develop a method to prove ECQC for pure states analytically,
> similar to the case of the original CQC conjecture.

The dimension-5 construction, pp. 22–23, §4.4.2, reads:

> These matrices are generated by the following special matrices, where note that,
> $M_0$ itself is the quantum Fourier matrix of order 5, and is one of the MUB bases in this dimension:

$$
D=\operatorname{diag}(1,\omega,\omega^4,\omega^4,\omega),\qquad
M_0=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}(\omega^{xa})_{x,a=0}^{4}.
$$

> Based on these two matrices $D$ and $M_0$ and remembering that identity matrix,
> which is denoted by $M_1$ in the following, is also part of the six MUBs found in this dimension,
> we generate the four remaining MUB bases:

$$
M_1=I,\quad M_2=DM_0,\quad M_3=D^2M_0,\quad M_4=D^3M_0,\quad M_5=D^4M_0.
$$

> where $\omega=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{5}}$ is the fifth root of unity.

The formal encoding uses these generators with indices in $\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z$.
The standard additive character is exactly $x\mapsto\exp(2\pi i x/5)$.
Both parties use the same indexed basis, with joint probability
$|\sum_{x,y}\overline{m_{i,a}(x)}\overline{m_{i,b}(y)}\psi(x,y)|^2$.
This convention agrees with the paper's dimension-3 isotropic calculation:
the quadratic bases give $p_{g_i g_j}=1/9$ and zero mutual information.
The existing quantum and classical information definitions use natural logarithms;
division by $\log 2$ expresses both quantities in bits, including the zero-probability convention.
