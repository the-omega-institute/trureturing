# Combinatorial Solution of the Eclectic Spin Chain

C. Ahn, L. Corcoran and M. Staudacher, arXiv:2112.04506v1; JHEP 03 (2022) 028.

Appendix A, printed p. 24, introduces the matrix of the power of the Hamiltonian between the elementary-state level spaces and states:

> “We claim that the rank of A^(k) is always maximal:”
>
> “rank(A^(k)) = min(d_{S−k}, d_S).” (A.4)

Section 3.2, printed p. 10, states:

> “As before we define W_S^{L,M} to be spanned by elementary states with this level S.”

The same page introduces Eq. (3.19) with:

> “The Hamiltonian acts on (3.16) as”

In the K = 1 static sector the wall 3 is fixed at the right. The other L−1 letters contain M−1 twos. The level counts the pairs in which a 2 precedes a 1, and the Hamiltonian changes each adjacent 21 into 12 with coefficient one. The rational elementary-state encoding addresses the rank claim for all 1 ≤ M ≤ L and all k ≤ S. The rank of this rational linear map is the rank of its integer matrix over ℚ; comparison with other characteristic-zero fields is a separate scalar-extension step.

C. Ahn and M. Staudacher, “Spectrum of the Hypereclectic Spin Chain and Pólya Counting”, arXiv:2207.02885v1; Physics Letters B, doi: 10.1016/j.physletb.2022.137533. Its introduction, printed p. 1, states:

> “They rest on a compelling and extensively checked but unfortunately still unproven non-shortening conjecture.”

The K = 1 rank assertion is distinct from the multi-wall, cyclic and eclectic-universality questions.
