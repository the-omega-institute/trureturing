# Conformal scaling of Hausdorff measure

## Source and scope

The article appears in *Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS*, volume 50,
pages 171–202. Section 1 defines conformal densities and, on printed page
174, gives Hausdorff measure as an example. Its example assumes a Borel
subset of a compact manifold with finite positive Hausdorff measure in
one Riemannian metric. Under a positive continuous change of local length
scale, the measure scales by the same factor raised to the Hausdorff
exponent. The explanation uses increasingly small metric covers.

The exponent applies to the change in lengths. If a Riemannian metric
tensor is multiplied by a factor, its associated length scale is the
square root of that factor.

## Upper-half-space application boundary

For the hyperbolic upper half-space, the local length scale relative to
Euclidean coordinates is the reciprocal of the positive height.
The corresponding density at natural Hausdorff exponent $d$ is
$\operatorname{height}^{-d}$.

The cited finite-positive-mass example supplies the classical conformal
scaling principle. An identity of ambient measures on the whole
three-dimensional half-space, including Hausdorff two-measure that is
not assumed sigma-finite, requires a separate argument. Local two-sided
metric distortion, a countable disjoint Borel cover, and an
extended-nonnegative-real squeeze provide that argument without dividing
by the mass of a set.

This application keeps the original Euclidean L2 coordinate metric and
the original native hyperbolic metric. The Hausdorff exponent varies
while the ambient dimension remains three. Applying the same
dimension-dependent normalization to both metric Hausdorff measures
preserves the density identity. A plane chart and its coordinate
Jacobian remain additional inputs to a geometric surface-area formula.
No novelty claim is made for the conformal scaling principle.
