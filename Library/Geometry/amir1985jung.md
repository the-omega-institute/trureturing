# Relative centers in finite-dimensional normed spaces

Amir defines the relative Chebyshev radius by restricting the center to a
specified set. His self-Jung constant is the supremum of twice the radius
with the center in the convex set, normalized to diameter one.
Proposition 2.12, pages 9–10, proves the bound 2n/(n+1). Thus a nonempty
compact convex set in an n-dimensional normed space has a center in that
set of radius at most n/(n+1) times its diameter.

The common-center bound for a compact subset K of the probability simplex
with m coordinates follows by taking its compact convex hull in the
affine hyperplane of total mass one. Translating this hyperplane gives
the (m−1)-dimensional zero-sum space with the restricted maximum norm.
The convex hull has the same diameter and stays inside the simplex.
The relative-center bound is therefore (m−1)/m times the diameter.
The coordinate-extrema proof gives this special case directly.

Bohnenblust's earlier ambient-center inequality is cited in Amir's
introduction: F. Bohnenblust, *Convex Regions and Projections in Minkowski
Spaces*, Annals of Mathematics 39 (1938), 301–308,
DOI 10.2307/1968786. An ambient center need not belong to the prescribed
simplex; the relative-center formulation is the relevant statement here.

The full-scale deletion-noise minimax equality additionally constructs
one common noisy observation for a family of probability sources.
That statistical formulation is not a statement of Amir's proposition.
