## Maximal cell decomposition

For a simplicial set $X$ and a nondegenerate simplex $s$, let $C_s$ be the
subcomplex of simplices $x$ for which the cell generated by $s$ is not contained
in the cell generated by $x$. The preimage of $C_s$ under the characteristic
map $\Delta^{\dim s}\to X$ is its entire boundary. That characteristic map is
injective away from the boundary, even when boundary faces are identified.
If $s$ is maximal in the order on nondegenerate simplices, its generated cell
and $C_s$ cover $X$. These facts construct the pushout

$$
X \cong C_s\mathbin{\cup}_{\partial\Delta^{\dim s}}\Delta^{\dim s}.
$$

The nondegenerate simplices of $C_s$ are exactly the nondegenerate simplices
$t$ of $X$ with $s\not\leq t$. For finite $X$, their number is strictly smaller
than that of $X$, since the inclusion misses $s$.

## Finite cell induction

Let $P$ be a proposition on simplicial sets in a fixed universe. Suppose $P$
holds for every initial simplicial set and is preserved by every pushout that
attaches a standard simplex along its boundary, provided $P$ holds both for
the preceding simplicial set and for that boundary. Then $P$ holds for every
finite simplicial set. The proof uses induction first on a finite dimension
bound and then on the number of nondegenerate simplices. The boundary has a
smaller dimension bound, and the costar has fewer nondegenerate simplices.
This applies to simplicial sets with identifications and requires no
regularity hypothesis on the boundary attaching map.

These are existing upstream results. Their cell decomposition supports later
comparisons between simplicial and singular homology; the induction theorem
alone makes no Euler-characteristic or surface-classification assertion.
