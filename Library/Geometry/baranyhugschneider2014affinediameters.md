# Affine-diameter existence

Bárány, Hug and Schneider, *Affine diameters of convex bodies*,
arXiv:1403.7158v2; Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society
**144**, 797–812, DOI 10.1090/proc12746.

Section 1 defines an affine diameter of a convex body as a segment whose
endpoints lie in distinct parallel supporting hyperplanes. Section 5,
in the paragraph beginning “Concerning the left-hand inequality in (1.4)”,
states that “any point of a convex body belongs to an affine diameter”.
It cites assertion 3.3 of V. Soltan, *Affine diameters of convex bodies—a
survey*, Expositiones Mathematicae **23** (2005), 47–63, bibliography item [9].
This note uses the existence statement explicitly reported by Bárány,
Hug and Schneider; it does not assert a separate inspection of Soltan's text.

The general-relative-position assumption in the surrounding counting
argument is not a hypothesis of this general existence fact. Smoothness,
strict convexity, symmetry and a polyhedral description are not required.
The source's convex bodies are finite dimensional. The application in the
existing ExclusionBallFaces Blueprint uses
a compact convex nontrivial neighborhood of zero in a real normed
direction space. Its centered existence inference combines pinned
compact maximization, Minkowski-gauge and separation results. The two
supporting values have opposite strict signs,
so zero lies strictly inside the resulting segment. It makes no claim about
the number of affine diameters or their uniqueness.

This source supplies the classical geometric fact. The original-state
effect normalization and whole-exclusion application belong to the
repository's corresponding derivation. No paper text or scan is redistributed.
