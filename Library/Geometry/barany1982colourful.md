# Colourful Carathéodory selection

Imre Bárány, *A generalization of Carathéodory's theorem*,
Discrete Mathematics **40** (1982), 141–152, Theorem 2.1 on printed page 141.
The sets in that theorem are arbitrary subsets of real Euclidean space.
Their convex hulls contain a common point, and the conclusion selects one
vector from every labelled set while retaining that point in the selected
convex hull. The statement imposes no finiteness, closedness, boundedness,
general position, disjointness or distinctness assumption.

The nearest-point exchange proof uses finite subsets internally, obtained
from each original convex hull membership by ordinary Carathéodory.
A nearest nonzero point in a colourful hull has a positive affine support
on its supporting hyperplane. That support is linearly independent and
uses at most d labels. A point of an omitted colour lies strictly across
the supporting level, so replacing that colour gives a strictly closer
point in the new hull. A minimum among the finitely many finite-core
colourful hulls therefore equals the common point. Translation treats an
arbitrary common point. The formal statement also includes dimension zero.
These are classical convex-geometric conclusions; no originality is claimed.
