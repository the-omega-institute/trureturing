## Reading of the statement

Substituting `x/(1-x)` into `x/(1-k*x)` gives `x/(1-(k+1)*x)`, so iterating formula (1) unrolls into
the nested square of the name. The statement to settle is that every solution of
`A(x) = x + A(x/(1-x))^2` has even coefficients at the odd indices `2n - 1` with `n > 1`.

## Scope of the recorded answer

The comment holds. For any integer power series `F`, the coefficient of `x^(2m+1)` in `F^2` is
`Σ_{i+j=2m+1} f_i f_j`; swapping `i` and `j` pairs the terms, and no term is paired with itself because
`2m+1` is odd, so the sum is twice an integer. Applying this with `F = A(x/(1-x))` and noting that `x`
contributes only at index one gives the claim. The functional equation does have a solution: the entry's
coefficient recurrence defines one, since substituting `x/(1-x)` sends the coefficient sequence to its
binomial transform `b`.
