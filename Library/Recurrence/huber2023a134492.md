# OEIS A134492

The entry is the sequence of Fibonacci numbers at indices divisible by six,
`0, 8, 144, 2584, 46368, 832040, ...`, with offset zero. Felix Huber's 2023
comment characterises the terms from the second onwards as exactly those
Fibonacci numbers which are perimeters of Pythagorean triangles.

## Reading of the statement

The comment says "a Pythagorean triple" with no qualifier, so the triples
range over all triples of positive integers `a, b, c` with `a^2 + b^2 = c^2`,
primitive or not. The three numbers are the two legs and the hypotenuse, so
their sum is the perimeter of the corresponding triangle.

Every such perimeter is even: writing a triple as `d` times a primitive one
with parameters `m > n >= 1`, `gcd(m, n) = 1`, `m - n` odd, the perimeter is
`2 d m (m + n)`. Consequently a Fibonacci number at an index not divisible by
three, being odd, is never a perimeter, and those indices agree with the
comment for trivial reasons.

## Scope of the recorded answer

The comment is false, and the recorded counterexample is the Fibonacci number
at index forty-five. That index is not a multiple of six, and the number is
the perimeter of the triangle `109441 * (3145, 2928, 4297)`, which comes from
the primitive parameters `m = 61`, `n = 24`.

It is the smallest counterexample: at indices not divisible by three the
Fibonacci numbers are odd, and at indices `3, 6, ..., 42` divisible by three
the comment holds. Further failures of the same kind occur at indices
`57, 63, 69, 75, 81`.

The counterexample lies inside the range the comment reports having checked.
Under the alternative reading that restricts the triples to primitive ones
the comment also fails inside that range, at indices `18` and `54`, where the
terms of the sequence are not primitive perimeters, and at index `81`.
