## Scope of the recorded answer

The comment holds, and for a structural reason: modulo 8 the generating function is the rational
function `(x^2 + 3x^3)/(1 - x^2)`, whose coefficients are `1` at even and `3` at odd indices from two on.
Put `R = (x^2 + 3x^3)/(1 - x^2)`, `D = 1 - x^2` and `N = x - x^2 - 4x^3`, so that `x - R = N/D`. Then
`R(x - R) = x^2 + x*R` modulo 8 reduces to the polynomial congruence
`N^2*D + 3*N^3 ≡ (x^2*D + x^3 + 3x^4)*(D^2 - N^2) (mod 8)`, whose two sides differ by a polynomial with
every coefficient divisible by 8; `D` and `D^2 - N^2` have constant term one and are units. The equation
determines its solution uniquely over any commutative ring: the map
`T(f) = x^2 + x*f - (f(x - f) - f)` sends two series of order at least two that agree below degree `d` to
series agreeing below degree `d + 1`. Hence the reduction of the integer solution is `R`. The same
contraction, iterated from zero over the integers, produces the solution.
