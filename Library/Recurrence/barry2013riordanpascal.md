## Source statement

Section 1, printed page 1:

> In this note, we investigate some properties of the two-parameter generalized
> Pascal triangle M^{(m)}(a, b) given by the Riordan array
> M^{(m)}(a, b) = ( 1/(1 − ax) , x(1 + bx)/(1 − ax)^m ).

Section 6, printed page 19:

> We can regard the matrix M^(m)(a, b) as the coefficient array of a family of
> polynomials P_n(x; m, a, b). We have
> P_n(x; m, a, b) = Σ_{k=0}^{n} T^{(m)}_{n,k}(a, b) x^k. For each n, the
> Christoffel-Darboux quotient
> ( P_{n+1}(x; m, a, b)P_n(y; m, a, b) −
> P_n(x; m, a, b)P_{n+1}(y; m, a, b) ) / (x − y)
> is the bi-variate generator of a (n + 1) x (n + 1) matrix D_n(m, a, b).

Later on printed page 19:

> It is interesting to compare the matrices defined by the quotient above with
> those defined by Σ_{j=0}^{n} P_j(x; m, a, b)P_j(y; m, a, b). In the case
> of monic orthogonal polynomials, such expressions lead to the same matrices.
> However, M^{(m)}(a, b) is not in general the coefficient array of a family of
> monic orthogonal polynomials, and hence the two family of matrices will in
> general differ. In the case m = 2, a = b = 1, we can conjecture the following.

Conjecture 32, printed page 20:

> **Conjecture 32.** The matrix D̃_n(2, 1, 1) with generating function
> Σ_{k=0}^{n} P_j(x; 2, 1, 1)P_j(y; 2, 1, 1) is given by
> M_n^{(2)}(a, b)^t M_n^{(2)}(a, b). We then have
> |D̃_n(2, 1, 1)| = 1 for n ≥ 0. In the above the notation M_n denotes the
> matrix formed from the first (n + 1) rows and columns of M.

Section 2 defines a Riordan array `(g,f)` by saying that its `i`-th column is
generated by `g(x)f(x)^i`, with the first column indexed by zero. Thus the entry
`T^{(m)}_{n,k}(a,b)` is the coefficient of `x^n` in `g(x)f(x)^k`.

## Reading and formal scope

The page-20 lower summation index is printed as `k=0`, while the summand uses
`P_j`. The preceding page prints the same kernel with `j=0`, so the formal sum
uses `j=0,...,n`. The right side of Conjecture 32 retains generic `(a,b)` in its
typography, but the immediately preceding sentence and the left side both fix
`m=2` and `a=b=1`; the formal matrix therefore uses `(a,b)=(1,1)`. The generic
reading already fails at `n=1`.

The phrase "with generating function" uses the coefficient-array convention
stated immediately above for the Christoffel-Darboux quotient: matrix entry
`(i,k)` is the coefficient of `x^i y^k`. The formal bivariate polynomial is a
polynomial in `x` whose coefficients are polynomials in `y`. Finally,
`T_{n,k}` is the coefficient of `g f^k`, `P_n` is the row polynomial, and `M_n`
is the first `n+1` rows and columns. The result concerns every natural `n`,
including zero, and does not assert Conjecture 31.

The first six rows printed on page 2 are
`[1]`, `[1,1]`, `[1,4,1]`, `[1,9,7,1]`, `[1,16,26,10,1]`, and
`[1,25,70,52,13,1]`; they agree with OEIS A114188.
