# Causal Classification of Spatiotemporal Quantum Correlations

M. Song, V. Narasimhachar, B. Regula, T. J. Elliott and M. Gu, arXiv:2306.09336v2 (2024-09-23); Phys. Rev. Lett. 133, 110202 (2024). Quotations are from the v2 TeX source.

Spatial and temporal compatibility:

> PDOs that lie outside of $\calS$ are referred to as \emph{aspatial}, and those that lie outside of $\calT$ are referred to as \emph{atemporal}.

The atemporality measure:

> Consider first a \emph{forward atemporality} measure $\overrightarrow{f}$ that is zero if and only if $\mathbf{R}_{AB}$ has statistics consistent with a temporal distribution mechanism from $A$ to $B$; and a \emph{reverse atemporality} measure $\overleftarrow{f}$ that is zero if and only if $\mathbf{R}_{AB}$ has statistics consistent with a temporal distribution mechanism from $B$ to $A$.

Temporal PDOs (Eq. (3)):

> \R_{AB} = \left(\id_A \ten \overrightarrow{\Lambda} \right) \K_{AB}

> where $\K_{AB} \equiv \left\{ \brho_A \ten \frac{\I_B}{2}, \S_{AB} \right\}$ with $\brho_A \equiv \tr_B \R_{AB}$ being the first marginal, $\{\cdot,\cdot\}$ the anti-commutator, $\I$ the identity operator, and $\S$ the swap operator.

The question:

> Some entangled states are not atemporal, so is atemporality a strictly stronger notion of quantum correlations? And if so, is it guaranteed by steering or Bell non-locality~\cite{wiseman2007steering}?
