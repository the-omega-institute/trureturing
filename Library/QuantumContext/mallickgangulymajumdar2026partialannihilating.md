# On the characterization of partially entanglement breaking and annihilating channels

B. Mallick, N. Ganguly and A. S. Majumdar, arXiv:2411.19315v2 (v1 28 November 2024); J. Math. Phys. 67(8) (2026), 082201. Quotations are from the v2 TeX source.

Schmidt number (Section II):

> A bipartite density matrix $\rho$ has Schmidt number $r$, if in every possible decomposition of $\rho$ into pure states, i.e. $\rho= \sum_{k} p_k {\ket{{\psi}_k}} {\bra{{\psi}_k}}$, with $p_k \ge 0$, at least one of the pure states ${\ket{{\psi}_k}}$ has Schmidt rank $r$.

The sentence continues with the existence of a decomposition whose pure states all have Schmidt rank at most $r$.

The remark on partially entanglement breaking channels (Section III):

> the set of \(r\)-partially entanglement breaking channels is, in general, not closed under tensor products for \(r>1\).

Local and non-local channels (Section IV):

> A channel $\mathcal{E}$ is said to be local if it can be expressed as a tensor product of individual channels, i.e. $\mathcal{E} = {\mathcal{E}}_1 \otimes {\mathcal{E}}_2$. If a quantum channel can not be written in the above form, then it is said to be a non-local channel.

> Let, $\mathcal{E}$ be a quantum channel acting on subsystem B. We call $\mathcal{E}$ to be a non-local $r$-partially entanglement annihilating channel if $\mathcal{E}$ can not be written as ${\mathcal{E}}_1 \otimes {\mathcal{E}}_2$ and SN$({\mathcal{E}(\rho_{B})}) \le r$, for all $\rho_{B} \in \mathcal{D(H_B)}$, where, $r < d$ and $B$ is a bipartite system $\in \mathcal{D}({{\mathbf{C}}^d \otimes  {\mathbf{C}}^d})$.

The open question (Conclusion):

> We have also shown that $r$-partially entanglement breaking channels are not closed under tensor products, motivating the investigation of whether a similar property holds for $r$-partially entanglement annihilating channels.

The source also contains, commented out, a draft proposal that the tensor product "may not be non-local r-PEAT", followed by an informal proof sketch through the Schmidt number of a tensor product of pure states and the remark "[doubt in writing]". Neither is part of the published article.
