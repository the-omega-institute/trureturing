## Verified locator

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adq4467

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06234

The arXiv v4 PDF, printed page 22, Supplementary Material V, states:

> We have shown this explicitly for odd d ≤ 17 and conjecture it to hold for odd d in general.

Equations (V3) and (V4) assert, respectively,

$$
W_d\leq 3+\frac{9D_{\mathrm{GME}}(\rho)}d,
\qquad
F_{\mathrm{ghz}}(\rho)\geq\frac{W_d-3}{9}.
$$

Equation (V5) is the sufficient operator inequality

$$
\mathcal W_d\preceq9|\mathrm{ghz}_{3,d}\rangle\langle\mathrm{ghz}_{3,d}|+3\mathbf1.
$$

The bases and the nine triples are specified in (V1)–(V2) on the same page.
The triples are retained as nine labelled settings; their multiplicities also
specify the one-dimensional case. The three-party GHZ vector is equation (5).
Definition 1, printed page 3, states: “For an arbitrary n-partite state ρ, its GME-dimension is” followed by equation (2), the
minimum, over mixtures across bipartitions, of the largest Schmidt number.
The Schmidt number is the minimum possible largest pure-state Schmidt rank
in a convex decomposition. The matrix rank of the coefficient array is the
pure-state Schmidt rank.

The repository proves (V3) and (V4) for every odd dimension, including composite
dimensions, using the quadratic-form version of (V5). It does not determine
the exact non-GHZ spectrum or prove an even-dimensional analogue.

Printed page 3 also states:

> The Schmidt rank is the rank of the reduced density matrix, which is necessarily equal to one for a product state but larger for pure entangled states (it generalizes to the Schmidt number for mixed states [33]).
