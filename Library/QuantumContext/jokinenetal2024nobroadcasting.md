# No-Broadcasting Characterizes Operational Contextuality

P. Jokinen, M. Weilenmann, M. Plávala, J.-P. Pellonpää, J. Kiukas and R. Uola, arXiv:2406.07305v1 (the only version); Phys. Rev. Lett. 133(24) (2024), 240201. Quotations are from the v1 TeX source.
- The macros `\hT`, `\hA`, `\hS`, `\hL`, `\lh`, `\hi`, `\N` and `\btn{n}` are expanded to $\mathcal T$, $\mathcal A$, $\mathcal S$, $\mathcal L$, $\mathcal{L(H)}$, $\mathcal H$, $\mathbb N$ and $1\mapsto n$.
- `\tr{X}` is written $\operatorname{tr}(X)$, and citation commands are omitted.

Subtheories and non-contextuality:

> We are here interested in theories that consist of some convex subset of quantum states and a convex set of effects $E$, i.e. operators with $0 \leq E\leq I$, that includes the identity operator and yes-no questions, i.e., if an effect $E$ is part of the theory, then also $I-E$ is. Such a theory is called measurement and preparation non-contextual if $\operatorname{tr}(\rho E)=\sum_{\lambda} \operatorname{tr}(\rho \tilde G_{\lambda})\operatorname{tr}(\tilde\sigma_{\lambda}E)$ for all states $\rho$ and effects $E$ of the theory. Here $\{\tilde G_\lambda\}_\lambda$ and $\{\tilde \sigma_\lambda\}_\lambda$ satisfy $\operatorname{tr}(\rho \tilde G_{\lambda}) \geq 0$ and $\sum_\lambda \operatorname{tr}(\rho \tilde G_{\lambda}) = 1$ for all states of the theory, and $\operatorname{tr}(\tilde\sigma_{\lambda} E) \geq 0$ and $\operatorname{tr}(\tilde\sigma_{\lambda}) = 1$ for all $\lambda$ and all effects $E$ of the theory.

Pseudo-broadcasting:

> we say that $(\mathcal T,\mathcal A_1,\dots,\mathcal A_n)$ is $1 \mapsto n$\emph{-pseudo-broadcastable} if there is a trace-preserving map $\Xi: \mathcal{L(H)} \to \mathcal L(\mathcal H^{\otimes n})$ such that for all $i \in \{1,\dots, n\}$, $\rho \in \mathcal T$, and $\{A_{i,k}\}_k \in \mathcal A_i$ it holds that $\operatorname{tr}(A_{i,k} \operatorname{tr}_{i^c} \Xi(\rho)) = \operatorname{tr}(A_{i,k}\rho)$, $\operatorname{tr}(\Xi(\rho) \otimes_{i=1}^n A_{i,k}) \geq 0$.

Theorem 5 (Theorem `thmgpt`):

> A subtheory of quantum theory characterized by the allowed states $\mathcal T$ and allowed measurements $\mathcal A$ is measurement and preparation non-contextual if and only if $(\mathcal T, \mathcal A)$ is $1 \mapsto n$-pseudo-broadcastable for all $n \in \mathbb N$.

The conjecture, in the main text:

> This is likely not a coincidence since the scenario is generated by $3$ measurements and we conjecture that for a scenario generated by $k$ measurements $1 \mapsto k$-pseudobroadcasting coincides with non-contextuality.

and in Appendix D:

> One can also see that $\mathcal S(\mathcal A)$ is a Cartesian product of $k$ simplexes if $\mathcal A$ is generated by $k$ independent measurements. This leads to a conjecture that if $\mathcal A$ is generated by $k$ measurements, then $1 \mapsto k$-pseudobroadcasting is equivalent to non-contextuality. This is only a conjecture since even if $\mathcal A$ is generated by $k$ measurements, $\mathcal S(\mathcal A)$ in general is only a subset of a Cartesian product of $k$ simplexes. We leave resolving this issue and proving or disproving the conjecture for future work.
