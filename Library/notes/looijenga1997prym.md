# Prym blocks and global factorization

## Verified locator

Eduard Looijenga, *Prym representations of mapping class groups*,
*Geometriae Dedicata* 64 (1997), 69-83.
DOI: 10.1023/A:1004909416648.
https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1004909416648

## Source scope

Finite abelian cover homology supplies arithmetic Prym representations with
independence and almost-surjectivity properties on suitable finite-index
mapping-class subgroups. The cyclic-cover image result is also stated in
Bader, *Prym representations of the handlebody group*, Theorem 1.5,
DOI 10.1007/s10711-024-00911-5. Bader's handlebody theorem does not substitute
for a theorem about the whole mapping-class group.

## Correspondence to the theory

Sections 33-39 of SURFACE_CONGRUENCE_OBSERVERS fix
N2=ker(pi_g -> H1(Sigma_g,F2)). For odd l, the homology kernel of
pi_g/[N2,N2]N2^l splits into a 2g-dimensional trivial block and
4^g-1 sign blocks of dimension 2g-2. Projectivization removes lift ambiguity.

For a separating curve of type (h,g-h), the canonical lift has square-zero
homology increment of rank (4^h-1)(4^(g-h)-1) and exact outer order l.
In genus at least three, separating twists distinguish the sign-block
kernels on the Johnson kernel. Their full joint image and the symplectic
permutation action yield the specified congruence quotient
PSp(2g-2,F_l)^(4^g-1) semidirect Sp(2g,F2).

The volume derives concrete subgroups satisfying the curve-orbit inclusion
for those specified inputs. For a fixed nonabelian finite simple quotient of
the whole mapping-class group, factorization through this action is equivalent
to factorization through ordinary homology modulo 2l. Normality and transitive
block permutation prevent selection of one Prym factor as a global quotient.

## Boundaries

The rank formula concerns the canonical lift, not every deck-modified lift.
The full-product and global simple-quotient results require g>=3 and odd l.
Their PSp simplicity input is classical; rank-one and even-field exceptions
are outside this domain.

Specified positive congruence examples do not handle arbitrary prescribed
finite-index subgroups. Prime-power refinements over the same mod-two cover
retain the stated obstruction for a fixed nonhomological simple target;
they do not exclude other finite characteristic quotients or other covers.
Cross-characteristic semisimple block formulas require l not dividing the
base-group order and cannot be replaced by defining-characteristic formulas.
