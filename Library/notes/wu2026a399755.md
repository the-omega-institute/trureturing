# Wu's A399755 conjecture

The saved entry attributes A399755 to Chai Wah Wu, September 10, 2026.
Its exact name is:

> Numbers k such that the generalized Pell equation x^2-(k^2+1)*(y^2+1) = 0 has more than one fundamental solution in (x, y).

Its named conjecture is:

> Conjecture: A399491(k) < 4*k^3+3*k if and only if k is a term of this sequence.

The companion A399491 name is:

> The smallest integer m > n such that (n^2+1)*(m^2+1) is a perfect square.

A399491 is attributed to Zhining Yang, August 30, 2026. Its September 10
comment by Chai Wah Wu states the one-class implication for equality with
the cubic bound and conjectures the converse. The companion defines the
least partner, whereas A399755 tests membership of k in the set of
parameters with multiple generalized solution classes. It is not a claim
about the kth enumerated value of A399755.

## Mathematical scope

For natural k>0, write D=(k:Int)^2+1. The source equation is
x^2-D*y^2=D on arbitrary integral pairs. The class relation is multiplication
by an integral norm-one unit: for integer u,v with u^2-D*v^2=1, the pair
(r,s) is sent to (r*u+D*s*v, r*v+s*u). This is the relation documented by
Robertson in `D5/L/robertson2004generalizedpell`, pages 12-14. It includes
simultaneous negation via (-1,0). Neither positive-only solutions,
primitive solutions, squarefree D, nor fractional maximal-order units are
part of this model.

More than one fundamental solution means more than one generalized
solution class, expressed by two inequivalent integral norm-D solutions.
This differs from the fundamental positive solution of the norm-one
equation. The entry's diop_DN program and the versioned SymPy docstring's
one-tuple-per-class convention support this terminology; software execution
is not trusted mathematical evidence.

The exact target combines existence of a natural m with
k<m, IsSquare((k^2+1)*(m^2+1)), and the minimum condition
that every natural n>k satisfying the same square predicate has m<=n.
For that least m it asserts the strict cubic equivalence to two inequivalent
integral solutions. The quantifier is all k>0. The source conjecture remains
a conjecture in this citation; its statements and programs are not premises
proving that target. The full model and target are in
`Problems/oeis-a399755-pell-class-successor.md`.
