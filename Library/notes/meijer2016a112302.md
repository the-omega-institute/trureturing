# Meijer's Lerch recurrence conjecture

The exact anchor is Johannes W. Meijer's COMMENTS paragraph of June 27,
2016 in OEIS A112302:

> With Phi(z, p, q) the Lerch transcendent, define LP(n) = (1/n) * sum(Phi(1/2, n-k, 1) * LP(k), k=0..n-1), with LP(0) = 1. Conjecture: Lim_{n -> infinity} LP(n) = A112302.

The citation year is the comment's date. A112302 names the real Somos
constant through its decimal expansion; its individual digits are not the
limit target. Revision 121, dated April 20, 2026 at 00:17:02, retains the
conjecture wording without changing its 2016 date.

The entry's FORMULA section credits Jonathan Sondow (April 7, 2013) for
product_{n>=1}n^{1/2^n}, and Jwalin Bhatt (April 2, 2025) for
exp(sum_{n>=2}log(n)/2^n). These are entry-level contribution credits,
not claims of earliest discovery. SomosConstant reindexes the logarithmic
series by n=j+1 and includes the zero term log(1).

D5/L/weisstein2026lerch fixes the classical Lerch normalization from
Weisstein's Lerch Transcendent, equations (1) and (6):
Phi(1/2,s,1)=2 Li_s(1/2).
D5/L/coffey2015somosseries records the earlier known identity
sum_{k>=1}(2 Li_k(1/2)-1)/k=log(sigma_2), from Proposition 5(b),
equation (1.25) of its original preprint. The identity evaluates the
constant in the LP proof; no LP-convergence theorem is attributed to Coffey.

MathWorld's Somos article, equations (6) and (10), and section 3 of
Sondow--Hadjicostas give the product and logarithmic sum. Guillera--Sondow's
Somos examples give integral/product representations. Their nonlinear
quadratic recurrence g_n=n*g_(n-1)^2 is distinct from LP; these background
identities alone do not establish Meijer's limit.
