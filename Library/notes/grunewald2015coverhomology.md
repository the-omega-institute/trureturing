# Cover homology and virtual arithmetic quotients

## Source scope

The paper associates arithmetic groups to rational irreducible
representations of finite groups. When the finite group can be generated by
fewer than g elements, the associated arithmetic group is a virtual quotient
of Mod(Sigma_g). Finite-index subgroups on both sides are part of the scope;
a prescribed quotient of the whole mapping-class group does not follow.

## Correspondence to the theory

Sections 27-32 of SURFACE_CONGRUENCE_OBSERVERS fix a characteristic
finite-index surface subgroup N. For centerless S=pi/N and trivial induced
outer action, removing the unique inner base action gives a corrected
representation Psi on H1(N,F_l). If C is the principal outer kernel for
pi/[N,N]N^l and K=ker(Psi), then K/C embeds in H1(S,H1(N,F_l)).
Equality holds when l does not divide |S|. A specified nonabelian finite simple
target factors through the outer action exactly when it factors through Psi.

The relative Frattini argument identifies an l-group kernel in the outer
action on Q/Phi(P), for a normal l-subgroup P of a finite Q. Thus deeper
l-group refinements over the same N have the same factorization ability toward
that simple target as the first mod-l homology layer. Under the stated base
hypotheses, a finite joint family at several primes succeeds only if one
first homology layer succeeds. These particular implications are derived in
the volume, not attributed to a numbered theorem of the cited paper.

## Boundaries

A virtual arithmetic image does not identify the kernel of a separately
specified quantum representation. The finite factorization test must use
paired images of the same mapping-class generators in the fixed target and
in Psi. It does not itself produce a successful cover or prime.

The conclusions retain the centerless, outer-trivial base hypotheses and the
same source homomorphism throughout. They neither equate all outer kernels
nor assert that arbitrary finite quotients admit these refinements, and they
do not resolve the general curve-orbit congruence conjecture.
