# Eisenstein congruences and the weight boundary of the WSS application

## Primary record

Dan Fretwell and Jenny Roberts, *Hilbert modular Eisenstein congruences of
local origin*, Journal of Number Theory 280 (March 2026), 861-896,
DOI 10.1016/j.jnt.2025.09.009.
The corresponding arXiv record is 2411.06987v2, revised March 3, 2026.

https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2025.09.009
https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06987

The primary publisher and arXiv abstracts state existence of Eisenstein
congruences over arbitrary totally real fields in parallel weight k>=3,
between eigenforms at level mp and Eisenstein series at level m, with p a
prime ideal not dividing m. The moduli come from Hecke L-values and Euler
factors; the work also treats a newform question and allows nontrivial
character. This note uses those abstract-level scope statements and the
publication metadata. It does not assert that all technical hypotheses or
proofs of the full paper have been checked here, and cites no uninspected
theorem number as an input.

## Precise transfer boundary

`Problems/wall-sun-sun-golden-unit-lift.md`, CTG.4-CTG.7, constructs an
Eisenstein-type coefficient system b(n)=sum_(d|n)chi_5(d) and computes its
exact congruence module against the COMPLETE finite-order golden dihedral
tower. The family consists of weight-zero Maass forms with parameter zero
and even Artin representations. Neither parallel holomorphic weight at
least three nor the other hypotheses of a holomorphic congruence-existence
theorem follow from that construction.

Accordingly, CTG proves its module and explicit trace-combination statements
by the polynomial relation Q_k(2)=p^k and the actual integral coefficient
lattice. It does not apply this paper to establish existence of the golden
character. A nonzero abstract congruence ring alone is not a new WSS prime.

This reference supplies a relevant frontier comparison for how independent
L-value or local-factor information can support a congruence existence
theorem in its proper category. Any transfer to the fixed-golden Maass
problem requires a separate theorem retaining its weight, parity, conductor
and field conditions. No such transfer theorem is claimed in this note.
