# The original whole positive-pair question

> In Corollary 1 we showed that R_n^m(x,y) tends to zero under a sufficient
> condition. Does R_n^m(x,y) tend to zero hold for every x,y > 0?

Set a=(x+y)/2 and d=(x-y)/2. The ratio is
Gamma(1+x)Gamma(1+y)/Gamma(1+a)^2, and the finite expansion is the sum,
for 1 <= k < m, of d^(2k) zeta(2k,1+a)/k. The Hurwitz function here is
the real series sum over j >= 0 of (j+b)^(-s), with its actual real shift b.
The remainder is the difference between the logarithm of that ratio and
this finite expansion. Corollary 1 explicitly takes m to infinity.

For every positive pair, c_n=d^2/(n+1+a)^2 lies in [0,1). The real Euler
limit identifies the logarithm of the ratio with the sum of -log(1-c_n).
The nonnegative logarithmic series can then be summed in either order.
This gives the coefficient series on the entire positive quadrant,
including positive integer Gamma arguments. On the diagonal all remainders
are zero. This is a pointwise convergence statement.

The source's Taylor points depend on the expansion order and must satisfy
the exact Taylor remainder equality. An arbitrary point in (0,c_n) does
not specify that remainder. On the diagonal that interval is empty;
equation (4) still determines the zero remainder. No Taylor-point family
is assumed by the formal statement.

The Euler limit, Gamma positivity, logarithmic Taylor series and p-series
comparison are classical background. Merkle, *Gurland's ratio for the
gamma function*, DOI 10.1016/j.camwa.2004.01.016, is relevant older work.
Its full-text overlap remains unverified. The versioned source and bounded
bibliographic searches do not establish priority or absence of an earlier
identity. Open Problems 1 and 2 are one family; Open Problem 3 remains open.
