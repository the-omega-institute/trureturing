## Classical theorem and fixed-parameter consequence

Let $\alpha_i$ be algebraic numbers different from zero and one,
choose fixed logarithm branches, and let $b_i\in\mathbb Z$.
For the nonzero form $\Lambda=\sum_i b_i\log\alpha_i$, the theorem
gives

$$
\log|\Lambda|>-C(n,d)
\prod_{i=1}^n h'(\alpha_i)\,h'(L),
$$

where $d$ is the degree of the field generated by the $\alpha_i$
and $L$ is the coefficient linear form. The modified heights and
explicit $C(n,d)$ are defined on those pages. For fixed algebraic
numbers and $B\ge\max(e,|b_1|,\ldots,|b_n|)$, the following
height estimate $h'(L)\le\log B$ yields a polynomial lower bound
$|\Lambda|>B^{-C_*}$ with a fixed $C_*>0$.
The nonzero-form hypothesis remains necessary; multiplicative
independence is not an additional assumption of this stated theorem.

## Correspondence with the parity amplitude

For an algebraic candidate $r\in(0,1)$ in section 68 of the
[spectral-boundary volume](../../docs/develop/theory/PARITY_HIDDEN_ARROW_SPECTRAL_BOUNDARY.md),
choose $\alpha_1=1+r$, $\alpha_2=1-r$ with real natural logarithms,
and coefficients $Q_n,P_n$. The form is

$$
\Lambda_n=Q_n\log(1+r)+P_n\log(1-r)
=(-\log(1-r))Q_n\bigl(\alpha-P_n/Q_n\bigr)>0.
$$

Positivity comes from the original decimal tail, not an assumed
absence of multiplicative relations. Since $0<P_n<Q_n$, the
coefficient height grows like $\log Q_n$, while the tail in the
[Liouville note](bugeaud2004rationalapproximation.md) is bounded by
$2\,10^{-Q_n^5}$. Those scales contradict the fixed polynomial
lower bound and exclude an algebraic $r$. This is a classical
logarithmic-form application, not a new transcendence theorem.

The constants are fixed only after fixing the hypothetical algebraic
numbers. This argument supplies no uniform Diophantine separation
for a growing family of posterior centers. It does not prove the
conditional modulo-one probability estimate.
