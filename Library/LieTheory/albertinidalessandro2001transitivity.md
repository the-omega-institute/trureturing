# Sphere transitivity in the defining complex representation

The primary text is [arXiv:quant-ph/0106128v2](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0106128v2),
§4, Theorem 4 and Lemmas 4.1–4.3. Lemma 4.2 and its Appendix proof
give unitary conjugacy of the compact symplectic embedding. The year
and arXiv DOI identify the initial publication; the mathematical
locators refer specifically to v2.

The group assertion used here concerns a connected unitary subgroup
acting transitively on the full complex unit sphere in its actual
defining representation. Its Lie algebra is $\mathfrak{su}(n)$ with
or without the scalar $iI$, or, when $n=2m$ is even,
$\mathfrak{sp}(m)$ with or without $iI$. The source proves the
unitary representation restriction, not merely a list of abstract
Lie algebras of groups acting on a real sphere. In particular its
Lemma 4.2 identifies the symplectic alternative by an actual unitary
change of basis.

The exact consumers are [VIII, Lemma 113.1, Proposition 113.2 and
Theorem 114.1](../../docs/develop/theory/QUANTUM_ORIGIN_BILATERAL_POSITIVE_RESET_RIGIDITY_AND_SCALE_OBSTRUCTION_VIII.md).
There a compact projectively transitive $G\le U(n)$ is enlarged to
$M=\mathbb T G$. Phases make the action transitive on vectors, and a
finite-component orbit argument proves that $M^0$ is sphere transitive.
This $M^0$ is compact, connected and effective in the actual defining
complex representation. It contains the scalar circle, so its Lie
algebra contains $iI$. The source classification and uniqueness of
the connected matrix subgroup with a given Lie algebra therefore give

$$
M^0=U(n)\quad\text{or}\quad
M^0=\mathbb T\operatorname{Sp}(m),\qquad n=2m,
$$

after unitary conjugacy. The second expression is the central-product
image of phases and the standard $\operatorname{Sp}(m)$, not a
faithful direct product. This classifies $M^0$, not arbitrary $G$.
The coincidence $SU(2)=\operatorname{Sp}(1)$ is harmless; the consumer
already settles all $2\le n\le5$ by its sign-rank obstruction. Its
remaining symplectic stabilizer is the full embedded
$\operatorname{Sp}(m-1)$ on the quaternionic orthogonal complement
of a fixed quaternionic line, with $n\ge6$.

Only this mature mathematical classification and its representation
statement are reused. The consumer does not infer controllability of
original gate words, Lie-algebra generation by their generators,
availability of full unitary or symplectic controls, or a physical
Schrödinger equation. Enlarging a mathematical symmetry group by
phases supplies no native operation. No exceptional real-representation
alternative is substituted for the actual complex unitary theorem.
