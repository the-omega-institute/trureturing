# Main eigenvalues and canonical double covers

## Verified locator

DOI: 10.7151/dmgt.2386

Source: https://arxiv.org/html/1906.05790v1#S5.Thmlemma8

Question 5.8 of arXiv:1906.05790v1 asks:

> Let G and H be two graphs with CDC(G) ≃ CDC(H). Do G and H have the same main eigenvalues?

The journal version credits Irene Sciriha and Luke Collins and gives the
same question as Question 16, p. 522 (PDF p. 16). The following Remark 17
reports a check on at most eight vertices.

## Definitions and scope

The source abstract defines a main eigenvalue using a real adjacency
eigenvector that is nonorthogonal to the all-ones vector. This inner product
is its coordinate sum. The canonical double cover is the direct product
with the complete graph on two vertices. The disjoint sum representation
labels the two copies by the two summand injections, with a cross-layer edge
exactly when the corresponding base pair is adjacent.

The question compares sets of main eigenvalues under an arbitrary graph
isomorphism of the covers. The finite graph setting includes disconnected
graphs and allows zero as a main eigenvalue. An empty graph has no vector
with a nonzero coordinate sum.

## Related literature

Mizzi, *arXiv:2603.27559v4*, §7, describes the same question as still open.
Teranishi's equitable quotient result gives the general main-spectrum
method. The two vertices covering each base vertex form a cell of an
equitable partition whose quotient is the base graph. See Yan and Wang,
arXiv:2201.01101v1, Lemma 3.
