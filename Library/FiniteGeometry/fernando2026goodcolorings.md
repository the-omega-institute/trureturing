# Good colorings of doubled triangles

## Verified locator

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2611.12065
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12065v1#Thmquestionx2
- Version and location: arXiv:2611.12065v1, second concluding question in §6.

The question asks if the two-sided coloring is the only good coloring with
exactly three times the side length in folds, up to symmetries of the
triangulation and a global color swap.

## Definitions and related results

Definition 2.1 counts black and white incident face corners at each vertex
and requires their difference to be divisible by three. A fold is an edge
with incident faces of different colors. Section 2.3 identifies the
triangulation with two copies of the triangular subdivision glued along
their boundaries. Proposition 3.3 gives the two-sided coloring and counts
its boundary folds. Sections 4.1–4.3 prove the lower bound of three times
the side length using three families of rings and the crossing edges they
share.
