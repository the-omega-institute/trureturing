Chapter 7, Question 7.0.2, printed p. 104 and PDF page 112, asks whether one can find a necessary and sufficient condition on a bipartite graph G for T(G) to contain U₂,₄ as a minor. The paragraph immediately following the question explains why ordinary subgraph containment of a minimal presentation does not give such a test in general. The question and that paragraph agree in arXiv v1 and the York University dissertation. The dissertation title page gives May 2026. [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02586v1#page=112) · [YorkSpace record](https://hdl.handle.net/10315/44283) · [YorkSpace PDF](https://yorkspace.library.yorku.ca/bitstreams/f62eb6b1-5823-4769-b8d9-c3052ce3e737/download)

Lao's §2.2.1 identifies the designated side G_N with the whole matroid ground and G_M with the indexed presentation members. Distinct presentation indices may have identical neighborhoods. An isolated vertex of G_N remains a loop on the ground. Theorem 4.1.8 gives a sufficient pattern with private neighbors, and Figures 4.2–4.3 show the limits of subgraph containment. Figure 4.7 shows that adding edges can change whether the minor is present. Theorem 4.1.13 credits D. J. A. Welsh, *Matroid Theory* (Academic Press, 1976), p. 169, for the abstract characterization by a rank-(r−2) flat lying in at least four copoints. The page attribution follows Lao's quotation and restatement. [Lao, Chapter 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02586v1#Ch4)

Edmonds and Fulkerson established the matroid structure of partial transversals. Hall's representative theorem supplies the deficiency argument on the given presentation. Bonin and Savitsky's Theorem 3.3 gives the graph rank formula, which supplies an alternative way to find the deficient subset once the rank bound is known. Bonin's transversal matroid notes, §2.1 and Theorem 2.6 with its corollaries, and Lao's Proposition 5.4.2 and Lemma 6.2.1 give related neighborhood results for bases, circuits and unions of circuits. [Edmonds–Fulkerson](https://doi.org/10.6028/jres.069B.016) · [Hall](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-10.37.26) · [Bonin–Savitsky](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2015.09.055) · [Bonin's notes](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/blogs.gwu.edu/dist/3/152/files/2016/04/TransversalNotes-2ltphpb.pdf)

Tutte's binary excluded-minor criterion and the binary transversal classification address the underlying matroid. Sali–Simonyi, Theorems 5–6, discuss forest presentations and the graphic characterization, and Hliněný, §7, uses a series-minor characterization for a recognition result. These comparisons concern the matroid or existence of a different presentation. The criterion for Lao's question uses the full neighborhood in the supplied graph. The primary text of de Sousa–Welsh (1972), pp. 55–59, remains unverified. [Tutte](https://doi.org/10.6028/jres.069B.001) · [Sali–Simonyi](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(97)00218-5) · [Hliněný](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-45138-9_41) · [de Sousa–Welsh](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-247X(72)90030-3)

José, §9, uses the supplied graph for fundamentalness, and Toft, Theorems 1.1–1.3, uses an auxiliary graph to test transversality after a specified one-element contraction. The exact result here concerns arbitrary native minors of the supplied presentation and asks for a matching onto the common neighborhood for each pair of surviving vertices.
