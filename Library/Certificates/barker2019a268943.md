OFFSET (%O): 1,1. Each repetition is an adjacent equal pair; overlapping
pairs are retained. The example (6,4,6,5,4) permits nonadjacent recurrences.
Successive equality-event labels advance modulo eight. The empty event list
and a singleton event list are permitted. Lean positions are zero based;
source position i corresponds to Lean position i-1. The count is the cardinality
of the literal subtype of Fin n → Fin 8, including the unique empty word at
n=0. The source generating series starts at n=1 and has constant coefficient zero.
Square roots and the radical identity are over the reals.

## Counting argument

Prepending a letter partitions words into three states: no equality event;
a first event that is the cyclic successor of the first letter; and the remaining
words with an event. Their counts U, A, B start at (8,0,0) and transform as
(U,A,B) ↦ (7U,B,U+8A+6B). Their total counts satisfy
c(n+4)+34c(n+2)+56c(n+1)=13c(n+3), with c(1)=8, c(2)=64, c(3)=504.
Coefficientwise power-series identities give the rational formula.
Recurrence uniqueness and the roots 7, 3−√17, 3+√17 give the radical formula.

## Regular-relation manuscript

Haobo Ma and Wenlin Zhang, *Barker and Hardin's cyclic-repetition formulas
and an extension of Fried's enumeration*, revision 2, is the source of the
regular incoming relation statement. Haobo Ma's affiliation is ChronoAI
Pte Ltd, Singapore. Wenlin Zhang's affiliations are the National University
of Singapore and ChronoAI Pte Ltd, Singapore. The manuscript is licensed
CC BY 4.0. Its source is trureturing-outreach release 445035226, `note.tex`,
18249 bytes, SHA-256
`be0e37ddb0ab257065cb75b334c08491a7929a83a356611b951d13f66d7b2406`.

For q at least two, successive equality-event labels form a chain in a
loopless relation R on Fin q. Each label has exactly d predecessors,
including d=0. The literal word cardinality, rather than a sequence defined
by recurrence, is the subject of
`BarkerRegularRelationCounting.relation_enumeration`. Its positive-index
series has zero constant coefficient; the empty word has cardinality one.
The companion Scribe gives the full first-letter/suffix correspondence and
the cyclic, fixed-point-free permutation and unequal-label applications.

The exact-event extension is
`BarkerRegularRelationCounting.event_enumeration`. Its `eventCount R n r`
is the cardinality of the original admissible-word subtype with event-list
length exactly r. For r at least one its positive-length series is
`q*d^(r-1)*x^(2*r)/((1+x)^(r-1)*(1-(q-1)*x)^(r+1))`.
This includes indegree zero with `d^0=1`. Zero-event words have positive
series `q*x/(1-(q-1)*x)` and a separate unique empty word. Eventful fibers
vanish below length `2*r` and have shortest count `q*d^(r-1)`.
The complete marked eventful series is
`y*x*(1+x)*U/((1+x)*(1-(q-1)*x)-d*y*x^2)`, with
`U=q*x/(1-(q-1)*x)`. It is interpreted in `Q[[x]][[y]]`, using the unit
inverse of the displayed denominator. Both coefficients are connected to
actual event-number fibers; no evaluation of an arbitrary y-series at one
is required.

The unequal-label special case is prior work of Sela Fried,
arXiv:2410.07237v3, Section 2.8, Theorem 10, equation (18), and
*Journal of Integer Sequences* 28 (2025), Article 25.4.3, Section 3.2,
Theorem 4 and Corollary 5. Fried describes positive-index counts but displays
a rational function with constant coefficient one. It is the empty-inclusive
series 1+S in the present convention. Its homogeneous recurrence has onset
n at least one. This specialization introduces no priority or new settlement
claim for Fried's enumeration or the original eight-letter problem.

The binary wordwise statement is the revision-2 binary corollary's substring
interpretation, also identified there with A027383. Its formal theorem is
`BarkerRegularRelationCounting.binary_substring_equivalence`. It quantifies
over every actual list over Fin 2 and every actual contiguous sublist, using
the original overlapping event extraction and successor modulo two. Zero
maps to the integer step -1 and one to +1; the bound is on the signed
integer count of ones minus zeros. Both directions use actual prefix and
substring identities, including the initial zero height and all empty or
short boundaries. This is separate from equality of enumeration sequences;
the tree, necklace, folding and arithmetic interpretations remain excluded.
