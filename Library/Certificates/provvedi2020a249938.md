## Source quotations

> This sequence is a periodic sequence modulo m, and if m is the k-th prime, the periods of {a(n)} over k-th prime is the sequence of the number of nonzero quadratic residues modulo k-th prime, for all k>0.

> Example: k=9, m = prime(9) = 23, for n>0, {a(n) mod 23} generates a period of 11 elements {3, 6, 14, 22, 5, 3, 10, 2, 4, 5, 0}, hence A130290(9) = 11

> a(n) = Sum_{k=0..2*n} k! * Stirling2(2*n, k) for n>=0.

The prime clause uses the positive-index sequence, as specified by the example.
The least positive period is the cardinality of the nonzero squares in ZMod p,
including the square 1 at p = 2. The finite expression is the existing
StirlingTransformTotientPeriod.T with constant weight one, at argument 2*n.
The theorem covers every prime and asserts both periodicity and minimality.
The arbitrary-composite-modulus clause and the separate divisibility-by-288
conjecture are outside its scope.

## Mathematical mechanism

Pairing opposite residues in a finite exponential expansion gives square
frequencies with coefficients 2^(-(i+1)) + 2^i. Three disjoint translates bound
the zero-coefficient fiber; more than half the square frequencies survive.
Vandermonde uniqueness and polynomial root counting force every positive
period to be at least the number of nonzero squares. The primes 2 and 3 give
constant positive-index sequences with least period one.
