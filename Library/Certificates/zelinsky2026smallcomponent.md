# Radical component density and primitive non-deficient integers

## Source

Joshua Zelinsky, *Must a Primitive Non-Deficient Number Always Have a Small Component?*,
Journal of Integer Sequences 29, Article 26.4.3; arXiv:2005.12115v5,
section 3, pages 9–10.

> Let B be the set of numbers satisfying p_i^{a_i+1} < 2R for all i.

Here n is the product of p_i^{a_i}, and R is its radical.

> Note that B has natural density zero.

> It seems natural to ask for an asymptotic for B(x).

Conjecture 10 states that (B intersection C)(x)/B(x) tends to zero,
where C is the set of primitive non-deficient numbers.
Conjecture 12 states the same limit for the odd members C_O of C.
Conjecture 14 quantifies over every nonempty finite set M of primes
and states the same limit for the members C_M of C with no prime divisor in M.

Conjecture 11 replaces the denominator B(x) by C(x) and asserts limit zero.
Conjecture 13 uses denominator C_O(x), and Conjecture 15 uses denominator
C_M(x) for every nonempty finite prime set M; both assert limit zero.
The component condition and the families are identical in all six
conjectures; the denominators distinguish the two groups of limits.
