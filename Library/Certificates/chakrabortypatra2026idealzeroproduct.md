# Ideal zero-product probability in finite commutative rings

## Verified locator

Sukrit Chakraborty and Sourav Kanti Patra, arXiv:2608.14618v1 (2026).
DOI: https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2608.14618

Definition 2.1 in §2 counts ordered tuples from the full ideal type, including the
zero and unit ideals, and divides the number having zero ideal product by the
total number of tuples. The formula applies for each integer k at least two.
[Versioned Definition 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14618v1#S2.Thmtheorem1)

## Source claim

> Let \(R\) be a finite commutative ring with identity and let \(k\ge2\).
> If \(\zeta_k(R)=1-\frac{1}{2^k},\) then \(R\) is a field.

Proposition 4.1 proves the stated probability for a finite field. Conjecture 4.2
in §4.1 asks for the converse from equality at a given k.
[Versioned Proposition 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14618v1#S4.Thmtheorem1) ·
[Versioned Conjecture 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14618v1#S4.Thmtheorem2)

## Prior results

Behboodi and Rakeei, *The Annihilating-Ideal Graph of Commutative Rings I*,
arXiv:0808.3187v2, Theorem 2.7, includes the case of a square-zero ideal
of zero divisors in their classification of complete annihilating-ideal graphs.
Graph edges capture zero products of distinct nonzero proper ideals in the
binary square-zero extension. Computing the ordered probability also requires
diagonal products and products with the zero and unit ideals.
[Versioned Theorem 2.7](https://arxiv.org/html/0808.3187v2#p72)

## Scope

The binary square-zero extension by a two-dimensional vector space has six
actual ideals and 27 ordered zero products out of 36 at k = 2. Its nonzero
square-zero element excludes the field conclusion. The bounded literature
search for the conjecture located no earlier settlement. Completeness of
reverse-citation coverage and priority remain unverified.
[Problem and literature scope](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/33639)
