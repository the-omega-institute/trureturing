## Verified locator

Source: https://oeis.org/A131758

The source is unpaginated. Revision 48 has the following consecutive COMMENTS
lines, attributed to Tom Copeland:

```text
The P(n,t) are related to the Laguerre polynomials through
  P(n,t) = (-1)^n n! [ (1-t)^(n+1)} ] Sum_{k>=0} [ (t^k*Lag(n,k+1) ] = Sum_{m=0..n} a(n,m) * t^m
where a(n,m) = (-1)^n n! [ Sum_{k=0..m} (-1)^k * C(n+1,k) *Lag(n,m-k+1) ] .
Conjecture for the polynomial array:
The greatest common divisor of the coefficients of each polynomial is given by A060872(n)/n or, equivalently, by A038548(n).
```

The FORMULA line reads:

```text
a(n,m) = (-1)^n*n!*Sum_{k=0..m} (-1)^k*C(n+1,k)*Lag(n, m-k+1).
```

The COMMENTS specify:

> Lag(n,x) is a Laguerre polynomial and T(n,t) is a Touchard / Bell / exponential polynomial.

> where S2(k,j) are the Stirling numbers of the second kind and C(m,k), binomial coefficients.

The ordinary order-zero Laguerre expansion is
L_n(x) = Σ(j=0..n) (−1)^j binom(n,j) x^j / j!, as in
[DLMF 18.5.12](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.5.E12) at α=0. The rational finite
expansion is inserted directly into the coefficient formula. The integer row
contains all n+1 coefficients, including degree n; its content is the
nonnegative gcd of their absolute values. Integer realizations are asserted
to exist for every n≥0, and the proposed content equation is asserted for
every such realization at n≥1.

[A038548](https://oeis.org/A038548) states, verbatim:

> Number of divisors of n that are at most sqrt(n).

Its offset is 1. The count is encoded by positive natural divisors d with
d*d≤n. The alternative shift D(n+1) is outside the formal claim.

The complete ninth row is
