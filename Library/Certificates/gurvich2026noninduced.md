## Source statement

> Let (H, s, t) be a two-rooted inherent graph such that s ≠ t, d_H(s) = 1, and let H′ be obtained from H by deleting the unique edge incident with s. Then (H′, s, t) is inherent.

The background convention orders the roots so that the first has degree at
most that of the second. Copies are ordinary subgraph copies. An extension
attaches two fresh distinct vertices to the ordered roots. To close it, a path
between those vertices must avoid every copy vertex. A containing host
confines the pattern when none of its copies is avoidable.
[Definitions](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06636v1#page=4)

## Relation to the refutation

Theorem 3.1 gives inherence of the intact six-vertex path with ordered roots
0 and 3, viewed as the type-I comb with parameters 4, 0 and 2. The checked
argument specializes the maximal connected set proof of Lemma 3.4. The
four-arm host supplies the obstruction after deletion. Proposition 6.3
concerns paths rooted at both endpoints. In the counterexample the second
root is internal. [Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06636v1#page=10)
