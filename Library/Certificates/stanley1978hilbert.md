# The idealization and its positive ideal

Richard P. Stanley, *Hilbert Functions of Graded Algebras*, Advances in
Mathematics 28 (1978), 57–83, Example 4.3, printed pp.70–71,
DOI 10.1016/0001-8708(78)90045-2.

The example constructs an Artinian Gorenstein algebra by adjoining a shifted
dual module as a square-zero ideal. Its Hilbert vector is
`(1,13,12,13,1)`. This mechanism is classical; implementing its coefficient
model or applying it to another question does not establish originality of
the mechanism.

For a truncated polynomial algebra with monomials through degree `t`, the
dual action is the transpose of multiplication. In the coefficient basis,
`e_a f_b = f_(b-a)` when `a ≤ b` coordinatewise, and is zero otherwise.
This description uses no factorial or division, and therefore makes sense
over every field. The all-field coefficient model and the exact bridge to
Bergman's Question 23 require their own proof; they are not inferred merely
from the name Gorenstein or from the five Hilbert-vector entries.

The entire unital idealization has a constant basis vector in addition to
the positive ideal. When `t=3` and there are three variables, the displayed
Hilbert vector has total dimension 40. Its positive ideal has dimension 39.
These two ambient algebras must not be identified when reporting dimensions.
