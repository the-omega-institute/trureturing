# OEIS A254074

The NAME of A254074 states:

> Numbers k such that the decimal expansions of both k and k^2 have 5 as the digit with the smallest value and 9 as the digit with the largest value.

Chai Wah Wu's COMMENT of September 10, 2017 states:

> The first digit of a term is either 7, 8 or 9 and the last digit is either 5, 6 or 7. Conjecture: no term is a multiple of 5. - _Chai Wah Wu_, Sep 10 2017

The AUTHOR line names:

> _Felix Fröhlich_, May 03 2015

The sequence author is listed first in the note metadata, followed by the
conjecture author. The year 2017 records the conjecture; the sequence AUTHOR
line is dated May 3, 2015.

Only the second sentence of Wu's comment, "Conjecture: no term is a multiple
of 5," is settled. The preceding first-digit and last-digit assertion is not
claimed.

The route assumes a multiple of 5 whose digits are all at least 5, forcing
its last digit to be 5. Writing the number as `10q+5` gives a square congruent
to 25 modulo 100; once the number is at least 59, the tens digit 2 is present
in the square and contradicts minimum digit 5.

An exhaustive scan through 12,000,000 found exactly one sequence term,
759576, whose square is 576955699776, and found no term divisible by 5. The
single observed term makes this bounded scan thin, and the scan carries no
proof of the universal statement.
