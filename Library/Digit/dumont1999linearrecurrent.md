# Representations with a digit cap one below a multiple of the radix

## Verified locator

DOI: 10.4064/aa-88-4-371-396

Source: https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa88/aa8847.pdf

Lemma 6.6 (p. 386) and Proposition 6.8 (p. 387); Proposition 6.9 (pp. 387–388) as cited below.

## Statement

Jean-Marie Dumont, Nikita Sidorov and Alain Thomas, *Number of representations related to a
linear recurrent basis*, Acta Arithmetica 88 (1999), no. 4, 371–396,
DOI 10.4064/aa-88-4-371-396.

Lemma 6.6 (p. 386) splits each digit below d·u as a quotient below u and a remainder below d,
and the remainders form the canonical radix-d expansion; with u = d this gives the count
[n/d] + 1. Proposition 6.8 (p. 387) states that the count with digit cap d·u − 1 at n equals
the sum of the cap u − 1 counts at 0, …, [n/d], and that it has exact order n^{log_d u}.

Proposition 6.9 (pp. 387–388) treats the alphabet of size d² − 1, that is the digit cap
d² − 2, and gives the growth exponent log_d((d − 1 + √(d² + 2d − 3))/2) < 1. This locator and
formula are as cited in the-omega-institute/trureturing-requests#308; the PDF host refused an
automated fetch, so the verbatim proposition text was not re-read (ASSUMED-UNVERIFIED for the
exact wording).
