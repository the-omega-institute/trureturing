# Subgroup conjugacy ratios of finite groups

The primary source is arXiv:2508.14267v2, posted 9 August 2026;
the first version was posted 19 August 2025. The title, authors and
CC BY 4.0 license are given on the arXiv abstract page and HTML.
The versioned HTML is the source for the locators below.

## Verified locator

- DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.2508.14267.
- Primary URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14267v2.
- Version and license: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14267v2.
- Section 1: https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14267v2#S1.
  All groups in the paper are finite. Here $L(G)$ is the entire subgroup
  lattice, $k'(G)$ is its number of conjugacy classes, and
  $d'(G)=k'(G)/|L(G)|$. The section states multiplicativity of $d'$
  for direct products of groups of pairwise coprime orders.
- Section 2, Lemma 2.1(ii) and Proposition 2.2(ii):
  https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14267v2#S2.
  Lemma 2.1(ii) describes a nonabelian split extension
  $G_{p,q,n}=N\rtimes P$, where $N\cong C_p$,
  $P\cong C_{q^{n-1}}$, $[N,\Phi(P)]=1$, $p,q$ are primes,
  $q\mid p-1$, and $n\geq2$. Proposition 2.2(ii) states
  $d'(G_{p,q,n})=2n/(2n+p-1)$.
- The proof of Proposition 2.2(ii), equation S2.Ex12:
  https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14267v2#S2.Ex12.
  It explicitly states both counts
  $k'(G_{p,q,n})=2n$ and $|L(G_{p,q,n})|=2n+p-1$.
- Section 2, Corollary 2.4 already gives attainment of $a/(a+1)$;
  Theorem A gives density of the nilpotent-group values in $[0,1]$.
- Section 4, Problem 4.1: https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14267v2#S4.
  For integers $1\leq a<b$, the authors ask whether some group has
  $d'(G)=a/b$. They propose choosing $b-a$ groups of coprime orders
  with ratios $(a+i-1)/(a+i)$ and taking their direct product.
  Problems 4.2 and 4.3 concern different structural and extremal questions.

## Scope and parameter correspondence

`CyclicPrimePowerAction.split_counts` concerns primes `p,q`, a natural
`s>0`, coprimality of `p,q`, and an actual `ActionData p q s`.
The latter contains a homomorphism
$C_{q^s}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(C_p)$ whose generator image
has exact order $q$. `SplitGroup A` is its actual semidirect product.
Set $n=s+1$, $N=C_p$, and $P=C_{q^s}$. Then $n\geq2$ and the
generator acts nontrivially, while its $q$th power acts trivially.
The Frattini subgroup of this cyclic prime-power $P$ is generated by
that $q$th power, so the action has the source condition $[N,\Phi(P)]=1$.
For the source's primes with $q\mid p-1$, `actionData` constructs such
an actual action using Cauchy's theorem and the cyclic-generator extension.
The primes are distinct, so the count theorem's coprimality condition holds.

The Lean count theorem works for every supplied actual action in this
scope, without assuming the source's count formulas or its classification.
Its whole-lattice bijection gives $2s+p+1=2n+p-1$ subgroups. Its
inner-conjugacy quotient bijection gives $2s+2=2n$ classes. Both counts
include bottom and top; `s=1` is the source boundary case `n=2`.
These numerical statements are literature-attested, while their Lean
proofs supply kernel evidence. No mathematical novelty is claimed for them.

`SubgroupConjugacyRatio` uses precisely the source observable:
`latticeCount G = Nat.card (Subgroup G)` and
`classCount G = Nat.card (Quotient (MulAction.orbitRel (ConjAct G)
(Subgroup G)))`. This is subgroup conjugacy by elements of the actual
group, rather than subgroup isomorphism or conjugacy of elements.
`coprime_product_counts` additionally constructs bijections for both
counts separately; its ratio consequence is the known Section 1 identity.

`RationalSubgroupConjugacyAttainment.rational_attainment` is the
repository proof of the universal question, not a theorem asserted by
the source. It supplies fresh consecutive-ratio factors of orders
coprime to the accumulated group and telescopes them. Its final target
has every integer pair $1\leq a<b$ and a genuine finite-group witness.
Both counts are positive, so $b k'(G)=a|L(G)|$ is equivalent to $d'(G)=a/b$.
It assumes no nilpotence, supersolvability, bounded range or reduced fraction.
