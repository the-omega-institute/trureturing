# Sequences in groups with distinct partial products

Basil Gordon, *Pacific Journal of Mathematics* **11** (1961), 1309–1313,
Theorem 1, proof (ii), pp. 1309–1311.

Gordon starts the ordering with the identity and includes all nonempty partial
products. In additive notation, deleting that first zero gives a word containing
each nonzero group element once. Its empty prefix and all its nonempty prefixes
are pairwise distinct. The nonzero-total hypothesis excludes the identity-only
group, which is a degenerate exception to the literal printed equivalence.

The construction interleaves the negatives of mixed-radix coordinates with their
coordinatewise translates by one. Odd coordinate moduli make doubling invertible.
The first nonzero digit of a predecessor carry can therefore be recovered from
the directed difference. A complete borrow through zero gives exactly zero.
No coprimality between odd radices, including repeated factors, is required;
Chinese remaindering is used only between the even prime-power factor and the
product of the odd radices.

The theorem concerns the full nonzero group. Deleting labels changes prefix sums
and does not settle the corresponding arbitrary-subset problem. The mathematics
and construction are published standard results; no originality is claimed.
