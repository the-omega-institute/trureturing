# Finite-group representation degrees and the RT monomial obstruction

## Source and locator

Literature status: `literature-attested`.

Constantin Teleman, *Representation Theory*, Lent 2005, [primary lecture notes](https://math.berkeley.edu/~teleman/math/RepThry.pdf), §13, Theorem 13.6, printed pp. 31–32. The theorem states irreducible-degree divisibility by the finite group order. For a finite two-group, every irreducible degree is therefore a power of two. Complete reducibility implies that every odd-dimensional complex representation contains a one-dimensional constituent.

## RT use and boundary

[RT IV §111](../../docs/develop/theory/ARITHMETIC_HOLOGRAPHIC_RT_IV.md#111-spectral-parity-in-the-original-monomial-symmetry-obstruction) uses the two-group consequence as an intermediate standard fact and includes an elementary index-two proof of that consequence. Its new original-channel consumer constructs a finite determinant-one lift group from an actual correct-output Gram, retains the original fibre partition, and uses one common invariant line to perform reciprocal phases on both complete local channels.

The resulting bound applies when one actual correct-output Gram has a transitive fibre-preserving monomial symmetry and an eigenvalue of odd multiplicity. The opposite channel may be arbitrary and both channels may leak. A rank of the correct-output Gram is not automatically the leaking channel's Kraus rank. The classical representation theorem does not supply the original-channel norm bound, the finite fibre-symmetry reduction, or a statement about arbitrary nonsymmetric channels. No new general representation-theory result is claimed.
