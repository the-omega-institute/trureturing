# The all-prime sixth-order commuting-graph question

## Verified locator

- DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.2305.07301.
- Primary URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07301v5.
- Source: https://arxiv.org/src/2305.07301v5, member `acmm-July25.tex`,
  SHA-256 `853854e603c1f53790f78a98c41ec2fcc21c0b033505926d9cb27d6773139ea2`.

The source's Section 9, Q4, page 40 asks:

> What can be said about pairs of groups with isomorphic commuting graphs? Do non-isoclinic examples exist for all orders $p^6$ with $p$ prime?

Only the second sentence is addressed here. Vertices are all group elements,
with two distinct elements adjacent exactly when they commute. The source
commutator is $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$, and the derived group is generated by
these commutators. Isoclinism requires compatible group isomorphisms on the
central quotients and derived groups, intertwining the commutator maps.
There is no exclusion of the prime two. The source credits O'Brien with
examples at primes two, three and five; those cases alone do not answer Q4.

## Construction and evidence boundary

The coefficient rings are the integers modulo the square of the prime and
the dual numbers over its prime residue field. Their Heisenberg triples have
isomorphic full commuting graphs because a three-chart decomposition
preserves equality of cross products. The derived groups have different
prime-power annihilation behaviour. The native theorem and its applicability
are described by the corresponding Blueprint.
