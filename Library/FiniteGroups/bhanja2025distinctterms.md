# Sumsets with a minimum number of distinct terms

J. Bhanja, *Integers* **25** (2025), A12, arXiv:2307.03977v2.
The at-most-distinct restriction permits repetition and limits the number
of different summand values, rather than requiring all summands distinct.

## Verified locator

Journal: https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/z12/z12.pdf

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03977v2

§4, “Further Remarks”, printed p. 11. Verbatim text fragments from the
arXiv v2 text extraction (the superscript is extracted as `(≤r)`):

> Another sumset that may come immediately to the reader’s mind is the sumset
> h(≤r) A, which contains those elements of the group G that are the sum of h elements
> of A with at most r distinct elements. The sumset hA is a special case of h(≤r) A
> for r = h.

> Question 1. Is it always the case that the minimum size of both the sumsets hA
> and h(≤r) A are the same in any abelian group G, where the minimum runs over the
> subsets of G of size (say) k?

The preceding paragraph explicitly asks this question for integers r ≥ 2.
The formal answer is negative: in an elementary abelian 2-group, odd
h ≥ 3 and a subset larger than half the group give restricted sumset A
and ordinary sumset G. These identities hold for every subset in that
cardinality range, so they determine both minima.
