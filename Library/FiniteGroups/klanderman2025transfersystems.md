Conjecture 4.2, printed page 15, `AMS_Proceedings.tex` lines 962–964:

> Let T be a G-transfer system with two connected components, one containing e
> and one containing G. If the connected component containing G only has the
> edge G → G, then T is not LSP and is compatible with the transfer system
> generated by adding the edge e → G to T.

Section 2 assumes throughout that G is finite (TeX line 93). Definition 2.1
(lines 99–107) requires a partial order on all actual subgroups, refining
inclusion, reflexive, composable, closed under restriction and G-conjugation.
Restriction along L ≤ H sends K → H to K ∩ L → H ∩ L = L.
Antisymmetry is implied by refinement of inclusion.

Definition 2.14 (lines 494–500) orders compatibility as (T₁,T₂): T₁ ⊆ T₂ and,
for B,C ≤ A ≤ G, T₁(B,A) and T₂(B ∩ C,B) force T₂(C,A).
Saturation, the least saturated hull and LSP occur at lines 524, 552–559.
LSP means that every compatible greater system is the hull or the complete
inclusion transfer system. The published conjunction has no non-abelian,
saturation, cosaturation or additional group-family hypothesis.

## Ordinary generation and orientation

The primary source's TeX line 478 cites Jonathan Rubin, *Detecting Steiner and
linear isometries operads*, Glasgow Mathematical Journal 63 (2021),
DOI [10.1017/S001708952000021X](https://doi.org/10.1017/S001708952000021X),
Construction A.1. Its Theorem A.2 identifies ordinary generation as the smallest
transfer system containing the generators: conjugation and restriction, then
reflexive/transitive closure. The formal `generated` uses this leastness
characterization as an intersection of all containing ordinary systems.

Mazur et al., [arXiv:2401.13523v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13523v2), Lemma 3.6,
adds a separate compatibility-closure step after ordinary transfer closure.
That step is absent from the conjecture's stated generation operation.
[arXiv:2604.00335v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00335v1), Definition 2.16,
uses (Oa,Om), with Om contained in Oa; its order is opposite to the primary's.
The formal compatibility predicate follows the primary exactly.
