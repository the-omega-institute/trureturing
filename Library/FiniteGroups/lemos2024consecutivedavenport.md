## Source statements

Printed p. 1: “An ordered subsequence T of S is an ordered sequence of the
form T = gᵢ · gᵢ₊₁ · … · gⱼ₋₁ · gⱼ for some 1 ≤ i ≤ j ≤ k.”
Ordered sequences are lists and ordered subsequences are nonempty contiguous
infixes, with multiplication taken in the given order.

Printed p. 2: “When A = {1}, we call S a consecutive product-one sequence
and a consecutive product-one free sequence, respectively.”

Printed p. 2: “Let C_A(G) be the smallest positive integer ℓ such that every
sequence over G, with length at least ℓ, has an A-weighted consecutive
product-one subsequence.” “For A = {1}, we omit the index A.”
The unweighted constant is the natural-number infimum of the positive thresholds.

Printed p. 3, Conjecture 3.4: “For every finite group G, it holds C(G) = |G|.”

## Scope

The formal statement covers every finite group, including the trivial group.
Its proof combines the finite prefix-product pigeonhole principle with the
quotient word of an enumeration of the group. The source's abelian and
metacyclic cases and its indicated direct products are within this scope.
Larger weight sets are outside this statement. The inverse characterization
and extremal-word count are not separately formalized here.
