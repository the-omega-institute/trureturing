# The size-four question on distinct powers

Theorem 1.1 considers every positive integer $n$, every finite group $G$ having no elements of orders $2$ through $n+1$, and every $n$-element subset $A$ of $G$. Its equivalence relation on $A$ is equality of generated cyclic subgroups. With each class of size at most three, the theorem orders $A$ so that the $i$th element raised to the $i$th power gives pairwise distinct results.

Section 2 separates the classes by preservation of the generated subgroup under each allowed exponent. The proof assigns complementary endpoint pairs to the two-element classes, consecutive triples to the three-element classes, and the remaining exponents to singletons. The formal size-four proof uses this subgroup separation and extends the allocation.

Remark 2.2 gives the coefficient block $\{4,5,6,7\}$ and the multiset $\{1,1,1,-1\}$ in $\mathbb F_{11}$ as an obstruction to the direct prime-field argument. It asks whether the class bound can be raised to four. Section 9 repeats the question and separately asks about the unrestricted case $n=7$. In the size-four result the four generators have distinct coordinates modulo their full common order. The prime-field reductions can repeat.
