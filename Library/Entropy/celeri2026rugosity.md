# Rugosity as a measurement-induced relative entropy

Let a prescribed finite POVM have positive Born masses at a full-rank
state. For a Hermitian trace-zero tangent, the corresponding score
observable weights each effect by the directional derivative of its
mass divided by its mass. A zero POVM effect has zero Born mass.
The proved domain indexes the effects of positive Born mass.
The finite-matrix sensitivity loss splits
into four nonnegative terms. They measure the squared BKM distance from the
logarithmic tangent to the score observable, the gap between the ordinary second moment and the
BKM second moment, the dispersion within POVM effects, and the
residual of the classical score projection. At zero score second moment,
the directional derivative vanishes and the normalized sensitivity
term is zero.

For reverse relative entropy the outcome score has Pearson
chi-squared variance. A point-mass reference gives the coefficient
in Equation (14) for the binary flat-state test. For forward relative
entropy a positive reference leads to the relative entropy variance.
The affine Born curve and its derivative depend on the state, tangent,
and POVM. A Gibbs reference and a thermal reset supply the physical
interpretation of the BKM cost.

The Fisher contraction and its equality theory have earlier
foundations. Gao, Li, Marvian and Rouzé, arXiv:2302.02341, include
Lemma 4.9, which already supplies the BKM distance remainder and
its aggregate complement under a measurement. The present derivation
resolves that complement into a logarithmic-mean gap and POVM
sandwiches, then accounts for the additional classical score
projection in each relative-entropy readout.
