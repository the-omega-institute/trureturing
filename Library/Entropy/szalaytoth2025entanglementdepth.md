# Alternatives of entanglement depth and metrological entanglement criteria

Sz. Szalay and G. Tóth, arXiv:2408.15350v3; Quantum 9 (2025), 1718. Quotations are from the v3 TeX source, with the macros `\field`, `\pinv` and `\Dim` expanded to $\mathbb{}$, $\hat{}$ and $\operatorname{Dim}$, `\mset` written as braces, and citation commands omitted.

Integer partitions:

> Such properties are labeled by \emph{integer partitions} of $n$, $\hat{\xi}=\{x_1,x_2,\dots,x_{|\hat{\xi}|}\}$, which are multisets, containing the $x\in\hat{\xi}$ parts $x\in \mathbb{N}$, such that $\sum_{x\in\hat{\xi}}x=n$.

The power sum:

> For $q\in\mathbb{R}$, let us have the \emph{power sum} $s_q(\hat{\xi}) := \sum_{x\in\hat{\xi}} x^q,$

The entanglement dimension:

> For $d=\dim(\mathcal{H}_i)\geq2$, let us have the \emph{entanglement dimension} (or \emph{effective dimension}) $\operatorname{Dim}_d(\hat{\xi}) := \sum_{x\in\hat{\xi}} d^x,$

The two expectations (Section 4.7):

> Although several of these equations have nontrivial solutions (for example, $2^2+2^2+2^2=3^2+1^2+1^2+1^2=12$ for $n=6$, $q=2$, or $2^3+2^3+2^3+2^3=3^3+1^3+1^3+1^3+1^3+1^3=32$ for $n=8$, $q=3$), these  seem to be rather sporadic. On the other hand, for $q\not\in\mathbb{N}$ the equation becomes transcendental, and we expect the injectivity of $s_q$.

> For the case $d\not\in\mathbb{N}$, we expect the injectivity of $\operatorname{Dim}_d$, although the meaning of $\operatorname{Dim}_d$ is not established for noninteger $d$, which is just a parameter then.
