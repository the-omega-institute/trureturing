# Published square-product boundary values

The primary source is arXiv:2505.19642v1, 26 May 2025. Its submission history
was checked on 28 October 2026 and lists v1. The introduction states
wdim_1(K_n square K_n) = floor((4n-2)/3). The k=2 line of Theorem 1.1 gives
wdim_2(K_n square K_n) = ceil(4n/3) for n >= 3.

At n=3 these are 3 and 4. They are exactly the d=2 boundary values for the
ternary deficits t=5 and t=4, since kappa(2)=6. They receive no novelty credit.
Their role in the retained threshold theorem is to rule out every cutoff
at most two; the three-landmark L and four-corner square realize the values.
