# Malkoun, Finite graphs and configurations of points

J. Malkoun, arXiv:2508.13472v3 [math-ph] (7 September 2026; v1 19 August 2025, v2 9 March 2026); Journal of Geometry and Physics, DOI 10.1016/j.geomphys.2026.105993 (Crossref: title, author and journal match; issue date January 2027). Quotations are from the v3 TeX source, with the macro `\Real` written as $\operatorname{Re}$. The journal text was not read.

The groups and the function (Section "A conjecture in matrix analysis"):

> Given an equivalence relation, say $\sim$, on $[n]$, we let $P_\sim$ be the group of all permutations of $[n]$ which preserve the equivalence classes of $[n]$ under $\sim$. Let $\sim_i$, for $i = 1, 2$, be two equivalence relations on $[n] = \{1, \dots, n\}$, with associated permutation groups $P_1$, $P_2$, respectively

> Let $H$ be a Hermitian positive semidefinite $n \times n$ matrix. We define
> $f_{\sim_1, \sim_2}(H) = \sum_{\sigma_1 \in P_1} \sum_{\sigma_2 \in P_2} \prod_{i = 1}^n H_{\sigma_1(i), \sigma_2(i)}.$

Conjecture D:

> Under the above hypotheses, we have $\operatorname{Re}( f_{\sim_1, \sim_2}(H) ) \geq |P_1 \cap P_2| \, \prod_{i = 1}^n H_{i i}.$

The source records that the discrete and indiscrete relations give Marcus's permanent analogue of the Hadamard inequality, proves Conjecture D when one relation is finer than the other, proves that Conjecture D implies its Conjecture C for trees, and reports that numerical simulations for small $n$ found no counterexample. The same inequality is MathOverflow question 480580 (Malkoun, 13 October 2024), which proves the case $n=3$ with classes $\{1,2\},\{3\}$ and $\{1\},\{2,3\}$.
