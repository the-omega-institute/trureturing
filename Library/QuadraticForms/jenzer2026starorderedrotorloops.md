## Source statement

Remark 5.7 in the TeX, rendered Remark 5.5, page 19, “On the Scope, with Numerical Evidence”:

> The hypotheses of Theorem 5.5 can be sharpened at two points. First, for τ = id no star condition is needed: that is Theorem 5.2, whose pairing argument cancels the odd off-diagonal terms in pairs. Second, this pairing argument fails termwise for τ ≠ id. Nevertheless, numerical evidence suggests that the formula also holds without the star condition in that case: tested on the triangle family from Remark 4.4, which violates the star condition, under all six permutations of {1, 2, 3} with random parameter sets; the largest deviation encountered was in the range of double machine precision. A general proof would have to capture the cancellation of the odd off-diagonal terms in coarser groups than pairs; we have not determined such a grouping.

Theorem 5.5, page 18, “Permuted Loop Formula”:

> Let B₁, …, Bₖ be an anticommuting unit family of type −1 that satisfies the star condition, and let τ be a permutation of {1, …, k}. Then

$$
\langle\Theta_k(\tau)\rangle_0
 =\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}}\eta(|S|)(-1)^{\iota(\tau,S)}
  \prod_{i\in S}w_i\prod_{i\notin S}(1-w_i).
$$

```tex
Let \(V\) be a real vector space of finite dimension \(n\) with a
non-degenerate quadratic form \(q\), and let
\(\mathcal{C} = \mathrm{Cl}(V, q)\) be the associated Clifford algebra,
generated by \(V\) under the relation \(v^2 = q(v)\) for \(v \in V\).
For the foundations we follow \citep{Lounesto2001} and \citep{Porteous1995}; for
the notation in geometric algebra \citep{DoranLasenby2003} as well as
\citep{HestenesSobczyk1984}.

For an orthonormal basis \(e_1, \ldots, e_n\) of \(V\), the products
\[e_A = e_{a_1} e_{a_2} \cdots e_{a_r}, \qquad A = \{a_1 < a_2 < \cdots < a_r\} \subseteq \{1, \ldots, n\},\]
with \(e_\emptyset = 1\) form a basis of \(\mathcal{C}\) as a real
vector space, \(\dim \mathcal{C} = 2^n\). We call \(e_A\) a \emph{basis
blade} of grade \(r = |A|\).
```

```tex
\begin{definition}[Scalar Projection]\label{definition:D2-1}
 For
\(x = \sum_A x_A e_A \in \mathcal{C}\),
\(\langle x \rangle_0 := x_\emptyset \in \mathbb{R}\) is called the
\emph{scalar part} of \(x\); the map
\(\langle \cdot \rangle_0 : \mathcal{C} \to \mathbb{R}\) is the
\emph{scalar projection} or grade-0 projection. It is linear.

\end{definition}
```

```tex
\begin{definition}[Reversion]\label{definition:D2-2}
 \emph{Reversion} is the unique
anti-automorphism \(\widetilde{\;\cdot\;}\) of \(\mathcal{C}\) with
\(\widetilde v = v\) for \(v \in V\); on a blade of grade \(r\) it acts
as \[\widetilde{e_A} = (-1)^{r(r-1)/2} e_A, \qquad r = |A|,\] and for a
product \(\widetilde{xy} = \widetilde y \, \widetilde x\) holds. The
grade sign \((-1)^{r(r-1)/2}\) is found in \citep[Eq.~92]{Hitzer2012}, \citep[Eq.~4.52]{DoranLasenby2003} and \citep[\S 22.1]{Lounesto2001}.

In particular, \(\widetilde B = -B\) for every bivector \(B\), since
\(r = 2\) gives \((-1)^{1} = -1\).

\end{definition}
```

```tex
\begin{definition}[Rotor]\label{definition:D2-3}
 Let \(B \in \mathcal{C}\) be a
bivector with \(B^2 = -1\) and \(\theta \in \mathbb{R}\). Then
\[U(\theta) = \exp\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2} B\right) = \cos\frac{\theta}{2} + B \sin\frac{\theta}{2}\]
is called the \emph{rotor} generated by \(B\) with parameter \(\theta\).

The exponential series converges and, because \(B^2 = -1\), yields
exactly the given trigonometric form. We have
\(\widetilde{U(\theta)} = \cos\frac{\theta}{2} - B \sin\frac{\theta}{2} = U(-\theta)\)
and
\(U(\theta)\widetilde{U(\theta)} = \cos^2\frac{\theta}{2} - B^2 \sin^2\frac{\theta}{2} = 1\).

\end{definition}
```

```tex
\begin{definition}[Subset Product]\label{definition:D2-4}
 Let
\(B_1, \ldots, B_k \in \mathcal{C}\) be given. For
\(S = \{i_1 < i_2 < \cdots < i_m\} \subseteq \{1, \ldots, k\}\) we set
\[B_S := B_{i_1} B_{i_2} \cdots B_{i_m}, \qquad B_\emptyset := 1.\] The
factors always stand in ascending index order. We write throughout
\[c_i := \cos\frac{\theta_i}{2}, \qquad s_i := \sin\frac{\theta_i}{2}, \qquad w_i := s_i^2 = \sin^2\frac{\theta_i}{2} \in [0,1]\]
and for a subset \(S\)
\[a_S := \prod_{i \in S} s_i \prod_{i \notin S} c_i.\]

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\pagebreak

\end{definition}
```

```tex
\begin{definition}[Anticommuting Unit Family]\label{definition:D3-2}
 A family
\(B_1, \ldots, B_k\) of bivectors of a real Clifford algebra is called
an \emph{anticommuting unit family} of type \(-1\) if
\[B_i^2 = -1 \quad (1 \le i \le k), \qquad B_i B_j = -B_j B_i \quad (i \neq j).\]

\end{definition}
```

```tex
\begin{definition}[Star Condition]\label{definition:D3-3}
 An anticommuting unit family
satisfies the \emph{star condition} if
\[\langle B_S \rangle_0 = 0 \qquad \text{for all } S \neq \emptyset.\]

The choice of names is justified by Lemmata 3.1 and 3.5: every star
family is an anticommuting unit family and satisfies the star condition.
We do not investigate the converse; Chapter 8.4 names it as an Open
Front (OF 1).

\end{definition}
```

```tex
\begin{definition}[Period-Four Sign Law]\label{definition:D5-1}
 We set for
\(m \in \mathbb{N}_0\)
\[\eta(m) := (-1)^{m} \cdot (-1)^{m(m+1)/2} = (-1)^{m(m+3)/2}.\]

\end{definition}
```

```tex
\begin{definition}[Permuted Loop]\label{definition:D5-2}
 Let \(\tau\) be a permutation
of \(\{1, \ldots, k\}\). We set
\[Q_k^{(\tau)} = \widetilde U_{\tau(1)} \, \widetilde U_{\tau(2)} \cdots \widetilde U_{\tau(k)}, \qquad \Theta_k(\tau) = P_k \, Q_k^{(\tau)},\]
so that \(\Theta_k(\mathrm{id}) = \Theta_k\) and
\(\Theta_k(\tau_{\mathrm{rev}})\) for the reverse permutation
\(\tau_{\mathrm{rev}}(j) = k+1-j\) gives the trivial product
\(P_k \widetilde{P_k} = 1\) from Chapter 1.1. For
\(S \subseteq \{1, \ldots, k\}\) let
\[\iota(\tau, S) := \#\{(i, j) \in S \times S : i < j,\ \tau^{-1}(i) > \tau^{-1}(j)\}\]
be the \emph{inversion count} of \(\tau\) on \(S\).

\end{definition}
```

```tex
Let \(\mathcal{C}\) be a real Clifford algebra and let
\(B_1, \ldots, B_k \in \mathcal{C}\) be bivectors with
\[B_i^2 = -1, \qquad B_i B_j = -B_j B_i \quad (i \neq j).\] For real
parameters \(\theta_1, \ldots, \theta_k\) we set
\[U_i = \cos\frac{\theta_i}{2} + B_i \sin\frac{\theta_i}{2}, \qquad \widetilde U_i = \cos\frac{\theta_i}{2} - B_i \sin\frac{\theta_i}{2},\]
where \(\widetilde{\;\cdot\;}\) denotes reversion. Each \(U_i\) is a
rotor: \(U_i \widetilde U_i = 1\).

We consider the \emph{ordered chain} \[
P_k = U_1 U_2 \cdots U_k
\] and, with the same index order, \[
Q_k = \widetilde U_1 \widetilde U_2 \cdots \widetilde U_k,
\] as well as their product
\[\Theta_k = P_k \, Q_k = (U_1 \cdots U_k)(\widetilde U_1 \cdots \widetilde U_k).\]
```

## Scope

Anticommuting conjugations eliminate nonempty scalar components except possible odd full support. The balanced exterior word has even parity, so its odd top coefficient vanishes and cancels that remaining aggregate. The statement includes zero trigonometric coefficients and all source signatures. It does not classify star families.
