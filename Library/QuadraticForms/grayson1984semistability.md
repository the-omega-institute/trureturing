# Reduction theory using semistability

## Verified locator

Daniel R. Grayson, *Reduction theory using semistability*, Commentarii Mathematici
Helvetici 59 (1984), 600–634. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02566369).

Earlier quadratic-form reduction theory appears in U. Stuhler,
*Eine Bemerkung zur Reduktionstheorie quadratischer Formen*, Archiv der Mathematik
27 (1976), 604–610. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01224726).

## Classical conclusion and correspondence

The Euclidean-lattice conclusion is the canonical semistability filtration in
Grayson, *Reduction theory using semistability*, pp. 600–634
(DOI: 10.1007/BF02566369), in the discussion of semistability and canonical
filtrations for lattices. Its factors are semistable and their slopes strictly
decrease. Stuhler, *Eine Bemerkung zur Reduktionstheorie quadratischer Formen*,
pp. 604–610 (DOI: 10.1007/BF01224726), is the earlier quadratic-form source.
This article-level locator attests the classical Euclidean filtration, not each
identity in the repository's combined theorem.

For the Euclidean specialization, subobjects are saturated integral sublattices;
their rank is the dimension of their real span, and their degree is minus the
logarithm of their positive covolume. Intersection is the meet and saturation of
the sum is the join. The repository derives the required covolume inequality
from compatible integral bases and Gram determinants. Its exact saturation-index
factor, equality criterion, arbitrary-interval presentation and combined endpoint
identities are repo-derived, rather than attributed verbatim to the articles.

## Scope and abstract model

The classical setting is reduction theory for Euclidean lattices and quadratic forms,
using semistability and canonical slope filtrations. It supplies the mathematical
background for the repository's Harder–Narasimhan construction.

The repository model instead assumes an arbitrary lattice, a strictly increasing
integer-valued rank satisfying the modular identity, and a supermodular real degree.
Its interval slopes use degree and rank differences. The three abstract existence,
uniqueness and extreme-slope statements are repository derivations under these explicit
hypotheses; this citation does not assert an identical theorem in either article.
An application to Euclidean lattices additionally requires saturated subobjects,
subspace covolumes and their supermodularity inequality. No such application or
operator-norm slope comparison is part of the abstract statements.
