## Main assertion

For an $n$ by $n$ complex Hermitian positive semidefinite matrix $H=(h_{ij})$
and subgroups $G,K\leq\Sigma_n$, the source defines

$$
f_{G,K}(H)=\sum_{(\sigma,\tau)\in G\times K}
  \prod_{i=1}^{n}h_{\sigma(i),\tau(i)}.
$$

It asks:

> Does there exist a positive constant $c(n,G,K)$ depending on $n$, $G$ and $K$
> such that the following holds?
> $\Re(f_{G,K}(H))\geq c(n,G,K)\prod_{i=1}^n h_{ii}$.

The constant is chosen before the universally quantified matrix. The diagonal
entries of a Hermitian matrix are real. Accordingly, the native real inequality
uses $\prod_i\Re(h_{ii})$; `diagonal_product_eq_real` proves that its real embedding
equals the source's complex diagonal product.

The main assertion allows arbitrary subgroups. The author's separate interest
in products of symmetric groups on disjoint subsets, together with rank-two
matrices, is a restriction and is not the assertion refuted here. The separate
question with a trivial subgroup also remains outside this settlement.

## Counterexample in the source domain

Use the source's labels $1,\ldots,7$, the cyclic subgroups generated by

$$
g=(1\ 2\ 5\ 6\ 4\ 3\ 7),\qquad
k=(1\ 2\ 7\ 5\ 6\ 4\ 3),
$$

and the complex Gram matrix $H=V^*V$, where

$$
V=\begin{pmatrix}1&3&1&3&1&1&3\\2&2&-2&2&0&-3&-2\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Both generators have order seven. The complete double sum over their subgroups
is $-23944$, whereas the diagonal product is $549250$. Thus the proposed lower
bound fails for every positive real constant for this fixed subgroup pair.
The additional strict-positivity question fails on this same matrix; this is
one counterexample family and one main-assertion settlement.

## Literature scope and unresolved boundaries

The source qualification inspected the revision-8 body, the supplied complete
revision history, the primary comments, the linked question
[MO456435](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/456435/), and David Speyer's
[answer 456542](https://mathoverflow.net/a/456542). The latter refutes a further
arbitrary-operator generalization and explicitly leaves the original pure-tensor
question uncertain. An arbitrary-operator counterexample alone supplies no
Gram-matrix counterexample to the present statement.

The checked literature also includes arXiv:2508.13472v3, §4, Conjecture D and
Proposition 4.2; Zhang, arXiv:1608.02844v1; Wanless, arXiv:2202.01867v1; and
Tran, arXiv:2101.03428v5. Conjecture D restricts to Young subgroups and the
proposition treats nested partitions. The Schur-power spectral and related
permanent inequalities in the other references do not state a settlement of
the arbitrary-subgroup rectangular cross-sum identified above.

The bounded primary and literature checks found no earlier equivalent complete
settlement in that corpus. Zero answers on the inspected main page is only a
page observation. Exhaustive citation coverage, worldwide priority and absence
of a settlement outside the checked corpus are not established. The Young
subgroup restriction and the trivial-subgroup question are not decided here.
