## Conjecture 4.8

Page 18, Conjecture 4.8:

> The statement of Theorem 4.5 holds for any $x\in\mathbb P^1$ for which the corresponding VBKM algebra satisfies (4.3). In other words, the VBKM algebra $\tilde\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal F_x)$ contains no ideals not intersecting $\mathfrak{h}$.

Condition (4.3), on the same page, is:

> $\alpha(c_j)=0$ if and only if $\alpha(b_j)=0$ for any $\alpha\in\{\alpha_i,\gamma_k:i\in I,k\in J\setminus j\}$.

Here $\mathfrak{h}$ is the Cartan space, $\alpha_i$ the simple roots, and
$\gamma_j$ the Virasoro roots. The complex pairing is evaluated on the
actual Cartan generator images in the presented algebra. The associated
VBKM algebra is the quotient by the local ideals defined in Section 3.3.
The explanatory sentence of the conjecture writes the initial presentation
$\tilde\mathfrak{g}$; Theorem 4.5 writes its locally quotiented algebra $\mathfrak{g}$.
A counterexample with zero local ideal addresses both readings.

## Definitions and normalization

Section 3.2 specifies

> We define the Lie algebra $\tilde\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal F)$ generated by $\tilde\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal F')$, $L_n^j$, $c_j$ with the following additional relations:

$$
[L_n^j,L_m^j]=(n-m)L_{n+m}^j+\delta_{n,-m}(n^3-n)c_j,
\qquad [\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}]=0,
$$
$$
[h_i,L_n^j]=n\gamma_j(h_i)L_n^j,
\qquad L_0^j=b_j,
$$
$$
[L_n^j,L_m^k]=0\quad(nm<0,\ k\ne j),
\qquad [e_i,L_n^j]=[f_i,L_{-n}^j]=0\quad(n<0).
$$

The central generator in this presentation is one twelfth of the usual
Virasoro central-charge generator. Section 3.3 states:

> We define $\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal F):=\tilde\mathfrak{g}/\tilde I$ and call it the Virasoro-Borcherds-Kac-Moody algebra associated to $\mathcal F$.

Positive modes are indexed by positive naturals; the zero mode is a Cartan
element. Root decompositions are finitely supported multiplicities, without
an ordering of repeated summands. Homogeneous nonzero pairs $(s,t)$ represent
projective points. At $s\ne0$, the degree element is
$b_j+\tfrac12(t/s)^2c_j$; at $s=0$ the BKM datum has the additional positive
mode roots. No triangular decomposition or injectivity of the Cartan image
is assumed in the presentation.
