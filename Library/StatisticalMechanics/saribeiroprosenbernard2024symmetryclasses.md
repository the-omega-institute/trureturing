## Source statements

§1: "Conservation of probability requires that the Markov generator, an N × N matrix L, satisfies the three properties: L_ij ∈ R, (2) L_ij ≥ 0, i ≠ j, (3) Σ_{i=1}^N L_ij = 0, ∀j. (4)"

§2: "If L has a commuting symmetry, U L U^{−1} = L, (5) then, L and U can be simultaneously diagonalized. Inside each block of L, U acts trivially as a multiple of the identity. If no further commuting symmetries exist, we call such a block an irreducible block. … It is, however, natural to conjecture that the block containing the invariant measure of the Markov process (henceforth referred to as the steady-state block) can still be recast into Markov form. Indeed, consider an initial probability vector fully supported in the steady-state block. Under the Markov dynamics, the initial probability vector evolves into another admissible probability vector. … We conclude that the steady-state block maps probability vectors into probability vectors and it should be possible to write it in Markov form."

Appendix A: "To proceed, we assume that the steady-state block corresponds to the symmetric sector of U and, furthermore, that p_{a|i} ∈ {0, 1}. (A3)"

## Reading used by the repository

The symmetry U is real orthogonal and commutes with L; the steady-state block is the column space of an orthonormal real matrix V that is an entire eigenspace of U, is invariant under L and contains a stationary probability. It is irreducible when every complex unitary commuting with the restriction A = VᵀLV is a scalar. "Recast into Markov form" is read as: some invertible complex matrix S makes S⁻¹AS a real matrix satisfying Eqs. (2)–(4).
