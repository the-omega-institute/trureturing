B. Basu-Mallick, C. Datta, F. Finkel, A. González-López, arXiv:1503.08231v1
[cond-mat.stat-mech, hep-th, math-ph, nlin.SI] (2015); Nuclear Physics B 898

$(q^2)_j\equiv\prod_{i=1}^j(1-q^{2i})$, and the partition function of the

> where $f_{N,k}(q)$ is given by

$f_{N,k}(q)=\frac{q^{N-k}+q^{k}}{1+q^N}\,\qbinom N{k}{q^2}$.

The conjecture (Section 3):

> Although it is well known that the $q$-binomial coefficient $\qbinom N{k}{q^2}$ in \eq{m40} is indeed an even polynomial in $q$ of degree $2k(N-k)$~\cite{Ci79}, it is not clear whether $f_{N,k}(q)$ is also a polynomial. In fact, we have verified that this is the case for a wide range of values of $N$ and all $k\le N$. We conjecture that this is true in general

and in the conclusions (Section 6):

> Although this conjecture can be easily checked numerically, we have not been able to find an analytic proof thereof using the properties of $q$-binomial coefficients.
