# Increasing eigenfunctions and the hard-core application boundary

The primary source is [arXiv:math/0305056v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0305056v1).
This note cites the source without redistributing its text or PDF.

Lemma 3, attributed to Y. Peres, obtains an increasing eigenfunction
for the second eigenvalue by applying powers of a monotone kernel
to increasing functions and taking spectral limits. Lemma 4 uses
the Ising FKG inequality to identify the eigenvalue of a strictly
increasing eigenfunction. Those are distinct arguments with
different required hypotheses.

Lemma 5 and Corollary 6 specialize to ferromagnetic Ising on a
cycle, with zero external field, positive couplings and uniform
vertex selection. The source proves that linear spin functions
form an invariant subspace and obtains a linear gap eigenfunction.
It also treats a straight line through a zero-boundary-coupling
limit. This is an Ising result, not a hard-core path theorem.

For the positive weighted hard-core path, particle-hole inversion
on one bipartite class gives a monotone legal configuration poset.
To use the projection method there, one must establish monotone
conditional updates and show that increasing functions span this
legal carrier. Principal-upset indicators provide the finite-poset
spanning argument; the continuous-time semigroup then avoids any
negative transition eigenvalue issue.

The hard-core site update contains a neighboring quadratic
occupation product at interior sites. Nacu's linear invariance
cannot be imported into that model. An increasing gap eigenfunction
can instead be paired with the strictly ordered staggered occupation
sum through the actual positive-conductance Dirichlet form. The
resulting overlap bound and its classical/quantum correlation
application are separate deductions from the hard-core law.

Nonzero slow overlap does not identify a linear signal's immediate
Rayleigh quotient with the gap, nor imply a uniform finite-time
readout, bounded polynomial degree or physical control protocol.
