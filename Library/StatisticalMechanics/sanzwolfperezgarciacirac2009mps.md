# Sanz, Wolf, Pérez-García and Cirac, Matrix product states: Symmetries and two-body Hamiltonians

M. Sanz, M. M. Wolf, D. Pérez-García and J. I. Cirac, Phys. Rev. A 79, 042308 (2009); arXiv:0901.2223v1. Quotations are from the v1 TeX source.

A translationally invariant MPS is $|\Phi\rangle=\sum_{i_1,\dots,i_N}\operatorname{tr}(A_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_N})|i_1\cdots i_N\rangle$ with Kraus operators $A_i\in\mathcal M_D$. It is invariant under a group $G$ if $u_g^{\otimes N}|\Psi\rangle=e^{i\theta_g}|\Psi\rangle$ for a unitary representation $u_g$. It is injective if for some $n$ the map $\Gamma_n(X)=\sum_{i_1,\dots,i_n}\operatorname{tr}(XA_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_n})|i_1\cdots i_n\rangle$ is injective. Section IV proves that an $SU(2)$-symmetric MPS with even physical dimension cannot be injective. It gives an injective MPS of physical dimension $D^2-D$ invariant under a $U(1)$ symmetry whose generator has only non-zero eigenvalues, and proves that physical dimension $2$ with $U(1)$ symmetry excludes injectivity. > Let us remark that this counter-example is applicable to spin $\ge \frac{5}{2}$. Indeed, one can prove Theorem \ref{} for $U(1)$ and spin $\frac{1}{2}$, which is the content of the following proposition. The case of spin $3/2$ remains an open question.
