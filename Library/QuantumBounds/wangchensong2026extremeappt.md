## Source question

Section VII, Conclusions, page 29, asks:

> Fourth, what is the achievable upper bound of distinct eigenvalues of extreme two-qutrit AP states?

The state space consists of positive semidefinite, trace-one operators on
$\mathbb C^3\otimes\mathbb C^3$. Absolute PPT requires the partial transpose
of every global unitary conjugate to be positive semidefinite. Extremality
means membership in the extreme points of the convex set of those states.
Distinct eigenvalues are counted without multiplicity.

The answer formalized in QutritExtremeSixLevels is six, including attainment.
The journal edition's full text remains ASSUMED-UNVERIFIED; the arXiv source
contains the quoted question.

## State definition

Definition 1, page 3:

> A separable (resp. PPT) state ρ is called absolutely separable (resp. PPT) if it remains separable (PPT) regardless of what global unitary operation is applied. That is, for any unitary matrix U, the state UρU† is separable (resp. PPT).

The formalization uses the PPT case. Density matrices are positive semidefinite
and have trace one; the partial transpose is taken on the second local factor.
