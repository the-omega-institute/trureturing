## Verified locator

[OEIS A235289, revision 7, June 18, 2022](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oeis/oeisdata/571197984aeda7b8b680fe3a1f6a43f9898cd441/seq/A235/A235289.seq) is unpaginated. Its NAME is

> T(n,k) is the number of (n+1) X (k+1) 0..3 arrays with every 2 X 2 subblock having its diagonal sum differing from its antidiagonal sum by 4 (constant-stress 1 X 1 tilings).

The relevant FORMULA lines are

> Empirical for column k (the recurrence for k=2..7 also works for k=1; apparently every row and column satisfies the same order 6 recurrence):
>
> k=2..7: a(n) = 3*a(n-1) +3*a(n-2) -15*a(n-3) +4*a(n-4) +18*a(n-5) -12*a(n-6).

The OFFSET is `1,1`.

## Mathematical use

The count includes every literal filling of the rectangle by 0, 1, 2 and 3
whose contiguous blocks have absolute diagonal difference four. The row
and column indices of the table are positive. The common recurrence is an
identity over integers and applies when the varying index is at least
seven. A row or column can also satisfy a recurrence of smaller order.

Checkerboard complementation turns the block rule into a choice of block
sums two or ten. Blocks sharing an edge must make the same choice. Global
complementation exchanges the two classes, so the count is twice the count
of sum-two fillings. In those fillings, the adjacent horizontal sums in
each row determine the next row's sums by complementing each sum in two.
The resulting alternating row multiplicities yield the recurrence.
Transposition interchanges its two axes. The square-diagonal recurrence
and the proposed extensions to other alphabets are separate claims.
