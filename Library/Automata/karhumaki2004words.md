## Statement and correspondence

For finite words u and v, Theorem 1 equates commutation, uv=vu, with
membership of both words in the submonoid generated by a common word t.
The proof explicitly handles an empty input before considering the case
of two nonempty words. It then removes a shorter prefix and applies
induction to a commuting pair with smaller total length.

`CommutingWords.commute_iff_common_power` represents words as lists,
concatenation as list append, and membership in the singly generated
submonoid by natural exponents of the existing `wordPower` operation.
Zero exponents and the empty common word are allowed. When only one input
is empty, the other input can be chosen as the common word, with exponents
zero and one in the appropriate order.

The report introduces a finite alphabet in Section 3. The Lean statement
uses an arbitrary alphabet type in any universe, with no `Finite`,
`Fintype`, or `DecidableEq` assumption. The length descent and prefix
splitting do not require a finite alphabet. This is a formal extension of
the stated source scope; it carries no novelty assertion.

The equivalence uses only clauses (i)-(ii). It makes no assertion about
primitive-root uniqueness, a shortest common root, or clause (iii)'s
nontrivial relations.
