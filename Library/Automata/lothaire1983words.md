## Statement and correspondence

For every universe level u, every type A in Type u, and every nonempty
finite word w over A, exactly one nonempty word r satisfies: a finite
word v commutes with w if and only if v is a natural concatenation power
of r. The exponent zero gives the empty word. The claim imposes no
finiteness, ordering, inhabitedness, or decidable equality condition on
A. An empty alphabet admits no nonempty input w, and infinite alphabets
remain in scope.

Words are represented by List A, concatenation by list append, and
natural powers by D5.S1.Words.Powers.wordPower. The uniqueness is of the
literal list r, not merely of its length, its conjugacy class, or a
chosen pairwise common root. Its centralizer-generating property forces
it to be primitive: a proper positive-power root would itself commute
with w and hence be a power of r, contradicting positive lengths.

The argument chooses a shortest nonempty positive-power root r of w.
Any other positive-power root s of the same w commutes with r by the
Fine–Wilf bridge below. The public pairwise theorem
CommutingWords.commute_iff_common_power writes r and s as powers of t;
then t is a positive-power root of w. Minimal length forces r=t.
Consequently every commuting word is a power of the same r. Two proposed
centralizer generators are powers of each other; their nonzero lengths
force the exponents to be one and the lists to be equal.

Only Proposition 1.3.2 and the correct nonempty uniqueness sentence of
Proposition 1.3.1 are cited. The first common-power clause displayed in
1.3.1 is not used with unrestricted zero exponents. This is a classical
classification, with no novelty claim.
