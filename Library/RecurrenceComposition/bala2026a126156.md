## Source statements

> Expansion of e.g.f. sqrt(sec(sqrt(2)*x)), showing coefficients of only the even powers of x.

> Conjecture: the sequence {a(n)} reduced modulo a positive integer k is eventually periodic with the period dividing phi(k), where phi(n) = A000010(n). An example is given below. - _Peter Bala_, Mar 01 2026

> E.g.f.: Sum_{n>=0} a(n)*x^(2*n)/(2*n)! = sqrt(sec(sqrt(2)*x)). - _David Callan_, Jan 03 2011

The offset is zero. The rational series A has constant coefficient one and
satisfies A² cos(√2 x) = 1, with the cosine coefficient at an even j equal to
(-2)^(j/2)/j! and at an odd j equal to zero. The sequence term is
(2n)! [x^(2n)] A; its factorial expression is used directly in the universal
period assertion. The triangle A126155 is an alternate source representation;
the formal argument uses the EGF and does not assume a triangle identity.

## Mathematical boundary

The repository derivation excludes every eventual onset for a positive period
dividing φ(4)=2. Integer derivative polynomials give two-step differences
congruent to two modulo four; parity follows by induction. This refutes the
universal period-divides-totient assertion. A universal bound of 2φ(k) and a
general odd-modulus theorem remain open here.
