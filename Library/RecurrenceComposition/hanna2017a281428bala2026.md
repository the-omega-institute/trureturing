## Verified locator

Source: https://oeis.org/A281428

The source is OEIS A281428, internal revision 49. Its NAME and Peter Bala's
COMMENTS item 1 state, verbatim (the entry has no page numbers):

> E.g.f. C(x) satisfies C(x) = cosh( Integral C(x)^4 dx ).

> 1) If N is not divisble by either 2 or 3 then there exists n_0 such that N divides a(n) for all n >= n_0.

The EXAMPLE fixes the normalization and compressed indexing:

> E.g.f.: C(x) = 1 + x^2/2! + 17*x^4/4! + 865*x^6/6! + 88865*x^8/8! + 15335425*x^10/10! + 3993275825*x^12/12! + 1462392957025*x^14/14! + 716611617346625*x^16/16! + 452780458211706625*x^18/18! + 358439197464543820625*x^20/20! +...

Thus a(n)=(2n)![x^(2n)]C, not n![x^n]C. The formal reading is over
rational power series, with C(0)=1 and integral constant coefficient zero.
The hyperbolic cosine of an argument F with constant coefficient zero is
(exp(F)+exp(-F))/2. Divisibility means equality to N times an integer;
it does not mean divisibility in the field of rationals. The source has
no DOI or arXiv identifier.

The proof combines integer factorial-normalized coefficients with ordinary
coefficients whose denominators have no prime factors other than 2 and 3.
For N positive and coprime to 6, factorial divisibility and Euclid
cancellation give the sufficient onset (N+1)/2, using natural division.
Minimal onsets, the universal onset 6 modulo 385, and item 2 remain open here.
