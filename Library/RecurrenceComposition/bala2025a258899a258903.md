## Source statements

Both COMMENTS fields contain the same conjecture sentence, quoted verbatim:

> Conjecture: the sequence a(n) taken modulo a positive integer k is eventually periodic with the period dividing phi(k).

A258899 NAME:

> E.g.f.: 2 - exp(2) + Sum_{n>=1} 2^n * exp(x^n) / n!.

A258899 FORMULA:

> For n >= 1, a(n) = Sum_{d divides n} 2^d * n!/(d!*(n/d)!).

A258903 NAME:

> E.g.f.: 2 - exp(2) + Sum_{n>=1} 2^n * exp(3*x^n) / n!.

A258903 FORMULA:

> For n >= 1, a(n) = Sum_{d divides n} 2^d * 3^(n/d) * n!/(d!*(n/d)!).

Both entries have offset zero and a(0)=1. The A258903 COMMENTS example
modulo 7 begins at n=1; it does not change the value at index zero.

## Mathematical interpretation

The Lean definitions use the two finite FORMULA expressions with the stated
initial value. The natural divisor quotient is exact on positive divisors.
The factorial denominator is positive. For a factorization n=de with d,e>=2,
d+e<=n and factorial divisibility gives d!e! dividing n!; at each endpoint
the quotient is 1. Natural-number division therefore agrees with the
integer quotient appearing in the source formulas.

The two assertions independently quantify a positive natural modulus m,
an onset N, a positive period T dividing Nat.totient m, and all n>=N.
The module proves both with N=2*m+4 and T=Nat.totient m, including m=1.
Every proper-factor term vanishes modulo m after that onset, leaving
u*v^n+v*u^n for arbitrary natural weights u,v. The existing nonunit-inclusive
power-period theorem then applies to the two bases. No minimal onset or
minimal period is claimed.
