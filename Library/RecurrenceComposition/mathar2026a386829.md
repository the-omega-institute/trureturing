# Mathar recurrence and D-finiteness conjectures

## OEIS A386829

The sequence definition is due to Seiichi Manyama. Mathar's FORMULA entry
asks for D-finiteness with the displayed recurrence. The definition sums
3^k * 2^(n-k) * binomial(2*n+1,k) * binomial(2*n-k,n-k)
over every k from zero through n, including both endpoints.
The offset is zero. The recurrence is interpreted on its entire valid
nonnegative-index domain n >= 2; the source does not explicitly state an onset.

The official export is revision 12. Approval of that entry is editorial
approval, and does not certify the conjecture. The finite source comparison
with A385669, A386763 and A386830 supplies no complete proof of this assertion;
worldwide priority and exhaustive absence of a prior proof are unverified.

## OEIS A001722

The entry is **Generalized Stirling numbers [n+7,7]_5**. Crstici's formula is

`a(n) = Sum(k=0..n) (-1)^(n+k)*binomial(k+2,2)*5^k*Stirling1(n+2,k+2)`.

The signed first-kind convention is the coefficient of X^K in
`Product(i=0..N-1)(X-i)`, with empty product one. This convention retains the
sign in the displayed sum; it is not the unsigned natural Stirling number.

Mathar's formula-field conjecture reads:

> Conjecture D-finite with recurrence a(n) +3*(-n-5)*a(n-1)
> +(3*n^2+27*n+61)*a(n-2) -(n+4)^3*a(n-3)=0.

The source states no onset inequality. The complete natural-index domain of
these four terms is n >= 3, including n=3. D-finiteness is interpreted for
the ordinary rational formal series with coefficients a(n), and requires
a nonzero homogeneous polynomial-coefficient differential operator. No
convergence hypothesis or initial-value definition is substituted for the sum.
The source records a conjecture, not a proof of either assertion.

## A001722 source qualification

The bounded literature and duplicate scope is stated in
[issue #27903](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/issues/27903).
The inspected A001722/A1722/M5061/N2191 identifier searches have no exact
full-assertion match in that scope. Generalized-Stirling hits about the
monic-annihilator transform and Maier boson ordering concern different
statements. The recorded Janjic, Plouffe and Niu readings provide no verified
specialized proof of both original clauses. Generic identities are reusable
proof ingredients; their availability alone does not certify a prior published
settlement. Worldwide openness, priority and exhaustive absence of prior proofs
remain unverified.
