# Compressed descriptions and fixed finite values

The survey's title, author, year and DOI are verified by its Crossref record. It is a bibliographic pointer for SLP-compressed strings. Its full text has not been verified here, and no theorem or precise complexity bound from the survey is imported. The finite-algebra argument below is self-contained elementary exposition, not an attribution of its statement to a numbered result in that source and not a novelty claim.

## Fixed finite algebra evaluation

Fix a finite group $G$, a finite alphabet $\Sigma$, a generator interpretation $\eta:\Sigma\to G$, and its finite multiplication table. Suppose that for every value in the submonoid generated by $\eta(\Sigma)$ a representative word over $\Sigma$ is fixed. Their maximum length $C$ is a constant because this value set is fixed and finite. The empty word may represent the identity. These data are independent of the input.

An input consists of finitely many explicitly encoded nodes, each either a letter or an ordered concatenation of two referenced nodes, with a designated root. References must be valid and the dependency graph acyclic. Its bit encoding length is $B$ and its node count is $s$. Shared subexpressions denote repeated word occurrences in the root's unfolding, rather than multiple executions of the value-computation node.

**Proposition (Polynomial acquisition of a bounded representative).** A single deterministic algorithm outputs a word of length at most $C$ having the same group value as the fully unfolded root, in $\operatorname{poly}(B)$ bit operations.

Proof. Validate the encoding and obtain a topological order. At a letter store $\eta$ of its label; at a concatenation node store the ordered product of its two stored values. By induction on that order, every stored value equals the value of its unfolded subword. There are $O(s)$ fixed-table multiplications, each operating on constant-size labels. Output the fixed representative of the root value.

The $O(s)$ count concerns algebraic multiplications, not free pointer access. Input parsing, cycle detection, topological sorting, reading and storing references, and retrieving child labels all have ordinary polynomial bit implementations; even repeated scans of the explicit node list suffice. Output cost is bounded by the fixed representative length. No expanded word is needed. The proof uses a homomorphic value map and finite closure; no particular group identity is assumed. $\square$

For the fixed matrices $a=-iX,b=-iY$, the generated group is $Q_8$. The empty word and $a,b,ba,a^2,a^3,b^3,ab$ represent its eight values, with maximum length three. The exact FIB application, its matrix multiplication convention, binary-index shortcut and future-environment limitations are stated in [Second-order relation completion §116](../../docs/develop/theory/AURIC_FIB_SECOND_ORDER_RELATION_COMPLETION.md#116-固定-pauli-端口上的-fib-统一短编译).

The finiteness hypothesis is substantive. If the group, multiplication table, generators, target precision or representation dimension grows with the input, their description, construction, arithmetic and representative lengths must be counted separately. Equality of endpoint values also does not reproduce the original word, its parse tree, execution clock or intermediate records. Requiring that expanded output can impose a cost proportional to its length. This argument is not a general short-circuit compiler for arbitrary quantum circuits.
