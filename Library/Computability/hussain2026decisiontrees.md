## Verified locator

Section 6, Question 3 asks:

> In case (c), is $h^{\mathrm{rd}}_L(n)$ eventually constant?

Case (c) is Theorem 1(c). Its hypotheses are a finite alphabet with at
least two letters, a language closed under scattered subwords, and
finite Hom and Het. The theorem gives uniform boundedness of the
recognition depth. Question 3 asks for equality to one constant on a tail.
[Question 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.27454v1#S6.I1.i3.p1.1) · [Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.27454v1#Thmtheorem1)

Definitions 3–6 specify the deterministic tree, its complete paths and
their required labels on the length-$n$ promise. Definition 8 takes the
maximum over all complete paths and sets the optimum to zero when the
promise is empty. [Definitions 3–8](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.27454v1#S2)

The two exponent sets use the patterns $a^e b a^e$ and $a^e b^e$,
respectively, with $a\ne b$. The finite-dimension hypotheses are
equivalent to finiteness of those sets, with the empty-set dimension
convention included. [Definitions 1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.27454v1#Thmdefinition1)
