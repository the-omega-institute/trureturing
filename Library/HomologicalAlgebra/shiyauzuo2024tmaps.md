## Source statement and conventions

> Conjecture 4.79. Let F be an arbitrary field, let A be a local Noetherian F-algebra with maximal ideal m, and let Q be a fixed T-map. Suppose A/m ≃ F Then, for an ideal I ◁ A, I is T-principal if and only if I is T-full and Δ(I) is T-dependent.

The quotation normalizes line wrapping and mathematical typography.

- Definition 4.1 (English p. 1195, original p. 200) sets Q(f) equal to the
  ideal generated by the values of finitely many linear component maps and
  requires Q(af) ⊆ Q(f) for all a and f. Remark 4.3 makes those component
  maps part of the data. The paragraph after Example 4.5 defines
  T-principality by I = Q(f) for some f.
- Definition 4.6 (English p. 1196, original p. 201) defines Q(J) as the ideal
  sum of the Q(f) for f in J. The sum over a generating tuple of J gives the
  same ideal. Definition 4.8 on the same pages gives
  Δ(I) = {f | Q(f) ⊆ I}. Remark 4.9 identifies Δ(I) as an ideal.
- Definition 4.66 (English p. 1213, original p. 219) defines T-fullness by
  Q(Δ(I)) = I. Proposition 4.67 states that T-principality implies fullness.
- Definition 4.68 (English p. 1214, original p. 219) uses a generating
  tuple g of J, S = A[y₁, …, yᵣ], and σ = ∑ᵢ gᵢyᵢ. The components act
  coefficientwise on σ, and their values generate Q(σ). The colon
  (Q(σ) : Q(J)S) must contain a polynomial outside mS. The paper also gives
  an equivalent projective-localization formulation. Proposition 4.69 states
  independence of the choice of generators of J. The Lean predicate uses
  the colon formulation with an existential finite generating tuple in the
  Noetherian setting.
- Section 4.6 assumes F infinite for Theorem 4.76 (English p. 1216,
  original p. 221). Lemmas 4.72–4.73 and Remark 4.74 (English p. 1215,
  original p. 221) identify the infinite-field step through homogeneous
  polynomials on the rational projective space. Remark 4.77 and Lemma 4.81
  relate generators to rational points and coefficient reduction.

## Scope of the refutation

The literal negation of the conjecture follows from one example over the
two-element field. Take the trivial square-zero extension by a vector space of
dimension three and let I be the maximal ideal, the kernel of the scalar
projection. That projection identifies the residue field with the ground
field as an algebra over the ground field. The three component values of a
nilpotent element use the forms u, v and u + v on separate axes. The ideal is
T-full and its antiderivative ideal is itself. The polynomial UV(U + V) lies
in the colon and survives reduction modulo the extended maximal ideal, but
every binary pair makes one of the forms vanish. Every nilpotent input
therefore misses an axis, and a unit has value ideal A. This refutes the
sufficiency direction over arbitrary fields. Theorem 4.76 retains its
infinite-field hypotheses.
