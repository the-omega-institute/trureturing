# OEIS A350900

Werner Schulte's row formula defines exact natural-number gcd quotients.
The row-sum statement identifies their sum as a Dirichlet convolution:
A002618(n) is `n * phi(n)`, and A057660(n) is
`Sum_{d|n} d * phi(d)`. The module SchulteGcdQuotientRowSumConvolution concerns only this
identity-weight specialization. The arbitrary-function statement uses the
same ordered square with an arbitrary complex weight on each exact quotient.

OEIS A373059 identifies the same double sum as the row sums of A350900.
Seiichi Manyama recorded the equivalent formula in revision 11 on May 24,
2024, at 11:06:04 EDT:

`Sum_{d|n} phi(n/d) * (n/d) * sigma_2(d^2)/sigma(d^2)`.

OEIS A057660 gives
`sigma_2(d^2)/sigma(d^2) = Sum_{e|d} e * phi(e)`, so this is the same
convolution formula, although A350900 continues to label its row-sum formula
as a conjecture. The A373059 formula is prior documentation of the equivalent
identity; no first-discovery or first-resolution claim is made here.

László Tóth's survey *A Survey of Gcd-Sum Functions*, Journal of
Integer Sequences 13 (2010), Article 10.8.1, records Cesàro's general formula

`Sum_{k=1..n} f(gcd(k,n)) = Sum_{d|n} f(d) * phi(n/d)`

for an arbitrary arithmetic function `f`, and records A057660 as the sum of
the orders of elements of a cyclic group. This general fibre-counting theorem
explains the proof mechanism, but the survey does not state the nested
A350900 row-sum identity.

## Row-sum literature scope

The complete inspected histories of A350900 (13 revisions) and A373059
(19 revisions), together with Tóth's 2010 survey, arXiv:2110.07271,
arXiv:1306.1020, and Bordellès's 2015 *Journal of Integer Sequences* paper,
yielded no complete prior proof of the A350900 row-sum identity. The result is
`not-found-in-searched-scope`, not a claim that the proof is globally novel.

Material limits are that A057660 revisions 63 through 1 were not inspected;
the Gould-Shonhiwa 1997 full texts, Tóth's 1998 regular-convolution paper,
some cyclic-group sources, and the U338 solution were not obtained.

## Arbitrary-function statement and applicability

The COMMENTS sentence in revision 13, May 24 2024 at 11:58:43, is:

> Conjecture: Let f be an arbitrary arithmetic function. Define for n > 0 the sequence a(f; n) = Sum_{i=1..n, k=1..n} f(gcd(i,n)/gcd(gcd(i,k),n)); a(f; n) equals Dirichlet convolution of f(n)*A000010(n) and A057660(n); if f is multiplicative, then a(f; n) is multiplicative; row sums of this triangle use f(n) = n (see formula section).

The entry is attributed to Werner Schulte, January 21 2022. The statement
is interpreted for every function from positive integers to complex
numbers and every positive n. The quotient is natural, exact and positive:
gcd(gcd(i,k),n) is a positive divisor of gcd(i,n). Thus arbitrary extensions
at zero give the same ordered-square sum. The convolution has the
pointwise product f(d)*phi(d) as its first argument and H(m)=Sum_{e|m}
e*phi(e) as its second argument. Multiplicativity uses f(1)=1 and the
coprime positive product law. The separate triangle-symmetry comment is
not part of this assertion.

The classical single-gcd Cesaro identity in Toth's survey is an ingredient,
not the displayed nested-quotient formula. Bordelles's *A Multidimensional
Cesaro Type Identity and Applications* (2015), Proposition 2, concerns
arbitrary complex functions of separate gcd(i_j,n) values. Its asymptotic
statements with complete multiplicativity do not supply the unrestricted
nested-quotient assertion. Shonhiwa's *Investigations in number theoretic
functions* (1996), Chapter 2, printed pages 51--87, gives single-gcd Cesaro,
weighted lcm and divisor-convolution identities. No equivalent whole
arbitrary-function assertion was located in that inspected chapter. These
are bounded correspondence conclusions, without a claim of global
absence or priority.

The originals Gould--Shonhiwa, *Functions of GCD's and LCM's*, Indian
Journal of Mathematics 39(1) (1997), 11--35, and Shonhiwa--Gould, *A
generalization of Cesaro's function and other results*, 39(2) (1997),
183--194, remain unread. The thesis is not identified with either article.
Their contents, the complete later citing network and global historical
priority remain ASSUMED-UNVERIFIED. An equivalent whole prior result in
these or other uninspected sources is not excluded.
