# Finite measurable moments and actual convex hulls

The primary preprint is math/0502473v2, 4 May 2005, six pages,
SHA256 `58b258713c29a68c09f93d1d5f0bdff409f2804435ca1861133b9ded2c1ccb47`.
Corollary 2 on pages 3–4 treats arbitrary measurable finite moments and
actual point evaluations. Remark 2 on page 3 identifies actual convex-hull
membership for a probability measure. Compactness, continuity of the moment
map and closedness of its image are not hypotheses.

For an integrable map into a finite-dimensional real normed space, its
probability barycenter belongs to the convex hull of its range. The project
proof uses induction on dimension. If the range spans a proper linear
subspace, integration and the problem restrict to that subspace. Otherwise,
a supporting functional at a hypothetical missing zero barycenter has one
sign on the range and integral zero. It therefore vanishes almost
everywhere. Repairing the null set by an actual zero-functional point reduces
the problem to the functional's proper kernel. Translation gives the general
barycenter statement. A finite convex combination then gives exact cubature.
Any prescribed conull set can contain every selected point, by repairing its
complement before applying the barycenter theorem. A zero measure uses no
labels. The formal interface asserts finite existence without a numeric
cardinality bound.

For a positive trace-preserving complex-linear map from an actual complex
L1 space into finite matrices, trace domination proves continuity and
constructs coordinate complex measures from the map's indicator action.
The existing positive-history density theorem supplies matrix densities;
L1 density of simple functions extends their set identities to all real L1
inputs. Reweighting by the sum of two nonnegative input densities gives a
finite measure. Two matrix moments and one integrable real cost can then be
atomized together. Positive homogeneity of the closed perspective preserves
that one specified cost exactly, including when the convex endpoint values
are infinite. This does not preserve every convex cost or the whole profile
simultaneously, and does not assume existence of an optimizer.
