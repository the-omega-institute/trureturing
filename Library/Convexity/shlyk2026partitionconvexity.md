# Shlyk on the convexity index of integer partitions

The convexity index of a nonvertex partition is the least number of other
partitions of the same total in a convex representation. The source uses
natural multiplicities of the positive parts. A representation uses positive
real coefficients summing to one. Repeated identical summands can be grouped
without changing the average, so the minimum uses distinct summands.

Section 1.2 states eventual existence for each index at least two. Section
3.1 restates this assertion and also asks whether the class sizes decrease
with the index at a fixed total. Conjecture 1 in the 2018 preprint gives these
as separate sentences. Its first sentence explicitly uses a strict threshold
depending on the index. The earlier paper gives small-index results through
index four. The 2026 paper reports 38 index-five examples at particular totals.

The formal theorem proves the whole eventual-existence assertion. At a
constructed total, the supported partition fiber consists of one center and
its indexed alternatives. Each alternative is forced by its own positive
coordinate of the center, and the equal-weight average attains the lower
bound on cardinality. Adding a single part larger than half the new total
preserves the exact index. Class-size monotonicity and the least admissible
total for a general index remain separate questions.

Strong aggregation of nonnegative integer equations into a single equation
has precedents in Gordon H. Bradley, *Transformation of Integer Programs to
Knapsack Problems* (1971), and I. G. Rosenberg, *Aggregation of Equations in
Integer Programming* (1974). The partition argument uses aggregation on a
specific congruence fiber with distinct positive part sizes, then transfers
the exact convexity index to every strictly larger total above twice the
constructed total. The literature screen found no exact settlement in the
consulted editions and indexed related work. The publisher's journal full
text, parts of the citation record, and global priority remain unverified.
