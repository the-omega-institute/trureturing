## Source statement

Section 7, Question (5), arXiv v2 p. 21, journal p. 380:

> Does an integral Fourier matrix s exist with a column k such that ∀i sᵢₖ ≠ 0 and ∃i sᵢₖ ≠ ±1?

Definition 4.1 (arXiv v2 pp. 8–9) starts:

> Let s be an integral Fourier matrix, i.e. s ∈ ℤ^{n×n} and

Its conditions (1)–(4) are the first column sᵢ₁ = 1 and det(s) ≠ 0; s sᵀ = diag(d₁,…,dₙ); √dⱼ sᵢⱼ = √dᵢ sⱼᵢ; and the integrality of every sum ∑ₖ sₖᵢ sₖⱼ sₖₗ/dₖ. Definition 3.1 (arXiv v2 pp. 3–4) states:

> Here, √dᵢ is chosen greater than 0.

Definition 4.1 calls these matrices integral Fourier matrices before imposing the extra congruence conditions. No T-matrix is required for Question (5). The defining matrix equation determines row norms; the reversed indices in the explanatory norm sum of Definition 3.1 do not add another equation.

## Scope

The existential question has a negative answer by a weighted column-square identity and integer-square rigidity. This does not classify integral Fourier matrices, decide the equal-norm question, or establish the existence of a T-matrix.
