## A paid large-prime collision term

$$

\tag{M3}
$$

$$
 |r-r_*|<10^{-3},\qquad |m-m_*|<10^{-3},
\tag{M4}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_u|A_{{\rm hi},u}|^2
 &\ll_\varepsilon Q^{-1/3+\varepsilon}F_6(Q,L)
                         \mathcal P_J^2(1+H)^C\\
 &\ll_\varepsilon Q^{T_*-1/100+\varepsilon}
                         \mathcal P_J^2(1+H)^C,\\
 F_6(Q,L)&=Q+L+Q^{5/6}L^{1/3}+Q^{1/3}L^{5/6}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{M5}
$$

**Proof.** The actual coefficient is supported on $q_h\asymp L$ and is
bounded by a fixed profile constant times $\tau_3(h)\ll_\varepsilon

$q_h\ge P_0^6=Q^2>CL$. Thus the collision survives in $a$.
For $B=CL/q_b^6$, elementary ideal counting gives

$$
 \#\{a:q_a\le B,\ \exists p,\ q_p\ge P_0,\ p^2\mid a\}
 \ll B\sum_{q_p\ge P_0}q_p^{-2}\ll B/P_0.
\tag{M6}
$$

Only primes with $q_p^2\le B$ enter the count. The bound uses ordinary
ideal counting and its convergent tail; no new rough-prime sieve is
invoked. Consequently, for each fixed $b$,

$$
 \sum_a|L^{-1/2}c_{\rm hi}(ab^6)|^2
 \ll_\varepsilon Q^\varepsilon P_0^{-1}q_b^{-6}\mathcal P_J^2.
$$

The exact character at this column is

$$

 \|A_0\|_2\le\|A\|_2+\|A_{\rm hi}\|_2.
\tag{M7}
$$

## A lower bound for the complete remainder coefficient

For every fixed nonzero complex smooth annular $V_M,V_S$, on any compact
subset of $0<m<r<3m$ containing M4, for sufficiently large $Q$,

$$
 \sum_h|c_0(h)|^2\gg_{V_M,V_S} {L\over(\log Q)^4}.
\tag{M8}
$$

The constants may also depend on the compact set and fixed arithmetic
data. This is an assertion about the actual coefficient, not the row
moment or a sixth-power-compressed coefficient with cancellations between
$b$ values.

**Proof.** Set $P_1=Q^{(r+m)/2}$ and $P_2=Q^{(r-m)/2}$.
The strict wedge gives $P_2\ll N\ll P_1$, with fixed power gaps on
the compact set. Choose four distinct good primes with norms
$P_1,P_2,N,N$ up to fixed relative factors. Choose their small normalized
intervals so that $q_{p_1}q_{p_2}/D$ lies in an interval on which $|V_M|$
is bounded below, and both $q_{p_3}/N,q_{p_4}/N$ lie where $|V_S|$
is bounded below. Such intervals exist even for complex profiles. The
last two can be chosen disjoint inside a nonzero interval.

For $h=p_1p_2p_3p_4$, the only divisors in the plain annulus are
$p_3,p_4$: the unit and $p_2$ are too small, $p_1$ is too large,
and every divisor with at least two primes is too large. Hence the
**complete** original coefficient has exactly two summands, both with
$d=p_1p_2$ and $\mu(d)=1$:

$$
 c_0(p_1p_2p_3p_4)
 =2V_M(q_{p_1}q_{p_2}/D)V_S(q_{p_3}/N)V_S(q_{p_4}/N).
\tag{M9}
$$

There are no other divisor terms available to cancel this value. The
fixed-field prime-ideal theorem, in the form of the pinned primary's
`lem:ray-prime-normalizer`, gives respectively
$\gg P_1/\log Q,P_2/\log Q,N/\log Q,N/\log Q$ primes in these
fixed relative intervals. Unique ideal factorization gives bounded
multiplicity for $h$. There are therefore $\gg L/(\log Q)^4$ such
squarefree $h$, proving M8. If fixed ray classes are needed, choose
compatible fixed classes and use the corresponding fixed extension;
no growing-ray prime theorem is invoked. The lower constant is for fixed
profiles, not arbitrary profiles varying with $Q$ or height.

Multiplication by an allowed good-ideal ray character has modulus one;
negative orientation has the same norm. In particular,
$\sum_h|c_0(h)|^2\ll LQ^{-\delta}$ is false for every fixed
$\delta>0$. At $x_*$ the generic sieve exponent is

$$
 J_6(x_*)={1\over3}+{5\sqrt6\over9},\qquad
 J_6(x_*)-T_*=1-{5\sqrt6\over18}>0.
\tag{M10}
$$

One cannot pay that gap by a power saving in the complete coefficient
norm. M8 does not prove sharpness of the generic sieve on $c_0$, give a
row-moment lower bound, exclude inter-column cancellation, or refute the
mixed-moment target. The four-prime packet diagnoses the full coefficient;
it is not substituted for the complete physical rows or polynomial.
