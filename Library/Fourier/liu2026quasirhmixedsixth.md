The source's finite exceptional-row treatment remains separate. $Q$ is
physical norm, never the norm of a primitive conductor.

Fix an original presentation $\psi_u(h)=\nu(h)\chi_h(u)^{\pm1}$, with
conjugation as the meaning of the negative orientation even at zero.
Use the same presentation and selected norm height in all three slots.
For original smooth annular profiles, put

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=Q^r,\quad N=Q^m,\quad L=DN^2=Q^x,\quad x=r+2m,\\

\end{aligned}
\tag{M1}
$$

The last equality is exact zero-extended multiplicativity. There is no

 r_*={\sqrt6\over3},\quad m_*={\sqrt6\over6},\quad

\tag{M2}
$$

## A paid large-prime collision term

Let $P_0=Q^{1/3}$ and define the internal coefficient split

$$
 c_{\rm hi}(h)=c(h)\mathbf1_{\exists\,p\notin S:
                       q_p\ge P_0,\ p^2\mid h},\qquad
 c_0=c-c_{\rm hi},\qquad
 A_{b,u}=L^{-1/2}\sum_hc_b(h)\psi_u(h),\quad b\in\{0,\mathrm{hi}\}.
\tag{M3}
$$

Conditional on de Faveri's theorem and the owned transport, throughout

$$
 |r-r_*|<10^{-3},\qquad |m-m_*|<10^{-3},
\tag{M4}
$$

one has, for every prescribed $\varepsilon>0$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_u|A_{{\rm hi},u}|^2
 &\ll_\varepsilon Q^{-1/3+\varepsilon}F_6(Q,L)
                         \mathcal P_J^2(1+H)^C\\
 &\ll_\varepsilon Q^{T_*-1/100+\varepsilon}
                         \mathcal P_J^2(1+H)^C,\\
 F_6(Q,L)&=Q+L+Q^{5/6}L^{1/3}+Q^{1/3}L^{5/6}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{M5}
$$

**Proof.** The actual coefficient is supported on $q_h\asymp L$ and is
bounded by a fixed profile constant times $\tau_3(h)\ll_\varepsilon
Q^\varepsilon$. Write $h=ab^6$ uniquely with $a$ sixth-power-free,
without imposing $(a,b)=1$. On M4 one has $1<x<2$. For sufficiently
large $Q$, a prime of norm at least $P_0$ cannot divide $b$, since then
$q_h\ge P_0^6=Q^2>CL$. Thus the collision survives in $a$.
For $B=CL/q_b^6$, elementary ideal counting gives

$$
 \#\{a:q_a\le B,\ \exists p,\ q_p\ge P_0,\ p^2\mid a\}
 \ll B\sum_{q_p\ge P_0}q_p^{-2}\ll B/P_0.
\tag{M6}
$$

Only primes with $q_p^2\le B$ enter the count. The bound uses ordinary
ideal counting and its convergent tail; no new rough-prime sieve is
invoked. Consequently, for each fixed $b$,

$$
 \sum_a|L^{-1/2}c_{\rm hi}(ab^6)|^2
 \ll_\varepsilon Q^\varepsilon P_0^{-1}q_b^{-6}\mathcal P_J^2.
$$

The exact character at this column is
$\chi_a(u)^{\pm1}\mathbf1_{(b,u)=1}$, with its remaining original
zeros and fixed twist $\nu(b^6)$. Apply the owned finite-family transport
and Theorem 1.1 at row norm $Q$ and column norm at most $CL/q_b^6$.
Enlarge $(b,u)=1$ only within this fixed-$b$ positive squared norm.
The four sieve terms are monotone in column length, hence bounded by a
fixed multiple of $F_6(Q,L)$. Minkowski sums the row norms with weight
$q_b^{-3}$; $\sum_bq_b^{-3}<\infty$. This proves the first bound.

For $1<x<2$ the largest exponent in $F_6(Q,Q^x)$ is
$J_6(x)=1/3+5x/6$, so the collision exponent is $5x/6$. At $x_*$ its
margin below $T_*$ is

$$
 T_*-5x_*/6={5\sqrt6-12\over18}.
$$

On M4, $x<x_*+3/1000$ and

$$
 {5\sqrt6-12\over18}-{1\over400}-{1\over100}>0.
$$

This proves M5, including fixed support constants and sufficiently large
$Q$; bounded remaining $Q$ is absorbed in the constant. The indicator in
M3 commutes with all original profile derivatives. Apply the owned finite
parameter Sobolev argument after the same estimates for those derivatives.
It preserves the original envelope and one polynomial height cost.

The split includes collisions between every pair of slots and triple
collisions. On M4 each plain slot contains at most one copy of a prime
$q_p\ge P_0$, since $2/3>m$; the Möbius slot is squarefree. There is
no roughness condition on the other prime factors. In particular, $c_0$
retains every squarefree column and every column whose squared prime
divisors are smaller than $P_0$, with all small-prime gcds and sixth-power
tails. It is not a total-squarefree projection or a replacement row family.

## Exact remainder equivalence and interference

On the identical complete physical row space, $A=A_0+A_{\rm hi}$.
Both triangle inequalities give

$$
 \|A\|_2\le\|A_0\|_2+\|A_{\rm hi}\|_2,\qquad
 \|A_0\|_2\le\|A\|_2+\|A_{\rm hi}\|_2.
\tag{M7}
$$

Under M5, existence of a strict exponent saving below $T_*$ for $A$
is equivalent to existence of one for $A_0$, after decreasing the saving
below $1/100$. This statement includes the original uniformity and costs;
it is not a proof of either saving. In particular,

$$
 \|A\|_2^2=\|A_0\|_2^2+\|A_{\rm hi}\|_2^2
                   +2\Re\langle A_0,A_{\rm hi}\rangle.
$$

If $\|A_0\|_2^2\ll Q^{T_*-\eta+\varepsilon}$ with the common costs,
Cauchy bounds the cross term by those costs times
$Q^{T_*-(\eta+1/100)/2+\varepsilon}$. Without that remainder estimate
this interference has not independently been paid below $T_*$.

The fixed-order owner's lower-order-block proof uses only divisor bounds,
the owned family/profile transport and row/column scales at least one.
It therefore applies to $c_0$ at column scale $L$, including its original
finite corrections $\omega_j(h)$. In its notation each of
$H_{2,j}/L,H_{3,j}/L,H_{6,j}/L$ is $\ll Q^{1+\varepsilon}$ with the

## A lower bound for the complete remainder coefficient

For every fixed nonzero complex smooth annular $V_M,V_S$, on any compact
subset of $0<m<r<3m$ containing M4, for sufficiently large $Q$,

$$
 \sum_h|c_0(h)|^2\gg_{V_M,V_S} {L\over(\log Q)^4}.
\tag{M8}
$$

The constants may also depend on the compact set and fixed arithmetic
data. This is an assertion about the actual coefficient, not the row
moment or a sixth-power-compressed coefficient with cancellations between
$b$ values.

**Proof.** Set $P_1=Q^{(r+m)/2}$ and $P_2=Q^{(r-m)/2}$.
The strict wedge gives $P_2\ll N\ll P_1$, with fixed power gaps on
the compact set. Choose four distinct good primes with norms
$P_1,P_2,N,N$ up to fixed relative factors. Choose their small normalized
intervals so that $q_{p_1}q_{p_2}/D$ lies in an interval on which $|V_M|$
is bounded below, and both $q_{p_3}/N,q_{p_4}/N$ lie where $|V_S|$
is bounded below. Such intervals exist even for complex profiles. The
last two can be chosen disjoint inside a nonzero interval.

For $h=p_1p_2p_3p_4$, the only divisors in the plain annulus are
$p_3,p_4$: the unit and $p_2$ are too small, $p_1$ is too large,
and every divisor with at least two primes is too large. Hence the
**complete** original coefficient has exactly two summands, both with
$d=p_1p_2$ and $\mu(d)=1$:

$$
 c_0(p_1p_2p_3p_4)
 =2V_M(q_{p_1}q_{p_2}/D)V_S(q_{p_3}/N)V_S(q_{p_4}/N).
\tag{M9}
$$

There are no other divisor terms available to cancel this value. The
fixed-field prime-ideal theorem, in the form of the pinned primary's
`lem:ray-prime-normalizer`, gives respectively
$\gg P_1/\log Q,P_2/\log Q,N/\log Q,N/\log Q$ primes in these
fixed relative intervals. Unique ideal factorization gives bounded
multiplicity for $h$. There are therefore $\gg L/(\log Q)^4$ such
squarefree $h$, proving M8. If fixed ray classes are needed, choose
compatible fixed classes and use the corresponding fixed extension;
no growing-ray prime theorem is invoked. The lower constant is for fixed
profiles, not arbitrary profiles varying with $Q$ or height.

Multiplication by an allowed good-ideal ray character has modulus one;
negative orientation has the same norm. In particular,
$\sum_h|c_0(h)|^2\ll LQ^{-\delta}$ is false for every fixed
$\delta>0$. At $x_*$ the generic sieve exponent is

$$
 J_6(x_*)={1\over3}+{5\sqrt6\over9},\qquad
 J_6(x_*)-T_*=1-{5\sqrt6\over18}>0.
\tag{M10}
$$

One cannot pay that gap by a power saving in the complete coefficient
norm. M8 does not prove sharpness of the generic sieve on $c_0$, give a
row-moment lower bound, exclude inter-column cancellation, or refute the
mixed-moment target. The four-prime packet diagnoses the full coefficient;
it is not substituted for the complete physical rows or polynomial.

## The actual quadratic residual after pair Poisson

Reuse the generic Poisson construction in the fixed-order owner. First
majorize the complete positive row norm by its fixed smooth nonnegative
annular majorant, and only then expand it. For an actual ratio pair let
$\xi$ be its primitive inducing character with full conductor $f$, and

sixth-power-free expansion in $\ell$, divisor sum over $r\mid R$,
primitive ramified factors and entire nonzero dual sum remain unchanged.
There is no comparison of signed contractions by enlarging rows.

For a diagnostic pair take squarefree coprime $h,k$ in the same original
primary ray class. The existing formulas give $f=hk$ and root number
$\eta\gamma_1(h)\gamma_{-1}(k)$, where
$\gamma_{-1}(k)=\chi_k(-1)\overline{\gamma_1(k)}$ and $\eta$ is
the retained fixed-class phase. For positive orientation the actual
paired coefficient, apart from the unchanged transform and $\ell,r$
factors, is $\eta\chi_k(-1)B_a(h)\overline{B_a(k)}$, with

$$
 B_a(h)=\omega_j(h)c_0(h)\gamma_1(h)\overline{\chi_h(a)}.
\tag{M11}
$$

It vanishes when $(a,h)\ne1$. On M9, writing
$V=V_M(q_{p_1}q_{p_2}/D)V_S(q_{p_3}/N)V_S(q_{p_4}/N)$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
 B_a(h)&=2V\omega_j(h)\gamma_1(h)
                         \prod_{i=1}^4\overline{\chi_{p_i}(a)},\\
 \gamma_1(h)&=\prod_i\gamma_1(p_i)
                 \prod_{i<j}\chi_{p_i}(p_j)\chi_{p_j}(p_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{M12}
$$

Both allowed plain-slot assignments give the same value, including every
Gauss and reciprocity phase. Their quadratic residual does not cancel.
No scalar $\gamma_1(h)$ formula is being asserted for nonsquarefree $h$.
For the $\ell=1$ term and a fixed $r\mid\operatorname{rad}S$, the
natural dual scale is $q_a\asymp L^2/Q$; at M2 its $Q$ exponent is
$2x_*-1$. This observation discards none of the other $\ell,r,a$ terms.

Here is an exact finite coefficient obstruction. Fix such an $h$ and a
disjoint same-class squarefree $k$ with nonzero original coefficient.
Let $G$ be the full unit group modulo $hk$ and the fixed bad modulus.
By CRT there is $z\in G$ with $\chi_{p_1}(z)=-1$, and $z=1$ at
every other good prime in $hk$ and at the bad modulus. A good sextic
local character is surjective. Multiplication $a\mapsto az$ preserves
all local cubic data $\chi_p(a)^2$ and fixed bad-ray labels, but

$$
 F(a):=B_a(h)\overline{B_a(k)},\qquad F(az)=-F(a).
\tag{M13}
$$

Thus the orthogonal projection of $F$ onto cubic-data-only functions on
the uniform finite group $G$ is zero. Indeed, every such function $g$
is invariant under $z$, so averaging and translating by $z$ gives
$\langle F,g\rangle=-\langle F,g\rangle=0$. In particular,

$$
 \mathbb E_{a\in G}|F(a)-g(a)|^2
 =|\omega_j(h)c_0(h)\omega_j(k)c_0(k)|^2
                         +\mathbb E_{a\in G}|g(a)|^2.
\tag{M14}
$$

This rejects an exact cubic-only coefficient replacement and a small
mean-square coefficient error on that complete finite group. It gives
no lower bound on the actual weighted dual annulus or physical-row
moment. Multiplication by $z$ need not preserve that annulus.

The general moving quadratic/cubic coupling is already in the owner.
In its complete unpaid order-six block, the good conductor of $\xi^3$
is the concrete parity difference

$$
 D_2(h,k)=\prod_{v_p(h)-v_p(k)\ {\rm odd}}p
 ={r_2(h)r_2(k)\over\gcd(r_2(h),r_2(k))^2}>1.
$$

Small-prime gcds and sixth-power tails do not remove this component.
On the disjoint squarefree test its conductor is $hk$. This identifies
the part that must be estimated jointly, rather than supplying its bound.

## Conditional neighborhood moment and every detector witness

Assume that there exist fixed $\eta,\rho>0$ such that, uniformly for

$$
 |r-r_*|<\rho,\qquad |m-m_*|<\rho,
$$

every original fixed label and orientation and the entire original
annular profile/height class satisfy

$$
 \sum_{u\ {\rm complete\ physical\ rows}}|M_u(Q^r)|^2|S_u(Q^m)|^4
 \ll_\varepsilon Q^{T_*-\eta+\varepsilon}
                         \mathcal P_J^2(1+H)^C.
\tag{H}
$$

The hypothesis includes quantitative uniformity for the finitely many
parameter derivatives required by the source smooth calculus. $J,C$
are fixed independently of $Q$, row, label and length pair in this
neighborhood. H is unproved. A pointwise bound for one fixed profile,
one pair of lengths, a primitive-conductor family or a sparse physical
sector does not satisfy H.

If instead a bound of this form is assumed only for the complete $A_0$
from M3, with parameters $\eta_0,\rho_0>0$, first set

$$
 \rho=\min\{\rho_0,1/2000\},\qquad
 \eta=\min\{\eta_0,1/200\}.
\tag{H0}
$$

This is a smaller open intersection with M4's paid collision
neighborhood, not an extension of M5 to $\rho_0$. M5 and M7 then give
H on this smaller neighborhood with the displayed $\eta$, retaining
the cross term and common costs. All consumer choices below must use
these reduced parameters. A remainder assertion outside that intersection
alone has no such implication.

$\Theta$ for both actual C80 sides, all labels, orientations and
conjugates. Fix $\alpha>\alpha_0=51/100$ and put
