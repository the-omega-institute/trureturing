# Square-integrable kernels and bounded integral operators

The Encyclopedia of Mathematics entry, revision 47226, was last edited on
June 5, 2020. It credits B. M. Bredikhin as originator. Its opening definition
gives the operator with kernel K in L2 of the product measure, acting by
integrating K(x,y)f(y), with no complex conjugation on either factor.

The formal construction allows distinct measurable spaces and sigma-finite
measures. Cauchy--Schwarz on almost every section bounds the squared output
by the section's squared L2 norm times the squared input norm. Fubini then
gives the bound by the product of the kernel and input L2 norms. Passing to
equivalence classes yields a continuous complex bilinear operation, hence
a continuous linear map from kernels to bounded operators of norm at most one.
These steps spell out the classical construction; no originality is claimed.

The source attests the integral-operator construction. Its further claims
about compactness, adjoints and spectral expansions are outside the formal
statement here. The construction does not imply a sharp Fourier multiplier
estimate for a prescribed cosine-integral kernel.
