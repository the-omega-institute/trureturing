# OEIS A393856

Paul D. Hanna's entry of March 11, 2026 defines the generating function by
`A( x - x*A(4*x)/4 ) = x` and conjectures `a(n) == 1 (mod 5)` for `n >= 1`.
The Lean module constructs the unique integer solution, proves the functional
equation, and proves the congruence as the case `q = 4` of a parametric theorem.
The entry's separate conjecture about odd coefficients is not stated as a theorem
anywhere, and it does not need to be: it already follows from frozen content
filed under a different entry. Reducing the defining equation modulo two, the
scaling factor four vanishes and every term with `j >= 2` drops, leaving
`B(X + X^2) = X`, hence `B + B^2 = X`. The frozen
`D5/S3/Arith/ArtinSchreierTracePowersOfTwo` proves `S^2 + S = X` for its own
series `S`; the two constant coefficients are zero and one, so in characteristic
two `B + 1` and `S` are roots of the same equation with equal constant
coefficient, and `eq_of_square_add_eq_square_add` identifies them. Adding one
changes no positive coefficient, so `reducedSeries_eq_artinSeries` together with
`a396808_first_conjecture` gives the odd-coefficient classification directly.

The same reduced object closes two neighbouring entries, so they are recorded
here rather than in notes of their own. OEIS A195194, the nested reversion
`Inv(x - x*Inv(x - 2*x*Inv(x - 3*x*...)))`, conjectures the same power-of-two
parity support; modulo two its multipliers alternate, the layer with multiplier
two reduces to `X`, the tower collapses, and `B + B^2 = X` appears again. The
frozen `D5/S3/Arith/SelfReferentialInverseSeriesParity.unit_constant_support`
classifies that object, and `ZMod.intCast_eq_one_iff_odd` converts the
coefficient statement into the oddness predicate. Its first terms are
`1, 1, 4, 29, 312, 4528`, with odd indices through fifty exactly
`1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32`.

More generally the whole even-scaling branch of this reversion family shares the
one reduced object: modulo two, `A(x - x*A(k*x)/k) = x` loses every term with
`j >= 2` whenever `k` is even, so `k = 4` and `k = 6` give the same equation. The
integer sequences differ at once — `a(5)` is 8466 for `k = 4` and 71744 for
`k = 6` — while the reductions modulo two agree at every index through forty. A
distinct A-number in that branch is therefore not a distinct target.

Writing that chain out as a new Lean declaration would be a second source for a
truth the graph already carries, so it is recorded here instead.
