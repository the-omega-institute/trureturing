# OEIS A396798: compositional iterates modulo eight

Paul D. Hanna's June 16, 2026 entry defines the ordinary integer series
`A(X)=Sum_{n>=1} a(n)X^n` by `A=X+I4(A)*I5(A)`, where Ik is the k-fold
compositional iterate and the product is ordinary multiplication. There is
no factorial scaling. Revision 12, dated June 17, states these comments:

Fourth:

> Conjecture: [x^n] A^4(x) == 0 (mod 8) for n > 2.

Fifth:

> Conjecture: [x^n] A^5(x) == [5,1,1,5] repeating (mod 8) for n > 1.

Eighth:

> Conjecture: [x^n] A^8(x) == 0 (mod 8) for n > 1.

`IterateProductFourFiveEighthModEight.generatingSeries` is the common
zero-constant integer source, constructed by coefficient stabilization.
`IterateProductFourFiveEighthModEight.result` proves the eighth statement.
`IterateProductFourFiveFourthModEight.result` and
`IterateProductFourFiveFifthModEight.result` use that same definition and
prove the fourth and fifth statements, respectively. The fifth period
starts at n=2: residues 0 or 3 of n-2 select 5, and residues 1 or 2 select 1.

In the fourth and eighth arguments, the source equation and uniqueness
identify its reduction modulo four with X/(1-X). For the fourth iterate,
the second iterate reduces to X+2X^2 modulo four. An exact integer quotient
writes its reduction modulo eight as G=X+2X^2+4B. Substitution annihilation
and ring identities give G composed with G=X+4X^2. The eighth argument
instead writes the fourth iterate as X+4B and uses square-zero iteration.

For the fifth iterate, let F be A modulo eight, P=I4(F) and J=I5(F).
The two existing results and source-derived low coefficients give
P=X+4X^2 and I8(F)=X. Substituting P into F=X+PJ gives J=P+XF. Eliminating
F and multiplying out in characteristic eight yields
`(J-X)(1-X^4)=5X^2+X^3+X^4+5X^5`. The geometric inverse of the unit
1-X^4 then gives the fifth period for every n>1. No proposed full formula
for A or unproved second-iterate strong congruence is a premise.

The fourth, fifth and eighth statements were preregistered in #7950,
#7961 and #7943, respectively. The original first comment's n>=1 period
conflicts with its own a(3)=9; that previously recorded non-kernel
observation is not a new formal refutation. Comments 2,3,6,7 and a proposed
correction of the first comment retain their separate obligations.

no earlier settlement of these three clauses in their stated scope.
They do not establish global priority or exclude differently named results.
