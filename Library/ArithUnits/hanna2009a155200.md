## Verified locator

Source: https://oeis.org/A155200/internal

Primary source: OEIS A155200, revision #39. The conjecture is in the
COMMENTS line attributed to Paul D. Hanna, September 1, 2009; an OEIS entry
has no page number. A000120 is the binary ones count, including value zero
at index zero.

## Source statements

```text
%N G.f.: A(x) = exp( Sum_{n>=1} 2^(n^2) * x^n/n ), a power series in x with integer coefficients.
%C Conjecture: highest exponent of 2 dividing a(n) = A000120(n) = number of 1's in binary expansion of n, so that a(n)/2^A000120(n) is odd for n >= 0. - _Paul D. Hanna_, Sep 01 2009
%F a(n) = (1/n)*Sum_{k = 1..n} 2^(k^2)*a(n-k), a(0) = 1. - _Vladeta Jovovic_, Feb 04 2009
%O 0,2
```

The coefficients are ordinary coefficients of the displayed rational formal
exponential. Its exponent has zero constant coefficient and coefficient
`2^(k^2)/k` at every positive index. The conclusion states the existence of
an odd integer quotient, rather than assigning a valuation to zero.

## Integrality background

Peter Bala's A155200 comment gives the Gauss-congruence integrality argument.
Sela Fried, *Further proofs of conjectures from the OEIS*,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24832v1, Theorem 43, proves integrality of
`exp(Σ_{n≥1} q^(n^m) x^n/n)` for nonnegative integers `q,m`. This is the
integrality background at `q=m=2`; it does not state the exact valuation.
The factorial digit-sum identity and the general weighted-permutation
recurrence are prior results. The additional residue invariant is that
`n! a(n)/2^n` is an integer congruent to one modulo four.
