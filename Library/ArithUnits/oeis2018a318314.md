A068068 counts positive odd unitary divisors: divisors d for which d is odd and
gcd(d,n/d)=1. The denominator is the positive reduced rational denominator,
including den(0)=1. A006519 is the highest power of two dividing n. A317934 is
independently multiplicative with value 2^(e-s(e)) at every prime power, where
s(e)=A000120(e) is the binary digit sum. A011371(e)=e-s(e), A000035(p)=p mod 2,
and A005187 has value zero at zero and recurrence T(e)=e+T(floor(e/2)) for e>0.
The source domain is every positive integer; prime-power exponents include zero.
Integer subtraction is ordinary subtraction, so nonnegativity is a proof obligation.

## Mathematical mechanism

Put C(k)=binom(2k,k)/4^k. Its generating series B satisfies B(x)^2=1/(1-x).
The coefficients of (1+x)B(x^2) are C(floor(e/2)), and its square has constant
coefficient one and every positive coefficient two. Thus the independent rational
solution is the product of C(v_2(n)) and C(floor(v_p(n)/2)) over odd prime divisors.
The actual odd-unitary-divisor filter is multiplicative: the divisor-product
bijection splits oddness and the unitary condition across coprime factors.
Both it and the rational Dirichlet square are therefore determined by their
prime-power values, which are the local convolutions above. Removing the endpoint divisors gives the published recurrence;
strong induction gives uniqueness.

Kummer's binary digit formula gives v_2(binom(2k,k))=s(k). Each C(k) therefore
has odd numerator and denominator 2^(2k-s(k)). Products have no cross-cancellation,
so the global denominator exponent is v_2(n)+sum_{p|n}(v_p(n)-s(v_p(n))).
This gives the entire block, including the halving recurrence and parity formula.
The source theorem `whole_block` proves the entire conjunction, using
`root_square` for the global actual-count identity and `root_source` for equality
with the original recurrence. The Problems dossier states the exact scope and
the remaining audit and independent verification boundaries.
