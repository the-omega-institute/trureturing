The unpaginated OEIS A163921 comment states:

> Conjecture: if F(x) = exp( Sum_{n>=1} L(n)*x^n/n ) is an integer series, then the g.f. that satisfies: G(x) = exp( Sum_{n>=1} G( L(n)*x )^n*x^n/n ) is also an integer series.

The same entry states:

> Another example of this is A157675 in which L(n) = 2^n.

The unpaginated entry https://oeis.org/A157675/internal has name

> G.f.: A(x) = exp( Sum_{n>=1} A(2^n*x)^n * x^n/n ).

and comment

> Conjectured to consist entirely of integers.

All series are ordinary formal power series over the rationals. A series is
integral when each coefficient is the image of an integer. For a series-valued
family f, the coefficient of degree N in the logarithmic sum is the finite sum
of coeff(N-n, f(n))/n over 1 <= n <= N. Consequently L(0) is unused. Substitution
into the formal exponential is legitimate because this logarithmic sum has
zero constant coefficient. The equation asserts existence of a solution and
integrality of every solution; no analytic convergence or positivity is assumed.

The proof combines the integer Euler ledger of the input, its prime congruences,
Frobenius and power lifting, and the sufficient Dieudonne-Dwork criterion.
The self-substitution operator raises coefficient agreement by one degree,
so its integral iterates determine its unique fixed point. For A157675 the input
is the integral geometric series 1/(1-2X).
