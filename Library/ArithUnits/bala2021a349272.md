> Let m be an even positive integer. We conjecture that the sequence defined by b_m(n) = Product_{k = 1..2*n+1} Fibonacci(m*k) / Sum_{k = 1..2*n+1} Fibonacci(m*k) is integral. The formula given below proves the conjecture in the present case m = 2. The cases m = 4 and m = 6 of the conjecture can be proved in a similar manner.

> More generally, if F(n,x) denotes the n-th Fibonacci polynomial we conjecture that, for each n, the rational function Product_{k = 1..2*n+1} F(m*k,x) / Sum_{k = 1..2*n+1} F(m*k,x) is an integral polynomial.

The polynomial sequence has initial values F(0,x)=0, F(1,x)=1 and recurrence
F(j+2,x)=x F(j+1,x)+F(j,x). The positive even restriction on m comes from the
first comment; n ranges over all natural numbers, including zero. The quotient
is an equality in the rational-function field, with a nonzero polynomial
denominator, and makes no pointwise division assertion at its roots.

The proof reduces even sampling to a determinant-one Lucas recurrence. The
odd partial sum and final odd term factor into complementary factors, yielding
an integer-polynomial multiplication identity. The denominator's final term has
unique greatest degree and coefficient one.
