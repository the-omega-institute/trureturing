# Classical moments of the Euler set

## Verified locator

DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.1612.07632

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.07632v2

The cited version is v2. Its original TeX is available at
https://arxiv.org/e-print/1612.07632v2 .

## Source statement

Section 1, page 1, defines the power sums and records the following display:

```tex
\mathcal{S}(k,\,n)=\sum_{i=1}^{\phi(n)}\alpha_{i}^{k},\,\,k=0,\,1,\,2,\cdots.
\mathcal{S}(0,\,n)&=&\phi(n)\\
\mathcal{S}(1,\,n)&=&\frac{1}{2}\phi(n)n\\
\mathcal{S}(2,\,n)&=&\frac{1}{3}\phi(n)\big(n^{2}+{(-1)}^{w}\frac{1}{2}R(n)\big).
```

Here the Euler set consists of the positive integers less than and coprime
to n, w is the number of distinct prime factors, and R(n) is their product.
The formulas are used for n greater than one. The convention at n=1 comes
from the linked OEIS entries, not from these classical formulas.
Petridi credits Pólya–Szegő, chapter VIII, exercise 27, for the second moment.

## Scope

These are classical ingredient identities. The preprint does not state
Eldar's later adjacent-equality conjecture. Its higher-power and Artin
questions are not settled by the adjacency proof.
