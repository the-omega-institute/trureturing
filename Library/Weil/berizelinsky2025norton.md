# Norton and perturbed prime-product thresholds

## Source statement

Beri and Zelinsky, arXiv:2505.08160v2, page 8:

> Question 6. Is lim_{n→∞} a(n) − KS(n) = ∞?

With p_n the n-th prime and p_1=2, a(n) is the least positive k for which

$$
\prod_{r=n}^{n+k-1}\frac{p_r}{p_r-1}>2.
$$

The corresponding KS(n) is the least positive k for which

$$
\prod_{r=n}^{n+k-1}
  \left(\frac{p_r}{p_r-1}+\frac1{p_r^2}\right)>2.
$$

These are strict threshold lengths over consecutive primes. The source
lists KS(3)=5.
