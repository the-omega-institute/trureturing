# A400361 and the exact second-order constant

## Primary statement

The authoritative source is [OEIS A400361 revision 17](https://oeis.org/A400361),
with machine-readable text at https://oeis.org/search?q=id:A400361&fmt=text.
Its NAME is `a(n) = A018804(n) - A384628(n)`. The offset is one.
[A018804](https://oeis.org/A018804) is Pillai's sum of gcd(n,k), and
[A384628](https://oeis.org/A384628) is the sum of gcd(n,floor(n/k)), both for
1 <= k <= n. Subtraction is signed before the real embedding.

The revision-17 COMMENT states:

> Conjecture: Sum_{k=1..n} a(k) = (3/Pi^2)*n^2*log(n) + C*n^2 + o(n^2), with C = (3/Pi^2)*(-1/2 + 2*gamma - 6*zeta'(2)/Pi^2) - Pi^2/12 - (1/2)*Sum_{d>=1} phi(d)*(digamma(1+1/d) + gamma)/d^2 = -1.2036....

The entry credits Ctibor O. Zizka with the sequence. The conjecture line has
no individual attribution; no conjecture-author credit is inferred from
other entries. gamma is the Euler-Mascheroni constant, phi is Euler's
totient, and digamma is the logarithmic derivative of Gamma. The displayed
decimal is source context and is not a numerical certification here.

## Classical part and attribution

Laszlo Toth, *A Survey of Gcd-Sum Functions*, Journal of Integer Sequences
13 (2010), Article 10.8.1, gives the classical Pillai mean with secondary
coefficient

$$
\frac{3}{\pi^2}\left(2\gamma-\frac12-
             \frac{6\zeta'(2)}{\pi^2}\right).
$$

Original text: https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL13/Toth/toth10.html
and https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL13/Toth/toth10.tex.
This Pillai asymptotic and the Mobius/hyperbola methods are prior
mathematics, not a separate problem resolution or priority claim.
The native proof uses the existing Pillai divisor formula, Mathlib's
harmonic-number limit and zeta-Mobius Dirichlet identity, then proves the
required transfer estimates. No external proof code is reproduced.

## Mathematical correspondence

The real restriction of Riemann zeta has derivative at two equal to the
real part of its complex derivative. At the positive arguments 1+1/d,
the derivative quotient of the real Gamma function equals complex digamma.
The proof includes both correspondences. Every positive d, including one,
occurs once as j+1. The coefficient series is absolutely convergent, with
nonnegative summand bounded by pi^2/(6*d^2). The final remainder is little-o
along all natural indices at infinity, hence along all positive integers.
No stronger effective error term or numerical value of C is asserted.
