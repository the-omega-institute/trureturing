## The weight question

Equation (1) and its defining sentence state verbatim:

> The semigroup (T(t))t≥0 is called L∞-bounded, if there exists M > 0 such that ‖T(t)f‖∞ ≤ M‖f‖∞, for all f ∈ L²(Ω) ∩ L∞(Ω). (1)

Equation (3) states verbatim:

> (3) ‖gT(t)f‖∞ ≤ ‖gf‖∞ for all f ∈ L∞(Ω) ∩ L²(Ω)

Problem 22 and its comment state verbatim:

> Problem 22 (L∞-contraction for a weight problem). In the setting of Problem 21 assume that the semigroup (T(t))t≥0 is L∞-bounded, i.e., satisfies (1) for some M ≥ 1. Does there exist a function g ∈ L∞(Ω) satisfying g > 0 µ-a.e., 1/g ∈ L∞(Ω), and (3)? Comments. In general, this might be more than one can hope for.

The setting is a σ-finite measure space, a closed sectorial sesquilinear form on a dense subspace of L², its associated operator A, and the bounded analytic semigroup generated by −A. The volume is an edited problem list; the weight question is attributed here to its §6, without assigning individual authorship.

## Repository-derived result

The theorem `D5.S3.Quantum.Semigroups.LinftyWeightContraction.result : ¬ claim` gives a two-point counterexample. With counting measure, take V = ℂ² and the form a(u,v) = ⟨Au,v⟩ for

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\-2&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
T(t)=e^{-t}\begin{pmatrix}\cos(2t)&-\sin(2t)\\\sin(2t)&\cos(2t)\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The matrix form is sectorial with constant 2, and the semigroup has sup-norm bound 2 for t ≥ 0. A positive weight has ratio r = g₀/g₁. Contractivity would require both e⁻ᵗ(cos(2t) + r sin(2t)) ≤ 1 and e⁻ᵗ(cos(2t) + r⁻¹ sin(2t)) ≤ 1. Their right derivatives at zero require 2r ≤ 1 and 2/r ≤ 1, which are incompatible. The Lean proof obtains a strictly positive time violating one inequality from its positive derivative at zero.

Writing A = I + 2J, one has J* = −J and AA* = A*A = 5I, so A is normal and unitarily diagonalizable. For |arg z| ≤ θ < arctan(1/2), its eigenvalues satisfy Re(z(1±2i)) ≥ |z|(cos θ − 2 sin θ) ≥ 0. Thus ‖exp(−zA)‖₂→₂ ≤ 1, and the entire matrix exponential is bounded analytic on that sector.

On a finite space every strictly positive weight and its inverse are bounded. Closedness, density, identification of the associated operator, and bounded analyticity are the finite-dimensional correspondence with the source setting, rather than separate predicates of the matrix module.
