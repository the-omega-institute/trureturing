# Two involutions and exact-length products

The proof of Theorem 1.3 on page 280 identifies the free product of two
order-two groups with the infinite dihedral group: its elements are powers
of `uv` or products `(uv)^n v`. This supplies the classical normal-form
background, rather than a stated exact-length interval theorem. The native theorem
`D5/S3/Combinatorics/GroupWords/InvolutionExactLength.exact_length_products`
proves exact-length set equalities for any group, without a uniqueness or
infinite-order hypothesis. It does not claim mathematical novelty.

Write `S={u,v}` and `R=u*v`, where `u*u=v*v=1`. For each natural `m`,
`S^(2*m)` is the image of the integer interval `[-m,m]` under `k ↦ R^k`,
and `S^(2*m+1)` is the image of `[-m,m+1]` under `k ↦ u*R^k`.
Pointwise powers of sets count exactly the prescribed number of factors,
not at most that many factors. Their zero power contains the identity.
Images allow coincident powers, so finite-order and trivial cases remain
in scope. No cardinality follows without separate injectivity evidence.

The live intermediate argument proves that `m` products from
`{1,R,R⁻¹}` give exactly the powers with exponent in `[-m,m]`. At a
successor stage, the two extreme exponents are obtained by appending an
inverse or a forward step; all interior exponents admit a zero step.
Squaring `S` gives this three-step set. Left multiplication by `u` or `v`
then produces the two overlapping intervals for odd word length.

This group theorem alone establishes no matrix minimal polynomial,
linear independence, Choi rank or minimum environment dimension.
Those applications require their own linear-algebra and channel bridges.
