# Sun's A006077 Hankel determinant assertion

The source is arXiv:1308.2900v5, physical page 17, Conjecture 3.16.
Its sequence is exactly

\[
w_n=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/3\rfloor}(-1)^k3^{n-3k}
\binom n{3k}\binom{2k}k\binom{3k}k,
\qquad W(n)=\det[w_{i+j}]_{0\le i,j\le n}.
\]

It asserts that \(W(n)=0\) for \(n\equiv1\pmod3\), and that otherwise
\((-1)^{\lfloor(n+1)/3\rfloor}W(n)/6^n\) is a positive odd integer.
The definition includes \(n=0\). The PDF SHA256 is
`bb993599c738cc88ee2ef8e34209234b37631e169807dc69de124609aa738657`.

In [v6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2900v6), physical page 18,
Remark 3.16 explicitly credits the entire zero branch to C. Krattenthaler.
The additional prime congruence in that version is a separate assertion and
is outside the statement here.

The proof uses the multinomial moments
\(c_r=\binom{2r}r\binom{3r}r=(3r)!/(r!)^3\).
Their positive moment representation is established prior mathematics:
K. A. Penson and A. I. Solomon, *Coherent states from combinatorial sequences*,
[arXiv:quant-ph/0111151v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0111151v1),
physical pages 2--3, equation (6) and Example 9, give these exact moments
with endpoint \(27\). The present beta integral construction realizes them as
\(27^r(1/3)_r(2/3)_r/(r!)^2\).

The binary coefficient indicator is \(\epsilon(r)=1\) exactly when \(r\)
is a positive power of two. The two determinant families modulo two are
the ordinary and once-shifted Rueppel families. Johann Cigler,
*A curious class of Hankel determinants*,
[arXiv:1803.05164v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05164v2), physical page 1,
defines \(a_n=1\) when \(n+1\) is a power of two and studies its integer
Hankel determinants; Section 6 studies shifted sequences. The formal
argument here proves the required modulo-two recurrences directly.
No originality is claimed for these determinant or moment ingredients.

These citations identify the source assertion and established ingredients;
they do not certify current openness or priority of the complete combination.

Weighted binomial invariance is also established prior mathematics:
Michael Z. Spivey and Laura L. Steil, *The k-Binomial Transforms and the
Hankel Transform*, Journal of Integer Sequences 9 (2006), Article 06.1.1,
[original article](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL9/Spivey/spivey7.html),
Section 6, Theorem 6.1. Their falling k-binomial transform has coefficients
\(\binom ni k^{n-i}\), exactly the weighted translation used here.
