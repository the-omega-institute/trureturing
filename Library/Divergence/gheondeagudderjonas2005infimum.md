# Positive overlap localization and finite reverse tests

Gheondea, Gudder and Jonas, *Journal of Mathematical Physics* **46**, 062102,
study existence of infima in the natural order on quantum effects. The
publisher abstract gives explicit infima against projections, a spectral
criterion for the pair A and I-A, and comparison with Ando's infimum problem.
The DOI, title, authors and publication year are verified against Crossref.
The common-support localization is credited as an antecedent; no theorem
from this paper is assumed as a Lean supplier for the classification.

For the formal result, the objects are arbitrary finite positive
semidefinite complex matrices A and B. Their traces are not normalized.
The classical source class consists of all finite alphabets Fin k and
actual density-matrix preparations. The actual f-cost is the sum of the
closed scalar perspective, including its proved possibly infinite values
on the axes and zero at the origin. Its infimum over all k is maximalValue.
The hockey quantity is the curvature integral of actual positive-overlap
optimizations plus the finite affine mass.

For every real f convex on the strictly positive ray, the repository's
scalar construction supplies its actual right-derivative Stieltjes
curvature, local finiteness and the full closed-perspective representation.
If that curvature has full support and maximalValue is real finite,
maximalValue equals the integrated hockey quantity exactly when PSP
commutes with PCP, where S=A+B, C=normalize(S,A), and P is the spectral
projection of C onto (0,1). The implication from this commutator holds for
every convex generator as an equality of extended values. Finite-cost
approximate tests, rather than general-f attainment, prove necessity.

The source question is Hiai, Mosonyi and Tomamichel, *Hockey stick
f-divergences*, arXiv:2607.08760v1, Proposition V.1, equations V.124--V.127
and the final equality question. Their positive-overlap hockey formulas
are visible in https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08760v1 . Hiai, *Quantum
f-divergences in von Neumann algebras II. Maximal f-divergences*,
arXiv:1807.03118v1, Theorem 4.2, Lemma 6.1 and Theorem 6.3, supplies prior
canonical spectral and operator-convex results. These antecedents are not
claimed as new. The repository proof consumes its own actual finite
spectral profile, integrated quadratic criterion and analytic transfer.
No Bayer--Teichmann cubature or general-convex attainment theorem is used.

This finite-source result does not establish equality with all L1 source
classes and does not prove the arbitrary von Neumann algebra statement.
Operator-convex generators with the stated curvature and finite-value
conditions are included by the general convex statement. Worldwide
priority remains unresolved. Bibliographic retrieval is not Lean
verification or official acceptance.
