# OEIS A401248 Conjecture 1

The Online Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences attributes A401248 to Omar E.
Pol. Its name and first conjecture are:

> Minimum number of terms in a 2-dense sublist of divisors of n.

> Conjecture 1: a(n) = A384222(n,1).

The conjecture is dated October 28, 2026. The pinned official OEIS data at
commit `77073cc8105b8042be07ec0d885da5ea5960ff3c` identifies the statement
independently of subsequent edits. Its A401248 bytes have SHA-256
`0cfa03cb95893eb8e5bf9d7b633b2e55ff3ad6afc7765f22a2c6024c5f942c3b`.
The corresponding A384222 bytes have SHA-256
`43d7748aa4fd013104e0e964731196a867aa206a9196afe9bc6d8aa8997acf06`.

A384222 defines the row as the lengths of the maximal contiguous sublists
of the complete increasing positive-divisor list, keeping adjacent a,b
joined when b<=2a and cutting precisely when b>2a. Its explicit indices are
n>=1 and k>=1. A401248 is the smallest row entry. Thus Conjecture 1 says
that the first block length is at most each block length, for every
positive integer. Equality at ratio two and the singleton at n=1 are
included. Conjecture 2, involving A237271, is a separate assertion.

## Prior mathematics

Andreas Weingartner, *Practical numbers and the distribution of divisors*,
arXiv:1405.2585v3, pages 2-3, gives the t-dense prime-prefix
characterization. Lemma 3, page 6, gives the unique decomposition m=ur
with least prime factor of r exceeding theta(u). Taking theta(u)=2u
provides the dense/rough factorization used here. Gérald Tenenbaum,
*Sur un problème de crible et ses applications* (1986), Lemma 2.2,
gives the corresponding maximum consecutive-divisor ratio
characterization. These ingredients are prior mathematics.

The proof here selects a largest interval-dense divisor of n. Multiplying
all divisors of this factor by the complementary gcd component supplies
an injective dense copy inside every actual maximal block. This yields
the first-block cardinality comparison; the dense-factor construction is
proved internally rather than assumed as an extra hypothesis.

## Search scope and limits

The pinned OEIS entry and the current official data both retain the label
Conjecture 1 and contain no proof of this assertion. Repository searches
include public and private D5 declarations, Scribe, Library, Problems,
theory sources and digestion data. The existing palindrome and maximum
modules use the same blocks but supply different conclusions. Searches of
pinned Mathlib `56a005855ea17e0b0958f73ec801ae140b0a1679` found no exact
first-block minimum theorem in the searched divisor and number-theory
scope. A GitHub Lean-code query for A401248 returned four unrelated
hexadecimal-substring matches, rather than a theorem about this sequence.

The preregistration's bounded original-source readings cover Weingartner,
Tenenbaum, Sierpinski's practical-number criterion, and Rodriguez
Caballero's divisor-interval interpretation. They did not identify a full
block-cardinality minimum theorem. Unindexed or differently worded
equivalent corollaries remain unexcluded. No global originality or
priority certification follows from this qualification.
