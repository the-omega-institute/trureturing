# A124056 positive-integer occurrence

Quet's sequence has offset one and starts with one. Its next value counts indices
in the entire existing prefix whose values divide the current value, with
multiplicity and the current index included.

Della Corte's MathOverflow question 376929 asks about occurrence for this exact
recurrence. Sommacal's question 515669 explicitly asks whether every positive
integer occurs. The latter's revision one has identifier
`594621DD-640C-44F9-8BF7-1E725DADE64D`; the authenticated OEIS entry is revision 24.
These locators identify the problem and do not supply a completed Lean proof.

The local formalization proves the exact positive-integer occurrence assertion
in `full_positive_occurrence`. Its proof combines the actual recurrence's
contributor-rank and distinct-predecessor bounds with weighted ordered prime
words. A fixed polynomial exponent gives one fractional exponent that works
for every Dirichlet exponent above one. Finite smooth and rough sums then give
a uniform sublinear multiplicity bound under a missing residue class. Finite
occurrence of three would force one fixed missing modulus for every prefix,
contradicting the sequence's unboundedness. The exact infinite-three implication
therefore gives every positive integer at a positive source index.

This identifies a compiled mathematical result; it makes no priority or global
literature claim. The incomplete escape registrations and the independent
review boundary are specified in the corresponding Problems entry.
