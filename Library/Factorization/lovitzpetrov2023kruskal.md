# Connected product families and their factor families

## Source and locator

Literature status: `literature-attested`.

The primary mathematical locator is [arXiv:2103.15633v2, Proposition 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.15633v2#S4), in section 4, printed pages 10–11. A vector family is connected when no nonempty proper partition splits its span as a direct sum. Proposition 5 proves that connectedness of a family of nonzero product tensors implies connectedness of each factor family. The proof applies a linear functional to a nonzero vector in the intersection of the two product spans.

The [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15633) identifies the journal version as Forum of Mathematics, Sigma 11 (2023), e27, with the DOI above. The proposition number used here refers specifically to the arXiv v2 text.

## RT application and boundary

In [RT IV §114](../../docs/develop/theory/ARITHMETIC_HOLOGRAPHIC_RT_IV.md#114-original-fibre-stress-positivity-and-two-fibre-rank-saturation), the product Gram has rank seven and a one-dimensional kernel whose every coordinate is nonzero. Its eight product records form a circuit, hence a connected family. The cited implication rules out a direct-sum split of the first factor records into two rank-two halves. The public proof includes that special case directly by applying the projection onto one record span along the other.

The new RT content is the same-common-stress curved-tangent argument, positive original stress-fibre blocks, the two-fibre stress-rank saturation, and their conditional minimum exclusions. The circuit inheritance step itself is known. This note attributes only that step; it does not claim that the cited paper proves an RT channel-norm bound, a complete rank-(7,4) exclusion, or the unrestricted quarter optimum.
