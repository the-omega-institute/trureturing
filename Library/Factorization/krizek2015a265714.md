# Reciprocal-sigma threshold minima

The definition in revision 23 is “a(n) = the smallest number k such that
floor(Sum_{d|k} 1/sigma(d)) = n.” Its conjecture says that a(n) is also the
smallest number k for which the sum is at least n. The index starts at one.
Here sigma(d) is the sum of all positive divisors of d. Both sets must be
nonempty for every positive index, with no upper bound on index or
positive argument, including index one.

Robert Israel's comments give multiplicativity, the prime value
1 + 1/(p+1), divergence of the associated prime product, and existence
of every level. These are prior source observations, not new claims of
the repository proof. The prime-power expansion is an elementary
consequence of the divisor-sum definition; revision 23 does not state
that formula explicitly. The separate question about integral values above one and
the proposed numerical values at indices four through seven are outside
this assertion.
