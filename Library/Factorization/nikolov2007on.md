# Nikolov–Segal strong completeness and an abelian Lean precursor

## Retrieved primary source

Nikolay Nikolov and Dan Segal, *On finitely generated profinite groups, I:
strong completeness and uniform bounds*, Annals of Mathematics 165 (2007),
171–238, DOI <https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2007.165.171>.
The publisher's paper was retrieved from
<https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v165-n1-p05.pdf>.
Theorem 1.1 on printed page 172 states: “Every finitely generated profinite
group is strongly complete.” The immediately following sentence specifies
topological finite generation; the preceding definition of strong completeness
includes that every subgroup of finite index is open. This full theorem permits
nonabelian groups. Its literature statement is not itself a Lean proof.

## Exact compiled quantitative statement and mechanism

The named theorem
`LeanEval.NikolovSegalDependency.compact_abelian_power_quotient` has a
commutative group `G` with a topological group structure, compactness and
Hausdorffness, a finite set `S` with
`(Subgroup.closure (S : Set G)).topologicalClosure = ⊤`, and `n : ℕ` with
`0 < n`. It concludes that the range of `powMonoidHom n` is open and its
subgroup index is at most `n ^ S.card`. Writing `d = S.card`, this is
openness of `Gⁿ` together with `[G : Gⁿ] ≤ nᵈ`. This abelian quantitative
precursor is described separately from Theorem 1.1; no novelty is asserted.

Compactness makes the continuous power-map image compact, and Hausdorffness
makes it closed. The closed subgroup `P = Gⁿ` therefore gives a Hausdorff
quotient `Q = G/P`. The reused exponent declaration says that every element
of `Q` has nth power one. The images of the generators define a homomorphism
from a product of `d` cyclic groups of order `n` into `Q`. Its finite image
is closed and contains the dense subgroup generated by those images, so it
is all of `Q`. Surjectivity gives the index bound; closedness and finite
index then make `P` open.

For any finite-index subgroup `H` of this abelian `G`, taking `n = H.index`
and using the reused quotient-power equivalence puts `Gⁿ` inside `H`.
Openness of the smaller subgroup implies openness of `H`. The final check
uses compactness and total disconnectedness and derives Hausdorffness from
the singleton connected component of the identity. It therefore checks the
abelian specialization without introducing an extra Hausdorff premise.

The checks establish equality `[G : Gⁿ] = nᵈ` for
`G = Fin d → Multiplicative (ZMod n)`, and construct a generating set of
cardinality `d` when `n > 1`. Thus the bound is attained by finite products
of cyclic groups; `n = 1` and the empty generating set are also checked.
All three unchanged files passed scoped Lean compilation against the pinned
library. Printed axiom closures of their named declarations contain only
`propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. No Lake package build or
full-root rebuild was performed for this source-only publication.

## Nonabelian gap and admission boundary

For nonabelian groups the power map need not be a homomorphism and its image
need not be a subgroup. The finite cyclic-product surjection above relies
on commutativity. It does not establish closedness of general verbal
subgroups or the uniform finite-group word-width results used in the
paper's full argument. The abelian files do not prove the arbitrary
nonabelian LeanEval `nikolov_segal` statement and are not an official submission.

This note returns literature context, immutable reusable proof sources and
the exact existing dependency to the Library. It adds no D5 formalization,
theory chapter, Reg wrapper, atom, deposit or freeze. The new abelian
formalized mathematics remains outside trureturing `dev`; admission of a
reference note does not admit that formal code. The publication's campaign
attribution is `trureturning`, with no acceptance, score or rank claim. The
separate Gardam unit-submission branches and legacy issue 1932 remain separate.
