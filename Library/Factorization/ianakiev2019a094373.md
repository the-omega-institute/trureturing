# Ianakiev's shortest-palindrome conjecture

The COMMENTS entry attributes the following complete statement to Ivan N.
Ianakiev, Aug 20 2019:

> Take A007843 and count the repeated values. The result is 1,1,2,1,3,1,2,1,4,1,2,1,3,1,2,1,5,.... Build a third sequence, where a(1) = 1 and a(n) equals the length (greater than 1) of the shortest palindromic subsequence of consecutive terms of the second sequence starting with a(n) of the second sequence. The third sequence starts 1,3,5,3,9,3,5,3,17,3,5,3,9,3,5,3,33,.... Conjecturally, in the third sequence: (1) the indices of the first occurrence of each value form the present sequence and (2) for n>1, a(n) is in the a(n-1)-th position. - _Ivan N. Ianakiev_, Aug 20 2019

The preceding Frank M Jackson formula identifies the present, zero-offset
sequence as A(0)=1 and A(n)=2^(n-1)+1 for n>0. A007843 has offset zero and
is the least positive k for which 2^n divides k!. Thus its initial value is
1, and its first constant run is a singleton. The third sequence has offset
one and stipulates its first value to be 1; minimizing lengths greater than
one at that first position would give a different sequence. Consecutive
terms mean a contiguous block, rather than a scattered subsequence.

In clause (2), both displayed a's refer to the present sequence A094373,
while the position refers to the third sequence. The preceding formula and
the displayed third-sequence prefix fix this reading: at n=2 it says the
third sequence's value at position 2 is 3. Reading both a's as the third
sequence itself contradicts that displayed prefix.
