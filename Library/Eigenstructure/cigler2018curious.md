# A curious class of Hankel determinants

Let \(a_n=1\) if n+1 is a power of two, and zero otherwise.
Section 1, equation (1.1) and Theorem 1.1 (physical pages 3–4), evaluate
\(\det[a_{i+j}]_{i,j<n}=(-1)^{\binom n2}\).
Section 2, Theorem 2.1 (physical page 6), gives a sign recurrence for
\(D(n)=\det[a_{i+j+1}]_{i,j<n}\): if \(2^{k-1}\le n<2^k\),
\(D(n)=(-1)^n D(2^k-1-n)\). Together with D(0)=1 it makes every D(n)
a sign. Here `epsilon(r)=a_(r-1)` for r>0, so the formal S(n) and T(n)
are respectively these ordinary and once-shifted determinants reduced
modulo two. Both are therefore one. This cites the determinant evaluations;
the formal parity-splitting proof is a direct proof of their modulo-two
consequence, not a claim that the paper states the formal recurrence verbatim.
