# On a Conjecture Related to Eigenvalue Perturbations

Lei-Hong Zhang and Ren-Cang Li, arXiv:2609.25552v1, §1 Problem 3 and §5.
The versioned locator for both quotations below is
**arXiv:2609.25552v1, §1 Problem 3 and §5**.

> Problem 3: Relate min_{W nonsingular} ‖W∘G‖_ui ‖W^{-1}∘G^T‖_ui to min_{P permutation} ‖P∘G‖_ui^2.

> In fact, for rank-one G, Romeo and Tilli [23, Theorem 1.2] proved (1.1) with c1=1 and the unitary restriction (2.3) with c2=1. They explicitly left the corresponding nonsingular version of Problem 3 unresolved; see [23, Remark 3.1].

## Mathematical scope

Here $G$ is a complex square matrix, $W$ ranges over nonsingular complex
matrices, $P$ ranges over permutation matrices, and the same unitarily
invariant norm is used in both factors. The second Hadamard factor uses
$G^{\mathsf T}$, the plain transpose, and $W^{-1}$, the inverse.

For $\operatorname{rank}(G)\le1$, the delivered assertion states that the
infimum over invertible $W$ equals the squared permutation minimum and is
attained at a minimizing permutation. The explicit formulation includes
the zero matrix and dimension zero. It does not settle the rank-at-least-two
question in Zhang–Li §5.

## Related literature and boundaries

Zhang–Li cite Romeo–Tilli, *Linear Algebra and its Applications* **326**
(2001), 161–172, Theorem 1.2 and Remark 3.1. Their previously established
rank-one unitary case is distinguished from the nonsingular case in the
quotation. The wording of Romeo–Tilli Remark 3.1 is known here only through
Zhang–Li's citation; the original remark has not been independently read.

Bhatia–Kittaneh–Li (1997), §4 Remark 2, supplies a related method precedent:
a balanced average of a diagonal matrix and its inverse. The argument here
uses $(tW+t^{-1}W^{-*})/2$ for an arbitrary invertible complex matrix.
This method comparison does not attribute the rank-one nonsingular
variational conclusion to that precedent. Classical singular-value and
symmetric-gauge background is recorded in
[the Bhatia note](bhatia1997matrixanalysis.md).
