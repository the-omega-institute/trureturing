# Spectral extremum for a declared response loss

Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 35(11), 652–655.
Crossref, the PMC bibliographic record and the original page scans agree
on the title, author and DOI. Theorem 1 on scanned page 653 states that
for a Hermitian transformation with decreasing eigenvalues, the largest
and smallest sums of `q` eigenvalues are respectively the maximum and
minimum of the sum of its quadratic forms on `q` orthonormal vectors.
The proof on that page expands in an orthonormal eigenbasis.

The FIB boundary geometry volume §23 uses the maximum for a finite
response Gram matrix. Its factor of three comes from the declared pure
vector representation, its expected squared loss from the stated
identity covariance, and its maintenance threshold from a specified
resource function. Finite-horizon spectral approximation does not prove
invariance under repeated projected dynamics or sufficiency for arbitrary
new contexts. This note does not attribute those additional contracts to
Fan's theorem.
