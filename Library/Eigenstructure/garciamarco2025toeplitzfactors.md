# On the minimum number of Toeplitz factors of a matrix

The source is arXiv:2506.16432v1, submitted 2025-06-19. The original source archive contains `Toeplitz-GMS.tex`. The abstract/history, HTML and original TeX give the same conjecture. The arXiv history inspected on 2026-10-28 lists only v1 and no journal reference. The TeX uses `\date{\today}`; a rendered creation date does not identify another version.

The introduction, equation (1), defines ordinary complex Toeplitz matrices by

> M_{i,j}=M_{i+1,j+1} for all i,j=1,...,n-1.

The introduction defines a Toeplitz decomposition as an ordered tuple of same-size factors whose product is the matrix. It defines `Toep(M)` as the minimum such length and `Toep_n` as the maximum over all complex n by n matrices. Section 5.2 defines `Toep_n^Inv` as the minimum number of factors sufficient for every invertible matrix of that size.

Conjecture 5.1, Section 5.2, original TeX lines 1170–1175 (`super-conjecture`):

> There exists a constant C > 0 such that every matrix can be expressed as a product of an invertible matrix and C Toeplitz matrices. In particular, Toep_n ≤ Toep_n^Inv + C.

The ambient field is the complex numbers, and singular matrices are included. The rank-(n−1) proposition in Section 4 explicitly writes `M = P T_1 T_2`, with P invertible, and its proof writes `M = P_0 rref(M)`. This fixes the left-multiplier interpretation. The positive integer C counts factors and is uniform in both dimension and matrix. Identity padding makes exact and upper-bounded lengths equivalent. Two independently free invertible endpoint multipliers constitute a different statement.

The Lean claim is the first factorization assertion F in full. Its refutation does not logically refute the numerical assertion J introduced by “In particular”. No converse J ⇒ F or unbounded numerical gap is established. Conjecture 5.2 concerns row equivalence to a quasi-Toeplitz matrix and is excluded from this target; Conjecture 5.3 concerns exact worst-case Toeplitz numbers and is also separate.

## Parameter obstruction and sources

Parameter-count obstructions for structured matrix products are established methodology. Ye and Lim, *Every Matrix is a Product of Toeplitz Matrices*, Found. Comput. Math. 16 (2016), 577–598, use dimension counts for generic lower bounds; the present source recalls this in its introduction and Section 5.3. Their general factorization of arbitrary matrices uses two endpoint multipliers, and does not give the uniformly bounded single-left-multiplier assertion F. The common-field argument uses the standard transcendence-degree bound for finitely generated fields, as in the Stacks Project, tags [030D](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/030D) and [030H](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/030H). No novelty of this methodology is asserted.

For a proposed positive C, choose r = 4C, n = 2r, and an r by r complex matrix X with algebraically independent rational coordinates. For M = [[I,X],[0,0]], invertible left multiplication preserves its actual graph row space. A product of C Toeplitz matrices has entries in the one field generated by all C(2n−1) diagonal coefficients. A nonsingular selection of actual projected rows recovers X over that field. Its transcendence degree must therefore be both at least r² and at most C(2n−1), whereas C(2n−1)+C = r². The coefficients of the free invertible multiplier are not counted.
