# Bounded cocycles and continuous invariant sections

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/src/1101.3523v4 .

The Main Theorem in Section 2 considers a minimal action by continuous maps of
an arbitrary semigroup on a compact metric space X. Its fibre is a proper
CAT(0) space or a Hilbert space. A continuous cocycle of fibre isometries has a
continuous invariant section when one fibre orbit over a specified basepoint
is bounded. The composition order is

    I(gh,x) = I(g,a(h,x)) I(h,x).

For real translations, set I(g,x)(t) = t + c(g,x). The invariant-section equation
becomes psi(a(g,x)) - psi(x) = c(g,x). The same actual action and cocycle occur
in the bounded-orbit premise and the transfer equation. Neither invertibility
of the base maps nor commutativity of the semigroup is needed.

The formal theorem reconstructs a unique continuous transfer normalized at a
specified b, with the equivalence

    (for every g, |c(g,b)| <= C)
    iff
    (there is a unique continuous phi with phi(b)=0,
     phi(a(g,x))-phi(x)=c(g,x) for every g,x,
     and |phi(x)|<=C for every x).

Its base is compact Hausdorff and its index type needs only a multiplication
with the supplied action and cocycle composition equations. C is an arbitrary
real, including zero and negative values. These interface and normalization
claims are not quoted as the source's literal metric-space statement, and no
priority or originality is asserted.

The real proof minimizes nonempty compact forward-invariant subsets of the
bounded skew-orbit closure. Comparing a minimum with its vertical translates
forces singleton fibres. Compact projection yields a continuous potential.
Normalization and density propagate the original bound C rather than 2C.

General CAT(0), infinite-dimensional Hilbert, matrix conjugacy and cocycles
with reflections are outside this formal theorem. No article code or
substantial article text is reproduced.
