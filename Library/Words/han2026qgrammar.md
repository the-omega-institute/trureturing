# Han, Ji and Xiong, q-derivative grammar

A q-derivative grammar consists of a set of indexed variables, a rule assigning to each variable an expression, and
an order that rearranges the variables of a word. The q-derivative D replaces one variable of a word by its rule,
raises the indices of the variables to its right by one, and rearranges the result by the order; it is extended
linearly to formal sums of words with coefficients in ℤ[q]. Appendix III records counts of distinct terms of Dⁿ for
several grammars. For the grammar G_Sec with rules x_j ↦ q^j(1 + x_j x_{j+1}), y_j ↦ q^j y_j x_{j+1} and the
descending interleaving order, Conjecture III.7 gives the number of distinct terms of Dⁿ(y₀) as an explicit cubic
quasipolynomial in n.

The module `D5/S3/Combinatorics/QGrammar/SecGrammar` proves Conjecture III.7.
