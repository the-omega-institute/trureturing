# Iteration-depth spectrum no-gap conjecture

For a finite alphabet Σ and arbitrary languages A and B contained in Σ*, the
source defines literal k-insertion with empty factors allowed, then iterates a
single fixed positive degree k from stage zero B. The minimum iteration depth
of a word is the least stage at which it appears for some positive degree. The
conjecture in Section 10 states that if a depth r occurs, every depth from zero
through r occurs.

This note records the published conjecture and its formal settlement. It is distinct from
the finite-language theorem in Section 10 and from the insertion-degree
conjecture in Section 11.

## Formal settlement

The frozen declaration
`D5/S1/Words/HughesIterationDepthNoGap.result` proves the Section 10
arbitrary-language conjecture. For every finite alphabet (including the empty
alphabet), arbitrary languages `A,B`, and natural numbers `q ≤ r`, attainment
of minimum depth `r` implies attainment of minimum depth `q`. Each insertion
uses `k` source factors and `k+1` base factors, all allowed to be empty; their
respective concatenations belong to `A` and to the preceding stage. The output
is `x₁y₁…xₖyₖxₖ₊₁`, interleaving base and source factors. Stage zero is `B`,
and a single positive `k` is fixed throughout each history.
Minimum depth excludes appearance at every earlier stage through any positive
degree, even a different one. No finiteness of `A` or `B`, membership of epsilon
in `A`, or monotonicity in depth is assumed. Empty and epsilon-only languages
are included; an empty spectrum satisfies the conclusion vacuously.

The theorem uses `iterationDepthSpectrumAt closedSourceZero`, which is
extensionally equal to the published zero-based spectrum. Empty-pair padding
lifts histories to a common degree, so a minimum-depth successor has a
predecessor of exactly the preceding minimum depth. Induction gives the full
downward closure.
