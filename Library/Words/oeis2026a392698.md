## Unformalized bijection route

The worker noticed the following simplification while reading the source.
This is a mathematical derivation in prose, not a kernel-checked theorem,
not an imported literature result, and not an OpenProblemResolutionClaim.
Formal implementation remains stopped under the explicit Library-only rule.

For decreasing positive q, the two-step identity is
s_j - s_(j-2) = q_j - q_(j-1) for j >= 3. Both parity subsequences of s
are weakly decreasing. Odd-position s-values are positive, and even-position
s-values are nonpositive, by the two-step sign argument in the brief. Thus
the two subsequences cannot collide with each other. All s-values are
distinct exactly when every within-parity step is strict, which is exactly
q_2 > q_3 > ... > q_k. In list notation the condition is strictness of the
tail, while q_1 >= q_2 is allowed to be equality.

The map to strict partitions of n+1 is consequently
`[q_1,q_2,...,q_k] -> [q_1+1,q_2,...,q_k]` for nonempty q and
`[] -> [1]`. The first inequality becomes strict and the tail is already
strict; the weight increases by one. Conversely, a strict positive partition
p of n+1 is nonempty. Send [1] to [], and otherwise decrease its largest
part by one. If p has at least two parts, p_1 > p_2 >= 1 ensures the decreased
largest part stays positive and is at least p_2. If p has one part other than
1, its decrease is positive. The tail remains strict. These operations are
inverse, including the n=0 singleton case.

This avoids the apparent Frobenius-weight obstruction: reconstruct q from
the alternating sums, then increase only its largest part. From (A,B), the
reconstruction interleaves q_(2i-1)=A_i-B_(i-1), q_(2i)=A_i-B_i with B_0=0.
The route still requires a Lean proof of the strict-tail characterization,
the partition-level forward/inverse maps, positivity, weight and inverse
laws, and the resulting finite-cardinality equality. None is claimed as
kernel-verified here.
