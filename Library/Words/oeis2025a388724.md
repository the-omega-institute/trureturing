## Published proof and decision

Decision: **Library-only; published-proof specialization**. This is a
literature stop, not a successful bind-only probe or a new open-problem
resolution claim. The full publisher HTML was opened:
M. Archibald, A. Blecher, S. Elizalde and A. Knopfmacher,
*Subdiagonal and superdiagonal partitions*, Afrika Matematika 36, article 77
(2025), DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s13370-025-01282-0.
Crossref and the publisher give publication date 2025-04-07, before A388724.
The relevant proved statements are **Theorem 2.3 and its proof**, together
with **Lemma 1.3 and its proof**. This decision is based on those proofs,
not on the title, a matching sequence, or an unproved OEIS formula.

Here is the complete specialization. Fix k>=2. Subtract 1 from each part
of a nonempty strict-superdiagonal increasing partition y of maximum k.
The resulting positive increasing partition x has x_i>=i and maximum
m=k-1. Adding 1 is its inverse. Thus strict maximum k and weak maximum
m are equinumerous, across all weights; weight preservation is unnecessary.

The proof of Theorem 2.3 establishes a bijection from weak superdiagonal
partitions with largest part m to pairs (P,h), where P is a Dyck path of
semilength m and 0<=h<ell(P). Here ell(P) is the number of final E steps.
It appends E steps to the partition boundary until (m,m), and records how
many were appended. Its explicitly proved inverse deletes those final steps.
Therefore the desired column count is L_m = sum_{P in D_m} ell(P).

Let C(z)=sum_{m>=0} C_m z^m and
F(z,x)=sum_{m>=0} sum_{P in D_m} x^ell(P) z^m, with ell(empty)=0.
Specializing the polynomial recurrence proved in Lemma 1.3 at q=1 gives
F(z,x)=1+x*z*F(z,x)*C(z), hence F(z,x)=1/(1-x*z*C(z)).
Differentiate the finite row polynomials in x and evaluate at x=1:

    L(z) = sum_{m>=0} L_m z^m
         = z*C(z)/(1-z*C(z))^2 = z*C(z)^3.

The Catalan equation C=1+z*C^2 implies
z^2*C^3=(1-z)*C-1. Coefficient comparison yields
L_m=C_(m+1)-C_m for every m>=0. This is exactly A000245(m), by
its verified defining formula and generating function. Consequently

    column(0)=column(1)=0,
    column(k)=C_k-C_(k-1)=A000245(k-1)  for k>=1.

For a list counted by the caller's bounded family B_k, a nonempty length r
satisfies r<y_r<=k, so r<k. There are finitely many such lists. The empty
list gives B_0=1; every other list has a unique maximum j in 1..k. Thus
B_k=1+sum_{j=1..k}(C_j-C_(j-1))=C_k, including B_1=1. This proves the
caller's Proposition A as a mathematical consequence of the cited published
proof. The paper does not explicitly name A388724 (it predates the entry),
and the coefficient specialization above is written here, not quoted as a
numbered corollary from the paper. The stronger weighted enumeration and
its bijection are already proved there; no new combinatorial argument is
needed to obtain the column identity.

The choice of route is therefore the **published Dyck-path bijection**:
it already records maximum part and the exact fibre size ell(P), which
makes the strict case a parameter shift followed by Catalan algebra. A new
inductive counting proof would reprove this known enumerative content.

resolution claim, Problems theorem dossier, freeze or cover is created.

## The printed OEIS generating function fails its own data

All fields of A388724 were read, including the Rascoe formula and program.
As literally printed, putting q=1 and writing C_j=Ca(j,1) gives

    P_0=1,
    P_m=t/(1-t) * sum_{j=1..m} t^j*C_(j-1)*P_(m-j),
    S=sum_{m>=1}P_m=t^2*C(t)/(1-t-t^2*C(t)).

This formal specialization is coefficientwise valid: ord_t(P_m)>=m+1,
so only finitely many m contribute to a fixed t coefficient. Its first
coefficients are 0,0,1,2,5,13,36,105,320,1011, whereas the actual columns
are 0,0,1,3,9,28,90,297,1001,3432. In particular [t^3]S=2, not 3.
More locally, the printed bivariate formula misses q^5*t^3, which is the
valid partition [2,3]. Thus the printed formula **does not imply the column
conclusion** and is not used as proof.

The displayed PARI program uses pl[m-j]/(1-t*q^(m-j)) instead. With pl
representing Pr, this is not the P(m-j) defined in the formula: that P
contains the additional factor t*q^(m-j). The observation identifies the
specific discrepancy; this worker has not edited OEIS or asserted which
historical transcription caused it. The published proof above is independent
of this error.
