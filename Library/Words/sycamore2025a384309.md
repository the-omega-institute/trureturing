## Statement and proof

Start at a(1)=1. For a positive position t, count positions 1 through t
whose terms have the same leading decimal digit as a(t). That count is
a(t+1). The theorem proves that each positive k has a finite occurrence
set, of size nine plus one when k=1.

There are nine digit classes. At least one occurs infinitely often by
the infinite pigeonhole principle. Its successive visits emit every
positive integer as a counter value. Each digit class contains infinitely
many positive integers, witnessed by d times powers of ten for 1<=d<=9.
Consequently every class is visited infinitely often. For each positive k,
each class emits k exactly once, since its counter increases by one at
each visit. These are nine distinct successor positions. The initial 1
adds the tenth occurrence of 1.
