# Binary Words Containing Few Abelian Squares

Conjecture 4 on printed page 1 states that a binary word of length n contains
at least floor(n/4) abelian squares and that, when equality holds, all of them
are trivial. An abelian square is a factor whose two equal-length halves have
the same Parikh vector. A trivial abelian square is a positive even power of a
single letter.

The paper's example `abaababa` has the six distinct abelian-square factors
`aa`, `abab`, `abaaba`, `baba`, `baab`, and `aababa`. Table 1 likewise counts
distinct factors and lists `abab` and `baba` in its length-four row.

This note attests the definitions and the published conjecture, not the
repository's refutation. Bounded checks of the arXiv version record, arXiv
abstract search, exact title and identifier searches, Semantic Scholar, and
Crossref found no later settlement. That search scope is not an exhaustive
claim about all literature.
