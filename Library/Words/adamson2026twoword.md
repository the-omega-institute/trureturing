# Equality of projections in two uniform words

Definition 1 starts with words w,v having the same alphabet A and defines an
edge between distinct a,b precisely when their two-letter projections in the
two words are equal. Definition 14 restricts both words to be k-uniform over
the vertex set. At k = 2 every vertex occurs exactly twice in each word;
therefore every vertex belongs to both alphabets.

The conclusion asks, without a number, for an explicit graph outside G₂.
Remark 30 already establishes existence nonconstructively by a graph-class
counting argument. Conjecture 31 is a different question: strictness of
G_k ⊆ G_(k+1) for every k. No connectedness or minimum-size condition is
attached to the explicit-graph request. Ordinary single-word alternation and
unions of graphs represented by separate words are different definitions.

The candidate U₂₄ has vertex set Fin 24 ⊕ Finset (Fin 24); a point is adjacent
to a subset exactly when it belongs to that subset, and there are no edges
within either part. Its proposed obstruction uses four insertion cuts per
subset vertex, with at most 49⁴ signatures for 2²⁴ different neighborhoods.
This note attests the source question and definitions, not a published answer.
