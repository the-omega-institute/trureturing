# OEIS A126347: q-Bell rows and Kurkov's recurrence

OEIS A126347 records the coefficient rows of the q-Bell polynomials and
attributes the displayed recurrence to the literature through Carl G.
Wagner, *Partition Statistics and q-Bell Numbers (q = -1)*, Journal of
Integer Sequences 7 (2004), Article 04.1.1. Jianping Pan and Tianyi Yu,
*Top-degree components of Grothendieck and Lascoux polynomials*,
arXiv:2302.03643v4 (journal version 2024), also state this recurrence and
attribute it to Wagner.

Pan and Yu identify the reversed polynomial as
`q^(binom(n,2)) B_n(q^(-1))`. Thus the triangular degree and monicity of
`B_n` are literature-attested facts. The formal module proves them locally
because the pinned Lean libraries do not provide this q-Bell family, but
does not present them as the new intermediate content.

- Conjecture `%F` (verbatim): Conjecture: R(n,n) is the (n+1)-th reversed row polynomial where R(0,0) = 1, R(n,k) = R(n-1,n-1) + x^n * Sum_{j=0..k-1} R(n-1,j) for 0 <= k <= n. - _Mikhail Kurkov_, Jul 06 2025
- `%A` (verbatim): _Paul D. Hanna_, Dec 31 2006, May 28 2007

The Wagner paper predates Kurkov's conjecture and does not contain the
triangular recurrence `R`. Pan and Yu likewise contain no occurrence of
Kurkov or "row polynomial" and predate the conjecture. They attest the
q-Bell recurrence and its reversal degree, not the identity between that
reversal and Kurkov's diagonal.

## Formal scope

The formal result concerns only Kurkov's quoted recurrence and the next
q-Bell row. Mathlib defines `Polynomial.reverse f` by reflection about
`f.natDegree`; monicity and degree `binom(n,2)` make this equal to reversal
of the fixed-length OEIS coefficient row. Without that fact, leading zero
slots in a fixed row would not be represented by `Polynomial.reverse`.

The formal `kurkovR` is total: row zero is zero away from column zero, and
later recurrence clauses accept every natural column. The diagonal at row
`n+1` reads only columns `j<n+1` of row `n`, so values outside the source
domain `k<=n` do not enter the theorem.
