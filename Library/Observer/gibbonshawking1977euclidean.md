# Euclidean horizon periodicity and thermal radiation

## Verified locator

Gibbons and Hawking give the regular imaginary-time period of the Euclidean
Schwarzschild section in the text after equation (2.7), p. 2753. Equation
(3.13), p. 2755, gives the entropy as one quarter of the horizon area.
[Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.15.2752)

Hawking derives the thermal particle spectrum in *Communications in
Mathematical Physics* 43, 199–220. Unruh obtains the accelerated-observer
temperature in *Physical Review D* 14, 870–892.
[Hawking](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02345020) ·
[Unruh](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.14.870)

Zurek's abstract on p. 1683 compares the entropy carried away over complete
evaporation with the initial entropy of an uncharged, nonrotating black hole.
Page's published comment gives the transmission-dependent entropy rates.
[Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.49.1683)
[Page's comment](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.50.1013)

## Formal correspondence

The conical calculation uses the leading Euclidean polar metric. The
spectral calculation solves the bosonic and fermionic normalization and
detailed-balance equations. The radiation model uses the horizon area and
an ideal thermal flux. The factor of four thirds for the radiation entropy
follows from the three-dimensional radiation equation of state and the
Schwarzschild mass-loss law. The physical spectrum with transmission
coefficients and its entropy flux depend on the field and the scattering
potential.
