# Abbott–Wechs–Costa–Branciard, Genuinely multipartite noncausality

A. A. Abbott, J. Wechs, F. Costa and C. Branciard, Quantum 1, 39 (2017); arXiv:1708.07663v2. Quotations are from the v2 TeX source.

An $N$-partite correlation $P(\vec a|\vec x)$ is 2-causal if it is a convex mixture, over nonempty proper groups $\mathcal K$, of correlations in which the parties of $\mathcal K$ act first and the remaining parties may depend on their inputs and outputs (Definition 2); the 2-causal correlations form a polytope whose vertices are the deterministic 2-causal correlations. In the lazy scenario each party has a binary input and outputs $0$ on input $0$. The inequality
$J_1(N)=\sum_{\emptyset\subsetneq\mathcal K\subsetneq\mathcal N}P(\vec a_{\mathcal K}=\vec1\,|\,\vec x_{\mathcal K}=\vec1,\vec x_{\mathcal N\setminus\mathcal K}=\vec0)-P(\vec a=\vec1\,|\,\vec x=\vec1)\ge0$
holds for all 2-causal correlations (Eq. (10)). The paper states:

> It is interesting to ask if this inequality is tight (i.e., facet inducing) for all $N$. For $N=2$ it reduces to the LGYNI inequality which is indeed tight, and for $N=3$ it was also found to be a facet. By explicitly enumerating the vertices of the 2-causal polytope for $N=4$ (of which there are 136 818 592) we were able to verify that $J_1(4)\ge0$ is indeed also a facet, and we conjecture that this is true for all $N$.
