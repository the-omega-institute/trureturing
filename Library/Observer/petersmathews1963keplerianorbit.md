# Transverse-traceless waves and the circular binary chirp

## Verified locator

Peters and Mathews derive the leading gravitational radiation from a Keplerian
orbit. Its circular specialization has eccentricity zero.
[Journal article](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.131.435)
[DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.131.435)

Carroll's Section 6 gives the transverse-traceless plane-wave polarizations
and quadrupole radiation. Peters derives the evolution of the orbit under
radiation losses.
[Lecture notes](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9712019)
[Orbital evolution](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.136.B1224)

## Formal correspondence

The spatial projection $P=I-nn^{\mathsf T}$ removes the propagation
direction. Subtracting half the trace on the remaining plane gives the
transverse-traceless projection. For propagation along $z$, the two
polarization amplitudes rotate through twice the spatial angle. Their
complex combinations transform by the phases $e^{\pm 2i\phi}$.

The third derivative of the trace-free circular quadrupole has squared
matrix norm $32\mu^2 r^4\omega^6$. Kepler's relation turns the resulting
power into a constant times $r^{-5}$. Newtonian energy balance gives
$r(t)^4=r_0^4(1-t/T_c)$ before $T_c$. The gravitational-wave frequency
is $f=\omega/\pi$, and $f^{-8/3}$ is linear in $1-t/T_c$.
