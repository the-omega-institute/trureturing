# A classical local ingredient for a constrained continuation

Milnor's *Morse Theory*, Annals of Mathematics Studies 51, Princeton
University Press (1963), is based on lecture notes by M. Spivak and
R. Wells. The author's
[publication list](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~jack/milnor-pub-2025.pdf)
and the publisher's DOI identify this work.

The [original book](https://math.stanford.edu/~ralph/math215b/Milnor.pdf),
printed pages 6–8, states and proves Lemma 2.2. The Morse lemma concerns
smooth local coordinates near a
nondegenerate critical point. The local quadratic form has as many
negative squares as the critical point's index. This general principle
is prior mathematics, not a new conclusion about quantum programs.

The quantum-volume application additionally needs an analytic parameter,
the direction of the critical-value displacement, and a connection
between particular canonical endpoints. Its proof supplies the needed
analytic two-variable coordinates by solving one critical-point
equation, taking Taylor remainders, and successively completing squares.
It does not attribute an analytic parameter-dependent theorem directly
to Milnor's smooth statement.

The channel-specific content is the sign of the displaced critical value
for the actual processor family, the identification of the two required
arms, and their continuation into a full exact preparation with specified
information and curvature data. A local saddle normal form alone proves
none of those global assertions.

This note paraphrases a classical result and its scope. No book pages,
figures or extended excerpts are redistributed.
