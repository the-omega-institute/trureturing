# The classical midpoint criterion

Remark I.1.4(1), printed page 4, states that a complete metric space is
geodesic if every pair of points has an exact midpoint. It also states
the radius-restricted version: midpoints for pairs at distance less than
r imply r-geodesicity. The remark states these criteria without giving
the midpoint-refinement proof.

The compact extended metric formulation uses only pairs at finite
distance and retains infinite distances between components. Its proof
can refine finite midpoint chains, round every real time to a chain
index with a uniform error, intersect closed sets in the compact product
of all pointwise map values, and use endpoint triangle saturation. This
route does not require a convergent subsequence in an uncountable product.
It is a proof of the compact case; the complete noncompact case of the
classical criterion is outside that formulation. No mathematical novelty
or priority is claimed.

The same book's I.5.18–5.19, printed pages 64–65, describes the disjoint
union metric and the chain quotient pseudometric. These passages do not
identify a specified hyperbolic gluing with a particular formal carrier.
That identification, preservation of the original quotient topology,
full-orbit finiteness and the geometric hypotheses of an application
need their own proofs.
