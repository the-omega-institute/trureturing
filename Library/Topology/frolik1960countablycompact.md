# Classical abundance, avoidance and closing-off

The article appears in Czechoslovak Mathematical Journal 10 (85) (1960),
no. 3, 329–338. The directly inspected primary passages are Lemma 2.8
and its proof, printed pages 333–334 (PDF pages 6–7), and Lemma 2.9 and
its proof, printed page 334 (PDF page 7). The reading uses the scanned
page images; the PDF text extraction contains no article text and is
not a transcription of these passages. This note supplies citations
and paraphrases, without reproducing the publisher PDF.

Write $\mathfrak c=2^{\aleph_0}$ and let $\beta P$ denote the Stone–Čech
compactification of $P$. Lemma 2.8 states that, for separable metrizable
$P$, every infinite $M\subseteq\beta P$ having no accumulation point in
$P$ has $2^{\mathfrak c}$ accumulation points in $\beta P$. The
no-accumulation-in-$P$ hypothesis is essential to this attribution; the
lemma is not an unqualified abundance assertion for every infinite
subset. It also states that a compact subspace of $\beta P\setminus P$
is either finite or has cardinal $2^{\mathfrak c}$.

The proof takes a countably infinite discrete $X\subseteq M$ and uses
normality of $P\cup X$ to extend bounded continuous functions from $X$.
It identifies the closure of $X$ in $\beta P$ with $\beta X$ and, on
printed page 334, explicitly invokes Pospíšil's theorem that the
Stone–Čech compactification of a countably infinite discrete space has cardinal
$2^{\mathfrak c}$. The footnote on printed page 333 credits M. Katětov
with communicating this simple proof. The Pospíšil and Katětov credits
are attested by Frolík; their original sources were not independently
read here.

Lemma 2.9 states that, for separable metrizable $P$ and
$M\subseteq\beta P\setminus P$ with $|M|<2^{\mathfrak c}$, there is a
countably compact subspace $R$ satisfying
$P\subseteq R\subseteq\beta P\setminus M$ and $|R|\leq\mathfrak c$.
Its proof selects accumulation points outside $M$ and iterates through
the countable ordinals. A countably infinite subset of the final union
lies in a bounded stage, and a later stage contains its selected
accumulation point. The cardinal estimate uses
$\mathfrak c^{\aleph_0}=\mathfrak c$ and $\aleph_1\leq\mathfrak c$.
This is primary provenance for the classical abundance, cardinal
avoidance and omega1 closing-off methods, not new project methods.

These lemmas differ from the repository's exact arbitrary T1 closure
theorem with an arbitrary map $q$, a good-set predicate, an admissible
seed and preservation of $q$-injectivity. They do not state that theorem
or supply pairwise distinct projected accumulation values. The
fixed-free-ultrafilter D-compact construction is separately attested in
[ginsburgsaks1975ultrafilters](ginsburgsaks1975ultrafilters.md); it is not
the arbitrary-$q$ theorem either. The two inspected primary pages do
not state the Feldman–Wilce finite-block graph endpoint or resolve
their Question 1. This is a passage-level comparison, with no claim
about absence elsewhere in the literature, novelty or priority.

The FreeUltrafilterCardinality module supplies the lower bound
$2^{\mathfrak c}\leq|\{u\in\operatorname{Ultrafilter}(\mathbb N):
u\text{ contains every cofinite set}\}|$. Its classical cardinal-supply
attribution is the Pospíšil theorem explicitly invoked by Frolík on
printed page 334. The module constructs an independent family from
finite traces, extends each Boolean assignment together with the
cofinite filter to an ultrafilter, and recovers the assignment by
membership. This links the cardinal-supply attribution only; Frolík's
inspected passage is not asserted to contain this exact finite-trace
implementation or to supply the later section-good selector or
pairwise distinct projected cluster values. No new set-theoretic
novelty is claimed.
