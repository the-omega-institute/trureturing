# Constructible incidence and geometric component count

The primary locators are [Theorem 29.23.3, tag 054K](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/054K)
and [Lemma 37.27.6, direct tag 055A](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/055A).
These are stable tags on the live Stacks Project. No immutable
body-revision identifier is specified by this citation; the
bibliography year does not pin the contents of the live pages.

Theorem 29.23.3, Chevalley's theorem, assumes a quasi-compact
morphism locally of finite presentation and sends locally
constructible subsets to locally constructible subsets. The
finite-type incidence morphisms over Noetherian complex varieties
in [VII, Theorems 105.2 and 106.3](../../docs/develop/theory/QUANTUM_ORIGIN_BILATERAL_POSITIVE_RESET_RIGIDITY_AND_SCALE_OBSTRUCTION_VII.md)
satisfy these hypotheses. Their constructible dense images contain
nonempty Zariski opens; no properness of the parameter map is used.

Lemma 37.27.6 assumes a finite-type morphism of schemes. For any
chosen point of the base, it gives a nonempty open in that point's
closure on which the number of geometric irreducible components
of the fibers is constant. The function is defined in Lemma
37.27.3 using a separably closed extension. The consumer takes
the generic point of the irreducible secant base.

For its Grassmannian incidence the fibers on the selected open
are zero-dimensional finite sets. Their geometric irreducible
components are their distinct geometric points, even if their
scheme structures have nilpotents. Thus the resulting constant
is the number of distinct entry components through the proved
bijection, not the length of a possibly nonreduced scheme.
Neither flatness nor properness is an extra premise of this use.

Tag 0553 is the whole section, not the direct lemma locator 055A.
The citations above concern only constructibility and generic
geometric component counts. They do not assert real connectedness,
entry-locus irreducibility, projective smoothness or a convex
classification. This note is citation-only and copies no primary
proof or full theorem body.
