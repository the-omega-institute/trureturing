## Scope and conventions

Fenn and Rourke, *Journal of Knot Theory and Its Ramifications* 1 (1992),
343–406, develop racks and their relationship with codimension-two links.
The quandle specialization additionally requires idempotence. Joyce's
*A classifying invariant of knots, the knot quandle*, *Journal of Pure and
Applied Algebra* 23 (1982), 37–65, DOI 10.1016/0022-4049(82)90077-9,
is an earlier source for the knot-quandle construction.

The convention here is Mathlib's left action: the crossing sends `(x,y)`
to `(x ◃ y,x)`, and its inverse sends `(x,y)` to `(y,y ◃⁻¹ x)`.
Products of permutations apply their right factor first. A closed algebraic
braid is colored by fixed points of its transport. In either signed Markov
stabilization, the extra color is uniquely the old final color; the inverse
map restricts to the old coordinates. Quandle idempotence is essential to
this coordinate argument. No finiteness, connectedness or faithfulness is
needed for the coloring-space equivalences. Finite quandles give integer
coloring counts.

These are classical coloring ideas, not a new knot invariant. The formal
objects are the literal Artin presentation and its algebraic Markov moves.
Identifying their quotient with geometric link closures requires a geometric
Markov theorem, which is not supplied by these algebraic statements.
