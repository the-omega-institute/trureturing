## Source statements

```text
%N A300595 O.g.f. A(x) satisfies [x^n] exp( n^3 * A(x) ) = n^3 * [x^(n-1)] exp( n^3 * A(x) ) for n >= 1.
%C A300595 It is conjectured that this sequence consists entirely of integers.
%F A300595 O.g.f. equals the logarithm of the e.g.f. of A300594.
%O A300595 1,2
%N A300594 E.g.f. A(x) satisfies: [x^n] A(x)^(n^3) = n^3 * [x^(n-1)] A(x)^(n^3) for n>=1.
%F A300594 E.g.f. A(x) satisfies: log(A(x)) = Sum_{n>=1} A300595(n)*x^n, a power series in x with integer coefficients.
%O A300594 0,3
```

## Formal interpretation

The series $F$ belongs to $\mathbb Q[[x]]$ and has constant coefficient zero.
For every $n\ge1$ the equation is
$[x^n]\exp(n^3F)=n^3[x^{n-1}]\exp(n^3F)$.
The integrality assertion is $[x^m]F\in\mathbb Z$ for every $m\ge1$.
Rational existence and uniqueness have no integrality premise.

Put $B=\exp(F)$. Its constant coefficient is one and its ordinary coefficients
satisfy $[x^n]B^{n^3}=n^3[x^{n-1}]B^{n^3}$ for every $n\ge1$.
The companion's EGF entries are $\beta_r=r![x^r]B$ for every $r\ge0$.
The primary uses $F=\log(B)$ with no factorial scaling of its coefficients.
The companion's affirmative integer wording supplies no proof or premise.
The source's programs, asymptotic and separate recurrence are not used by the
formal integrality argument.
