## Involutory antimorphisms and closure

ArXiv v1, page 3: “An involutory antimorphism is a map $\vartheta: {\mathcal A}^* \to {\mathcal A}^*$ such that for every $v,w \in {\mathcal A}^*$ we have $\vartheta(vw)=\vartheta(w)\vartheta(v)$ and moreover $\vartheta^2$ equals identity.”

“Consider an involutory antimorphism $\vartheta$ over $\mathcal A$. A finite word $w$ is a $\vartheta$-palindrome if $w=\vartheta(w)$. The $\vartheta$-palindromic closure $w^\vartheta$ of a word $w$ is the shortest $\vartheta$-palindrome having $w$ as prefix.”

Finite words are `List α`. `InvAntimorphism` records the two laws, the palindrome condition is `θ.toFun w = w` (Mathlib’s `Function.IsFixedPt θ.toFun w`), and `IsClosure θ w z` says that `z` has prefix `w`, is a `θ`-palindrome, and has length at most every other such extension. The closure bound is $|w|\le |z|\le 2|w|$; the lower inequality need not be strict.

## Generation and normalization

ArXiv v1, page 4, Definition 1: “Let $\mathcal A$ be an alphabet and $G$ be the set of all involutory antimorphisms on $\mathcal A^*$. Let $\Delta=\delta_1\delta_2\ldots$ and $\Theta=\vartheta_1\vartheta_2\ldots$, where $\delta_i\in\mathcal A$ and $\vartheta_i\in G$ for all $i\in\mathbb N$. The infinite generalized pseudostandard word $\mathbf u(\Delta,\Theta)$ generated by the generalized pseudopalindromic closure is the word whose prefixes $w_n$ are obtained from the recurrence relation
$w_{n+1}=(w_n\delta_{n+1})^{\vartheta_{n+1}},\quad w_0=\varepsilon.$
The sequence $\Lambda=(\Delta,\Theta)$ is called the directive bi-sequence of the word $\mathbf u(\Delta,\Theta)$.”

Page 4, Definition 2: “A directive bi-sequence $\Lambda=(\Delta,\Theta)$ of a binary generalized pseudostandard word $\mathbf u(\Delta,\Theta)$ is called normalized if the sequence of prefixes $(w_n)_{n\ge0}$ of $\mathbf u(\Delta,\Theta)$ contains all palindromic and $E$-palindromic prefixes of $\mathbf u(\Delta,\Theta)$.” Page 5, Section 3.3: “A normalized directive bi-sequence can be defined similarly as in Definition 2 over multiliteral alphabet.”

Infinite words are `ℕ → α`. Their length-`m` prefixes are the literal expression `List.ofFn (fun i : Fin m => u i)`. `Generates` states the recursion and that each stage is such a prefix. `IsNormalized` requires every prefix fixed by some involutory antimorphism to occur as a stage. The directive indices start at one; the entries at zero do not participate in closure steps.

## Conjecture 29

ArXiv v1, pages 20–21, Section 6:

> Consider an alphabet $\mathcal A$ with $\#{\mathcal A}=d$ and $G$ the set of all involutory antimorphisms on ${\mathcal A}^*$. Let $\mathbf u(\Delta, \Theta)$ be a $d$-ary generalized pseudostandard word, where $\Delta$ is a sequence of letters from $\mathcal A$ and $\Theta$ is a sequence of antimorphisms from $G$. Then $\mathbf u$ is periodic if and only if the following conditions are met:
>
> 1. The normalized directive bi-sequence is of the form $(\widetilde{\Delta}, \widetilde{\Theta})=(v\delta_1\delta_2\delta_3\ldots, \sigma \vartheta_1 \vartheta_2\vartheta_3\ldots)$, where $|v|=|\sigma|$ and $\vartheta_i(\delta_{i+1})=\vartheta_j(\delta_{j+1})$ for all $i,j \in \mathbb N$.
> 2. For all $i \in \mathbb N$, if $w$ is a $\vartheta_i$-palindrome, then $\vartheta_{i+1}(w)$ is a $\vartheta_{i+2}$-palindrome.

The alphabet is arbitrary and finite. The $d$-ary requirement is `Function.Surjective u`; periodicity is `∃ p, 0 < p ∧ Function.Periodic u p`. The finite prefix length is `k`, and the two tail conditions quantify over `i,j ≥ 1`. Letter images are one-letter words. The claim also asserts existence of a normalized directive generating the same infinite word. It does not assert a computable normalization algorithm or a formula for the least period.

## Mathematical scope

The arbitrary-alphabet argument uses earliest insertion for normalization, nonincreasing positive gaps and a constant product of adjacent letter permutations for sufficiency, and global reflection of periodic prefixes for necessity. The least-period identity $p=g\operatorname{ord}(\rho)$ remains open here.
