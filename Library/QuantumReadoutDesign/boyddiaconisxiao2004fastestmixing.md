# Fastest mixing and the constrained site-kernel family

The primary source is *SIAM Review* 46(4), 667–689,
[DOI:10.1137/S0036144503423264](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0036144503423264),
available as the [author-hosted PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/fmmc.pdf).
This note cites the source without redistributing its text or PDF.

The paper minimizes the second-largest eigenvalue modulus of a
Markov transition matrix under the stochastic and graph-support
constraints. It supplies a convex formulation, a semidefinite
program and dual bounds. Its extensions include reversible chains
with a specified equilibrium distribution.

For the positive FIB activity law, the phase-information matrix
$H_\omega$ gives a random-scan heat-bath transition matrix
$P_\omega=D_\psi^{-1}(I-H_\omega)D_\psi$.
The matrix $I-H_\omega$ is a convex combination of orthogonal
projections, so its spectrum is nonnegative. Its second-largest
eigenvalue modulus is therefore one minus the nonconstant gap
of $H_\omega$.

This is a fastest-mixing problem inside the affine family
generated by three fixed site kernels. The two endpoint edges
share a site-selection probability. Optimizing all graph-edge
probabilities independently would enlarge the feasible set.
The paper's general convex and dual mechanisms retain their
supplier ownership; the exact FIB site weights and their
positive-activity uniqueness require their own certificate.
