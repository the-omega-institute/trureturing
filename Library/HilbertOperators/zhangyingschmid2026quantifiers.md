Conjecture 1, Section 4.3, PDF page 10:

> A finite number of preparations is sufficient for the theory-independent certification of any nonclassical measurement with a finite number of outcomes (through the violation of some noncontextual inequality).

The Lean statement treats every positive finite Hilbert dimension and outcome count. Preparations are normalized density matrices, with one certain output per setting; every finitely supported real preparation identity remains imposed. Ontic sums use `HasSum` over arbitrary discrete types. The finite preparation family and separating inequality can depend on the measurement. Continuous measurable ontic models and uniform quantitative preparation, noise or sample bounds are outside this statement. The optional $k d^2+1$ pure-preparation bound is not formalized.

The compactness argument compresses the responses to one finite carrier and intersects the closed Born and preparation-identity conditions in a product of simplexes. Finite table separation supplies the actual inequality. Appendix E distinguishes such an inequality from a witness that assumes the numerical quantum descriptions.
