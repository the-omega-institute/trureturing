## Statement

For a set $X$ and a kernel $K : X \times X \to \mathbb{C}$ that is positive
definite in the sense that every finite matrix $(K(x_i, x_j))_{i,j}$ is positive
semidefinite, there is a unique Hilbert space $H$ of functions on $X$ such that
$K(\cdot, y) \in H$ for every $y$ and $f(y) = \langle f, K(\cdot, y)\rangle$ for
every $f \in H$ and $y \in X$. In particular
$K(x, y) = \langle K(\cdot, y), K(\cdot, x)\rangle$, so $K$ is the Gram kernel of
the map $x \mapsto K(\cdot, x)$ into $H$. This is the Moore–Aronszajn theorem; the
attribution of its first appearance to this paper, and Aronszajn's attribution
of the result to E. H. Moore, are as stated in secondary accounts of
reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces.

## Encoding and boundary

The repository theorem concerns the real reading of a ternary comparison
structure, which is symmetric with nonnegative finite Gram forms and is
therefore a positive semidefinite real kernel. It uses the reproducing kernel
Hilbert space construction of the classical theorem and adds a finite-rank
step: a uniform bound $r$ on the ranks of all finite Gram matrices is
equivalent to the kernel being the Gram kernel of a map into Euclidean
$r$-space. That finite-rank equivalence is a derivation in the repository; it
is not attributed to this paper.
