# The all-ray transition-probability theorem

The primary text is [arXiv:1407.0527v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1407.0527v1),
§1, the unnumbered statement headed **Wigner's theorem**, equation
(1). The journal publication is *Physics Letters A* **378** (2014),
2054–2057, DOI `10.1016/j.physleta.2014.05.039`.

Let $\mathcal P_1$ be all self-adjoint rank-one projections of a
complex Hilbert space. A map $f:\mathcal P_1\to\mathcal P_1$
preserving $\operatorname{Tr}(f(P)f(Q))=\operatorname{Tr}(PQ)$
for every pair is implemented by a linear or antilinear isometry
on the whole Hilbert space. The cited statement does not assume
bijectivity of $f$ or separability of the space. In finite dimension
the isometry is surjective, hence unitary or antiunitary. This
includes complex dimension two.

The exact consumer is [VIII, Lemma 116.1 and Proposition
116.2](../../docs/develop/theory/QUANTUM_ORIGIN_BILATERAL_POSITIVE_RESET_RIGIDITY_AND_SCALE_OBSTRUCTION_VIII.md).
There the supplied whole-pure bijection transports an original
affine symmetry to a bijection on **all** source rank-one projectors.
The intrinsic centers and gauges of the actual whole pair faces
give the overlap $\operatorname{Tr}(PQ)$ for every pair, including
the diagonal and orthogonal pairs. Thus the source theorem's entire
domain and all-pair preservation conditions hold exactly. Real
linear spanning by rank-one projectors then extends the resulting
intertwining identity to all Hermitian matrices.

This is the mature Wigner theorem, with Gehér as the directly
specified proof source. No new classification or quantum-symmetry
postulate is claimed. An orthogonality-only Uhlhorn theorem, with
its separate dimension and bijectivity conditions, is not substituted
for it. Preservation merely on a dense subset or selected orbit is
not the consumer's premise.

The theorem gives implementers individually. It provides no global
continuous or multiplicative choice of phase representatives. The
consumer constructs its compatible compact implementer graph
separately, permits antiunitaries for the full original group, and
does not infer native controls from these mathematical symmetries.
