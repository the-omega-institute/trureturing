# Triple products and projective unitary equivalence

T.-Y. Chien and S. Waldron, arXiv:1312.5393v1 (19 December 2013). The
journal version, *A characterization of projective unitary equivalence of
finite frames and applications*, appeared in SIAM J. Discrete Math. 30,
976–994 (2016); it was not compared with v1.

Theorem 2.2(2) states that two finite sequences of vectors whose frame
graphs are complete (all inner products nonzero) are projectively unitarily
equivalent if and only if their triple products agree. The proof writes the
triple product in polar form, fixes one index ℓ, and shows that the angles
of the two Gram matrices differ by a gauge φ_j − φ_k with
φ_j = θ_ℓj − θ′_ℓj.

Example 2.5 takes the n-cycle frame graph: vectors e_j + e_{j+1} and
e_n + z e_1 with |z| = 1. The only nonzero products of distinct vectors are
the norms, adjacent overlaps and the n-cycle product z, so for n > 3
different z give projectively inequivalent sequences with the same triple
products.

Theorem 4.5 states that a sequence is determined up to projective unitary
equivalence by its 3-products when the cycle space of its frame graph is
spanned by 3-cycles; Example 4.6 notes that this holds for chordal frame
graphs.

The paper characterises equivalence between two given sequences. A
statement that real triple products imply a real realisation was not found
in v1.

This note paraphrases the cited statements. No article, figure or extended
excerpt is redistributed.
