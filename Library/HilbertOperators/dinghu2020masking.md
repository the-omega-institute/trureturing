# Ding and Hu, Masking quantum information on hyperdisks

F. Ding and X. Hu, Phys. Rev. A 102, 042404 (2020), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.102.042404; arXiv:1909.11256v3. Quotations are from the arXiv v3 TeX source `masking.tex`.

For an orthonormal basis $\mathcal B=\{\phi_j\}_{j<m}$ of a subspace, $\boldsymbol r_{\mathcal B}(\psi)=(|\langle\phi_0|\psi\rangle|,\dots,|\langle\phi_{m-1}|\psi\rangle|)$. Definition 1 calls a set $\mathcal S$ of pure states a hyperdisk if some orthonormal basis $\mathcal B$ of $\operatorname{span}\mathcal S$ and a constant vector $\boldsymbol r$ with strictly positive entries satisfy $\boldsymbol r_{\mathcal B}(\psi)=\boldsymbol r$ for all $\psi\in\mathcal S$ and $\boldsymbol r_{\mathcal B}(\xi)\ne\boldsymbol r$ for all other pure states $\xi$. Definition 4 calls $\mathcal C$ a regular subset of the hyperdisk $\mathcal S$ if $\operatorname{span}\mathcal C\cap\mathcal S=\mathcal C$. A subset of a hyperdisk is the union of the hyperdisks it contains, and its optimal cover number, when finite, is the least number of hyperdisks needed to cover it.

The conclusion states:

> Nevertheless, it is an open question for a nondegenerate masking machine  whether the number of hyperdisks in the set of maskable states is always finite. A related open question is whether a regular subset of a hyperdisk can be covered with finite number of hyperdisks.
