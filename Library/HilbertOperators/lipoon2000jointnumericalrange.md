# Joint numerical range and real-plane replacement

Chi-Kwong Li and Yiu-Tung Poon, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 21(2),
668–678 (2000). The theorem numbers below were checked in the author-hosted
manuscript.

Theorem 1.2 characterizes convexity of the kth joint numerical range by
whether every point from the convex hull of rank-k orthogonal projections
has the same measurement values as a rank-k projection. At k = 1 the
convex hull consists of the positive trace-one matrices. Theorem 3.1 gives
convexity for a family simultaneously conjugate into the real span of the
diagonal matrix units and the symmetric nearest-neighbor matrix units.
For n = 2 this span is all real symmetric 2 × 2 matrices. [Author manuscript, pp. 2 and 5–6](https://cklixx.people.wm.edu/poon-1.pdf)

For a real isometric inclusion C of the complementary plane into four
coordinates, the four compressed coordinate effects CᵀEᵢᵢC are real
symmetric. The two theorems give a rank-one projection on the complexified
plane with all four expectations preserved. The Lean proof explicitly
diagonalizes the real part of the compression and uses a relative phase
of i to reproduce the two eigenvalue contributions. The four-dimensional
support count and the common row gauge explain why this plane is available.
