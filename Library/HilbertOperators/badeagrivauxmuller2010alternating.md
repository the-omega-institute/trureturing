# Inclination and cyclic projection rates

For closed linear subspaces M_1,...,M_N of a complex Hilbert space,
with N at least two and common intersection M, Section 3 defines the
inclination through the maximum individual distance divided by distance
to M. Theorem 4.1 in Section 4.1 relates vanishing inclination to failure
of uniform convergence of the cyclic product of their actual orthogonal
projections.

In the proof of implication (6) to (3), the lemma labelled lemma:4 and
the displayed estimate labelled eq:norm bound the initial residual of
every listed member by N squared times the centered one-cycle energy
loss. Section 4.2 gives the corresponding quantitative estimate for a
product visiting the family in m steps: its norm on the common
orthogonal complement is at most sqrt(1-ell squared divided by m squared).
Here ell is an admissible inclination lower bound. This is a sufficient
estimate; no optimality of its constant is asserted.

An explicit residual-observation formulation avoids an infimum over an
empty complement: for every vector x, some listed subspace U satisfies
ell times norm(x-P_M x) at most norm(x-P_U x). For any such ell with
0<ell<=1, the checked cyclic energy estimate has squared-error factor
(1-ell squared divided by m squared)^n. A maximal initial member residual
and the complete-cycle displacement bound give the converse from a
uniform geometric rate.

The checked statements use complete RCLike Hilbert spaces and a nonempty
finite ordered list. Real scalars, singleton lists, repetitions, zero
spaces, top and bottom subspaces, n=0, ell=1 and q=0 are included by the
proofs. These clauses are not attributed verbatim to the paper's complex
N>=2 setup. No article text or external Lean source is copied.
