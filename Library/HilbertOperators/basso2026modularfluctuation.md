## Finite Gibbs ladders

Basso constructs boost-energy counting statistics in the type-III horizon
algebra and proves a coherent-state uncertainty relation. Section VI asks
about arbitrary unitaries. The finite Gibbs ladder theorem gives a
finite-dimensional counterexample to a strictly positive forward
variance bound depending only on the temperature and forward mean.

## Forward variance for arbitrary unitaries

In the exact quasifree boost-invariant reference model of Sections II and
III, fix \(\beta>0\), \(a>0\), and an affine interval \((-d,0)\) ending at the
bifurcation surface. There are unitaries \(U_j\) in the von Neumann algebra
generated by data supported in that interval for which the forward
counting law has mean exactly \(a\) and variance tending to zero. Its
Araki entropy is \(\beta a\) by Eq. (30). The counting generator is the
reference boost generator \(L_\xi\) of the full past half-horizon.
The intrinsic modular generator of an interval algebra and transfer in an
exact curved geometry require separate identifications.

Use the real boost coordinate \(v=-\kappa^{-1}\log(-\kappa\lambda)\) on
\(\lambda<0\). Equation (7) acts by \(F(v)\mapsto F(v-s)\). In the positive
frequency coordinates \(g_F(\omega)=\sqrt{\omega/\pi}\,\widehat F(\omega)\),
using the Fourier sign \(e^{i\omega v}\), the symplectic form is
\(2\operatorname{Im}\langle g_F,g_G\rangle\) and the reference covariance
is \(C(\omega)=\coth(\beta\omega/2)\). To see the covariance, transforming
the distributional kernel of Eq. (8), including both coordinate
Jacobians, gives the boost-invariant kernel proportional to
\(-\sinh^{-2}(\kappa(v-v'-i0)/2)\). The KMS ratio of negative to positive
frequency is \(e^{-\beta\omega}\), and the symplectic form fixes their
difference. Their sum gives \(C\). The dynamics multiplies positive
frequencies by \(e^{is\omega}\). The transverse profile can be any fixed
real, smooth, compactly supported profile of unit norm.

Choose a smooth unit vector \(g\) supported in a frequency band of
width \(2\varepsilon\) centered at \(\nu>0\). To realize \(zg\) for complex
\(z\), divide the positive-frequency profile by \(\sqrt{\omega/\pi}\) and
extend to negative frequencies by conjugate reflection. The inverse
Fourier transform gives real Schwartz data. Cutting these data off in
boost time approximates them in the norm \(\|h\|_C=\|C^{1/2}h\|\).
The Weyl relations and the Gaussian state give strong convergence on the
span of Weyl vectors, then on the GNS Hilbert space by the unitary bound.
Thus the full half-horizon algebra contains the one-mode Weyl plane
\(W(zg)\).

The regular one-mode representation has von Neumann algebra
\(B(\ell^2(\mathbb N))\) acting with multiplicity. The restricted state is
Gaussian, with \(\omega_0(W(zg))=\exp(-c|z|^2/2)\) and
\(c=\langle g,Cg\rangle\). Its number-state weights are
\((1-r)r^k\), \(r=(c-1)/(c+1)\). This follows by summing the number-state
displacement diagonal against the geometric weights using the Laguerre
generating series. Let \(U_N\) cycle \(0,\ldots,N-1\) by one step upward
with wraparound and fix all higher levels. Under a rotation at constant
frequency \(\nu\), the energy transfers at the non-wraparound,
wraparound and fixed levels are respectively
\(\nu,-(N-1)\nu,0\), with probabilities
\(1-r^{N-1},(1-r)r^{N-1},r^N\). The second moment about \(\nu\) is
\[
  \nu^2\big[N^2(1-r)r^{N-1}+r^N\big].
\]

The difference \(U_N-1\) is the Weyl integral with kernel
\(K_N(z)=\pi^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}(W(z)^*(U_N-1))\) in the standard
oscillator representation. This is Schwartz since each matrix coefficient
between fixed number states is a polynomial times a Gaussian. For
a covariance-norm differentiable Weyl path \(u(s)\) with \(u(0)=u\)
and \(u'(0)=w\), the derivative on the reference vector is
\[
  W(u)\big(i\sigma(u,w)\Omega_0/2+i\Phi(w)\Omega_0\big).
\]
Here \(\|\Phi(w)\Omega_0\|=\|w\|_C\) by the Gaussian characteristic
function. The formula follows by expanding the Weyl product and using
\(\|(W(h)-1)\Omega_0\|\le\|h\|_C\).
For the actual boost and the constant-frequency model the tangent
difference at \(zg\) is \(iz(\omega-\nu)g\). Its derivative error is
bounded by \(\varepsilon(|z|\sqrt c+|z|^2)\). Integrating against the
Schwartz kernel gives graph-norm control of \(U_N\Omega_0\) for \(L_\xi\).
The same domain argument applies to \(U_N^*\) with kernel
\(\overline{K_N(-z)}\).

For \(\nu\in[a/2,3a/2]\) choose a translated family of band profiles.
Choose the bandwidth after \(N\), with lower band edge above \(a/4\).
Then \(r\le e^{-\beta a/4}<1\) uniformly. The frozen second moment
about \(\nu\) tends uniformly to zero, and the bandwidth can make the
graph error smaller than \(1/N\). For each \(N\), cut off the real
profiles for \(g\) and \(ig\) in boost time. The fixed-\(N\) family is
uniformly Schwartz in the band center. Smooth cutoffs converge uniformly
in \(H^2\), which controls the covariance norm and the covariance norm
after multiplication by \(\omega\). Normalize the second profile by
\(2/\sigma\) of the cutoff pair, a factor tending uniformly to one.
The normalized plane has the standard Weyl multiplier, so the same
Weyl integral gives a unitary even when the cutoff covariance is
anisotropic. The derivative bound with a polynomial in \(|z|\) and
uniform strong continuity on the compact family yield convergence in
graph norm and continuity of the actual mean in \(\nu\). Choose the
cutoff large enough for a further error at most \(1/N\).

The affine interval \((-d,0)\) becomes a boost half-line. A single
boost translates the entire fixed-\(N\) cutoff family into that half-line.
It preserves the reference vector and commutes with the energy spectral
projections, hence preserves the counting laws. The mean differs
uniformly from \(\nu\) by a quantity tending to zero. For large \(N\),
the endpoint means bracket \(a\), so continuity selects a center with
mean exactly \(a\). The variance is bounded by the second moment about
that center. A finite boost-time window bounded away from the
bifurcation surface remains a separate localization problem.

For any forward sequence \(P_j\) converging weakly to \(\delta_a\),
Eq. (29) gives the inverse law \(Q_j\) by
\(Q_j(dq)=e^{\beta q}P_j(-dq)\). For each \(R>0\) it follows that
\[
  Q_j((R,\infty))
  =1-\int_{q\ge -R} e^{-\beta q}\,dP_j(q)
  \longrightarrow 1-e^{-\beta a}.
\]
The integrand is bounded and continuous at \(a\). The inverse negative
first moment is bounded by \(1/(e\beta)\) via its exponential identity.
The inverse positive first moment is at least \(R Q_j((R,\infty))\).
Letting \(R\) grow gives divergence of the inverse mean, even as the
forward variance vanishes. The finite Gibbs theorem is kernel-checked.
The half-horizon derivation uses the analytic Weyl and KMS ingredients
above. Priority of the horizon construction remains unverified.
