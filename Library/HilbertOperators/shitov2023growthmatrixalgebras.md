## Verified locator

Page 1 of viXra:2308.0028v1, Remark 1 and Question 2, gives the exact-product convention and asks for the least universal \(w(n)\) for sets of complex matrices. Theorem 3 of that version gives \(n^2+2n-4\). The abstract of the journal article under the same title gives \(n^2+2n-3\). The locator for the question refers to the versioned preprint. [Versioned PDF](https://vixra.org/pdf/2308.0028v1.pdf#page=1) · [Journal version](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00023-026-01728-6)

## Formal correspondence

For \(n=3\), the generators form an arbitrary subset of \(M_3(\mathbb C)\). A word of degree \(k\) uses exactly \(k\) factors. Seven is the least positive universal degree for sets whose word span is full at some positive degree.
